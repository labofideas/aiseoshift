The title tag (`<title>`) defines the name of a web page. It appears as the clickable headline in search results, in the browser tab, and when the page is shared. It is one of the oldest and still most important on-page signals.

## Why it matters

The title tag tells both search engines and people what the page is about in a single line. Engines weight it heavily when matching a page to a query, and users decide whether to click based largely on it. A weak title can sink an otherwise strong page.

## How to write a good one

- **Lead with the primary topic.** Put the main term near the front where it carries the most weight.
- **Keep it within the display limit.** Roughly 50 to 60 characters shows without truncation on most results.
- **Match the query's intent.** A title that mirrors how people phrase the search earns more clicks.
- **Make each title unique.** Duplicate titles across pages confuse engines about which to rank.

## Titles and AI search

Google often rewrites titles when it judges the page title a poor match for the query, so a clear, accurate title reduces the chance of an unwanted rewrite. AI answer engines also use the title as a strong signal of what a page covers when deciding whether it answers a query. A precise, descriptive title helps a page get selected as a source.

Avoid keyword stuffing and vague labels. "Pricing" is weaker than "Managed IT Pricing: Per-Seat and Per-Device Models." Pair the title with a strong [meta description](/glossary/meta-description/) to control how the result reads.
