The Knowledge Graph is Google's structured database of entities and the relationships between them. Entities include people, places, organizations, and concepts, and the graph records how they connect: who founded what, what is located where, which works belong to whom.

## What it is

Rather than storing pages, the Knowledge Graph stores facts about things and links those facts together. It powers the entity panels that appear beside search results, the boxes that summarize a person, company, or place. Each entity has an identity in the graph that is distinct from any single web page describing it.

## Why it matters for AI search

AI search systems lean on entity data to verify who and what a source is before trusting it. When an engine decides whether to cite a brand or an author, a confirmed entity in the Knowledge Graph supplies a reliable anchor for identity and authority. A business that exists clearly as an entity is easier for an engine to recognize, disambiguate, and stand behind than one known only through scattered mentions.

## How to apply it

Establish a clean, consistent entity for your organization and key authors: a stable name, consistent details across the web, and structured data that states the relationships explicitly. Strong, unambiguous entity signals make a brand easier for the graph to model.
