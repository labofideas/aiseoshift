A backlink is a hyperlink on one website that points to a page on another website. Search engines treat each backlink as a signal that someone found the linked page useful enough to reference, which is why backlinks remain one of the strongest off-page ranking factors.

## Why backlinks matter

Google's original PageRank algorithm was built on the idea that a link is a vote. That logic still holds, with refinements. A link from a respected, topically relevant site carries far more weight than a link from a low-quality directory or a paid link farm.

AI answer engines extend the same idea. When an engine decides which sources to cite, the breadth and quality of sites linking to a page is part of how it gauges whether the page is a trustworthy source rather than an unknown one.

## What makes a backlink valuable

Not all links are equal. The signals that separate a strong backlink from a weak one:

- **Topical relevance.** A link from a site in your subject area counts more than an unrelated one.
- **Authority of the linking domain.** Links from established, trusted sites pass more value.
- **Editorial placement.** A link embedded in real content beats a link in a footer or a sponsored block.
- **Anchor text.** The clickable text gives context, though over-optimized exact-match anchors look manipulative.

## Common mistakes

Buying links at scale, swapping links in bulk, and chasing volume over relevance all invite penalties or simply get discounted. The durable approach is earning links by publishing content other sites genuinely want to reference.
