A meta description is a short HTML attribute that summarizes the content of a page. Search engines often display it beneath the title in results, where it acts as the page's sales pitch to a searcher deciding whether to click.

## Is it a ranking factor?

Not directly. Google has stated for years that the meta description does not influence rankings on its own. What it influences is click-through rate, and the engagement that follows a click is part of how engines judge whether a result satisfied the query.

So the meta description earns its place indirectly: a compelling one lifts clicks, and a page that consistently earns clicks and satisfies users holds its position better.

## How to write one

- **Stay within the display limit.** Around 150 to 160 characters shows without truncation.
- **Describe the value, not just the topic.** Tell the reader what they will get.
- **Include the query naturally.** Engines bold matching terms, which draws the eye.
- **Write a unique description per page.** Duplicates waste the opportunity and look careless.

## When engines rewrite it

Google frequently replaces the meta description with a snippet pulled from the page body when it judges that text a better match for the query. You cannot force your description to show, but writing a strong, relevant one raises the odds it is used and gives engines good material to pull from either way.

Treat it as part of a pair with the [title tag](/glossary/title-tag/): the title earns attention, the description earns the click.
