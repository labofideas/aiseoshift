A long-tail keyword is a search query made of several words that targets a specific need, such as "lightweight waterproof hiking boots for women" rather than the broad head term "boots." The name comes from the long tail of the search demand curve, where countless low-volume queries collectively make up the majority of all searches.

## Why they matter

Individually, long-tail queries might draw only a handful of searches a month. Together they account for a large share of all search traffic. They are easier to rank for because fewer sites target them, and they tend to convert better because the searcher knows exactly what they want. Someone searching "buy size 9 trail running shoes" is closer to purchasing than someone searching "shoes."

## How to find them

Mine Google autocomplete, the "People also ask" box, and "related searches" at the bottom of the results. Question phrases starting with how, what, and best often signal long-tail intent. Tools used in [keyword research](/glossary/keyword-research/) can filter by word count and difficulty to surface them at scale.

## How to use them

Build content around clusters of related long-tail queries that share one intent, rather than a separate thin page for each phrase. A thorough guide can rank for hundreds of variations at once. This approach also positions pages well for voice search and AI answer engines, which favor natural, specific phrasing.
