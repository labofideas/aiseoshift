A landing page is where a visitor "lands" after clicking a link in an ad, email, or search result. In conversion marketing the term means something narrower: a focused page built around one objective, such as capturing an email or selling a product, with distractions stripped away.

## What sets it apart

Unlike a homepage that serves many audiences, a dedicated landing page has a single call to action and usually removes the main navigation so visitors stay focused. Every element, from the headline to the form, supports one decision. This focus is why landing pages tend to convert better than general pages for paid campaigns.

## Core elements

- **A clear, benefit-led headline** that matches the ad or link that brought the visitor.
- **A single, obvious call to action** repeated where it makes sense.
- **Supporting proof** such as testimonials, reviews, or trust badges.
- **A short form** that asks only for what you truly need.

## Why it matters

For [pay-per-click](/glossary/pay-per-click/) campaigns, landing page quality feeds directly into Google's Quality Score, which affects both ad position and cost. A relevant, fast page lowers your cost per conversion. Message match also matters: if the ad promises a free trial, the page should lead with that free trial, not a generic pitch.

## How to improve it

Test one variable at a time, watch the conversion rate, and keep load times low, since slow pages bleed conversions before visitors ever read the offer.
