A zero-click search is one that resolves on the results page itself. The user types a query, reads the answer Google shows directly (a featured snippet, an AI Overview, or a Knowledge Panel), and never clicks through to a website. Roughly 65 percent of Google searches were zero-click as of 2024, a share that AI Overviews has continued to push higher.

## Why it happens

Search engines increasingly try to answer the question on the page rather than just point at sources. Simple factual queries (a definition, a conversion, a date) rarely need a click at all, and richer surfaces like AI Overviews now handle more complex questions the same way. The convenience for users comes at the cost of traffic for publishers.

## What it means for sites

The old scoreboard counted clicks. Zero-click search breaks that link between visibility and visits, because a page can shape the answer a user reads without ever receiving the visit. The value shifts toward being the named source: an AI citation or a snippet attribution that builds recognition even when no click follows.

## How to adapt

Treat citation as a goal in its own right, not a consolation for a lost click. Write passages that answer cleanly enough to be quoted, and strengthen brand and author signals so attribution actually registers with the reader who stays on the results page.
