Organic traffic is the stream of visitors who land on your site by clicking an unpaid listing in a search engine. If someone searches "best budget espresso machine," scrolls past the ads, and clicks your guide, that visit counts as organic. It is reported separately from paid, direct, referral, and social traffic.

## Why it matters

Organic traffic is often the most cost-effective channel because you do not pay per click the way you do with [pay-per-click](/glossary/pay-per-click/) advertising. Once a page ranks, it can attract visitors for months or years with no ongoing ad spend. Strong organic performance also signals that your content matches what people search for, which compounds over time as pages earn links and authority.

## How it is measured

Analytics tools attribute a visit to "organic search" based on the referrer and source. In Google Analytics 4, it appears under the Organic Search channel. Google Search Console reports the upstream picture: impressions, clicks, average position, and click-through rate for the queries that surface your pages.

## How to grow it

Publish content that answers real queries, target a healthy mix of head and long-tail terms, and improve pages that already rank on page two. Earn relevant backlinks, fix technical issues that block indexing, and keep the experience fast. Track query-level data in Search Console to spot pages losing position before traffic drops noticeably.
