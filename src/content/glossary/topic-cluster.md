A topic cluster is a way of organizing content around a single subject. One broad pillar page covers the topic at a high level, and a set of supporting articles each handle a narrower subtopic in depth. Internal links tie the supporting pieces back to the pillar and to each other, so the whole group reads as one connected body of work.

## What it is

The pillar answers the big question, and the cluster pages answer the specific follow-ups. A pillar on local SEO, for example, might link out to separate pages on Google Business Profile, review management, and local schema. Each cluster page targets its own queries while reinforcing that the site covers the parent topic thoroughly.

## Why it matters

Both Google and AI engines try to gauge whether a site has genuine depth on a subject before trusting it as a source. A well-linked cluster demonstrates that depth across many angles rather than in a single thin article. For answer engines deciding which sources to cite, that breadth of coverage is a signal the site is a reliable authority on the topic.

## How to apply it

Pick a pillar topic broad enough to support several articles, map the real questions people ask underneath it, and write a focused page for each. Link every cluster page to the pillar and connect related cluster pages where it helps the reader. For deeper coverage, see [our content strategy guide](/seo/content-strategy/).
