A/B testing, also called split testing, is a method for comparing two variants of something to learn which drives better results. You show version A (the control) to one group of visitors and version B (the variant) to another, then measure which produces more conversions, clicks, or whatever goal you set.

## How it works

Traffic is split randomly so the two groups are comparable. You change one element at a time, for example a headline, button color, or form length, and let the test run until you collect enough data to trust the result. Testing a single variable keeps the outcome interpretable, since a multi-change test cannot tell you which change caused the lift.

## Statistical significance

A result is only worth acting on when it reaches statistical significance, commonly a 95% confidence level. That means there is roughly a 5% chance the difference is random noise. Ending a test early because an early lead looks promising is a frequent error, since small samples swing wildly. Most tools recommend a minimum sample size and a run of at least one to two full weeks to cover weekly patterns.

## Why it matters

A/B testing is the backbone of [conversion rate optimization](/glossary/conversion-rate-optimization/). It replaces guesswork and opinion with evidence, so changes to a page are grounded in how real users behave rather than what the team assumes they prefer.
