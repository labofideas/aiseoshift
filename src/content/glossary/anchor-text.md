Anchor text is the visible, clickable wording inside a hyperlink. In the HTML `<a href="...">best running shoes</a>`, the phrase "best running shoes" is the anchor. It gives both readers and search engines a quick signal about the topic of the destination page.

## Why it matters

Search engines read anchor text as a description of the target page. A link pointing to a guide on title tags with the anchor "title tag best practices" reinforces what that page covers. This applies to inbound links from other sites and to your own [internal linking](/glossary/internal-linking/), where descriptive anchors help spread context across your pages.

## Types of anchor text

- **Exact match.** The anchor is the exact target keyword, such as "keyword research." Useful in small doses.
- **Partial match.** A variation, like "how to do keyword research."
- **Branded.** Uses a brand name, such as "Ahrefs."
- **Generic.** Phrases like "click here" or "read more" that carry little topical meaning.
- **Naked URL.** The raw address, such as example.com.

## Avoiding over-optimization

A natural backlink profile shows a mix of these types. When too many external links use the same exact-match commercial anchor, it looks manipulative and can trigger a Penguin-style devaluation. For links you control, write anchors that describe the destination plainly. For links you earn, let other sites phrase them how they choose rather than dictating exact keywords.
