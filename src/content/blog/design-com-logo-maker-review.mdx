Creating a logo that looks professional can take more time than expected, especially when you’re starting a business and don’t have a designer on hand. You need something that fits your brand, looks good across different platforms, and is easy to customize without spending hours learning design software.

We tried Design.com’s Logo Maker to see how well it handles that process. For our test, we used Healthy Smoothie, a fictional smoothie business, to explore the logo options, editing tools, AI features, and branded previews. We also looked at how easy it was to adapt a design for different uses, from business cards to websites and storefront signage.

Here’s what we found, including the features we liked, where the tool could be better, and what to know about pricing before getting started.

## Customizing the Healthy Smoothie Logo

Finding a design we liked was only the beginning. We then explored the editor to see how much we could change without having to start over.

### Change the Background Color

<figure class="post-figure">
<img src="/images/posts/design-com-logo-maker-review-inline-12.webp" alt="Design.com editor with the Edit Background panel open beside the Healthy Smoothie logo" width="1200" height="480" loading="lazy" />

We started with the background because it can affect how a logo looks against different surfaces. A light background might work well for a website, while a darker one could help a logo stand out on promotional materials.

We could explore different background colors and compare how they affected the overall appearance.

<figure class="post-figure">
<img src="/images/posts/design-com-logo-maker-review-inline-13.webp" alt="Healthy Smoothie logo on a yellow to pink gradient background" width="600" height="520" loading="lazy" />

For Healthy Smoothie, we would lean toward a clean, light background to keep the design fresh and make the logo easy to read.

It's worth checking the contrast before settling on a color. A background that looks attractive on the editor screen may not work as well when the logo is displayed at a smaller size.

### Adjust the Logo Colors

<figure class="post-figure">
<img src="/images/posts/design-com-logo-maker-review-inline-14.webp" alt="Design.com Edit Logo panel with color swatches for the Healthy Smoothie logo" width="1200" height="483" loading="lazy" />

Next, we explored the logo color settings. This is where we could move the design closer to the visual identity we had in mind for Healthy Smoothie.

For a smoothie business, greens can suggest fresh ingredients, while oranges, yellows, and reds can bring fruit to mind. We would use these colors selectively rather than combine too many shades in one logo.

We liked having color customization available during the editing process. It gives you room to experiment with different brand directions without having to choose an entirely new design every time you want to try a different palette.

### Explore Typography Options

<figure class="post-figure">
<img src="/images/posts/design-com-logo-maker-review-inline-15.webp" alt="Design.com font list open beside the Healthy Smoothie logo" width="1200" height="480" loading="lazy" />
</figure>

Typography can change the personality of a logo just as much as its icon or colors. A rounded font may give Healthy Smoothie a friendly, casual feel, while a simpler sans-serif typeface can create a cleaner, more modern look.

We explored the available typography options to see how the business name could look in different styles. One thing we would prioritize is readability. The name should remain clear on a small social media profile image and when printed on a business card.

We also recommend considering the length of your business name. Healthy Smoothie has two words, so the font and spacing need to work together without making the logo look crowded.

### Add or Adjust Design Elements

<figure class="post-figure">
<img src="/images/posts/design-com-logo-maker-review-inline-16.webp" alt="Design.com shapes panel showing smoothie-themed graphics" width="1200" height="475" loading="lazy" />

We also checked the elements customization options. These are useful when you want to adjust the visual details of a design or explore ways to make it more relevant to your business.

For Healthy Smoothie, imagery of fruit, leaves, or a simple smoothie cup could help communicate what the business offers. However, we'd avoid adding too many decorative elements. A logo needs to be recognizable at a glance, and too many details can make it harder to use at smaller sizes.

The best approach is to make a few deliberate changes, then compare the updated design with the original. That makes it easier to see whether each edit improves the logo or simply adds clutter.

## Design.com Logo Maker Features: What Stood Out to Us

After going through the logo creation and editing workflow, a few features stood out as particularly useful.

### A large selection of logo designs

One of the main benefits is the variety of designs available to explore. Instead of committing to a single concept immediately, you can compare different logo styles to see which direction suits your business.

For Healthy Smoothie, we'd look for designs that communicate freshness without relying on overly complicated illustrations. Browsing multiple options makes it easier to compare icon styles, typography, and layouts before choosing a favorite.

### Customization without advanced design skills

The editor brings together several important adjustments, including background colors, logo colors, typography, and graphic elements. We like this approach because it gives beginners room to refine a design without having to learn a professional design application first.

You can also explore features such as curved text and gradient colors, as well as a library of vector shapes.

### AI-assisted editing

The AI chatbot is another useful addition. It gives you an alternative to manually adjusting every detail and lets you describe a change in plain language.

We'd use it to explore creative directions or make targeted refinements, then check the output before deciding whether to keep the changes. It works best as part of the editing process rather than a replacement for reviewing the final design.

### Branding previews and additional tools

We also see value in being able to preview a logo in different contexts. Business cards, websites, and storefront signs each have different requirements, and mockups can help you spot potential readability or layout issues early.

Design.com also offers more than logo creation, with tools for business cards, social media graphics, websites, flyers, and other branded materials. This broader set of tools can be useful if you want to keep your branding consistent as your business grows.

## How Much Does Design.com Logo Maker Cost?

Design.com has a free option and paid subscriptions. The right choice depends on whether you need a basic logo or want access to a broader collection of branding tools.

| Plan | Monthly Billing | Annual Billing |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | $0 |
| **Starter** | $9/mo | $3/mo |
| **Value** | $14/mo | $4/mo |
| **Premium** | $19/mo | $5/mo |

Here's what to consider when choosing a plan:

- Free: A good starting point if you want to explore the tool and use an eligible free logo. Not every design is available for free download.
- Starter: Suits users who want a logo and access to additional design tools.
- Value: Adds website-building features to the Starter offering.
- Premium: Includes the Value features, plus tools such as a link-in-bio creator and digital business cards.

Design.com's purchasing information explains that paid subscriptions include logo files in formats such as PNG, JPG, SVG, EPS, and PDF. The plans also provide access to editing tools and other design resources.

One detail to keep in mind is that a subscription is tied to a particular logo. You can create variations of that logo, but creating a completely different logo requires an additional purchase.

Before paying, check the current checkout price and the terms of the specific plan you choose. Annual billing has a lower monthly equivalent, but it requires a longer upfront commitment.
