Ten of the largest news publishers on the internet received a combined 112 million monthly US visits from Google Search at their peak. By January 2026, that number had fallen below 50 million. A drop of more than 55 percent in under two years. Google AI Overviews answers the query before the user clicks. The traffic that used to flow to publisher pages is now staying inside Google.

On June 4, 2026, Google launched Search Profiles.

The timing is not a coincidence. Google Discover, the personalized content feed in the Google app, now accounts for approximately 68 percent of all Google referrals to news publishers, up from a fraction of that figure two years ago. Search Profiles are built specifically for Discover. They give publishers and creators a claimable page, a follow mechanism, and a direct line into followers' Discover feeds that does not depend on keyword rankings at all.

Whether that is a fair trade for the organic search traffic AI Overviews is absorbing is a legitimate question. But the mechanics of Search Profiles are useful for any publisher operating in 2026, and the Knowledge Panel connection has specific implications for AI SEO that go beyond Discover visibility.

## What a Search Profile Is

A Search Profile is a dedicated, claimable page inside Google Search and Google Discover that aggregates a creator or publisher's content across platforms. Think of it as a lightweight publisher hub hosted at `profile.google.com` that surfaces inside Google's own interfaces.

Each profile includes:

- Header banner image, avatar, bio, and handle
- Website link and links to connected social platforms (YouTube, Instagram, X, TikTok)
- A pinned carousel of up to 8 short-form videos from Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube
- A latest posts grid showing recent articles and social content
- A "Follow on Google" button
- A shareable direct URL

Google Product Manager Ibrahim Badr described them at launch: "Search profiles are a dedicated, shareable space to highlight content across platforms, and help audiences find accurate, up-to-date information about sources on Search. People can easily follow sources from their profile, so they're more likely to see that content on Discover, found on the home screen of the Google app."

The profile is accessible three ways: by tapping a creator name on a Discover card, through a Knowledge Panel on mobile search, or via a direct link.

## Who Can Claim One

At launch, Search Profiles are available to US-based publishers and creators meeting these thresholds:

| Platform | Minimum followers required |
|---|---|
| YouTube | 100,000 |
| Instagram | 100,000 |
| X (Twitter) | 100,000 |
| TikTok | 300,000 |

The follower count is evaluated per platform, not combined. A creator with 60,000 on YouTube and 60,000 on Instagram does not qualify by combining the two. They need 100,000 on a single platform.

Age requirement is 18 or older, with a public account, on a platform with content that complies with Google's policies.

To claim: visit `creators.google/profile` and connect a supported social account. In some cases Google has already auto-generated a profile for entities in the Knowledge Graph. Eligible creators may simply need to claim what already exists.

One important caveat from researchers who tracked the pre-launch beta: eligibility enforcement has not been perfectly consistent. Some profiles from the private pilot appear to predate or fall below the stated follower thresholds. As the feature scales, eligibility rules may be adjusted.

## The Secret Pilot Most Publishers Did Not Know About

Before the June 4 public launch, Google ran an invitation-only pilot with exactly 54 US-based publishers from March through May 2026. The researchers who uncovered it, Sylvain Deaure and Damien Andell of 1492.vision, analyzed nearly 47,000 publisher profiles across seven languages to identify the cohort.

The 54 pilot publishers had access to features unavailable on standard profiles: custom banner images, configurable links shelves, the ability to pin posts, and control over the display order of social links and content tabs. Approximately half of the cohort were regional newspapers and local TV stations.

Standard profiles launched to the wider public do not have these layout controls. What you get is the profile basics: bio, avatar, banner, links, and the follow mechanism. Full customization, for now, is limited to the pilot cohort.

## The Discover Mechanism: How Following Actually Works

The Follow button is the most strategically important element of the entire feature.

When a user taps "Follow on Google" on a Search Profile, they are adding that source as a personalization signal in their Discover feed. Google's Discover algorithm is AI-powered and built on a combination of topic interest signals, engagement history, and explicit follows. An explicit follow increases the probability that the creator's new content surfaces in that follower's Discover feed.

This matters because Discover has become the dominant Google referral channel for publishers. Between 2023 and 2025, Discover's share of Google referrals to news publishers climbed to approximately 68 percent. Traditional Google Search referrals to the same publishers fell from 51 percent to 27 percent of total referrals over the same period.

The follow is not a ranking factor for keyword search. It has no effect on where a page appears in a web search result. It is a Discover personalization signal exclusively. But for content-driven publishers and creators, that is where the audience attention is moving anyway.

Stuart Forrest, global director of SEO at Bauer Media, confirmed the directional shift: "We're definitely moving into the era of lower clicks and lower referral traffic for publishers." Discover follows offer something keyword rankings cannot: a subscriber-like relationship where the audience receives content directly rather than discovering it through a search query.

## The Knowledge Panel Connection: The AI SEO Angle

Here is where Search Profiles become relevant to AI SEO beyond just Discover traffic.

Google's official documentation states: claiming a Search Profile may trigger the creation of a Knowledge Panel for eligible creators and publishers who do not already have one. For those who already have a Knowledge Panel, claiming a profile enhances it with updated avatar, latest content, and a direct profile link.

Knowledge Panels are auto-generated by Google from the Knowledge Graph. An entity with a Knowledge Panel is a recognized entity in Google's semantic layer. That matters for AI features because Google AI Overviews and AI Mode draw citations from pages and entities that are well-established in the Knowledge Graph. An entity with a Knowledge Panel is more explicitly "known" to Google's systems than one without.

This does not guarantee AI citation. But entity recognition in the Knowledge Graph is a documented factor in how AI systems assess source authority. For publishers and creators who have struggled to obtain a Knowledge Panel through traditional means, claiming a Search Profile and meeting the eligibility threshold is a more direct path to that entity record.

Semrush noted this plainly: "Google notes that you may get a Knowledge Panel by claiming a search profile, which is good news for brands that have so far struggled to obtain a Knowledge Panel because those features contribute to better visibility."

Two specific limitations are worth noting:

**The follower wall locks out most publishers.** The 100,000 follower requirement on a single platform is a meaningful threshold. Many specialist publishers, local news outlets, and B2B content sites will not meet it regardless of their content quality or audience depth. Google has said it plans to expand capabilities but has not confirmed a lower threshold.

**Analytics are nascent.** 95 percent of publishers in the pre-launch pilot had no reliable way to measure whether profile follows were generating referral traffic. An Insights beta, powered by Search Console, was spotted in development but is not yet widely available. Publishers considering Search Profiles as a traffic strategy should not expect clean measurement data in the near term.

## What to Do If You Qualify

If you meet the eligibility threshold, the case for claiming a profile is straightforward. The downside is zero and the potential upside is meaningful.

**Step one:** Go to `creators.google/profile` and check whether a profile already exists for your entity. Google may have auto-generated one.

**Step two:** Connect at least one supported social account (YouTube, Instagram, X, or TikTok) with the required follower count.

**Step three:** Complete the profile: upload a header image, write a bio, add your website and social links, and configure which content surfaces in the pinned carousel.

**Step four:** Treat the Follow count as a metric worth tracking. Audience growth on Search Profiles is a new visibility signal that did not exist before June 2026.

**Step five:** If you do not already have a Knowledge Panel, monitor whether claiming the profile triggers one. If it does, verify and claim the Knowledge Panel through Google's standard process.

## The Bigger Shift

Search Engine Land framed it directly: "SEO's new goal in 2026 is recognition, not rankings." Search Profiles fit that framing. They are not a tool for ranking higher on specific queries. They are a tool for becoming a recognized entity in Google's systems: in the Knowledge Graph, in Discover personalization, and in the broader semantic layer that AI features draw from.

The publishers who treat Search Profiles as a Discover traffic play are thinking too narrowly. The entity recognition they build by claiming and maintaining a profile compounds over time in ways that pure keyword ranking does not.
