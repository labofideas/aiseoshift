The short version: doing SEO means making your website easy for search engines and AI answer engines to find, understand, trust, and recommend. You do it in five moves. Pick the questions your audience searches, publish genuinely useful pages that answer them, structure those pages so they are easy to read and extract, earn trust through real authority, and measure what gets found. Everything below expands each piece into a direct answer you can act on.

This is written as an FAQ on purpose. These are the exact questions people ask, and increasingly the exact questions they ask an AI assistant. Each answer stands on its own, so you can jump to the one you need.

## What is SEO in one sentence?

SEO (search engine optimization) is the practice of improving a website so it appears when people search for what it offers, both in traditional search results and in AI-generated answers. It works by aligning your content with what searchers want and with the signals search systems use to decide which sources to trust. For the fuller definition, see [what is SEO](/seo/what-is-seo/).

## How do I do SEO, step by step?

Do it in five steps, in this order.

1. **Research intent.** Find the questions and terms your audience actually searches, and understand what they want when they search them.
2. **Create the content.** Publish pages that answer those questions better and more specifically than what already ranks.
3. **Optimize on-page.** Structure each page with a clear title, descriptive headings, and a direct answer near the top so people and machines can extract it.
4. **Build authority.** Earn genuine references to your site, from links and, increasingly, from plain brand mentions across the web.
5. **Measure and iterate.** Track what gets found and cited, then improve the pages that are close and fix the ones that are not.

Most beginners skip step one and jump to writing. That is the most common reason SEO effort produces nothing. The research decides whether the rest matters.

## How do I do keyword research?

Start with the questions your customers ask you, then expand them. Type a core topic into Google and read the autocomplete suggestions and the People Also Ask box. Those are real queries. Note the ones that match what you offer and that you can answer with genuine depth.

Prioritize by intent, not just volume. A lower-volume query where the searcher is ready to act is worth more than a high-volume one where they are just browsing. Match each query to the kind of page it wants, an explainer, a comparison, a product page, and build that.

The deeper method is in the [keyword research chapter](/seo/keyword-research/). For the AI-search era, also look at the natural-language questions people ask assistants, since those are longer and more specific than classic keywords.

## What is on-page SEO and how do I do it?

On-page SEO is optimizing the content and HTML of an individual page so it matches a query and is easy to extract.

The essentials: a clear, descriptive title tag under about 60 characters, a meta description that earns the click, one H1, and descriptive H2 and H3 headings written as the questions people ask. Put a direct, self-contained answer near the top of the page, then expand below it. Use internal links to connect related pages.

The single highest-impact habit is front-loading the answer. Most AI citations come from the first part of a page, so the direct response should appear early, not after several paragraphs of setup. The full checklist is in [on-page SEO](/seo/on-page-seo/).

## What is technical SEO and what should I fix first?

Technical SEO makes sure search systems can crawl, render, and index your site without friction. It is the foundation the rest sits on.

Fix these first: make sure your important pages are indexable and not blocked in robots.txt, submit an XML sitemap, serve the site over HTTPS, make it work well on mobile, and keep page speed reasonable. Set a canonical URL on each page to avoid duplicate-content confusion. Add structured data (schema markup) so machines can parse your content without guessing.

You do not need to be a developer for most of this. Platforms like WordPress handle much of it through plugins. The full scope is in [technical SEO](/seo/technical-seo/) and the [schema markup guide](/seo/schema-markup/).

## How do I build authority and backlinks?

Authority is other credible sites and sources vouching for yours. Historically that meant backlinks. It still does, but the ground shifted.

Earn links by publishing things worth linking to: original data, genuinely useful guides, tools, and clear answers other people need to reference. Do not buy links or manufacture them. Google treats bought links and fabricated reference patterns as spam.

The newer half of the story is brand mentions. AI engines can read a plain text reference to your brand, with no link attached, and weigh it heavily. Being talked about, accurately and often, now matters as much as being linked to. The [E-E-A-T chapter](/seo/eeat/) covers the trust signals, and [do backlinks still matter for AI search](/blog/do-backlinks-matter-ai-search/) covers the shift.

## How is SEO different in the AI search era?

The mechanics of good SEO did not change. The destination did.

You used to optimize only to rank in a list of links. Now a large share of queries get answered by AI, in Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, and by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude. Those systems retrieve your page, read it, and lift the exact passage that answers the question. So your goal expanded from "rank on the page" to "be the source the answer is built from."

Practically, that rewards clear structure, self-contained sections, front-loaded answers, real author expertise, and freshness, the same fundamentals, aimed at extraction. This discipline is called answer engine optimization. Start with [what AI visibility is](/blog/what-is-ai-visibility/) and [how AI search retrieves content](/blog/how-ai-search-retrieves-content/).

## Can I do SEO myself for free?

Yes. The core work, research, writing, on-page structure, and internal linking, costs nothing but time and needs no paid tools.

Free tools cover most of what a beginner needs. Google Search Console shows how you appear in search and which pages get found. Google's own [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) is the authoritative reference. You only need paid tools once you want deeper keyword and backlink data at scale.

The honest tradeoff is time. Doing it yourself is free but slow to learn. Hiring help buys speed and experience, not a different set of fundamentals.

## How long does SEO take to work?

Expect three to six months to see meaningful movement, and longer in competitive markets. New sites take longer because search systems have little history to judge them by.

SEO is a compounding investment, not a switch. Early on, you build pages that earn little. Over months, the ones that prove useful gain trust, rank higher, and get cited, and the effort you put in earlier keeps paying off. AI citation visibility can sometimes shift faster than classic rankings, because answer engines re-evaluate sources more often.

## How much does SEO cost?

It ranges from free, if you do it yourself, to thousands per month for an agency. A capable freelancer or small engagement often runs a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars monthly. Enterprise programs run higher.

What you are paying for is time, expertise, and tools, not a secret. The fundamentals are public. The cost buys someone who knows how to apply them faster and avoid expensive mistakes.

## How do I measure SEO success?

Measure what gets found, not just what gets clicked. In classic search, track impressions, clicks, and average position in Google Search Console. Watch which pages earn traffic and which queries bring it.

In AI search, the metric shifts to citations and impressions inside AI answers, since much of the value never becomes a click. Track whether your brand appears in AI answers across engines, because their citations barely overlap. The measurement approach is in [measuring SEO](/seo/measuring-seo/).

## What are the most common SEO mistakes?

The recurring ones: writing before researching what people search, targeting volume while ignoring intent, publishing thin pages at scale, burying the answer deep in the page, chasing bought links, and treating SEO as a one-time project instead of an ongoing practice.

The subtlest and most expensive mistake is optimizing a single page while ignoring the site around it. Search systems now judge quality at the whole-site level, so a pile of thin pages drags down your best ones. Fewer, stronger pages beat many weak ones.

## Where should I start today?

Pick one question your ideal customer searches, one you can answer with real expertise. Write the best, clearest answer to it that exists, with the direct response near the top. Publish it, structure it cleanly, and add internal links to and from your related pages. Then repeat.

SEO is not a trick you apply once. It is the habit of being the most useful, most trustworthy answer to the questions your audience asks, over and over, until search systems and the people using them agree.

## FAQs

### How do I start doing SEO as a complete beginner?

Start with one question your audience searches and that you can answer with real expertise. Write the clearest, most useful answer that exists, put the direct response near the top of the page, give it a descriptive title and headings, and add internal links to related pages. Publish it, then repeat with the next question. Research before writing, since that decides whether the rest of the work matters.

### How long does SEO take to show results?

Most sites see meaningful movement in three to six months, and longer in competitive markets or for brand-new sites with little history. SEO compounds over time rather than switching on, so pages built early keep gaining trust and traffic as they prove useful. AI citation visibility can sometimes shift faster than classic rankings.

### Can I do SEO for free without paid tools?

Yes. The core work of research, writing, on-page structure, and internal linking costs only time. Free tools like Google Search Console and Google's SEO starter guide cover most beginner needs. Paid tools become useful only when you want deeper keyword and backlink data at scale.

### What is the most important part of SEO in 2026?

Being the useful, trustworthy source that both search rankings and AI answer engines want to surface. That means matching real search intent, structuring content so the answer is easy to extract, and earning genuine authority through useful work and honest brand mentions rather than manufactured signals.

### How is doing SEO for AI search different?

The fundamentals are the same, but the goal expanded from ranking in a list of links to being the source an AI answer is built from. AI engines retrieve your page, read it, and lift the exact passage that answers the query, so front-loaded answers, self-contained sections, clear structure, real author expertise, and freshness matter more than ever.
