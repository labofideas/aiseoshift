Patients researching dental implants do not search "dentist in [city]." They search "dental implants [city]," "how much do dental implants cost," or "are dental implants worth it." The practices that get cited in AI Overviews and rank for these queries are the ones with dedicated procedure pages authored by licensed dentists and marked up with proper schema.

This is the implementation guide.

## The anatomy of a procedure page

A great procedure page is 1,500 to 2,500 words. The template:

### Section 1: Patient-language summary

A 60 to 120 word summary AI engines can extract directly.

Example: "A dental implant is a titanium post surgically placed in the jawbone to replace a missing tooth root, topped with a custom crown. The full process typically takes 4 to 6 months including healing time. Dental implants are the most permanent and natural-feeling tooth replacement option, with success rates above 95 percent when placed by an experienced provider."

### Section 2: What is [procedure]?

A 200 to 400 word explanation. Anatomy, mechanism, what makes a candidate.

### Section 3: When you need [procedure]

The clinical indications and patient scenarios. AI engines extract this for "when do I need" queries.

### Section 4: What to expect

Walk the patient through the process step by step. Consultation, imaging, treatment, recovery. Realistic timelines.

### Section 5: Cost and insurance

Honest cost ranges (typically a range, not a fixed number) and insurance coverage commentary. This section is among the highest-trafficked and most extracted by AI.

### Section 6: Dentist's perspective

A 200 to 400 word quoted section from the dentist who performs the procedure. Strongest possible E-E-A-T signal — credentialed expert weighing in.

### Section 7: Alternatives

Honest acknowledgment of alternative approaches. "Dental implants vs. bridges vs. partial dentures." AI engines reward content that acknowledges trade-offs.

### Section 8: FAQs

8 to 12 questions in `FAQPage` schema.

### Section 9: Booking CTA

Same-day appointment availability, phone number, online booking.

## Schema for procedure pages

`MedicalProcedure` schema:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "MedicalProcedure",
  "name": "Dental Implant Placement",
  "procedureType": "Surgical",
  "bodyLocation": "Jaw",
  "preparation": "Initial consultation, CT imaging, treatment planning",
  "followup": "Healing period of 3 to 6 months before crown placement",
  "howPerformed": "Local anesthesia, surgical placement of titanium post into jawbone..."
}
```

Plus `FAQPage` schema for the FAQ section. See the [broader schema markup guide](/seo/schema-markup/) for full implementation.

## Production cadence

A realistic cadence is one procedure page per week. At that rate, 15 to 20 procedure pages are live within 4 to 5 months.

### Build a template once

Document with placeholders for procedure name, indications, process, FAQs. Filling a template is much faster than starting from blank.

### Batch the clinical content

The dentist blocks 1 to 2 hours per week. In each session, they dictate or write the "Dentist's perspective" sections and review drafts.

## Common mistakes that kill procedure pages

- **No dentist byline.** YMYL extraction fails.
- **Generic templates with only procedure name swapped.** Demoted as duplicate.
- **Overpromising results.** "Perfect smile guaranteed" trips state dental board issues.
- **No cost or insurance information.** Loses high-intent patients who need this data to decide.
- **No FAQ section.** Where most AI Overview citations come from.

## FAQs

### How many procedure pages does a dental practice need?

10 to 20 for general practices. Specialty practices (orthodontic, cosmetic, oral surgery) need 5 to 10 deep pages on their specialty.

### Can I use AI to draft procedure pages?

For initial structure and patient-language explanations, yes. For clinical accuracy and "Dentist's perspective" sections, the licensed dentist must contribute. AI-drafted health content without licensed review fails YMYL extraction.

### Should before-and-after photos go on procedure pages?

Yes, with explicit patient consent. Cosmetic procedure pages benefit dramatically from before-and-after imagery.

### What about cost ranges — are they really required?

Strongly recommended. Pages that address cost convert significantly better than pages that hide it. Use ranges with insurance commentary.
