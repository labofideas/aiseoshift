Most SEO teams spend 2026 optimizing for Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, and ChatGPT. They are missing a platform with a fundamentally different and often more valuable audience.

Microsoft Copilot is not just another AI chatbot. It is embedded across Bing search, the Edge sidebar, Windows 11, Microsoft 365, and Teams — which means it reaches enterprise employees, knowledge workers, and B2B buyers inside the tools they use all day. While Google AI answers consumer queries, Copilot frequently answers questions from decision-makers with budget authority.

If your business sells to enterprises, professional services clients, or B2B buyers, the case for Copilot optimization is compelling. The search volumes on Bing are smaller than Google's, but the commercial intent is often higher, and the competition for Copilot citations is dramatically lower. Most of your competitors have not thought about this yet.

This guide covers the specific signals that drive Copilot citations and what makes Bing optimization different from the Google-focused playbook most teams already have.

## Quick answer: 5 key signals for Copilot citations

1. **Bing Webmaster Tools + IndexNow** — get indexed fast and access Bing-specific SEO data
2. **Structured data** — Bing has specific schema preferences that differ from Google's
3. **B2B authority signals** — LinkedIn, Microsoft ecosystem presence, and industry publications
4. **Page experience** — Bing rewards fast, clean pages similarly to Google
5. **Bing Places for Business** — Bing's local business profile, overlooked by nearly every SEO team

Each of these matters because they feed the same Bing index that powers every Copilot touchpoint. You do not need to optimize separately for each surface. Win in Bing's index and you win across the entire Copilot ecosystem.

## Why Bing and Copilot are different from Google

Before getting into tactics, the distinctions matter because treating Bing as a minor clone of Google leaves performance on the table.

**Bing's ranking algorithm has meaningful differences.** Exact-match domain signals and keyword-in-anchor-text signals carry more weight in Bing than in Google. Bing is more conservative about freshness — it values established pages more and does not discount older, authoritative content as aggressively as Google does. Social signals, particularly LinkedIn shares and engagement, are indexed and factored by Bing in ways Google deprioritizes.

**Microsoft emphasizes authoritative sources at the institutional level.** Where Google evaluates individual pages and author credentials, Bing places more weight on domain-level authority and institutional reputation. A page on a well-established trade publication outperforms the same content on a newer domain more consistently in Bing than in Google.

**IndexNow changes the speed equation.** Bing co-developed [IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/documentation), an open protocol that lets sites notify search engines instantly when content is published or updated. Instead of waiting for a crawler to discover new pages, content submitted via IndexNow can be indexed in minutes. For time-sensitive content and rapidly updated resources, this is a significant advantage that has no equivalent in the Google ecosystem.

**The audience is different.** Bing's user base skews older, higher-income, and more business-oriented than Google's. Windows enterprise deployments mean Copilot is available by default in corporate environments. A study of Bing demographics consistently shows higher concentrations of business professionals compared to Google. If you are targeting CFOs, IT directors, procurement managers, or enterprise buyers, Bing's audience profile often matches better than Google's.

For broader context on how different AI platforms handle citations differently, see [how ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google cite differently](/blog/how-chatgpt-perplexity-google-cite-differently/).

## Signal 1: Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow

[Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters) is the Bing equivalent of Google Search Console — and it is dramatically underutilized. Most teams set up Google Search Console on day one and never think about Bing's equivalent. That is a missed opportunity.

**Set up Bing Webmaster Tools and:**

- Submit your XML sitemap. Bing processes sitemaps actively and uses them for crawl scheduling.
- Use the URL inspection tool to verify indexing status for priority pages.
- Review Bing's keyword data, which differs enough from Google's Search Console data to surface different query opportunities.
- Monitor for crawl errors that may not appear in Google's tooling.
- Access the site scan feature, which identifies SEO issues that affect Bing rankings specifically.

**IndexNow implementation** is the highest-leverage technical action for Bing/Copilot optimization. The protocol works by pinging Bing (and other participating engines) whenever you publish or update a URL. Implementation requires:

1. Generating an IndexNow API key
2. Hosting the key as a text file at your domain root
3. Sending POST requests to `https://api.indexnow.org/indexnow` whenever content changes

Most major CMS platforms have IndexNow plugins. WordPress has the Bing IndexNow plugin from Microsoft. Cloudflare supports IndexNow natively. If you publish content frequently, IndexNow reduces the lag between publication and Copilot visibility from days to hours.

## Signal 2: Structured data for Copilot

Bing's structured data processing overlaps significantly with Google's — JSON-LD is supported and preferred — but there are differences in what Bing emphasizes.

**Bing prioritizes Article schema with author markup.** For editorial content, Article schema with complete `author` and `publisher` properties signals credibility to Bing's evaluation systems. Bing places more weight on institutional publisher identity than Google does, so ensuring your Organization schema is complete and accurate matters more here.

**Sitelinks Searchbox schema** is specifically beneficial in Bing. Implementing the `SearchAction` property in your WebSite schema can enable a Bing sitelinks searchbox, giving your domain more real estate in branded queries and stronger brand entity recognition.

**FAQ schema for Copilot extraction** works similarly to how it functions for Google AI Overviews. Copilot extracts Q&A pairs from FAQ schema to construct direct answers. If your content addresses common questions in your industry, FAQ schema makes those answers machine-readable for Copilot.

**BreadcrumbList schema** improves Bing's understanding of your site hierarchy, which matters for Bing's more prominent display of breadcrumb navigation in search results compared to Google.

The foundational structured data guide covers implementation in depth: [schema markup for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/).

## Signal 3: Content authority signals for B2B

Bing's B2B audience means authority signals that are marginal for Google become significant for Copilot optimization.

**LinkedIn presence feeds Bing directly.** Microsoft owns LinkedIn, and the relationship between LinkedIn entity data and Bing ranking signals is not coincidental. For B2B content, having your authors, company, and subject matter mapped as LinkedIn entities strengthens Bing's ability to verify expertise. Author bylines linked to active, complete LinkedIn profiles carry authority weight in Bing that they do not in Google.

**Microsoft ecosystem content creates compounding authority.** Content published on Microsoft-affiliated platforms — Microsoft Tech Community, Microsoft Learn documentation, partner network blogs — carries domain authority in Bing's evaluation. If you are in a technology category, publishing technical contributions on Microsoft's own platforms creates citation signals that strengthen your main domain's standing with Bing.

**Industry publications with Bing authority.** Bing's emphasis on institutional authority means that coverage in established trade publications provides stronger Bing ranking lift than equivalent coverage in newer digital publications. Target authoritative industry publications that Bing already trusts for coverage and backlinks.

**Topical depth matters more than breadth.** A site with 50 deep, comprehensive pages on a specific B2B topic consistently outperforms a site with 200 shallow pages in Bing rankings. Bing's algorithm rewards sites that clearly own a topic over sites that cover many topics superficially. This aligns with the [answer engine optimization](/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization/) principle of building topical authority through focused depth.

## Signal 4: Page experience signals

Bing adopted Core Web Vitals as a ranking signal in 2021 and has maintained them since. The page experience signals that matter for Copilot citations closely mirror what matters for Google:

**Core Web Vitals targets for Bing:** Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint under 200 milliseconds, and Cumulative Layout Shift under 0.1. These thresholds are identical to Google's. Pages that meet these thresholds rank and get cited more reliably than pages that fail them.

**Mobile friendliness is a ranking factor.** Bing applies mobile-first evaluation similarly to Google for most queries, including Copilot-sourced answers.

**HTTPS is required.** Bing's crawler deprioritizes HTTP pages. If any section of your site still serves over HTTP, fixing it is foundational before any other optimization.

**Clean page structure with minimal interstitials.** Bing rewards pages where the content is immediately accessible without pop-up blockers, cookie consent overlays that obscure content, or aggressive interstitials. This is partly about user experience and partly about Bing's ability to extract content cleanly for Copilot.

**Page speed matters for Bing's server-side rendering.** Bing's crawler uses a rendering queue, and slow-loading JavaScript-heavy pages can take longer to index than equivalent static HTML pages. Prioritizing server-side rendering or static site generation improves Bing's ability to crawl and index your content accurately.

## Signal 5: Local SEO for Copilot — Bing Places for Business

This signal is the most neglected by SEOs who focus on Google. Bing Places for Business is Bing's equivalent of Google Business Profile, and it directly feeds Copilot's local answer capabilities. When someone asks Copilot for a recommendation near them, Bing Places data powers the answer.

**The neglect gap is your opportunity.** Because most local SEO effort concentrates on Google Business Profile, Bing Places profiles for most businesses are incomplete, unverified, or simply absent. In verticals with strong B2B local demand — professional services, office supply, technology vendors, consulting firms — this gap means that a well-optimized Bing Places profile can earn disproportionate Copilot visibility relative to the effort required.

**Set up Bing Places and:**

- Claim and verify your listing at [bingplaces.com](https://www.bingplaces.com/)
- Complete every field with the same accuracy and consistency as your Google Business Profile
- Add photos, business descriptions, and service listings
- Keep hours accurate and updated
- Sync with your existing Google Business Profile data using Bing's import feature — this is the fastest way to get a complete Bing Places listing if your GBP is already well-optimized

For businesses with multiple locations, create a separate Bing Places listing for each location with location-specific descriptions and photos. The [local SEO checklist for 2026](/blog/local-seo-checklist-2026/) covers the full framework, and the Bing Places layer is directly additive to that work.

## Tracking Copilot citations

Measuring Copilot visibility requires combining two data sources:

**Bing Webmaster Tools** provides impression and click data broken down by page and query. Watching this data over time shows which pages are gaining Bing visibility and which queries are driving traffic. It is the baseline for understanding your Bing performance.

**Traffic from bing.com/chat.** Copilot referral traffic appears in your analytics as traffic from `bing.com/chat` — this is distinct from standard Bing organic traffic (`bing.com`). Setting up a segment or filter in your analytics platform to isolate `bing.com/chat` referrals shows you which pages are being cited in Copilot responses and how much traffic those citations generate.

Benchmark these numbers and track month over month. As Copilot usage grows — particularly in enterprise environments where Microsoft 365 Copilot is deployed at scale — the `bing.com/chat` traffic line should grow if your optimization is working.

## FAQ

**Does Bing SEO automatically help Copilot visibility?**

Yes. Copilot's web grounding pulls exclusively from the Bing index. There is no separate optimization layer for Copilot — ranking and being indexed well in Bing is the mechanism. Every signal that improves your Bing ranking improves your Copilot citation probability.

**How much does Bing's market share matter?**

Bing commands roughly 3-4% of global search market share, but that number understates its commercial relevance. Enterprise Windows deployments mean Bing is set as the default in corporate environments. Edge is the default browser on all Windows machines. In B2B segments, Bing's effective reach is significantly higher than headline market share suggests.

**Is IndexNow only for Bing?**

No. IndexNow is an open protocol. Submitting to IndexNow notifies all participating search engines simultaneously, including Bing, Yandex, and others. Google has its own crawling infrastructure and does not participate in IndexNow, but the protocol still has value for Bing-focused optimization.

**How is structured data different for Bing versus Google?**

The schema types supported are largely the same, but Bing places stronger emphasis on publisher and Organization schema for establishing domain-level authority. Bing also processes Sitelinks Searchbox schema more prominently than Google. The core approach — JSON-LD, accurate implementation, schema stacking — applies to both.

**Does LinkedIn affect Bing rankings?**

Microsoft's ownership of LinkedIn creates a relationship between LinkedIn entity data and Bing signals, though Microsoft has not published a formal specification. In practice, teams that build strong LinkedIn presence for their authors and brand consistently report improved Bing performance for B2B content. The mechanism appears to be entity verification: LinkedIn profiles help Bing confirm that authors and organizations are real, credible entities.

**How do I know if Copilot is citing my content?**

Monitor your analytics for traffic from `bing.com/chat`. You can also manually test by asking Copilot questions in your topic area and observing which sources it cites. Bing Webmaster Tools will show impression growth in relevant queries as your citation presence improves.

## The B2B opportunity no one is taking

The strategic case for Copilot optimization comes down to a mismatch in effort and opportunity. Google AI Overviews are highly competitive — every SEO team is optimizing for them. Copilot citations are comparatively uncrowded, and the audience they reach is often more valuable for B2B businesses.

A B2B software company, a professional services firm, or an enterprise technology vendor that builds strong Bing authority today is positioning for a surface that will only grow as Microsoft deploys Copilot more deeply across its product ecosystem. By the time competitors notice, the citation positions will be occupied.

The playbook is straightforward: Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow for fast indexing, structured data with author and Organization schema for credibility, LinkedIn and Microsoft ecosystem presence for B2B authority, clean page experience for reliable extraction, and Bing Places to capture local intent. None of these require separate effort from your existing AI visibility work — they are additive signals on top of fundamentals you should already have.
