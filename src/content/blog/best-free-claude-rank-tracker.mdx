## The best free ways to track Claude

No single free tool does everything, so the free approach is a small stack. Each piece is genuinely free and each answers a different question.

**Manual query testing.** The most reliable free method. Keep a spreadsheet of the questions your buyers ask, run them in Claude on a fixed schedule with web search enabled, and record whether Claude cited your URL, named your brand, or cited a competitor. It is manual and does not scale past a couple dozen questions, but it is real, direct data about exactly what Claude says, and it costs nothing but time.

**AI crawler log monitoring.** Claude reaches web content through its crawler. Watching your server logs for ClaudeBot tells you, for free, whether Anthropic's systems are fetching your pages at all. If they never touch your content, no citation is possible, and that is a crawlability problem to fix before anything else. The mechanics are in [robots.txt for AI crawlers](/blog/robots-txt-for-ai-crawlers/) and [LLM crawler optimization](/blog/llm-crawler-optimization/).

**GA4 referral filtering.** Set up filtering in GA4 to isolate visits arriving from AI assistants. It is coarse and undercounts, since many AI interactions send no click, but paired with manual testing it gives you a free trend for whether Claude-driven traffic is growing.

**Free tiers and trials of real tools.** Several AI-visibility platforms offer a free entry that covers Claude: some hand out a batch of free monthly credits, others a short trial. That is enough to run an automated audit of your Claude standing once, see the citation-source list, and decide whether ongoing tracking is worth paying for.

## What is different about tracking Claude

Claude has its own character, and it changes what you watch.

Claude skews toward considered, professional use. A large share of Claude usage is work: research, analysis, drafting, and coding. The questions people bring it tend to be substantive, which means the content Claude reaches for tends to be thorough and trustworthy rather than thin. If your pages are shallow, Claude is a hard engine to win, and that raises the value of genuine depth. The trust signals that matter are in the [E-E-A-T chapter](/seo/eeat/).

Citations depend on web search being on. Claude cites web sources when its search capability is engaged. Your tracking should reflect how your audience actually uses it, so test with search enabled if that is how buyers reach answers about your category.

It is often an enterprise touchpoint. Because Claude is common inside companies, being the source it cites can influence a buying committee even without a public ranking. That makes Claude visibility worth measuring even when its raw query volume looks smaller than ChatGPT's.

## A free Claude tracking routine you can run in 30 minutes a month

Free tracking fails when it has no rhythm. Here is a concrete monthly routine that costs nothing and produces real data. It takes about half an hour once you have the question list.

Build the question list once. Write down 15 to 25 questions a real buyer would ask Claude on the way to choosing something like what you sell. Mix unbranded problem questions ("best way to do X"), comparison questions ("X versus Y"), and a couple of branded ones as a control. This list is the spine of the whole routine, so spend the time to make it real. Save it in a spreadsheet with a column for each month.

Run them in Claude with search on. Once a month, open Claude with web search enabled and ask each question as a buyer would. For each answer, record three things: did Claude cite your URL, did it name your brand without a link, and which competitors it cited instead. Codes as simple as "cited," "mentioned," or "absent" are enough. The competitor column is the most valuable, because it becomes your content roadmap.

Check the crawler and referral data. Once a quarter, glance at your server logs for ClaudeBot activity to confirm Claude is fetching your pages, and check your GA4 AI-referral filter for the traffic trend. These take minutes and catch the silent failure where Claude simply cannot reach your content.

Read the trend, not the month. A single month is noise. By the third or fourth month you will see whether your citation rate is climbing, flat, or slipping, and whether a specific content change moved a specific question. That trend line is the entire payoff, and it costs nothing but the half hour.

When the half hour becomes two hours because your list has grown, that is the signal to move to a cheap automated tool. Not before. The free routine is enough to prove whether Claude visibility matters for you at all.

## What to report about Claude visibility

Even free tracking should roll up into something a stakeholder can read in ten seconds. Report four things.

Citation share on your priority questions, as one percentage across the list you track. Its trend over months is the headline. Share of voice against named competitors, framed as "we appear in X percent of Claude answers where our rivals appear in Y." The gap list: the two or three high-value questions where a competitor consistently wins, with the page Claude cites. And the cost line: since this is free or near-free, the report should say so, because a zero-cost measurement that informs content decisions is an easy program to keep funded. ## Claude versus ChatGPT: why the results differ

Teams often assume their ChatGPT visibility predicts their Claude visibility. It does not, and understanding why sharpens your tracking.

The two models draw on different training and, when search is engaged, can weigh sources differently. More importantly, they are used differently. ChatGPT's usage is broader and more consumer, spanning quick questions, casual tasks, and everything in between. Claude's usage skews toward considered, professional work: analysis, drafting, research, and coding. That difference in who is asking, and what they are asking, changes which content each model reaches for.

The practical effect is that thin, broad content can sometimes surface in ChatGPT for a casual query but struggle in Claude, where the questions tend to be more substantive and the bar for a citeable source is higher. A page that wins in one may lose in the other for the same topic. This is exactly why tracking them separately is worth the effort: a single blended "AI visibility" number would average away the difference and hide where you are actually strong or weak.

It also means your fixes may differ by engine. Winning Claude often rewards genuine depth and demonstrated expertise more than winning a casual ChatGPT query does, so if your tracking shows you strong in ChatGPT but weak in Claude, the likely gap is substance, not keywords. The trust signals that close that gap are in the [E-E-A-T chapter](/seo/eeat/), and the dual-purpose approach that satisfies both is in [how to write content for humans and AI](/blog/how-to-write-content-for-humans-and-ai/).

## Common mistakes with free Claude tracking

**Tracking too few runs and trusting one answer.** Claude's answer can vary between runs. A single test showing you absent is not a verdict; read the pattern across several checks.

**Only testing branded questions.** If every prompt is your brand name, of course Claude knows you. Track the unbranded questions where you actually compete.

**Never upgrading when you clearly should.** Free is the right start, but if you are hand-running fifty prompts a week, a $29 tool pays for itself in time saved. Free is a phase, not a religion.

**Assuming ChatGPT visibility means Claude visibility.** They select sources differently. Track Claude on its own rather than assuming your ChatGPT wins carry over.

## What earns a Claude citation, specifically

Free tracking tells you where you stand. Knowing what Claude tends to reward tells you what to change. A few patterns show up consistently for Claude, and they lean harder toward substance than a casual-query engine would.

Depth that a generalist page lacks. Claude's questions skew professional and specific, so it reaches for sources that actually cover the topic, including the edge cases, rather than skimming it. A page that answers the surface question and then addresses the follow-ups a real practitioner would ask is more citeable in Claude than a thin overview that would satisfy a casual ChatGPT query. If your tracking shows you losing Claude questions to more thorough competitors, depth is usually the gap.

Demonstrated, verifiable expertise. Claude weighs the same trust signals that Google's raters and the other AI engines do: a named author with real credentials, first-hand experience, and claims that hold up. Anonymous, generic content is a weak candidate. Building that credibility is the slow work in the [E-E-A-T chapter](/seo/eeat/), and it pays off across every engine, not only Claude.

Clean, extractable structure. Claude, like all answer engines, needs to pull a specific claim out of your page without ambiguity. Clear headings phrased as the questions people ask, a direct answer immediately under each, and schema all make extraction easier. The mechanics are in [how to structure pages so AI can extract answers](/blog/how-to-structure-pages-ai-extraction/).

Corroboration from sources Claude trusts. A claim that appears only on your own page is a weaker citation candidate than one echoed across several reputable sources. When a competitor keeps winning a Claude answer, check whether their claim is corroborated elsewhere and yours is not. The durable, off-site side of that is in [AI citation building](/seo/ai-citation-building/).

The through-line is that Claude rewards genuine quality more than tricks, which is good news: the work that wins Claude also wins the other engines and holds up when the algorithms change.
