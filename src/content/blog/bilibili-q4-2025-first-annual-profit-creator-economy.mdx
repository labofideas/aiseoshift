On March 5, 2026 [Bilibili reported its Q4 and full-year 2025 results](https://in.investing.com/news/company-news/bilibili-q4-2025-slides-first-annual-profit-margins-hit-37-93CH-5275235): RMB 30.3 billion in annual revenue (up 13% year-over-year), gross margin at 37%, and the company's first full year of GAAP profit at RMB 1.2 billion after a RMB 39 million loss in 2024. The Q4 operating profit alone was up 299% year-over-year. After fifteen years of running at a loss, a top-five Chinese video platform with 366 million monthly users finally crossed into sustained profitability.

The interesting question is not whether the milestone happened. It is how. The answer is a working case study in what content-platform economics now look like in the AI era, and it has direct lessons for Western platforms, brand marketers buying into video advertising, and the creators those platforms depend on.

## TL;DR

Bilibili reached its first annual profit by combining three things: 23% advertising revenue growth driven by AI-enhanced targeting, an 8% lift in user time spent on the platform, and a 21% increase in average creator income that compounded into a deeper content moat. Advertising, not subscriptions or games, was the largest growth lever. The company has now guided to a long-term gross margin of 40-45% and adjusted operating margin of 15-20%, putting it firmly in profitable big-platform territory. The mechanics matter because every other content platform - YouTube, Roblox, Pinterest, TikTok, Discord - is running variants of the same playbook.

## The numbers, briefly

**Full year 2025:**

- Revenue: RMB 30.3 billion (+13% YoY)
- GAAP net profit: RMB 1.2 billion (first full year of profitability)
- Non-GAAP net profit: RMB 2,588 million (8.5% margin)
- Operating profit: RMB 2,442 million (vs. RMB 61 million loss in 2024)
- Net margin (Q4): 10.6%
- Gross margin (Q4): 37.0%

**Revenue mix (FY 2025):**

| Segment | Revenue | Share | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value-Added Services | RMB 11,928M | 38% | +8% |
| Advertising | RMB 10,058M | 33% | +23% |
| Mobile Games | RMB 6,395M | 21% | +14% |
| IP / Other | - | 7% | - |

**Users:**

- Q4 daily active users: 113 million (+10% YoY)
- Monthly active users: 366 million
- Monthly paying users: 36 million
- Average daily time per user: 107 minutes (+8%)
- 12-month retention among "official members": 80%

**Creators:**

- ~3 million creators earned income in 2025
- Average creator income: +21% YoY
- Pool of creators with 1,000+ followers grew over 30%
- More than 10 million users financially supported creators via "fan charging" (doubled YoY)

The numbers above are what matter for the analysis.

## What actually drove the turnaround

### Advertising grew 23%, faster than every other segment

The headline number from the segment breakdown is that advertising grew 23% for the full year and was up 27% in Q4 - the fastest-growing line on the income statement. This matters because advertising is the highest-margin revenue at scale for a content platform. Subscriptions cap out at what a single user will pay. Games are hit-driven and lumpy. Ad revenue compounds with time spent and audience size.

Two things drove ad growth specifically:

1. **AI-enhanced targeting.** Management called out AI-driven algorithms multiple times on the earnings call. With 113 million daily active users averaging 107 minutes per day, Bilibili has the kind of behavioral signal density needed for the targeting models to actually outperform the legacy contextual approach.
2. **Demographic concentration.** The platform's user base is 70% penetrated into China's Gen Z, average age 26.5, with new users averaging 22. That is the audience brand advertisers are willing to pay a premium to reach. Concentration on a specific demographic is itself a pricing advantage.

The lesson generalizes: content platforms that combine strong daily engagement with a clear demographic lock and AI-driven targeting now have the margin profile to fund creator incentives without bleeding cash. The shape of the YouTube, Pinterest, and TikTok businesses is the same shape.

### Time spent went up, not down

The market expected the opposite. As AI summarization and short-form everywhere compresses attention, the consensus has been that long-session platforms would shed minutes. Bilibili's average daily time per user instead climbed from 99 minutes to 107 minutes, up 8%. A platform that retains attention against the headwind of AI-generated summaries has pricing power most peers have lost.

### Creator income up 21%, with secondary effects

The single most important non-financial number in the release: average creator income on the platform grew 21% year-over-year. Combined with a 30% growth in the pool of creators above 1,000 followers, this is what content-platform economists call a "supply-side flywheel" - more creators earning more money produces more content, which produces more sessions, which produces more ad inventory, which funds higher payouts. Bilibili is now running this flywheel at a level where it self-funds.

The mechanics matter for any platform trying to compete for creators: [Intellectia's analysis of the earnings beat](https://intellectia.ai/news/stock/bilibili-reports-q4-profit-and-revenue-beat-driven-by-advertising-growth) calls out that the doubling of "fan charging" (over 10 million users now directly paying creators) shows the platform has built a working tip-based monetization layer on top of pure ad sharing.

### Operating leverage finally arrived

The most striking line item: Q4 operating profit grew 299% year-over-year on only 8% revenue growth. That is operating leverage - the cost base barely moved while revenue rose, so almost all of the incremental revenue dropped to profit. This is the same shape Meta showed in 2022-2023 and Netflix in 2018-2019 once their platforms reached scale.

## What this proves about content-platform economics

A few generalizable points the numbers settle.

**The "creator economy" is not a separate business - it is the content engine for an ad business.** Bilibili's RMB 10 billion advertising line and its 3 million income-earning creators are the same flywheel. Average creator income going up 21% is a marketing expense and a content acquisition strategy and a moat investment, all in one. The platforms that try to run creators as a cost center under-invest in the supply side and stall out.

**AI-driven ad targeting is the unlock for medium-sized platforms.** A few years ago, only the largest platforms (Meta, Google) could justify the engineering needed for the data-density-to-revenue conversion. AI-enhanced targeting models lower the threshold, which is why a platform Bilibili's size can now post 23% ad growth. Expect the same dynamic at Pinterest, Reddit, Snap, and similar mid-scale platforms in 2026.

**Time-per-user is still the master metric.** Despite every prediction that AI summaries would shorten sessions, Bilibili's time-per-user grew 8%. Platforms that win the format right ([long-form video with strong community signals](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/BILI/pressreleases/617308/bilibili-earnings-call-profit-milestone-and-ai-push/), in Bilibili's case) hold attention against the headwind.

**The path to profitability for content platforms is still operating leverage, not headcount cuts.** Bilibili kept its cost base flat. Revenue grew 13%. Profit followed. The companies that cut their way to profitability typically also cut their content engine and stall out the following year.

## What this means for marketers and SEO practitioners

You may not be buying advertising on a Chinese video platform. The relevant lessons still apply.

### Advertising budgets are flowing to platforms that show AI-enhanced targeting at scale

The Q4 2025 ad growth at Bilibili matches what is happening at Google ([covered in our Marketing Live 2026 piece](/blog/google-marketing-live-2026-ai-search-ads/)), Pinterest, and Reddit. The pattern is consistent: AI-driven targeting plus distinctive audience composition equals premium CPMs. If your media plan is still structured around demographics-and-interests targeting on legacy platforms, you are likely paying more per qualified impression than you would on platforms with stronger AI infrastructure.

### Video and creator-led content are now infrastructure, not channels

A platform where creators are growing 21% in income year-over-year is one where the audience is increasingly inside creator content, not brand pages or paid media. The marketing implication for brands is that creator partnerships, affiliate seeding, and product placement inside long-form video are no longer adjuncts to paid media. They are the only way to reach the audience while it is paying attention. This is also true in Western markets, where YouTube and TikTok have the same dynamic.

### The "multimodal search" thesis is being validated by the dollars

Video is rising as a search surface. People look up products, tutorials, and reviews on video platforms before (or instead of) Google. Bilibili's ad growth confirms this is now a paid-search-scale opportunity, not a niche channel. Our chapter on [search everywhere optimization](/seo/search-everywhere/) covers what to do about it.

### Content investment has compounding returns when the platform self-funds creators

The flywheel works only because the platform reinvests ad revenue into creator payouts. The lesson for owned-media operators (publishers, brands running content programs, agencies running content for clients) is the same: the budget you allocate to creators and writers is a content-acquisition cost, not a marketing-line-item cost. Treating it as the former produces compounding output. Treating it as the latter under-funds it. See [content strategy and topic clusters](/seo/content-strategy/) for the planning model.

## What to watch for the rest of 2026

Three signals worth tracking in the next two quarters:

1. **Whether Bilibili hits its margin guidance.** Management guided to 40-45% gross margin and 15-20% adjusted operating margin long-term. If the company gets there in 2026, the platform-economics playbook described above is fully validated. If it stalls, the question becomes whether the ad market or the creator flywheel was the weaker leg.
2. **Whether Western mid-scale platforms (Pinterest, Reddit, Snap) replicate the ad-growth pattern.** Their 2026 ad growth rates relative to peers will indicate whether the AI-targeting-at-mid-scale thesis holds outside of China.
3. **Whether time-per-user holds against AI summarization.** If platforms like YouTube and Bilibili keep showing time-per-user growth, the AI-summary erosion thesis weakens, and the long-form video moat strengthens. This affects every brand's media mix decisions for 2027.

## Frequently asked questions

**Did Bilibili make a profit in 2025?**
Yes. Bilibili reported its first full year of GAAP net profit in 2025 at RMB 1.2 billion, on revenue of RMB 30.3 billion (up 13% year-over-year). Q4 operating profit grew 299% YoY to RMB 838 million.

**What is Bilibili's revenue mix?**
For full-year 2025: Value-Added Services 38% (RMB 11.9B), Advertising 33% (RMB 10.1B), Mobile Games 21% (RMB 6.4B), and IP / other revenue 7%. Advertising was the fastest-growing segment at 23% YoY.

**How big is Bilibili's creator economy?**
Around 3 million creators earned income on the platform in 2025, with average creator income up 21% year-over-year. The pool of creators with more than 1,000 followers expanded by over 30%, and over 10 million users financially supported creators directly through fan charging - a number that doubled in 2025.

**Why did Bilibili's advertising revenue grow 23%?**
Management cited two main drivers: AI-enhanced ad targeting that lifted CPMs on its existing inventory, and stronger advertiser demand to reach its concentrated Gen Z audience (70% penetrated into the Chinese Gen Z cohort, average user age 26.5). Q4 ad revenue specifically grew 27%.

**What does Bilibili's profit milestone mean for other content platforms?**
It validates a platform-economics model where AI-driven ad targeting at mid-scale combines with creator-economy reinvestment to produce operating leverage. Expect the same pattern at Pinterest, Reddit, Snap, and other mid-scale video and community platforms in 2026.
