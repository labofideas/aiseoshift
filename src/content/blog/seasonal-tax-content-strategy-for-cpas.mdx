Tax-related search volume spikes in February through April and again in September through October for extensions and quarterly estimates. Most CPA firms respond by publishing only during these peaks. That is exactly backward.

AI engines form their citation lists year-round. The firms cited in March were judged authoritative in October. The firms cited in October were publishing in May, June, and July when most competitors were silent. This is the editorial calendar that wins.

## The four content seasons

Each season has distinct content needs and produces distinct ranking effects:

### Pre-season (December–January, August–September)

Content focus: year-end planning, deadline preparation, document checklists.

This is when prospective clients are starting to think about their tax situation. They are not yet in execution mode but they are researching. Content published in pre-season ranks for the in-season volume that arrives 8 to 12 weeks later.

Topics that work in pre-season:

- Year-end planning checklists
- Document organization guides
- "What changed this year" tax law updates
- Quarterly estimate calculations
- Q4 planning for business owners

### In-season (February–April, October)

Content focus: tactical "how to" content, common questions, document checklists.

The traffic peak. Searches are urgent and transactional. Users are looking for answers right now. Content published in-season ranks fast but has to compete with everything else firms publish in the same window.

Topics that work in-season:

- "How to" content for common situations
- Question-driven content ("can I deduct X")
- Document and form explainers
- Common-mistake content
- Last-minute deadline content

### Post-season (May–July, November)

Content focus: reflection, planning, advisory content.

The trough. Search volume is lower but competition is also dramatically lower. Content published in post-season compounds for the next year's pre-season and in-season ranks.

Topics that work post-season:

- "What we saw this tax season" recap content
- Quarterly business planning
- Mid-year tax planning
- Audit defense content
- Bookkeeping and operational content

### Year-round evergreen

Content focus: specialty depth, industry guides, advisory content.

Content that produces consistent value regardless of season. This is the backbone of AI citation visibility.

Topics that work year-round:

- Industry-specific tax guides (real estate, e-commerce, cannabis, etc.)
- Specialty deep-dives (cost segregation, R&D credits, 1031 exchanges)
- Entity selection and structuring content
- International and multi-state content
- High-net-worth planning content

## The editorial calendar that wins

The cadence that produces strong AI citation lists and steady ranking:

### Publishing volume targets

- **2 to 3 pieces per week year-round** as the base cadence
- **Increased to 4 to 6 per week** during pre-season ramps (Nov–Jan and Jul–Aug)
- **Steady through in-season** rather than spiking — quality over volume in peak periods

### Content mix by week

A typical week in November:

- 1 pre-season piece (year-end planning, document prep)
- 1 evergreen specialty piece (industry guide, technical deep-dive)
- 1 advisory or operational piece (audit defense, bookkeeping)

A typical week in March:

- 1 in-season tactical piece (common situation, deadline help)
- 1 evergreen specialty piece (preserving authority signals)
- 1 question-driven piece (common search queries)

A typical week in June:

- 1 post-season reflective piece (lessons from tax season)
- 2 evergreen specialty pieces (compounding the off-peak window)

### Why steady cadence wins

AI engines re-evaluate sources on a rolling basis. A firm that publishes 60 pieces in March is treated as a March spike, not a steady authority. A firm that publishes 60 pieces evenly across the year is treated as ongoing authority. The compounding effect is significant.

## Topic clusters that compound

Rather than publishing isolated pieces, build clusters around your specialty areas. Each cluster has:

- A pillar piece (2,500+ words) covering the specialty area broadly
- 8 to 15 sub-topic pieces (1,500 to 2,000 words each) covering specific aspects
- Internal linking that ties the cluster together

Example cluster: Real estate tax for short-term rental operators

- Pillar: "Tax strategies for Airbnb and short-term rental hosts"
- Sub-topics:
  - Schedule E vs. Schedule C for short-term rentals
  - Material participation rules for short-term rentals
  - Cost segregation for short-term rental properties
  - Self-employment tax considerations
  - 1099-K reporting for hosts
  - Section 280A and personal use rules
  - State and local lodging tax obligations
  - Bonus depreciation strategies
  - Bookkeeping setup for short-term rentals
  - LLC vs. S-Corp for rental businesses

A cluster of 10 to 15 deeply linked pieces dominates AI citations for the specialty. A single piece, however good, rarely does.

## How to source content topics

The best CPA content topics come from three sources:

### 1. Actual client questions

What questions do clients ask in calls and emails? Those are the questions other people are Googling and asking ChatGPT. The most expensive 30 minutes you can spend each month is having every CPA log their top 5 client questions from the past 30 days.

### 2. IRS and Treasury releases

When the IRS releases new guidance, AI engines need analysis. The firms that publish CPA-authored interpretations within 48 to 72 hours of major IRS releases capture significant search and AI citation volume.

### 3. Specialty publications and forums

Industry-specific forums (real estate investing forums, e-commerce seller communities, crypto Discords) are leading indicators for emerging questions. Topics that gain traction in these communities are usually 6 to 12 months ahead of mainstream search volume.

## Production workflow

A sustainable production workflow for a small to mid-sized firm:

### Editor or content lead

One person owns the calendar, topic selection, and review process. This can be the marketing lead, a junior partner, or an outsourced content director.

### CPA contributors

Each CPA partner contributes 2 to 4 pieces per month under their own byline. Pieces can be drafted by writers and reviewed/edited by the CPA, or drafted by the CPA directly.

### Review and publication

Every piece is reviewed by the bylining CPA before publication. Includes fact-checking, regulatory compliance, and disclosure language.

### Distribution

Publication on the firm's own site first. Optional: syndication to LinkedIn after 2 to 4 weeks. Republication to industry publications after 6 to 8 weeks.

## What to do this quarter

A 90-day plan for a firm that has been publishing inconsistently:

- **Month 1:** Choose 2 specialty areas. Build cluster plans of 10 to 15 topics each. Assign CPA bylines.
- **Month 2:** Ship 2 to 3 cluster pieces per week. Establish review process. Citation cleanup on existing content.
- **Month 3:** Continue cluster build. First pre-season content for the next deadline window.

Twelve months in: 100+ specialty pieces across 2 to 4 specialty clusters, full CPA author infrastructure, and steady AI citation visibility year-round.

## FAQs

### How long until seasonal content starts ranking?

In-season content can rank within 30 to 60 days for less-competitive long-tail queries. Pre-season content typically takes 60 to 120 days to mature for the season it targets. Evergreen content compounds slowly over 12 to 24 months.

### Should I delete or update old seasonal content?

Update rather than delete. Add an "Updated for [year]" notation, refresh data points, and remove obsolete references. Deletion wastes accumulated authority signals.

### How do I handle tax law changes mid-year?

Publish a dated analysis piece within 48 to 72 hours. Update affected evergreen content within 30 days. Reference the analysis piece from the updated evergreen content to consolidate authority.

### What about audit content, IRS notice content, and other reactive topics?

These are evergreen specialty topics, not seasonal. Build them into your specialty clusters. Audit defense, IRS notice response, and FBAR are all year-round queries.

### Can I outsource content production?

Yes, with two caveats: every piece must be reviewed by a credentialed CPA whose byline appears on it, and the outsourced writer must have substantive accounting domain knowledge. Generic outsourced content fails AI extraction for YMYL queries.
