Feature by feature: Rank Math wins on free-tier depth and codebase efficiency, Yoast wins on beginner-friendly writing guidance and ecosystem size, AIOSEO wins on WooCommerce and agency multi-site features, and SEOPress wins on price for anyone running more than one site. None of the four loses badly at the basics, titles, meta tags, sitemaps, and schema are handled competently by all of them, so this comparison is built around the categories where they actually diverge: free-tier generosity, technical footprint, ecommerce readiness, and licensing cost at scale.

If you already know what a WordPress SEO plugin does and just need to pick between these four, this is that decision, category by category.

## Category 1: Free-tier depth (Rank Math wins)

This is the starkest difference between the four. Rank Math's free version includes unlimited keyword optimization per post, a built-in redirect manager, 404 error monitoring, direct GA4 integration, Search Console data inside your dashboard, and 18 pre-defined schema types, features that Yoast, AIOSEO, and SEOPress either omit from their free tiers or gate behind a paid upgrade. If your budget for a plugin is exactly zero and you want the most capability for it, this category alone settles the decision.

SEOPress comes closest to matching Rank Math's free generosity, unlocking most of its own core feature set without payment, which is why it appears again below on pricing. Yoast and AIOSEO's free tiers cover the fundamentals solidly but hold back more of the extras for their premium versions.

## Category 2: Codebase size and site performance (Rank Math wins)

A less-discussed but measurable difference: Rank Math ships a noticeably smaller codebase than Yoast, roughly 61,100 lines of code at 11.8 MB versus Yoast's 97,100 lines at 32.4 MB, according to plugin analysis in 2026. A smaller codebase means fewer files loaded on every page request, which contributes to a lighter server footprint. On a resource-constrained host or a site already carrying a heavy plugin stack, that difference is not cosmetic; it shows up in real page-speed testing, covered in [best page speed testing tools](/blog/best-page-speed-testing-tools/).

This does not mean Yoast is slow in absolute terms, only that Rank Math is measurably leaner if your site is sensitive to every added script and stylesheet.

## Category 3: Beginner guidance and writing feedback (Yoast wins)

Rank Math's dashboard assumes a bit more comfort with SEO concepts out of the box, and its constant nudges toward its Pro tier can overwhelm a first-time user. Yoast's real-time content analysis, checking readability, keyword usage, and internal linking as you write with plain-language explanations, remains the clearest hand-holding of the four for someone publishing their first posts. It teaches SEO habits while you write rather than just scoring the result.

If this is your first WordPress site and you have never configured an SEO plugin before, Yoast's guidance closes that gap faster than the others, even though Rank Math's raw feature list is longer.

## Category 4: WooCommerce and ecommerce readiness (AIOSEO wins)

All in One SEO was built with agencies and WooCommerce stores in mind from early on, and it shows in the maturity of its product schema handling, its link assistant that surfaces internal linking opportunities across a large product catalog, and its flexible multi-site licensing aimed at agencies managing several client stores. Rank Math and Yoast both support WooCommerce adequately, but AIOSEO's ecommerce-specific tooling is more developed out of the box, without needing a separate plugin bolted on.

If your WordPress site is primarily a WooCommerce store, or you manage several client stores as an agency, this category matters more than free-tier depth.

## Category 5: Multi-site and agency pricing (SEOPress wins)

SEOPress's structural advantage is licensing: it unlocks nearly all of its core feature set, schema, redirects, Google Analytics integration, for one flat license that covers unlimited sites, rather than charging per site the way Yoast, Rank Math, and AIOSEO's premium tiers typically do. For an agency running SEO across ten or twenty client sites, that difference compounds into real annual savings compared to paying a per-site premium fee four times over.

SEOPress trades a smaller third-party ecosystem, fewer tutorials, fewer community plugins built around it, for that pricing advantage, which is a fair trade for a technical user managing many sites who does not need much hand-holding.

## Category 6: Support ecosystem and longevity (Yoast wins)

This category rarely shows up in feature comparisons, but it matters over a multi-year time horizon. Yoast has the longest track record and by far the largest surrounding ecosystem: more third-party tutorials, more theme and plugin developers who test compatibility against it specifically, and a larger community of users who have already hit and solved whatever edge case you are about to hit. If something breaks in an unusual way, the odds someone has already documented the fix are highest with Yoast.

Rank Math's ecosystem has grown quickly and is well documented for common issues, but it is younger and thinner for genuinely obscure edge cases. AIOSEO and SEOPress trail further behind on sheer volume of third-party content, though both maintain solid official documentation. If you are risk-averse about long-term support and want the safest bet regardless of feature count, this category alone might outweigh the others.

## Migrating between these plugins

Switching is common enough that all four anticipate it, and most ship a dedicated importer for at least one competitor's data, usually Yoast's, since it is the most common plugin to migrate away from. Rank Math in particular includes a built-in Yoast importer that reads meta titles, descriptions, and some redirect data directly.

Before switching on a live site: export your current settings and redirects, deactivate the old plugin completely rather than leaving it inactive, and check your rendered page source on a handful of key pages afterward to confirm no duplicate or orphaned schema is left behind from the old plugin. Give yourself a monitoring window of a couple of weeks after the switch to catch anything Search Console flags that a quick check missed.

## Where all four are functionally tied

Worth stating plainly so you do not overweight a non-difference: XML sitemap generation, canonical tag handling, basic Article and Organization schema, Open Graph tags for social sharing, and breadcrumb support are all handled competently by every plugin here. If your comparison is stuck on these categories, none of them differentiates the decision, and you should decide on the categories above instead.

## The bottom line

Rank Math wins if you are optimizing for the most free capability and the lightest codebase. Yoast wins if you are new to WordPress and want the clearest real-time writing guidance. AIOSEO wins if WooCommerce or agency multi-client work is your primary use case. SEOPress wins if you manage several sites and want one license instead of paying per site.

Pick the category that matters most for your actual site, not the plugin with the most reviews.

## FAQs

### Which WordPress SEO plugin has the best free version?

Rank Math, by a clear margin. Its free tier includes unlimited keyword optimization, a redirect manager, 404 monitoring, GA4 and Search Console integration, and 18 schema types, none of which the other three fully match without upgrading to a paid tier. SEOPress comes closest among the alternatives, unlocking most of its own core features free as well.

### Does plugin choice actually affect my site's speed?

Yes, measurably. Rank Math's codebase is roughly a third the size of Yoast's by lines of code and file size, which means fewer files loaded per page request. On a site already carrying a heavy plugin stack or running on a resource-constrained host, that difference shows up in real page-speed test results, not just in theory.

### Which plugin is best if I run a WooCommerce store?

All in One SEO (AIOSEO), which was built with WooCommerce and agency use as core use cases from early in its development. Its product schema handling and internal-linking tools for large catalogs are more mature out of the box than the general-purpose plugins, though Rank Math and Yoast both support WooCommerce adequately for a smaller store.

### Is SEOPress a good choice for a single personal site?

It can be, especially if you want most core features unlocked without paying, but its smaller user base means fewer tutorials and community plugins exist around it compared to Yoast or Rank Math. Its real advantage, one license covering unlimited sites, matters most if you manage several sites; for a single personal blog, Rank Math's free tier may serve you just as well.
