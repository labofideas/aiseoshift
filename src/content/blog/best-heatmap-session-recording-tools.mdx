The best heatmap and session recording tools in 2026 are Microsoft Clarity for a genuinely capable free option with no traffic limits, Hotjar (now part of Contentsquare) for teams that want surveys alongside heatmaps, and Crazy Egg for built-in A/B testing on top of click and scroll data. A heatmap tool records where visitors click, how far they scroll, and where their attention concentrates, then plays back individual sessions so you can watch exactly where someone got confused or gave up. This is a different diagnostic than an SEO tool: it does not tell you why a page fails to rank, it tells you why the traffic that does arrive fails to convert.

Rankings get you to the page. Heatmaps tell you why people leave it.

## What a heatmap tool actually shows you

Three distinct views, usually bundled together in one product. Click maps show exactly where visitors tap or click on a page, revealing when people try to click something that is not actually clickable, a strong sign of a confusing design. Scroll maps show how far down a page visitors typically get before leaving, which tells you whether your call to action is even being seen. Session recordings play back individual visits like a screen capture, so you can watch a real person hesitate, backtrack, or abandon a form in real time.

None of this touches rankings directly. It touches what happens after someone lands, which is exactly the part a keyword ranking cannot tell you anything about.

### Microsoft Clarity: the free option that does not feel like a free option

Microsoft Clarity offers click, scroll, and area heatmaps, unlimited session recordings, and no traffic limits, all at zero cost, which makes it an unusually strong free tier rather than a stripped-down teaser. It has also added AI session summaries and an AI chat feature that lets you ask questions about your recorded data directly, both included in the free tier. The tradeoffs are real but narrow: no funnel analytics, no built-in A/B testing, and your session data is stored on Microsoft's servers, which matters if that is a compliance concern for your industry.

For the overwhelming majority of sites that just want to see where visitors click and where they drop off, Clarity is the correct default, and there is little reason to pay for a competitor until you specifically need something it lacks.

### Hotjar: heatmaps plus direct visitor feedback

Hotjar merged into Contentsquare in mid-2025, and its heatmap and recording features remain intact, but its pricing structure changed: surveys are now a separate product at roughly $99 a month on top of a $49-a-month heatmap plan, so a team wanting both pays a combined minimum around $148 a month. What you get for that is the ability to ask visitors directly what confused them or what they were looking for, alongside the behavioral heatmap data, which closes the gap between "what happened" and "why."

Choose Hotjar if on-site surveys and direct qualitative feedback matter as much to you as the click and scroll data itself.

### Crazy Egg: heatmaps with A/B testing built in

Crazy Egg pairs its click maps, scroll maps, and session recordings with built-in A/B testing starting around $99 a month, which lets you test a page variant and watch the heatmap difference in the same tool rather than stitching together two separate products. Its focus has always leaned toward conversion optimization and landing page work specifically, rather than general site analytics.

Choose Crazy Egg if you are actively running landing page experiments and want the testing and the heatmap data in one subscription.

## What to actually watch for in a session recording

Installing a heatmap tool and never reviewing the data is common and wastes the whole exercise. Watch for these specific patterns.

Rage clicks, where a visitor clicks the same spot repeatedly in quick succession, almost always mean they expected something to happen and it did not, usually a button that looks clickable but is not, or a slow-loading element they thought was broken. This is the single highest-value signal in most session tools and is often flagged automatically.

Dead clicks on elements that look interactive but are not, an underlined phrase that is not a link, an icon with no function, quietly frustrate visitors without generating a support ticket or a bounce you can easily trace. A heatmap surfaces these because you see clicks landing on something that never responds.

U-turns in scroll behavior, where a visitor scrolls down quickly, pauses, then scrolls back up, usually mean they skimmed past what they were looking for and are searching for it again. If this happens repeatedly at the same point in an article, your structure is burying the answer that visitor came for.

Watch five to ten session recordings on your highest-traffic page this month, not a hundred. A handful of real recordings on the page that matters most teaches you more than a large sample you never have time to review.

## How this connects to SEO

A heatmap will not move your rankings directly, but it routinely surfaces the reason a page ranks and still underperforms. If your scroll map shows most visitors never reach the second half of an article, and your target keyword's answer is buried down there, that is a content-structure problem a ranking report would never reveal. The placement discipline this points back to is in [where to include keywords on a blog post](/blog/where-to-put-keywords-blog-post/) and [on-page SEO](/seo/on-page-seo/): the direct answer belongs near the top precisely because most visitors, and most AI crawlers extracting an answer, never make it further than that.

Session recordings are also a fast way to catch a confusing mobile layout or a broken interactive element that a page-speed tool would not flag, since those tools test loading, not usability.

## The bottom line

Install Microsoft Clarity first. It is free, has no meaningful traffic cap, and covers what most sites need. Add Hotjar if direct visitor surveys matter to your research process, or Crazy Egg if you are running structured A/B tests and want the testing and heatmap data together.

A ranking gets you the visitor. A heatmap tells you what happened after that, which is often the more fixable problem.

## FAQs

### What is the best free heatmap tool?

Microsoft Clarity, by a clear margin. It offers click, scroll, and area heatmaps plus unlimited session recordings with no traffic limits, at no cost, and now includes AI session summaries. Its main gaps compared to paid tools are the absence of funnel analytics and built-in A/B testing, which most sites do not need to get started.

### Is Hotjar still a separate product from Contentsquare?

Hotjar merged into Contentsquare in mid-2025 but continues to operate under the Hotjar name for heatmaps and session recordings. Its pricing structure changed, with surveys now sold as a separate product from the core heatmap plan, so a team wanting both should budget for roughly $148 a month combined rather than one bundled price.

### Do heatmap tools help with SEO?

Not directly, since they do not affect rankings. What they reveal is why traffic that already arrives fails to convert or engage, which often points to a content-structure problem, like a buried answer or a confusing layout, that also affects how well AI systems and search engines extract value from the page. Use heatmap data to fix the page after the ranking work has gotten someone there.

### What should I look for in a session recording?

Watch for rage clicks (repeated clicking on something that is not responding), dead clicks (clicks on elements that look interactive but are not), and scroll u-turns (scrolling down then quickly back up, which usually means the visitor skimmed past what they wanted). These three patterns point to specific, fixable problems far faster than reading raw analytics numbers, and reviewing five to ten recordings on your highest-traffic page teaches more than skimming a hundred.
