A few years ago, the chatbot widget in the corner of a website was something visitors learned to ignore. It answered three scripted questions and handed everything else to a contact form. That era is over. The bots businesses embed in 2026 read your help docs, resolve real support tickets, qualify leads while your sales team sleeps, and book meetings without a human touching the conversation.

The hard part is no longer making a bot talk. Any tool on this list can hold a fluent conversation. What separates a chatbot worth deploying from one that frustrates customers is grounding: whether its answers come from your actual documentation, pricing, and policies, or from a general model guessing at what your company does. A bot that invents a refund policy is worse than no bot at all.

This guide covers the chatbots businesses actually put on their sites, split across three jobs: deflecting support volume, capturing and qualifying leads, and acting as a general conversational assistant. Most teams pick based on which of those jobs hurts most, then check that the tool grounds answers on their own content and hands off cleanly to a human when it should.

## AI chatbots for customer support

These tools are built to deflect support volume. The job is to resolve a customer's question from your own documentation, escalate cleanly when they cannot, and reduce the number of tickets a human agent ever sees.

### Intercom Fin

Fin is Intercom's AI agent and one of the strongest support-resolution bots available. It reads your help center, past conversations, and connected sources, then answers customer questions autonomously, only escalating to a human when it genuinely cannot resolve the issue. Its grounding is tight enough that it tends to say "I don't know" rather than invent an answer, which is exactly the behavior you want in support.

The pricing model is the headline feature: you pay roughly $0.99 per resolution rather than per conversation, so you are billed for outcomes, not chatter. That aligns cost with value but can get expensive at very high volume, so model it against your ticket numbers first.

**Best for:** Teams that want autonomous, outcome-priced ticket deflection grounded in their help center.

**Pricing:** Usage-based at around $0.99 per resolution, layered on top of an Intercom subscription.

### Zendesk AI

Zendesk AI brings autonomous agents and AI-assisted replies into one of the most widely used help desks. If your support team already lives in Zendesk, the appeal is that the bot draws on the same knowledge base, ticketing, and routing you already run, with no separate platform to maintain. Its AI agents handle common queries and hand off to agents with full context attached.

It is less compelling as a standalone choice. The value comes from the integration, so it makes most sense for teams committed to the Zendesk ecosystem rather than those shopping for a bot in isolation.

**Best for:** Support teams already on Zendesk who want native AI agents inside their existing workflow.

**Pricing:** AI add-on pricing on top of Zendesk Suite plans, which start from around $55 per agent per month.

### Ada

Ada is an enterprise-grade automation platform built for high-volume, multilingual support. It is designed to resolve a large share of inbound conversations across chat, email, and voice, with strong language coverage and analytics that show exactly what the bot is and is not deflecting. Large consumer brands use it to handle support at a scale where every percentage point of automation is real money.

It is priced and built for enterprise, not for a small team testing the water. Expect a sales process and custom pricing rather than a self-serve sign-up.

**Best for:** Enterprises automating support at high volume across multiple languages and channels.

**Pricing:** Custom enterprise pricing.

### Freshchat

Freshchat is the messaging and chatbot product inside the Freshworks suite. It pairs live chat with AI-powered bots that can answer from your knowledge base and route conversations, at a price point friendlier to small and midsize teams than the enterprise platforms. If you use Freshdesk or other Freshworks tools, it slots in naturally.

The AI is capable rather than class-leading, but for the money it covers the core support-deflection job well, which makes it a solid value pick.

**Best for:** SMBs that want an affordable support bot, especially within the Freshworks stack.

**Pricing:** Free tier; paid plans from around $19 per agent per month.

## AI chatbots for lead capture and sales

These tools tilt toward the top of the funnel. They engage visitors, qualify them with a few questions, route hot leads to sales, and book meetings, turning anonymous traffic into pipeline.

### Drift

Drift pioneered conversational marketing, and it remains a strong fit for B2B teams that want chat to drive pipeline rather than deflect tickets. It greets visitors based on who they are, qualifies them in conversation, routes the promising ones to the right rep, and books meetings directly on a calendar. The playbook builder lets marketing teams trigger different conversations by page, campaign, or account.

It is built for sales-led organizations with a real revenue motion, and pricing reflects that. For a small site, it is overkill; for a B2B team where a booked demo is worth thousands, it earns its place.

**Best for:** B2B teams using website chat to qualify and route leads into a sales pipeline.

**Pricing:** Custom pricing, generally aimed at mid-market and enterprise.

### HubSpot Chatbot

HubSpot's chatbot is the natural choice when your CRM is already HubSpot. It captures leads, answers common questions, qualifies visitors, and writes everything straight into the same CRM your sales and marketing teams work from, so there is no syncing or data loss between the conversation and the contact record. The free tier makes it easy to start.

It is more of a rules-and-routing bot with growing AI features than a deep autonomous agent, but for lead capture tied to your CRM, that is usually enough.

**Best for:** Teams on HubSpot who want lead capture and routing wired directly into their CRM.

**Pricing:** Free tier; advanced features bundled into Marketing Hub or Service Hub paid plans.

### ManyChat

ManyChat is built around automation across messaging channels, including Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, and website chat. For businesses whose audience lives in social DMs, it captures and nurtures leads where conversations already happen, then pushes them toward a purchase or booking. Its flow builder is approachable for marketers without technical help.

It is less a website support bot than a messaging-led capture engine. If your demand comes through social and chat apps as much as your site, it is the specialist for that pattern.

**Best for:** Businesses capturing leads across social messaging channels alongside web chat.

**Pricing:** Free tier; paid plans from around $15/month based on contacts.

### Landbot

Landbot replaces the static form with a conversational one. Its no-code visual builder lets you assemble branching, chat-style flows that ask questions one at a time, which tends to lift completion rates compared with a long form. Marketers use it for lead qualification, surveys, and onboarding without writing code.

It is strongest as a structured capture tool rather than an open-ended AI assistant, though it has added AI blocks for more natural conversations. Think of it as a friendlier form, not a support agent.

**Best for:** Marketers who want no-code conversational forms that capture and qualify leads.

**Pricing:** Free tier; paid plans from around $40/month.

## Custom and conversational-assistant chatbots

These tools let you build a bot trained specifically on your own content, or assemble a fully custom agent. They are the route when off-the-shelf support and sales bots do not fit, or when grounding on your exact material is the whole point.

### Chatbase

Chatbase is one of the fastest ways to put a bot grounded in your own content on a site. You point it at your website, upload PDFs and docs, and it builds a chatbot that answers from that material, which you then embed with a snippet. Because the bot only knows what you feed it, grounding is straightforward and hallucination risk is lower than a general model left to guess.

It suits businesses that want a custom assistant without a development project. The free tier is enough to test, and paid plans scale up message volume and sources.

**Best for:** Teams that want a custom bot trained on their own docs and site, fast and without code.

**Pricing:** Free tier; paid plans from around $40/month.

### Botpress

Botpress is a developer-oriented platform for building custom conversational agents with real logic. It combines a visual flow builder with the ability to drop into code, connect APIs, and design complex branching automations that go well beyond question-answering. Teams that need a bot to take actions (check an order, trigger a workflow, query a system) reach for this kind of flexibility.

It expects more technical investment than a plug-and-play tool, so it is the right call when your requirements are specific enough that the simpler bots cannot meet them.

**Best for:** Developers building custom agents with complex logic and integrations.

**Pricing:** Free tier; pay-as-you-go usage-based pricing on top.

### Crisp

Crisp bundles a shared team inbox, live chat, and an AI bot into one affordable messaging platform aimed at small teams. The AI can answer from your knowledge base and handle routine questions, while humans take over in the same inbox when needed. For a small business that wants both human and automated chat without stitching tools together, it is a tidy all-in-one.

It is not as deep on autonomous resolution as the enterprise support platforms, but the combination of price and breadth makes it a practical choice for lean teams.

**Best for:** Small teams that want live chat and an AI bot in one affordable inbox.

**Pricing:** Free tier; paid plans from around $45/month per workspace.

### Tidio (Lyro)

Tidio is a popular SMB choice that pairs live chat with Lyro, its AI bot. Lyro answers customer questions from your content and can handle a meaningful share of common queries autonomously, while Tidio's wider toolkit covers both support and light sales automation. The free tier and approachable pricing make it easy for a small store or service business to start.

It sits comfortably between a pure support deflection tool and a lead-capture bot, which is part of why small businesses like it: one widget covering both jobs.

**Best for:** Small businesses wanting combined support and sales chat with an AI bot built in.

**Pricing:** Free tier; Lyro AI plans from around $39/month.

## What to look for in an AI website chatbot

The names matter less than how the bot behaves once it is live. A few criteria separate a tool that earns its keep from one that creates work.

**Grounding on your own data.** The single most important trait is whether the bot answers from your documentation, pricing, and policies rather than from a general model's guess. A bot that can ingest your help center, site, and files, and that declines to answer when it lacks a source, is far safer than a fluent one that improvises. Ask exactly what sources a tool trains on and how it handles questions outside that material.

**Clean handoff to humans.** No bot resolves everything, and the ones that pretend to do real damage. Look for smooth escalation that passes the full conversation, context, and customer details to a human agent so the person does not start from zero. The handoff is where good bots keep customers and bad bots lose them.

**Integrations.** A chatbot is only as useful as the systems it connects to. Check that it ties into your CRM, help desk, calendar, and the channels your customers actually use. A lead the bot captures but cannot write to your CRM is a lead half-lost.

**Pricing model.** Tools price by seat, by conversation, or by resolution, and the right model depends on your volume. Outcome-based pricing like Intercom Fin's per-resolution rate aligns cost with value but can climb at scale, while per-seat pricing is predictable but rewards you nothing for automation. Model your real ticket and lead numbers against each before committing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best AI chatbot for a website overall in 2026?

For autonomous customer support, Intercom Fin is the strongest overall choice because it resolves tickets from your help center on its own and prices by resolution rather than by conversation. For lead capture tied to a CRM, HubSpot's chatbot is hard to beat. The honest answer is that "best" depends on the job: pick a support-resolution tool if you are drowning in tickets, and a lead-capture tool if you are leaving pipeline on the table.

### What is the best free AI chatbot for a website?

Several strong tools offer real free tiers. Tidio, Crisp, and HubSpot all let you run live chat and a basic bot at no cost, and Chatbase has a free tier for building a bot trained on your own content. These are genuine starting points for a small business, though support volume, message limits, and advanced AI features are what you pay to unlock as you grow.

### What is the best AI chatbot for a small business?

Tidio with its Lyro AI is a popular small-business pick because one widget covers both support and light sales, with approachable pricing from around $39 a month. Crisp is a strong alternative for teams that want a shared inbox plus AI in one affordable place, and Chatbase suits businesses that want a custom bot trained on their docs without a development project. All three avoid the enterprise pricing and sales processes of the larger platforms.

### What is the difference between an AI chatbot for support and one for sales?

A support chatbot is built to resolve questions and deflect tickets, so it prioritizes grounding in your help docs and clean escalation to human agents. A sales chatbot is built to capture and qualify visitors, so it prioritizes engaging the right people, routing them to reps, and booking meetings. Intercom Fin, Zendesk AI, and Ada lean toward support, while Drift, HubSpot, and Landbot lean toward sales and lead capture. Some tools like Tidio aim to do both.

### Can an AI chatbot train on my own website content?

Yes, and this is the most important capability to check for. Tools like Chatbase, Intercom Fin, and Botpress can ingest your website, help center, and uploaded documents so the bot answers from your actual material rather than a general model's guess. A bot grounded this way is far less likely to invent policies or prices, and it declines to answer when it lacks a source, which is exactly what you want in front of customers.

### Do AI website chatbots hand off to human agents?

The good ones do, and the handoff is where they prove their value. A capable bot resolves what it can and escalates the rest to a human agent with the full conversation and customer context attached, so the person does not start from scratch. When you evaluate a tool, test the escalation path directly, because a bot that traps customers in a loop instead of reaching a human will cost you more goodwill than it saves in tickets.
