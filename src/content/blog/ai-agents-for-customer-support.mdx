The best AI agents for customer support in 2027 are Salesforce Agentforce for Service, Freshworks Freddy Copilot and Front, followed by Genesys Cloud, Dialpad, Five9, ASAPP, Talkdesk, Observe.AI and Zowie. We ranked them on published price, lifecycle coverage, controls, evidence and how well each one feeds your help content.

## Key takeaways

- Support has two jobs for AI. One is closing tickets with no human. The other is making the humans faster and better. This list covers the second job, and the evidence for it is stronger than for the first.
- The only study-grade number is old and narrow. An NBER study of 5,179 agents found a 14 percent productivity gain, and 34 percent for novices. Every handle-time figure from a vendor in this category is unaudited.
- Only five of ten vendors publish a price. Front, Freshworks, Dialpad, Salesforce and Genesys do, in different units. ASAPP, Observe.AI, Talkdesk, Five9 and Zowie make you ask.
- Gartner reports a gap between spend and return. AI spending by service leaders rose 38 percent against a 2 percent rise in budgets, and a conference headline says only 4 percent reached their expected ROI.
- Copilots and public content are one system. Almost every tool here drafts from your help articles, so a stale article ships as a confident draft.

## What changed in 2026

The conversation moved. In 2025 buyers asked whether a bot could replace a tier-one team. In 2026 the numbers started to push back, and the center of gravity shifted toward the person with the headset.

Gartner's April 2026 release, based on a survey of 321 service and support leaders fielded in September and October 2025, found that [85 percent are expanding human agent responsibilities](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-28-gartner-survey-finds-eighty-five-percent-of-service-and-support-leaders-are-expanding-human-agent-responsibilities-despite-expectations-of-mass-ai-layoffs) despite expectations of mass layoffs, and that 63 percent are shrinking frontline headcount only gradually through attrition. Gartner's Eric Keller put the thesis in one line: the advantage comes from combining AI efficiency with human judgment, empathy and experience.

Money followed hype first. A Gartner survey of 199 leaders, run in April and May 2026, found AI spending [up 38 percent while overall service budgets rose 2 percent](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-26-gartner-survey-finds-ai-spending-by-customer-service-leaders-has-surged-by-38-percent-despite-overall-service-and-support-function-budgets-rising-by-just-2-percent). Returns trail it. In a separate July release, Gartner reported that only [24 percent of service and support leaders showed positive financial returns](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-08-gartner-survey-finds-customers-are-three-times-more-likely-to-use-third-party-genai-than-company-provided-chatbots-for-customer-service) across their AI use cases, drawn from a mixed sample of 1,303 senior leaders across industries. Gartner's November conference [highlights page](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-11-05-gartner-customer-service-and-support-conference-day-2-highlights) carries a headline that only 4 percent of service leaders hit their expected ROI. We could not read the release behind that headline, so we have no sample size and no definition. Do not compare the 4 percent with the 24 percent. They come from different surveys.

Customers also moved. The same July release found customers are three times more likely to use a third-party GenAI tool than a company-provided chatbot in their latest service interaction, and that company chatbot use has been statistically flat since 2022. If your own bot is the least-used channel, the human with a good copilot is the safer bet.

## What counts as AI for customer support

Vendors blur four jobs. Separate them before you read any pricing page.

**Deflect.** A customer-facing agent answers and tries to close the ticket. Success is measured in resolutions or containment.

**Assist.** A copilot works beside the human. It drafts, summarizes, suggests articles and fills in wrap-up codes. The human sends the message.

**QA and coaching.** Software scores conversations, flags risk and feeds coaching. The promise is 100 percent coverage instead of a sample.

**Route.** Triage by intent, priority and skill, before or during the conversation.

Most vendors on this list touch two or three of these. We tag each one below. The pure play is rare, and it is worth knowing which part of your workflow you are actually buying.

For the wider definition of an agent versus a chatbot or a workflow, see our overview of [AI agents for business](/blog/ai-agents-for-business/).

## The market in numbers

Every figure below was read on the source page in November 2026. Vendor surveys are marked as vendor surveys.

**Peer-reviewed productivity.** The NBER working paper [Generative AI at Work](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161) studied 5,179 customer support agents using a generative AI conversational assistant. Issues resolved per hour rose about 14 percent on average and 34 percent for novice and low-skilled workers, with minimal impact on experienced workers. The authors also report better customer sentiment and higher employee retention. We read the abstract, not the full paper. It is one company and an older tool, but it is the only independent measurement on this list.

**Routine work.** Salesforce's seventh State of Service report, a vendor survey of 6,500 service professionals fielded in spring 2025, found that [reps using AI spend 20 percent less time on routine cases](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-service-report-announcement-2025/), about four hours a week. It also found 51 percent of leaders said security concerns had delayed or limited AI projects.

**Customers want a human.** Gartner's August 2026 release, built on 3,566 customers surveyed in February and March, found [87 percent say a route to a human is essential](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-08-04-gartner-survey-finds-87-percent-of-customers-say-companies-using-genai-for-customer-service-must-provide-access-to-a-human-agent0) when a company uses GenAI for service. That is a stated preference, not behavior, but it matches the 2026 pivot toward assist.

**Third-party AI.** The July release also found 58 percent of customers who used GenAI had it complete a task, and 74 percent in B2B. Gartner's conference page adds that 60 percent of B2B customers and 47 percent of consumers had consulted GenAI before buying.

**Attrition.** A Verint survey of 1,000 contact center agents in centers of 300 or more seats, [reported by No Jitter](https://nojitter.com/contact-centers/agent-attrition-rates-are-high-verint-survey-finds), found 31 percent likely to leave within six months, 46 percent among agents aged 18 to 34 and 8 percent among those 45 and older. The top reason was unrealistic performance expectations, at 47 percent. ContactBabel's estimate quoted in the same piece puts average US attrition near 30 percent. We could not verify any burnout figure from a primary source, so we use none.

**QA coverage.** A [third-party case study host](https://www.casestudies.com/company/observeai/case-study/sofi-unlocks-coaching-insights-and-efficiency-gains-with-100-interaction-coverage-via-qa-automation) summarizing an Observe.AI customer story says SoFi reviewed about 2 percent of interactions before automation and 100 percent after. One vendor customer, no method, but it shows the pitch.

**Vendor handle-time claims.** Five9 says Agent Assist [decreases average handle time by 27 percent](https://five9.com/products/capabilities/ai-summaries) on average. Talkdesk says Farfetch improved handle times by 50 percent. ASAPP says JetBlue saved 280 seconds per conversation. None gives a method. Hold all three against the NBER 14 percent.

The pattern in these numbers is plain. The independent evidence is modest and uneven, the vendor evidence is big and unaudited, and the largest benefit lands on your least experienced people.

## How we ranked them

This ranking uses vendor documentation, vendor pricing pages and published analyst commentary, opened in November 2026. We did not run hands-on tests, and no vendor gave us anything. That limits what we can say about draft quality, and we flag it where it matters.

Each product scores 1 to 5 on five criteria, for a maximum of 25. The scores are editorial judgment from the sources. Rescore with your own weights.

| Criterion | What earns a 5 | What earns a 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Price clarity | Published per-seat or per-unit price with the billing rule | Demo or quote only |
| Lifecycle coverage | Assist, QA, routing and deflection in documented features | One narrow function |
| Controls | Human review before send, supervisor guardrails, redaction, rating of suggestions | Controls implied, none shown |
| Evidence | Named customers with checkable numbers and a stated method | Logos and unattributed percentages |
| Knowledge loop | Drafts from your articles and writes resolved tickets back into them | Knowledge source is opaque |

Ties break on evidence, then controls. A published price helps a product's score. It says nothing about draft quality.

## The 10 agents, ranked

### 1. Salesforce Agentforce for Service (rep assist)

**Score: 20 of 25** (price 4, coverage 4, controls 4, evidence 3, knowledge loop 5). Lifecycle: assist, route.

The assist side of Salesforce is separate from its customer-facing agent. Per the [Salesforce service AI page](https://www.salesforce.com/service/ai/), the Service Rep Assistant builds Dynamic Plans from customer data, conversation history and Knowledge to guide a rep on any channel. Conversation Summaries create an issue and resolution summary on any case, which the rep reviews, edits and saves. Service Replies generates answers from the knowledge base. Enterprise Knowledge pulls third-party sources in through Data 360.

Who it suits: teams already on Service Cloud, where the data is already in place.

Pricing as published on Salesforce in November 2026: Agentforce for Service at $125 per user per month billed annually, which includes Generative Replies, Summaries, Answers, Knowledge Articles, an unmetered Employee Agent and Customer Signals Intelligence. A Max edition lists at $550 per user per month. The page adds that pricing is subject to change.

The standout is the knowledge loop. Saved summaries can feed the knowledge base, so a resolved ticket becomes tomorrow's article.

Real limits. The page carries no figures specific to rep assist. A Salesforce Trailhead module describes case summaries and resolution guidance grounded in your data, but states no requirements. And per-seat pricing means you pay for every rep, including the ones who never touch the feature.

Use case: a rep opens an escalated case, reads an editable summary of the thread, follows a generated plan for the next step, and saves the summary back to the case.

Verdict: the strongest closed loop on the list, if you already live in Service Cloud. If you do not, the platform cost is the real price.

### 2. Freshworks Freddy AI Copilot

Freddy Copilot is the agent-side layer for Freshdesk. The [Freshdesk support article](https://support.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/50000010359-overview-of-freddy-ai-for-ticketing) lists a writing assistant, summary generator for handoffs, reply suggester that uses your solution articles, solution article generator, sentiment analysis, canned response suggester, auto triage that recommends priority, group and status, an agent assist bot, usage reports, a thank-you detector that prevents tickets reopening on a courtesy reply, and live translation.

Who it suits: Freshdesk and Freshdesk Omni teams.

Pricing as published on [Freshworks](https://www.freshworks.com/freshdesk/pricing/) in November 2026: $29 per agent per month as an add-on on Pro and Enterprise, not available on Growth, assignable to any number of agents. Plans run $19, $55 and $89 per agent per month billed annually.

Standout: the reply suggester draws on your solution articles, and the generator writes new ones, so article quality and copilot quality are the same variable.

Real limits. It is an add-on on top of the seat price, and an admin must assign the Copilot role to each agent. The sentiment analysis here scores customers. If you want to score your own agents' tone, read the regulatory section first. The pricing page quotes a customer saying the product is a game-changer for the team, with no figure behind it.

Use case: a new agent opens a ticket, gets a suggested reply built from the right solution article, edits it, and the thank-you detector stops the courtesy message from reopening the ticket.

Verdict: the cheapest published entry among the broad copilots, and the clearest example of how a help center feeds an assistant.

### 3. Front

**Score: 18 of 25** (price 5, coverage 4, controls 4, evidence 2, knowledge loop 3). Lifecycle: assist, QA, deflect, route.

Front is a shared inbox and help desk. Who it suits: teams with email-heavy and relationship-style support, where the inbox is the product.

Real limits. The Copilot page offers no numbers and no customer example. Smart QA appears in navigation but we found no page describing how it works. Plan inclusion for external agents is unclear on the pricing page.

Use case: Copilot drafts reviewed by a person first, then eligible repeat inquiries move to Autopilot once the team trusts the output.

Verdict: the most transparent pricing in the field, and the only entry where human review before sending is stated as the default. Evidence is thin.

## How copilots change who gets found and cited

A search publication ranks support software because the support stack is now a content pipeline.

**Your articles become draft text.** Freddy's reply suggester proposes replies using your solution articles. Salesforce says Service Replies generate answers from the knowledge base. Genesys requires your knowledge to be uploaded to its Knowledge Workbench. Dialpad's coach pulls from connected sources that sync every 24 hours. Whatever is wrong in an article is wrong in a hundred drafts by lunchtime.

**Tickets become articles.** Salesforce says saved summaries can feed the knowledge base, and Freshworks generates solution articles from tickets. That is a good loop and a risky one. A bad resolution, summarized and published, becomes the next reference.

**Customers go around you.** Gartner found customers are three times more likely to use a third-party GenAI tool than a company chatbot, and that 47 percent of consumers and 60 percent of B2B buyers had consulted GenAI before buying. Gartner's pages do not say which sources those tools read, and we found no vendor-neutral study showing that restructuring a help center changes AI citation. Anything we recommend below is inference, not a measured result.

What we would do, in order of cost:

1. Publish policies for refunds, shipping, cancellation and warranty as public HTML with dates. Outside assistants can only quote what they can read.
2. Give every help article an owner and a review date, and review the ones your copilot suggests most often first.
3. Add accurate structured data where it fits. Our guide to [schema markup for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/) covers method, and [FAQ schema and AI SEO](/blog/faq-schema-ai-seo/) covers question pages.
4. Test what assistants say about your policies, and track it. [How to measure AI visibility](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/) shows what to track, and [where AI engines pull citations](/blog/where-ai-engines-pull-citations/) shows what they read.
5. Hold ticket-to-article automation behind a human editor.

## How to choose

Start from your constraint, not the feature list.

**You already run Service Cloud or Freshdesk.** Price your own platform's copilot first. Agentforce for Service and Freddy Copilot fit with the data you already hold, and neither asks you to move.

**You run an inbox-first team.** Front is the clearest fit, with a published price and review before send as the default.

**You run a voice contact center.** Compare Genesys, Dialpad, Five9 and Talkdesk on the same three questions: what does a seat cost with the AI included, how fast does an article change reach the coach, and how is redaction handled.

**You are mostly worried about quality.** Observe.AI and Dialpad lead with QA coverage. Ask how automated scores are calibrated against human reviewers, and how often.

**You have a regulated workflow.** Zowie's split between rules and language is worth a conversation, but ask for proof.

**You have a small team.** A $20 or $29 add-on is a cheap experiment. A custom quote almost never is.

If you are choosing a single customer-facing agent instead, our playbook on how to choose, deploy and measure an [AI agent for customer service](/blog/ai-agent-for-customer-service/) is the better starting point.

## Implementation, governance and risk

**Measure the baseline first.** The NBER result is a reminder that benefits differ by experience. Split your handle time and resolution data by tenure before you start, or you will not know who the tool helped.

**Pilot with new hires.** If the largest gain is for novices, the first group to give the tool is the one that most needs it. Compare them with a matched cohort that does not have it.

**Keep a person in the send path.** Gartner's finding that 87 percent of customers want a route to a human is about customers, but the same instinct applies to drafts. Front makes review before send the default. Do the same wherever you can, at least until your error rate is measured.

**Watch emotion scoring.** Article 5(1)(f) of the EU AI Act, per an [annotated copy](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/), bans AI systems that infer emotions in the workplace and in education, except for medical or safety reasons. A [CMS Law summary](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-commission-issues-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-part-ii) of the Commission's guidelines gives a call center example both ways. Tracking employees' emotions is prohibited, while tracking customers' emotions is outside the ban because customers are not in the workplace. Vendors rarely say which one a feature does. Customer sentiment scores, like Dialpad's AI CSAT or Freddy's sentiment analysis, are one thing. Scoring your own agents' tone in QA is another. We opened an unofficial copy of the Act and a law firm summary, and the page notes amendments we did not read, so confirm on the official text. Also note Article 50(3), which requires deployers of emotion recognition systems to inform the people exposed.

**Check call recording consent.** California Penal Code 632, per a [FindLaw copy](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/penal-code/pen-sect-632/), prohibits intentionally recording a confidential communication without the consent of all parties, with a first-offense fine of up to $2,500 per violation and up to a year in county jail. Tools that record or transcribe every call need a clear disclosure at the start. We did not verify other states.

**Handle card data.** PCI Security Standards Council [FAQ 1210](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/1210/) says storing card validation codes in call recordings after authorization violates Requirement 3.3.1, and that every effort must be made to keep them from being recorded, with suppression or redaction during entry. If your vendor's redaction is in an early adopter program, as Dialpad's page says, treat it as unfinished.

**Watch the cost curve.** Gartner expects, in a January 2026 release, that generative AI cost per resolution will exceed offshore human agent costs by 2030 (reused from our customer service post, not re-read this month). Per-seat pricing hides this. Per-resolution pricing exposes it.

None of this is legal advice. Have counsel check your markets.

## 2027 outlook

Three things look likely, and one does not.

The suites will absorb the point tools. Salesforce, Genesys, Five9 and Talkdesk already sell assist, QA and routing together. A stand-alone QA vendor will have to prove it is better than the one inside your phone system.

Pricing will move from seat to outcome, and the two will blur. Front already sells both a per-seat copilot and a per-conversation autopilot. Expect more vendors to do the same.

Role redesign is the real story. Gartner reports 85 percent of leaders adding tasks to the human role, and Salesforce reports that 86 percent of AI-using reps developed new skills. The job is turning into knowledge management with a customer attached.

What we do not expect is a clean answer on ROI. Gartner's 4 percent and 24 percent are not comparable, and neither tells you what your own queue will do. Run your own numbers.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between an AI agent and agent assist in customer support?

An AI agent talks to the customer and tries to close the ticket without a human. Agent assist works beside a human, with drafts, summaries, suggested articles and routing, and the person still sends the reply. Several products on this list do both, but they bill, fail and get governed differently.

### Does agent assist actually make support agents faster?

The best public evidence is an NBER study of 5,179 support agents, which found issues resolved per hour rose about 14 percent on average and 34 percent for novices, with minimal gains for experienced agents. Vendor handle-time claims such as 27 percent or 50 percent come without methods, so treat them as marketing until you measure your own queue.

### How much does an agent copilot cost?

Where prices are published in November 2026 they run from $20 per seat per month for Front Copilot and $29 for Freshworks Freddy Copilot to $125 per user per month for Agentforce for Service. Dialpad sells contact center tiers at $89, $119 and $149. ASAPP, Observe.AI, Talkdesk, Five9 and Zowie publish no figure.

### Is it legal to score my agents' emotions with AI?

In the EU, Article 5(1)(f) of the AI Act bans AI that infers emotions in the workplace, except for medical or safety reasons. A law firm summary of the Commission's guidance says tracking employees' emotions in a call center is prohibited while tracking customers' emotions is outside that ban. This is not legal advice, and you should read the guidance itself.

### Do I need to tell customers a call is recorded and transcribed?

In California, Penal Code 632 prohibits recording a confidential communication without the consent of all parties, so QA and coaching tools that record or transcribe every call need a disclosure at the start. Other states differ, and we did not verify them. Ask counsel before you switch on call analytics.

### Will agent assist reduce my headcount?

Gartner's survey of 321 service leaders found 85 percent are adding responsibilities to human agent roles and 63 percent are reducing frontline headcount only gradually through attrition. A Gartner conference headline in November 2026 says 21 percent of leaders have reduced headcount because of AI. The underlying release carries no method we could read.

### How does customer support AI affect AI search visibility?

Copilots draft from your help articles, and some turn resolved tickets into new articles, so the quality of your public content flows into both your agents and any outside assistant that reads it. Gartner found customers are three times more likely to use a third-party GenAI tool than a company chatbot, which makes clear public answers worth more, not less.
