A patient noticing blurry vision in 2026 does not call the first eye doctor in the phone book. They ask ChatGPT whether they need an optometrist or an ophthalmologist. They ask Perplexity which eye care clinics in their zip code accept VSP. ## Quick answer

The four things that drive optometry SEO in 2026: a deeply optimized Google Business Profile per location with vision insurance carriers listed explicitly, condition and procedure pages authored by licensed ODs (dry eye, glaucoma, LASIK co-management, diabetic eye exams), a systematic post-exam review velocity program, and [schema markup](/seo/schema-markup/) that makes the practice and its optometrists machine-readable. Most eye care practices are doing none of these well. The ones that compound all four dominate their markets.

- GBP must list specific vision insurance carriers (VSP, EyeMed, Spectera, Davis Vision, Superior Vision), not "most insurance accepted"
- Condition pages that rank are OD-bylined, 1,500+ words, and answer the specific questions AI engines extract
- Review velocity is the single fastest-moving lever, most optometry practices are dramatically underperforming their review potential
- `Optometrist` schema with `hasCredential` per OD is the credential signal AI engines need to trust and cite healthcare content

## Why optometry SEO changed in 2026

Three shifts changed the patient acquisition landscape for eye care practices:

1. **AI Overviews answer vision questions directly.** "What causes dry eyes," "do I need a referral for LASIK," "how often should adults get eye exams," "what is macular degeneration." Google synthesizes answers from a handful of trusted clinical sources. Practices cited as sources get brand impression at zero marginal cost. Everyone else gets nothing from that query.
2. **ChatGPT and Perplexity recommend specific practices.** Conversational queries like "which eye doctor near me takes EyeMed" or "best optometrist in [city] for contact lenses" now return actual practice names. Those recommendations are pulled from review profiles, structured data, and content depth, not paid placement.
3. **YMYL scrutiny applies to eye care.** Optometry content (recommendations about treatments, conditions, procedures, and medical decisions about vision) falls squarely under Google's Your Money or Your Life category. Practices without proper [E-E-A-T signals](/seo/eeat/) and OD-attributed clinical content are filtered out of AI recommendations.

This is part of a broader structural shift across healthcare. Dental and medical practices are experiencing the same dynamic. ## Pillar 1: Google Business Profile

GBP is the highest-converting patient acquisition surface for optometry practices. New patients check it before they call. For insurance-driven healthcare like optometry, GBP is often the final decision point.

The non-negotiables per location:

- **Primary category:** Optometrist (for OD-led practices) or Eye Care Center (for optical retail with clinical services)
- **Secondary categories:** every applicable one, Ophthalmologist if you have an MD on staff, Contact Lenses Supplier, Sunglasses Store if you carry optical retail, Low Vision Specialist if applicable
- **Vision insurance listed explicitly:** VSP, EyeMed, Spectera, Davis Vision, Superior Vision, Humana Vision, UnitedHealthcare Vision, Blue View Vision, named carriers, not generic language
- **Appointment booking:** link directly to your scheduling page or a booking integration; patients who cannot book online in two taps leave
- **30+ photos:** exterior, reception, exam lanes, OCT and imaging equipment, optical dispensary, and the ODs themselves
- **Weekly posts:** new patient promotions, vision health tips, frame arrivals, community events
- **Q&A seeded with patient questions:** "Do you accept VSP?" "Can I get same-day contact lens fittings?" "Do you see children?"
- **Hours that reflect reality:** including extended or Saturday hours if you offer them, updated for holidays

The pattern for dental GBP optimization at [Google Business Profile for dentists](/blog/google-business-profile-for-dentists/) follows the same logic, insurance visibility and category specificity are the two levers that move the local pack for insurance-driven healthcare providers.

## Pillar 2: Condition and procedure pages

This is the content investment with the longest compounding return. Each condition you manage and each procedure you perform deserves a dedicated page, 1,500 to 2,500 words, bylined to the OD with clinical expertise in that area.

Conditions and procedures that should each have their own page:

- **Dry eye syndrome**, the most searched optometry condition; cover symptoms, causes, in-office treatment (LipiFlow, IPL, meibomian gland expression), and at-home management
- **Glaucoma management**, suspect screening, monitoring protocols, IOP management, and when you co-manage with ophthalmology
- **Cataracts**, detection, referral criteria, co-management pre- and post-operatively
- **LASIK co-management**, pre-op candidacy screening, post-op care, what to expect; this is a high-value query because patients want to start locally
- **Diabetic eye exams**, annual dilated exam, retinopathy screening, co-management with primary care and endocrinology; diabetic patients are a high-LTV patient segment
- **Macular degeneration (AMD)**, risk factors, OCT imaging, AREDS2 supplementation discussion
- **Contact lens fittings**, standard soft, multifocal, toric, scleral; a separate page for each lens type is justified if you have fitting volume
- **Pediatric eye exams and amblyopia**, parents search extensively for pediatric eye care and first vision checks
- **Myopia management**, orthokeratology, MiSight contact lenses, and atropine; one of the highest-growth query categories in optometry
- **Comprehensive eye exams**, this should not be a thin page; cover what happens at each step, what conditions are detected, and how often each age group should be seen

Each page should answer the questions AI Overviews extract: what it is, who needs it, what to expect at the exam, how the condition is managed, and what the practice's specific clinical approach is. Byline it to the OD who performs that service. The [American Optometric Association](https://www.aoa.org) publishes clinical practice guidelines for most of these conditions, reference and link to them. That external anchoring signals clinical accuracy to both Google and AI engines.

## Pillar 3: Review velocity

Reviews are the most underused lever in optometry SEO. The average optometry practice has 30 to 80 Google reviews accumulated over years. A practice that runs a systematic post-exam review program can reach 200+ reviews within 12 months. That volume difference is visible in both the local pack and AI recommendations.

### The post-exam SMS ask

Send within 4 to 24 hours of the exam, while the patient is still thinking about their prescription and the new frames they ordered.

Template: "Hi [First name], thanks for visiting [Practice name] today. If Dr. [OD name] and our team gave you a great experience, we would really appreciate a quick Google review, it helps other patients find us. [direct Google review link]"

Personalization matters. Mentioning the OD by name converts 40 to 60 percent better than generic practice-level asks. Patients form a relationship with their eye doctor, not the business name.

### HIPAA-compliant framing

Never reference the visit type, diagnosis, or insurance in the outbound message. Acknowledge the visit generically. Responses to reviews also need HIPAA discipline, never confirm patient status, never reference clinical details even when responding to a patient who disclosed their own information.

### Direct review link

Do not ask patients to search for your practice. Generate your Google review link from Google Business Profile and use it directly in the SMS. Every additional tap between the request and the review box costs you conversions.

### Cross-platform consistency

Vision patients cross-check Yelp, Healthgrades, Zocdoc, and 1-800-Contacts for OD reviews. Active, complete profiles on all four signal that the practice is established and trustworthy. AI engines pulling local provider recommendations draw from multiple review sources.

## Pillar 4: Schema markup

Optometry is healthcare. Healthcare is YMYL. YMYL content without verifiable structured data is filtered out of AI recommendation sets. Every optometry practice needs the following schema infrastructure:

**Practice-level schema:**
- `Optometrist` type (or `MedicalBusiness` as the broader parent) on the homepage and each location page
- `name`, `address`, `telephone`, `url`, `openingHours`, `paymentAccepted` with specific vision insurance carriers
- `geo` coordinates for each location
- `hasMap` pointing to the Google Maps listing

**OD-level schema:**
Each optometrist on staff should have a `Person` schema entity on their bio page that includes:
- `name`, `jobTitle` ("Doctor of Optometry" or "Optometrist")
- `hasCredential` with OD degree, state license, and any board certifications (FAAO, specialty certifications)
- `worksFor` linked to the practice entity
- `alumniOf` with optometry school
- `knowsAbout` with relevant specialties

**Condition and procedure page schema:**
- `MedicalCondition` schema on dry eye, glaucoma, AMD, diabetic retinopathy pages
- `MedicalProcedure` schema on LASIK co-management, contact lens fitting, myopia management pages
- `FAQPage` schema on any page with a FAQ section

**Review schema:**
- `AggregateRating` surfaced on the homepage and OD bio pages

## E-E-A-T for optometrists

Google's quality rater guidelines treat optometry the same as any other clinical healthcare: [Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t) must be demonstrable at both the site level and the page level. AI engines have internalized these standards, they will not cite optometry content that lacks credentialed authorship.

What the E-E-A-T infrastructure looks like for an eye care practice:

**Named ODs on every clinical page.** Anonymous "our team" bylines fail YMYL extraction. The OD who performs dry eye treatments should byline the dry eye page. The OD who fits sclerals should byline the scleral lens page. If your practice has one OD, that OD's name goes on every clinical piece.

**Full bio pages with verifiable credentials.** Each OD's bio page should include: full name, OD degree and institution, state license number, any FAAO fellowship, specialty certifications (American Board of Optometry certification, specialty contact lens certifications, glaucoma co-management certification), and continuing education relevant to specialty areas. The [American Optometric Association](https://www.aoa.org/education/residency-programs) maintains residency and specialty program directories that can validate credentials.

**Published content outside the practice website.** Guest posts on optometry association blogs, quoted commentary in local health publications, or contributions to continuing education content build off-site authority that AI engines can verify.

**Clinical accuracy signals.** External links to authoritative clinical sources (AOA, American Academy of Optometry, NEI) on condition pages signal that the content is clinically grounded, not marketing copy. AI engines weigh this.

## FAQs

### How long does optometry SEO take to show results?

Local pack and GBP improvements show fastest, often within 60 to 90 days when categories, insurance carriers, and review velocity are addressed together. Condition-specific pages can rank for long-tail queries (dry eye treatment [city], myopia management [city]) within six to twelve weeks. AI Overview citations typically lag organic ranking by two to four months.

### Is optometry content always YMYL?

Yes. Any content that involves clinical recommendations, treatment decisions, or vision health falls under YMYL. That means every condition page, every procedure page, and any post about eye health needs licensed OD authorship and clinical accuracy. The standard is higher than most practices currently apply.

### Should individual ODs or just the practice name be cited on content?

Named ODs, always. An anonymous optometry practice page advising on glaucoma management does not carry the credential signal needed to pass YMYL extraction. The OD's name, degree, and state license need to be verifiable. AI engines look for the named human expert behind healthcare content.

### Can I use AI to write condition and procedure pages?

For first drafts, yes. The AI draft needs substantive clinical review and contribution from the named OD before publishing. AI-drafted health content without licensed OD review fails YMYL standards and can create clinical accuracy liability. The OD's contribution should be substantive, not cosmetic.

### How does vision insurance listing affect local pack ranking?

Insurance is one of the highest-intent filters in optometry search. A patient with EyeMed searching "eye doctor near me" who sees your practice listed with EyeMed explicitly is dramatically more likely to call than if they have to call and ask. GBP insurance fields, website insurance pages, and schema `paymentAccepted` fields all contribute to how AI engines surface your practice for insurance-specific queries.

### What schema type should I use for an eye care practice?

`Optometrist` is the most specific schema.org type for an OD-led practice. If the practice also has an ophthalmologist on staff, add `Ophthalmologist`. For practices that are primarily optical retail with clinical services, `MedicalBusiness` as a parent type with `Optometrist` as the `@type` covers the bases. Each OD on staff should have their own `Person` entity with `hasCredential` populated.

## What this looks like operationally

A 90-day plan for an optometry practice:

- **Month 1:** Full GBP audit and rebuild, categories corrected, vision insurance carriers listed explicitly, booking link added, 30+ photos uploaded. Review velocity SMS system deployed. First 5 condition pages drafted and OD-reviewed.
- **Month 2:** 5 more condition and procedure pages. Schema deployment: practice-level `Optometrist` schema, `Person` schema for each OD, `MedicalCondition` on condition pages. OD bio pages with full credential listings.
- **Month 3:** Remaining procedure pages (myopia management, LASIK co-management, specialty contact lens fitting). `FAQPage` schema on key pages. Citation cleanup across Healthgrades, Vitals, Zocdoc, and 1-800-Contacts.

Twelve months in: 15+ condition and procedure pages, OD authority infrastructure across all providers, 150+ recent reviews, and patient inquiries from specialty queries your zip-code competitors are not targeting.
