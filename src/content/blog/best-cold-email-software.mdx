## How cold emailing works now

Start with a number nobody agrees on. Belkins analyzed 7,530,489 cold emails sent in 2025 and found [a 0.45% reply rate](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates), counting unique replies against every email sent. Hunter's study of 11 million cold emails sent in 2024 put [the average campaign reply rate at 4.1%](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025). That is a gap of about nine times.

Part of it is definition. One study divides by emails sent. The other averages across campaigns. The two datasets also differ in year and in who sends. So when a tool's marketing page quotes a reply rate, ask what it divided by.

Hunter's report has a second finding worth keeping: campaigns of 50 recipients or fewer averaged a 5.8% reply rate, against 2.1% for campaigns of 1,000 or more. Small and targeted beats big and loud. Buy software for control, not for volume.

### The sender rules you cannot skip

Gmail and Yahoo now publish hard requirements, and every tool in this list sits on top of them.

Google's sender guidelines apply to everyone sending to Gmail: set up SPF or DKIM, keep valid forward and reverse DNS records, use a TLS connection, format messages to the RFC 5322 standard, and [keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126). Google recommends staying under 0.10%.

Bulk senders, meaning more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail, face more. They need SPF and DKIM and a DMARC policy, which can be set to `none`. The From domain must align with the SPF or DKIM domain. Marketing messages must support one-click unsubscribe and show an unsubscribe link in the body.

Yahoo's [sender best practices](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/) ask for the same core: SPF, DKIM, a DMARC policy of at least `p=none` that passes, and aligned domains. Add a list-unsubscribe header that supports one-click unsubscribe. Yahoo also says to honor unsubscribes within 2 days and to keep your spam rate below 0.3%.

If you stay under 5,000 a day, the bulk rules do not formally apply to you. The spam rate and authentication rules still do, and an unsubscribe line costs nothing. Add it.

### Warm-up, mailboxes and rotation

Three terms show up on every pricing page, so here is what they mean.

**Warm-up** is automated sending between mailboxes in a network, meant to build a sending history before you email real prospects. **Rotation** spreads a campaign across several mailboxes so no single inbox carries the whole load. **Mailboxes** are the real constraint: each tool caps something, whether it is emails sent, contacts or accounts.

Send from a separate domain, not your company's main one. If a bad week damages the domain's reputation, you want it to be a throwaway and not the address your invoices come from.

## How we ranked these tools

This ranking is based on public sources: each vendor's pricing and feature pages, opened in November 2026, plus the results currently ranking for "cold email software." We did not run hands-on tests, and we did not use review-site scores. The order is our judgment of practical fit for a sender who cares about inbox placement.

Four things set the order:

- **Deliverability controls.** Warm-up, rotation across mailboxes and sending limits you can adjust.
- **Mailbox economics.** Whether the plan caps connected accounts, since that decides your real cost at scale.
- **Price clarity.** A published price beats "book a call," and a price that contradicts itself loses points.
- **Free plan or trial.** You should be able to test before paying.

Reply.io is missing on purpose. Its pricing page returned an error when we tried to open it, and we will not quote a third party's version of its prices. Where a vendor page showed two different prices for the same plan, we say so in the entry.

One caution about the field. Most of the pages that rank for this keyword are written by vendors that appear on their own lists. That does not make the tools bad. It does mean you should test your shortlist on your own domain.

## The 9 best cold email software tools

### 1. Instantly

**Best for:** senders who want unlimited mailboxes and warm-up without a per-seat bill.

**What the AI does:** The pricing page lists an AI Sales Agent and an AI Reply Agent on its bundles. The credit plans add a Web Researcher Agent, an AI Email Writer and a bring-your-own LLM key. The outreach-only plans are sending infrastructure with little AI attached.

**Limits:** The page contradicts itself. Hypergrowth shows $358 a month on one card and $97 on the comparison table. It shows 125,000 emails a month in one place and 100,000 in another. Trial terms are not stated either, beyond a "Start free" button and a no-card line near the signup form.

**Pricing:** Outreach Growth is $47 a month, or $37.60 on yearly billing, for 5,000 emails and 1,000 contacts, with unlimited email accounts and unlimited warm-up. Lightspeed is $358 a month for 500,000 emails. All-in-one bundles start at $94 a month.

**Verdict:** The default for anyone who plans to send from several mailboxes. Resolve the Hypergrowth price with sales before you commit to it.

### 2. Smartlead

**Best for:** agencies and anyone running outreach for many clients.

**What the AI does:** The pricing page lists no AI features. What it sells is infrastructure. All plans include unlimited email accounts, and the FAQ says Smartlead rotates sending across connected mailboxes within a campaign automatically.

**Limits:** Warm-up is shown as "FREE" beside the Unlimited Smart and Unlimited Prime plans, but the feature grid does not make clear whether Base and Pro have it. The comparison table also prints $59 against every plan, which does not match the plan cards. Placement testing through SmartDelivery is a separate subscription, starting at $49 a month for 120 tests.

**Pricing:** Base is $39 a month ($32 yearly) for 2,000 contacts and 6,000 sends. Pro is $94 ($78) for 30,000 contacts and 90,000 sends. Unlimited Smart is $174 ($144) for 150,000 sends, and Unlimited Prime is $379 ($315) for 500,000. Every plan has a free trial with no card, and the page does not give its length.

**Verdict:** Pick it over Instantly when you manage mailboxes for other people. Ask which plan includes warm-up before you pay.

### 3. lemlist

**Best for:** teams that want email, LinkedIn, SMS and calls in one sequence.

**What the AI does:** lemlist lists lemAgent, signal, enrichment and messaging agents, AI reply intent detection and AI out-of-office detection. Warm-up through lemwarm is part of the Email plan.

**Limits:** The page gives a 14-day trial in its FAQ and a 30-day trial in a promotional banner. Monthly prices are not shown outright, so $69 for Email and $109 for Multichannel are inferred from the crossed-out figures. The FAQ says pricing is per user, yet the Email plan is flat with unlimited users.

**Pricing:** Email is $55 a month on yearly billing, with unlimited users and senders and 50,000 emails a month. Multichannel is $87 a month yearly per user, with unlimited emails and messages. Lead enrichment is a $20 a month add-on for 2,000 credits.

**Verdict:** The right choice when your outreach needs more than email. Overkill if you only send plain sequences.

### 4. Woodpecker

**Best for:** senders who want deliverability tooling in the box.

**What the AI does:** Woodpecker lists AI interest-level detection, reply sorting by interest and an AI email writer. The deliverability list is its real selling point: warm-up, inbox rotation, adaptive sending, a deliverability monitor, a spam words and links checker and a domain audit that checks SPF and DKIM.

**Limits:** Prices did not load when we checked. Every plan price field was blank and several add-ons showed a placeholder zero, so we cannot print a figure. The page also says three warm-ups are included while headlining free warm-up, which leaves the cap unclear.

**Pricing:** Plans are sold by emails sent per month, in tiers from 5,000 to 1,000,000. Annual billing is advertised at 33% off. The trial runs 14 days or 500 cold emails, whichever comes first, with no card. See the vendor site for prices.

**Verdict:** A strong fit if you want one tool to catch DNS mistakes before they cost you. Confirm the price on the live page.

### 5. Saleshandy

**Best for:** teams that want high send limits at a modest price.

**What the AI does:** The page lists an AI Sequence Copilot, AI-generated sequences, AI Variants and natural language search. It does not say which plans include which, only the AI credits: 100 on Starter, 500 on Pro and 1,000 on Scale.

**Limits:** The page says "unlimited" email accounts, but the sender rotation row lists 10, 100 and 1,000 accounts for the three plans. It also says "no metered credits" while every plan carries lead finder, AI and verification credits.

**Pricing:** Starter is $34 a month on yearly billing ($41 monthly) for 6,000 emails, 2,000 active prospects and one user. Pro is $76 ($99) for 100,000 emails, 30,000 prospects and 10 users. Scale is $149 ($189) for 200,000 emails. Every paid plan has a 7-day free trial.

**Verdict:** Good value if you read the rotation limits first. The numbers behind "unlimited" decide whether it works for you.

### 6. Apollo

**Best for:** people who need contact data and sending from one login.

**What the AI does:** Apollo's pricing page places AI features on the Professional plan. Sequencing is part of the product, and credits are pooled across the team.

**Limits:** The free plan connects Gmail accounts only, and paid plans open up Microsoft and other providers. Unused credits do not roll over. The page shows no mailbox limits and no warm-up feature, so Apollo is a weaker choice if inbox rotation is the point.

**Pricing:** A free plan that stays free with no time limit. Paid tiers are Basic, Professional and Custom. The page showed no dollar amounts, so check the live page.

**Verdict:** Start here if you are still proving that outbound works for your offer, then add a dedicated sender when volume grows.

### 7. Hunter

**Best for:** small senders who already use Hunter for finding emails.

**What the AI does:** Hunter's pricing page does not itemize AI features for its Sequences product. It is a simple sender attached to a well-known email finder.

**Limits:** The page lists recipients per sequence, not per month, and does not show warm-up or daily sending limits. Extra email accounts cost from $10 a month each on paid plans.

**Pricing:** Free gives 1 email account and 500 recipients per sequence. Starter is $34 a month on yearly billing ($49 monthly) for 3 accounts and 2,500 recipients per sequence. Growth is $104 ($149) for 10 accounts, and Scale is $209 ($299) for 20 accounts and 15,000 recipients.

**Verdict:** Fine for low volume and clean, researched lists. Not the tool for scale.

### 8. Mailshake

**Best for:** small sales teams that want an easy interface and a phone dialer.

**What the AI does:** Mailshake lists SHAKESpeare for AI email writing. Warm-up is listed as unlimited on every plan.

**Limits:** There is no free trial. The FAQ says payment is collected up front. The Starter plan allows 1,500 sends a month and one email address. Yearly prices show as two numbers on the page, and we inferred the second as the discounted rate.

**Pricing:** Starter is $29 a month. Email Outreach is $49, with unlimited sends and 2 addresses. Sales Engagement is $99, with 10 addresses and a power dialer. Yearly billing appears to bring these to $25, $45 and $85. Ready-to-use mailboxes are a $12 a month add-on.

**Verdict:** Decent for a small team that wants calls and email together. Paying before you can test is a real downside.

### 9. GMass

**Best for:** people who want mass email and follow-ups inside Gmail.

**What the AI does:** The pricing page lists no AI features. GMass is a mail merge tool that works inside Gmail, which is also where its limits come from.

**Limits:** "Unlimited" sending carries an asterisk: breaking Gmail's limits depends on your account configuration. Sequences, API access and A/B testing start on Premium, and MultiSend inbox rotation appears only on Professional. No trial length is stated.

**Pricing:** Standard is $29.95 a month, or $20 a month billed yearly ($249). Premium is $39.95 ($29, or $349 a year). Professional is $59.95 ($49, or $599 a year). Team plans are priced per user.

**Verdict:** Handy for a one-person operation that lives in Gmail. Those who need rotation have to pay for the top tier.

## Mistakes to avoid when buying

**Buying volume you cannot use.** A plan with 500,000 emails a month is useless if your list is 800 good prospects. Hunter's own data points the other way: small campaigns got the better replies.

**Skipping authentication.** Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC before the first send. Our guide to [email deliverability](/blog/email-deliverability/) covers the setup in plain terms.

**Sending from your main domain.** One bad campaign should not touch your company mail.

**Automating a message that does not work.** Software scales whatever you give it. Test your copy by hand first. Our [cold email templates](/blog/cold-email-templates/) are a reasonable starting point.

**Ignoring the unsubscribe.** Yahoo says to honor opt-outs within 2 days. A prospect who cannot leave will report you as spam instead, and Google and Yahoo both watch that rate.

**Forgetting the full bill.** The tool is the cheap part. Domains, mailboxes and data add up. Smartlead's own page prices mailboxes separately.

## FAQs

**What is the best cold email software overall?**

For most senders, Instantly. Its outreach plans list unlimited email accounts and unlimited warm-up from $47 a month. Smartlead fits agencies running many mailboxes, lemlist fits teams that want LinkedIn and calls alongside email, and Apollo is the pick if you also need contact data and want a free plan with no time limit.

**Is cold emailing legal?**

The software does not change the law. Rules such as CAN-SPAM in the US and GDPR in the EU apply to the message and to how you got the contact, whoever or whatever wrote it. Check the rules for each country you send to, identify yourself in the email, and give every recipient a working way to opt out.

**Do I need email warm-up?**

Mailbox providers watch how a new sender behaves, so ramping volume up slowly is the standard advice. Instantly, lemlist, Woodpecker, Saleshandy and Mailshake list warm-up plainly on their pricing pages. Smartlead's page is unclear for its lower plans, and Apollo, Hunter and GMass do not list it.

**How many cold emails can I send a day?**

The pricing pages sell monthly caps, from 1,500 sends on Mailshake's Starter plan to 500,000 on Instantly's and Smartlead's top plans. Per-mailbox daily limits depend on your mailbox provider and the tool's settings, not on the plan. The safer pattern is a modest volume per mailbox and more mailboxes, not more emails from one.

**Should I turn off open tracking?**

Hunter's study of 11 million cold emails found the average reply rate is lower for tracked campaigns, and said its deliverability experiments were still running. Belkins turned open tracking off for every campaign in its 2025 study. Neither proves tracking causes the drop, but plenty of senders now skip it.

**What is the difference between cold email software and a newsletter platform?**

Newsletter platforms are built for people who subscribed. Cold email tools send one-to-one sequences from mailboxes you connect, stop a sequence when someone replies, and rotate sending across several inboxes. Using a newsletter platform for cold outreach is the quickest way to get the account closed.

**How much does cold email software cost?**

Entry paid plans mostly sit between $29 and $49 a month, with Hunter and Apollo offering free plans. The tool is only part of the bill. Smartlead's page prices Google or Outlook mailboxes at $4.50 a month each plus $13 per domain a year, and Mailshake sells ready-to-use mailboxes at $12 a month.
