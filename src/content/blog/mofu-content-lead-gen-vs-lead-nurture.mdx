A "How-to Guide for X" lands a lead. Then the nurture sequence sends that same lead... a different how-to guide. Most of the time, that is the wrong move, and Semrush's content marketer survey data shows exactly why.

How-to guides are the single best format for generating a lead in the first place, at 44%. For nurturing that lead afterward, they fall to fourth place, at 33%, behind success stories, product overviews, and case studies. The job changes. The content rarely does.

## The format flip

Semrush asked content marketers what works best at two separate jobs: generating a lead, and nurturing one that already exists. The two lists share names, but the order is not close to the same.

For generating leads, the top five are how-to guides (44%), product overviews (40%), case studies (34%), landing pages (31%), and webinars (31%). For nurturing, the top five are success stories (43%), product overviews (38%), case studies (36%), how-to guides (33%), and customer reviews (28%).

Only two formats earn a top-three spot on both lists: product overview and case study. That is not a coincidence. Both answer a factual question, what does this do and does it work, that a total stranger and an almost-ready buyer are both still asking. Everything else on the list is job-specific.

How-to guides pull strangers in by solving a narrow problem for free, no purchase required. Success stories close an already-warm prospect by proving the thing worked for someone who looks like them. Those are different jobs. Use the wrong format for the job and you are explaining your product to someone who already evaluated it, or trying to earn trust from a stranger with a story about someone else's results they have no context for yet.

## The channel flip is sharper than the format flip

If the content-type shift is a reshuffle, the channel shift is closer to a reversal.

**Generating leads:** organic search (69%), email marketing (54%), social media (52%), paid advertising (46%).

**Nurturing leads:** email marketing (72%), social media (44%), organic search (28%), paid advertising (23%).

Organic search goes from the single best lead-gen channel to a distant third for nurturing, a 41-point drop. Email does the exact opposite, rising from the second channel to the first by a wide margin.

The logic is mechanical once you see it. Organic search is how a stranger finds you; it cannot nurture someone you have not captured contact details for yet. Email is how you reach someone directly once they have handed you that access, so it does not need to compete for attention the way search and social do. A channel optimized for discovery and a channel optimized for direct reach are not interchangeable, no matter how good the content running through either one is.

## What marketers actually measure at this stage

Conversion rate dominates: 75% of surveyed content marketers use it to judge MOFU content, nearly 20 points ahead of the next metric, number of leads (54%). Number of visitors (44%) and time on page (32%) follow.

Notice what is missing near the top. Backlinks (10%), social shares (20%), and comments (15%), the metrics that dominate top-of-funnel and brand-awareness reporting, barely register here. That tracks. A MOFU piece is not trying to earn an audience. It is trying to move a prospect Google or email already delivered one step closer to a decision, and conversion rate is the only number that actually answers whether that happened.

## What this means for your content calendar

Stop treating "MOFU content" as one bucket with one brief template. It is two jobs wearing one label.

The lead-gen brief should optimize for strangers: broad enough to rank and get shared, specific enough to be genuinely useful on its own, built around the problem rather than the product. The nurture brief should optimize for someone who already knows roughly what you do: proof, specificity, a named customer, a number they can check.

This split matters for AI search too, not just classic funnel thinking. AI engines answering a broad "how do I solve X" query are pulling from the same pool of content competing for organic lead-gen visibility, while an AI assistant helping someone already evaluating your category is closer to reading the nurture-stage proof content, if it exists in a form that is citable at all. The [full-funnel AI SEO playbook](/blog/full-funnel-ai-seo/) covers how awareness, consideration, and decision-stage queries each need different content built for AI citation, which is the same split showing up again from a different angle.

Two different jobs. Two different content types. Two different channels. Stop running one brief through both.
