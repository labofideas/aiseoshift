The best AI sales agents in 2027, on the evidence a buyer can check, are Bland AI, Amplemarket and Attio, followed by lemlist, Vapi, Cognism, Orum, Conversica, Backstory and Nooks. We scored controls, compliance statements, metric definitions, price clarity and scope. This is an editorial comparison, not legal advice.

## Key takeaways

- Adoption is ahead of proof. Salesforce's survey found 54 percent of sellers have used AI agents, while Gartner predicts fewer than 40 percent of sellers will say agents improved their productivity by 2028.
- Compliance is the quiet gap. Of the ten vendors, none documents CAN-SPAM or US calling-consent controls on the pages we read. Several list SOC 2 and GDPR, which is a different question.
- Metrics are mostly undefined. Almost every vendor quotes "3x more meetings" or "2x more replies" with no baseline, sample or period.
- Half publish no price. The five that do are the easiest to evaluate.
- Buyers now research vendors inside AI answers. G2 found 69 percent chose a different vendor than planned after a chatbot recommendation, which makes your own pages part of the sales funnel.

## What changed in 2026

Two years ago the question was whether an AI could write a decent cold email. In 2026 the question is whether you can let it send one.

The adoption numbers are high. Salesforce's [State of Sales report](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-sales-report-announcement-2026/), a survey of 4,050 sales professionals in 22 countries fielded in August and September 2025, says 87 percent of sales organizations use some form of AI and 54 percent of sellers have used AI agents, with nearly nine in ten planning to by 2027. The same report says high performers are 1.7 times more likely than underperformers to use prospecting agents. Salesforce sells agents, so read that as a vendor's survey.

The skeptical side arrived in July. Gartner's [July 28 release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-28-gartner-predicts-ai-agents-will-outnumber-sellers-10-to-1-by-2028-yet-fewer-than-40-percent-of-sellers-will-say-agents-improved-productivity) predicts that AI agents will outnumber sellers 10 to 1 by 2028 and that fewer than 40 percent of sellers will say the agents improved productivity. It draws on a survey of 210 chief sales officers and senior sales executives, fielded January to February 2026.

Then the market moved under the buyers. The Clari homepage now redirects to Salesloft, where Clari Forecast is sold alongside Salesloft's own products. People.ai now answers to a new name, Backstory, and its FAQ says so. Vendors are also exposing their data to other assistants, and five of the ten products we ranked advertise an MCP server or MCP access.

That is the context for the ranking.

## What counts as an AI sales agent

Three kinds of product get the label.

**Assistants.** They answer questions about your pipeline, summarize a call or draft a note. A rep reads the output and decides. Attio's Ask Attio and Backstory's assistant are in this group.

**Outbound agents.** They research an account, write a message and, depending on settings, send it. Amplemarket's Duo and lemlist's lemAgent sit here. The control question is whether a human approves before send.

**Conversational and voice agents.** They hold a live exchange by email, text or phone. Conversica, Bland and Vapi are in this group, and so is the agent side of Orum. The legal question is consent, because a message or call has already gone out by the time anyone reviews it.

Gartner's [June 2025 warning](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027) about agent washing applies: vendors relabel chatbots as agentic, only about 130 of thousands are real, and over 40 percent of agentic projects will be canceled by the end of 2027. For a general definition of agents against chatbots and workflow automation, see our guide to [AI agents for business](/blog/ai-agents-for-business/).

## The market in numbers

Every figure was read on its source page in November 2026.

**Adoption.** Salesforce: 55 percent of sellers use AI for prospecting, and the average seller spends 40 percent of their time selling. Fifty-one percent of sales leaders using AI say disconnected systems are slowing them down.

**The reality check.** Gartner: 60 percent of sales chiefs say their revenue number is largely driven by factors outside their control. Not every pipeline problem is a tooling problem.

**Project failure.** Gartner's agentic cancellation forecast comes from a January 2025 poll of 3,412 webinar attendees across all industries. It is a prediction, not an outcome.

**Buyers.** Gartner's May 2026 release, based on 645 B2B buyers surveyed in August and September 2025, says 69 percent prefer to validate AI-generated insights with sales reps, 67 percent prefer a rep-free experience, and 45 percent used generative AI, mainly to gather information on vendors and products. Buyers want speed from AI and a human to check it.

**AI answers as a channel.** G2's [Answer Economy report](https://company.g2.com/news/g2-research-the-answer-economy) surveyed 1,076 software buyers in March 2026. It found 51 percent start research with an AI chatbot more often than with Google, up from 29 percent in 2025, and 69 percent chose a different vendor than planned because of a chatbot recommendation. G2 sells software review services, so the sample skews toward software buyers.

**Forecast.** Gartner's [October 2025 prediction](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-21-gartner-unveils-top-predictions-for-it-organizations-and-users-in-2026-and-beyond) says that by 2028, 90 percent of B2B buying will be AI-agent intermediated, pushing over $15 trillion of B2B spend through agent exchanges. It is a forecast, and a bold one.

## How we ranked them

This ranking uses vendor pages opened in November 2026. We did not test any product, and no vendor gave us anything. It scores what a buyer can verify.

Each product scores 1 to 5 on five criteria, for a maximum of 25.

| Criterion | What earns a 5 | What earns a 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Controls | Documented human review before send, undo or permission limits | Silent on whether anyone approves |
| Compliance statements | Security certifications plus named outreach, consent or opt-out controls | Trust Center link only |
| Metric definitions | Headline numbers defined with baseline and period | Percentages with no definition |
| Price clarity | Published plan prices and what each includes | Quote only |
| Scope clarity | A clear statement of what the agent does and does not do | Many products under one label |

Ties break on controls, then compliance. A high score does not mean a product books more meetings. It means more of it can be checked.

Three disclosures. First, we sell AI search visibility services, so we benefit when sales teams care about being found by AI. We have no commercial relationship with any vendor here. Second, Anthropic's Claude helped draft this article and does not appear on the list. Third, other posts in this series on other sites cover HubSpot Breeze, Agentforce Sales, Apollo, Outreach, Salesloft, Clay, 11x, Regie.ai, Qualified, Gong, Warmly and Artisan. We left them out on purpose, so this is not the full market.

Two candidates were dropped. Reply.io blocked every automated fetch and third-party price roundups disagreed, so we gave it no score. Clari now lives on Salesloft's site.

## The 10 AI sales agents, ranked

### 1. Bland AI

**Score: 17 of 25** (controls 3, compliance 3, definitions 2, price 5, scope 4).

[Bland](https://www.bland.ai/) sells an enterprise voice platform for phone agents handling inbound and outbound calls, with SMS and web chat on top. Its use cases include appointment scheduling, qualification, payments and collections, and IVR replacement. Sales is one use among several. It says a forward-deployed engineer team builds the first agent with you, typically within two to six weeks.

Who it suits: large teams that want outbound or inbound phone agents and will pay for implementation help.

Pricing as published on [Bland](https://www.bland.ai/pricing) in November 2026: Start has no platform fee and $0.14 per minute of talk time, Build is $299 a month at $0.12 per minute, and Enterprise is custom. Transfers cost $0.05 or $0.04 per minute on the two lower plans. Start caps you at 10 concurrent calls and 100 calls a day, and Build allows 50 concurrent and 2,000 a day. Telephony is billed separately. The page says the rate covers the language model, speech-to-text and text-to-speech.

Controls: the homepage says complex edge cases route to human agents with full context, and observability shows why the agent made each decision. The pricing table puts warm and live transfers, guardrails and citations on Enterprise only, which sits awkwardly against the homepage promise. Ask what a Build customer gets.

Compliance: the page lists SOC 2 Type I and II, HIPAA, GDPR, ISO 27001 and a self-attested PCI DSS. It says nothing about calling consent, call-recording disclosure or telling the person they are talking to an AI.

Metrics are mixed. The page says 230-plus regulated companies use Bland and cites a customer cutting costs 92 percent against human calls. A $430 revenue figure appears with an unclear unit. Its own voice-realism benchmark scores Bland 0.91 against 0.99 for human speech, second overall.

Verdict: top of the list because you can price it and read its limits. Consent for outbound calling is your responsibility.

### 2. Amplemarket

**Score: 16 of 25** (controls 4, compliance 3, definitions 2, price 3, scope 4).

[Amplemarket](https://www.amplemarket.com/) combines a B2B contact database, multichannel sequences, a parallel dialer and an AI layer called Duo. Duo pulls CRM context, buying signals and public research to find reasons to reach out. Modules include Copilot, Accounts, Copywriter, Inbox for drafted replies and Voice for voice-message outreach.

Who it suits: outbound-led B2B teams that want data, sequencing and drafting in one product.

Pricing as published on [Amplemarket](https://www.amplemarket.com/pricing) in November 2026: Startup is $600 a month on an annual term, with 2 users and 27,000 contacts. Growth and Elite are custom, with 4 and 10 users. Startup includes 13,500 email credits and 600 phone credits per user per year. Duo Voice appears on Growth and above, and Duo Inbox is marked both an add-on and included on Elite, so confirm it.

Controls are the clearest of any outbound vendor here. The page says agents prepare outreach, and reps review and personalize it before starting conversations, and that Duo Inbox drafts replies for rep review.

Compliance: SOC 2 Type II, annual penetration tests, a public Trust Center, and a description as GDPR and CCPA aligned. Nothing on opt-out handling or CAN-SPAM.

Metrics are undefined: 200M-plus contacts, under 3 percent bounce, 3.2 times average reply rate, 3 times more meaningful conversations. The "meaningful" in that last one is the part to ask about.

Verdict: the best documented review-before-send model among outbound tools. Get the definitions behind 3.2 times in writing.

### 3. Attio

**Score: 15 of 25** (controls 3, compliance 1, definitions 3, price 5, scope 3).

[Attio](https://attio.com/ai) is a CRM with AI inside it. Ask Attio answers questions and updates records in plain language, Workflows builds automations from a described goal, Call Intelligence records and transcribes calls and flags buying signals, and MCP lets Claude or ChatGPT read and write CRM data.

Pricing as published on [Attio](https://attio.com/pricing) in November 2026: Free at $0 for up to 3 seats, Plus at $44 monthly or $35 on annual billing per user, Pro at $99 or $79, and Enterprise custom. AI runs on credits: Plus gets 500 per seat and 1,500 per workspace each month, Pro gets 1,000 and 10,000. Plus caps email sending at 1,000 a month, and Pro removes the cap.

Controls: OAuth with user-scoped permissions, and follow-ups are described as drafts. The page does not say whether approval is required before agent actions run, and it says agent tools have full read and write access within the user's permissions.

Metrics: it makes almost none, which is an honest way to score a 3. There are no time-saved numbers to define, and one testimonial says nothing quantified.

Real limits. The pages do not map AI features to plans or address call-recording consent. Attio is a system of record, not an outbound sender.

Verdict: the cleanest pricing in the field and no inflated claims. Pair it with a sender and keep humans on writes.

### 4. lemlist

**Score: 15 of 25** (controls 2, compliance 3, definitions 2, price 5, scope 3).

[lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com/) is a sales engagement platform covering lead data, multichannel sequences, deliverability tooling and a set of AI features. It lists lemAgent, intent signal agents, data enrichment agents, smart messaging, and an MCP server and CLI. The page lists lemAgent as new and gives no description beyond the name.

Who it suits: small outbound teams that care about deliverability and want published pricing.

Pricing as published on [lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com/pricing) in November 2026, on the yearly toggle: Email is $55 a month with unlimited users and 50,000 emails a month, Multichannel is $87 per user with unlimited emails and messages, and Enterprise is custom. Lead enrichment is a $16 add-on with 2,000 credits, and buying intent signals are $21 for 100 signals. Warm-up, called lemwarm, and deliverability protections come on every plan. The pricing page does not clearly map each AI feature to a tier.

Controls: the page says agents "suggest" sequences, copy and prompts, and does not say whether a user must approve before send. That is the central question for an outbound agent.

Compliance: it lists SOC 2, a data processing agreement, a sending policy and an anti-abuse page, with no explicit GDPR statement. The deliverability set is real: DNS tests, rotating inboxes and sending-limit management. For the mailbox rules that decide whether any of it lands, see our [email deliverability guide](/blog/email-deliverability/).

Metrics: the lead database is 650 million in the navigation and 600 million in the body of the same page.

Verdict: strong on price and deliverability, thin on agent approval. Ask who clicks send.

### 5. Vapi

**Score: 15 of 25** (controls 2, compliance 2, definitions 2, price 5, scope 4).

[Vapi](https://vapi.ai/) is a developer platform for building voice agents, with telephony, integrations and monitoring. Its listed use cases include lead qualification and appointment scheduling. You assemble the agent yourself, which means the compliance posture is largely yours.

Who it suits: engineering-led teams that want to build their own voice flow.

Pricing as published on [Vapi](https://vapi.ai/pricing) in November 2026: the No Success Package starts at $0 with 4 concurrent calls and $0.05 per minute of Vapi hosting, Core is $29 a month with 10 concurrent calls, Pro is 10 percent of the hosting fee with a $999 monthly minimum, and Premier is custom. Transcriber, model and voice costs are passed through, and telephony is billed by the carrier. The page's sample estimate for 1,000 minutes is $82 to $129 a month. HIPAA is a $2,000 monthly add-on.

Controls: guardrails are listed as an enterprise add-on, described as preventing hallucinations. Nothing on human handoff is on the pages we read.

Compliance: the homepage says Vapi is "SOC 2, HIPAA, and PCI compliant", while the pricing page does not mention SOC 2. Nothing on outbound-call consent.

Metrics: 1 billion calls, 2.5 million agents launched, 1 million developers and under 500 milliseconds of latency.

Verdict: the most transparent cost model for builders, and the least opinionated about sales. You own the script, the consent and the failure modes.

### 6. Cognism

**Score: 12 of 25** (controls 3, compliance 3, definitions 2, price 2, scope 2).

[Cognism](https://www.cognism.com/) is sales intelligence: contact and account data, signals such as intent and job changes, and CRM enrichment. It is not an agent. We include it because agents are only as good as the data they run on, and Nooks' FAQ says a Cognism, ZoomInfo or LeadIQ subscription is required for data inside Nooks. The new MCP and API access lets an assistant query Cognism directly.

Who it suits: teams selling into Europe who need phone-verified data.

Pricing as published on [Cognism](https://www.cognism.com/pricing) in November 2026: no dollar figures. Standard and Pro each include 5 seats, one credit is used per revealed contact, and CRM Enrichment is sold as an add-on. Intent data comes from Bombora.

Compliance: the best outreach-specific claim in the whole field, though a short one. The page says "extensive Do-Not-Call coverage across Europe", names GDPR and CCPA, and says its teams maintain parity with ISO 27001 and SOC 2 frameworks. Parity is not certification.

Metrics: "4,000-plus revenue teams," a 20 to 40 percent average pipeline increase, and phone-verified mobiles with up to 3 times higher connect rates, defined only as data checked by additional manual and automated verification.

Verdict: the vendor most likely to help you stay on the right side of a do-not-call list. It does not send anything.

### 7. Orum

**Score: 12 of 25** (controls 2, compliance 3, definitions 2, price 1, scope 4).

[Orum](https://orum.com/) sells an AI dialer and three named agents: Operator runs parallel dialing and handles screeners and phone trees so reps connect only when a person answers, Scout flags dead numbers and finds ones likely to be answered, and Coach scores calls and runs practice sessions.

Who it suits: SDR and BDR teams that live on the phone.

Pricing: Launch and Ascend plans with Webhooks and AI Coaching add-ons, per user, billed annually. No dollar amounts. The pricing page returned a 404 when we tried it.

Controls: the human stays on the call, because the dialer connects the rep when a person picks up. That is a different control model from a voice agent that talks for you, and an easier one to defend.

Compliance: the page lists SOC 2 Type II, ISO certification and GDPR readiness, and says it reduces spam risk through number rotation, country matching and reputation monitoring. It does not mention TCPA or consent.

Metrics: "1 billion real sales calls" behind the agents, 99-plus percent telephony uptime, and customer results of 2 times more meetings with fewer reps and 20 percent more meetings booked. No baselines.

Verdict: the safest design for phone outreach on this list, because a person speaks.

## How AI sales agents change who gets found and cited

This is the part most sales guides skip, and it matters more than the ranking.

Sales agents are readers of the open web. Amplemarket says Duo uses CRM context, buying signals and public research to decide whom to reach. Attio's pages list a web agent that researches the open web and enriches records. When a prospect's agent or a seller's agent forms an opinion about your company, the inputs are your site, your docs and what others say about you.

The buyer side is documented. G2's March 2026 survey found 51 percent of software buyers start in an AI chatbot, 85 percent think more highly of a vendor when AI includes it in an answer, and 69 percent chose a different vendor than planned. Gartner found 45 percent of B2B buyers used generative AI in a recent purchase, mainly for vendor and product information, and 69 percent still validate with a rep.

Our inference, labeled as such: the pricing page, the integrations list and the comparison pages you publish are now input to both your buyers' shortlists and your competitors' agents. If you hide pricing, an assistant has nothing to quote. In this ranking, five of ten vendors do exactly that.

Practical steps:

1. Publish plain-text pricing, limits and integrations, as Attio and Bland do.
2. Mark up your organization and products accurately. Our guide to [schema markup for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/) covers the method.
3. Check what assistants say about you, per [what is AI visibility](/blog/what-is-ai-visibility/).
4. Read the wider shift in [Google's search agents](/blog/google-search-agents/) and in [agentic commerce optimization](/blog/what-is-agentic-commerce-optimization/).

## Implementation, governance and risk

None of this is legal advice. Have counsel review any outreach program before it runs at scale.

**Email.** The FTC's [CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) lists eight requirements, including accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, a valid physical postal address and a clear opt-out honored within 10 business days. Each violating email can carry a penalty of up to $53,088. The guide says the law makes no exception for business-to-business email, and that hiring another company to send does not transfer your responsibility. That last line matters for agents: the vendor sends, you are still on the hook.

**Mailbox rules.** Google's [sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) apply to senders above 5,000 messages a day to Gmail and ask for a spam rate at or below 0.10 percent, never reaching 0.30 percent, along with SPF, DKIM, DMARC and one-click unsubscribe. An agent that sends faster than a human can burn a domain faster than a human can.

**Phone.** We read the text of [47 U.S.C. 227](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/227), the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. It makes calls using an automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice unlawful when made to cellular numbers and other listed lines without the called party's prior express consent, subject to listed exceptions, and the same for artificial or prerecorded voice calls to residential lines. The FCC has addressed AI-generated voices in a 2024 ruling. Its pages blocked our automated fetch, so we have not read it and do not summarize it. Read it, or have counsel read it, before you point a voice agent at a list. Our read of the vendors: none of Bland, Vapi or Orum documents consent controls on the pages we opened.

**Europe and the UK.** GDPR Recital 47, which we read on [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679), says processing personal data for direct marketing "may be regarded as carried out for a legitimate interest." Recital 70 says a person can object to direct marketing at any time and free of charge. Legitimate interest is a basis you have to justify, not a pass. The UK's electronic marketing rules sit in a separate regulation, PECR. The ICO's guidance blocked our fetch this session, so we say nothing about PECR beyond telling you to read it.

**Run a control group.** Give half your accounts to the agent and half to humans for a quarter. Compare meetings held, not meetings booked, and compare complaints and unsubscribes. Vendor percentages do not substitute.

## 2027 outlook

Expect three things.

Procurement will start asking for defined metrics and consent controls in writing, because the gap between "3x meetings" and nothing is too wide to ignore.

Agents will talk to agents. The MCP servers on half this list are the plumbing for Gartner's forecast of agent-intermediated buying, and your outbound will increasingly be read by software.

And the 40 percent cancellation forecast will probably land on outbound projects with no control group.

The sharp line: an agent that sends more messages is not a better salesperson.

### What are the best AI sales agents in 2027?

On our evidence-first rubric, Bland AI scores highest at 17 of 25, followed by Amplemarket, Attio, lemlist, Vapi, Cognism, Orum, Conversica, Backstory and Nooks. The ranking measures what a buyer can verify from vendor pages, not which product books the most meetings.

### Do AI sales agents replace SDRs?

Gartner predicts AI agents will outnumber sellers 10 to 1 by 2028 and that fewer than 40 percent of sellers will say agents improved their productivity. Its survey of 210 sales chiefs also found 60 percent say their revenue number is largely driven by factors outside their control.

### Is AI-driven sales outreach legal?

Often yes, with conditions. The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide says the law makes no exception for business-to-business email, and penalties reach $53,088 per violating email. In the US, 47 U.S.C. 227 makes calls using an artificial or prerecorded voice unlawful without prior express consent, subject to listed exceptions. This is not legal advice.

### Can an AI agent cold call people?

The statute we read bars calls to cell phones using an artificial or prerecorded voice without prior express consent. We could not open the FCC's 2024 ruling on AI voices, so we do not summarize it. Ask your counsel, and ask the vendor what consent and disclosure controls it provides.

### How does AI search change who buys from you?

G2's March 2026 survey of 1,076 software buyers found 51 percent start research in an AI chatbot more often than Google, and 69 percent chose a different vendor than planned because of a chatbot recommendation. If your product is not described clearly on pages an assistant can read, you are not in that shortlist.

### How much do AI sales agents cost?

Published prices in November 2026 range from free CRM seats at Attio to $600 a month for Amplemarket's Startup plan and $0.12 to $0.14 per minute for Bland's voice agents. Five of the ten vendors here publish no prices at all, so budget for a sales conversation.
