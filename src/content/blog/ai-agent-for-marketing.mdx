The best AI agents for marketing in 2027 are Lindy, Relevance AI, Goodie, Sprout Social, Gumloop, Metadata, Peec AI, AirOps, ActiveCampaign and Smartly, in that order on our rubric. We scored them on autonomy, price clarity, job coverage, evidence and oversight, across content, AI search visibility, social, email and ads.

**Our interest, stated plainly.** AISEOShift is an AI-search and SEO publication that sells and promotes AI-search services. We have a commercial interest in AI visibility being taken seriously. No vendor paid for placement, we use no affiliate links, and the visibility trackers here (Peec AI, Goodie, plus Scrunch and Otterly in passing) are ranked only on documentation we could open. The governance section repeats this.

## Key takeaways

- Marketing agents are mostly narrower than the label. Salesforce's State of Marketing report says only 13 percent of marketing organizations already using AI are using self-directed agents.
- Money is moving faster than readiness. Gartner's 2026 CMO Spend Survey found 15.3 percent of marketing budgets going to AI, and only 30 percent of CMOs think their organization has the infrastructure to get there.
- The law does not care that an agent did it. The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide says you cannot contract away your responsibility, and its fake-review rule covers AI-generated testimonials.
- For AI search, the agent is not the lever. Pages that Google can index and show with a snippet are the lever, and agents only change how many pages you make and how accurate they are.

## What changed in 2026

Two shifts, and one warning.

The AI-search trackers grew an action layer: Peec AI labels "Actions" and an "Agent" as new, and Goodie lists outreach, technical and content agents. The general platforms went after marketing by name too. Gumloop publishes marketing pages for ads, SEO, social and brand monitoring, and Lindy lists a media buyer role.

The warning is Gartner's. In a June 2025 forecast, covered by [w.media](https://w.media/over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-could-face-the-axe-by-end-of-2027-gartner/), it said more than 40 percent of agentic AI projects could be canceled by the end of 2027 over cost, unclear value or weak risk controls. It also said vendors rebrand chatbots and automation as agents, and estimated only about 130 of the thousands of agentic vendors are real. We could not open Gartner's own page (it returned 403), so that is the trade coverage of the release.

"AI marketing agents" covers a $25 monitoring dashboard and a platform that spends your ad budget.

## What counts as an AI marketing agent

Four things get called agents in marketing. An assistant drafts when you ask. Workflow automation runs fixed steps on a trigger. A copilot proposes actions inside a tool. An agent takes a goal, picks steps, uses connected tools and acts on a schedule, within approvals you set. Gumloop's docs say its agents "use your tools and data to do real work."

Our test is blunt. What does the product do on Tuesday at 3 a.m. while nobody is logged in? If the answer is nothing, it is an assistant. If the answer is "sends, publishes or spends", you need approvals and logs before you switch it on.

For the same question across the whole business, see [AI agents for business](/blog/ai-agents-for-business/). For the tool-level view, we keep separate lists: [50 AI tools for digital marketers](/blog/best-ai-tools-for-digital-marketers/), [AI email marketing tools](/blog/best-ai-email-marketing-tools/) and [AI social media tools](/blog/best-ai-social-media-tools/). ## The market in numbers

All figures were opened in November 2026. Where we read a trade write-up instead of the original, we say so.

**Agent use is still narrow.** Salesforce's State of Marketing report (10th edition, nearly 4,450 marketers), [as cited on Salesforce's own site](https://www.salesforce.com/au/marketing/streamlining-with-agentic-ai/), says only 13 percent of marketing organizations already using AI are using self-directed agents. Marketers expect to save up to eight hours a week with agents. The page does not give survey dates, and Salesforce sells marketing agents.

**Budgets are shifting.** Gartner's 2026 CMO Spend Survey, published May 11, 2026 and [summarized by Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/ai-remains-a-top-priority-for-cmos-but-spending-lags/819806/), surveyed 401 marketing leaders between January and March. CMOs put 15.3 percent of marketing budgets into AI, and 21.3 percent at organizations better prepared to scale it. Marketing is 7.8 percent of company revenue in 2026, up from 7.7 percent.

**Search behavior changed under all of this.** Pew Research, tracking 900 US adults and 68,879 Google searches in March 2025, [found](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) users clicked a traditional result in 8 percent of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15 percent when none did. About 1 percent of visits included a click on a link inside the summary. The data is from early 2025, and AI features have expanded since.

**Vendor numbers deserve a discount.** Metadata lists a 524 percent pipeline ROI for N-able, and ActiveCampaign claims 75 percent higher engagement. Neither is audited, and we use neither in scores. Samples, years and definitions differ across all of these figures, so do not read them as one dataset.

## What marketing agents change in AI search

This is where our interest in the subject is strongest, so we kept the claims narrow.

**Agents do not buy citations.** Google's [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says a page needs to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews and AI Mode. It says you need no special machine-readable files, AI text files or schema.org markup. A content agent cannot add anything to that eligibility list. It can only produce, refresh or fix pages that meet it.

**Volume is a risk.** Google's guidance on [generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) says using AI to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate its spam policy on scaled content abuse. The review also covers metadata such as titles and alt text. An agent that publishes fifty pages a week is a scaled content problem waiting for a trigger.

**Measurement is the second change.** Trackers (Peec AI, Scrunch, Otterly, Goodie) run prompts against models and record whether you appear. Peec AI says it runs each prompt once every 24 hours per model. That is a snapshot of a probabilistic system, not a ranking. See [what AI visibility is](/blog/what-is-ai-visibility/) and [how to measure it](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/).

**Clicks fall even when you win.** Pew's 8 versus 15 percent figure means visibility in an answer may not pay in sessions. Track branded search, direct traffic and conversions, not only mentions.

Do this in order. Make sure the pages that matter are indexed and readable as plain HTML ([how AI search retrieves content](/blog/how-ai-search-retrieves-content/)). Keep facts consistent across your site and profiles. Use agents for refresh before volume ([content flywheel](/blog/content-flywheel-ai-seo/)). Mark up what is true on the page ([schema for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/)). Then read the wider shift in [Google's search agents](/blog/google-search-agents/) and our look at [SEO AI agents](/blog/seo-ai-agents/).

## How we ranked them

We ran no hands-on tests. Everything comes from vendor documentation, vendor pricing pages and published reporting opened in November 2026. Vendors gave us nothing, and we did not contact any of them.

Each agent scores 1 to 5 on five criteria, for a maximum of 25.

| Criterion | What earns a 5 | What earns a 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | Plans and acts across tools on a schedule or trigger | An assistant with an agent label |
| Price clarity | Public prices and a clear usage rule | Demo or quote only |
| Job coverage | Handles several of the five jobs | One narrow task |
| Evidence | Checkable data, ideally not vendor-supplied | Marketing claims only |
| Oversight | Documented approvals, logs and access controls | Nothing shown |

Ties are broken by evidence, then oversight. The scores are editorial judgment. Rescore them with your own weights, and weight oversight heavily if the agent can spend money or send to customers.

One limit on the pool: it favors vendors with clear public pages, and it leaves out thirteen well-known marketing brands that other publications cover. It is not a full market map.

## Each job, and which agent fits

### Content marketing agents

AirOps fits a research-publish-refresh loop if you can pay for vendor-assisted setup. Gumloop suits people who would rather assemble the workflow. Lindy handles smaller recurring tasks. Whichever you pick, put an editor between the agent and the CMS. Our post on whether [Google penalizes AI content](/blog/will-google-penalize-ai-content/) covers the practical line.

### SEO and AI-search visibility agents

Goodie has the broadest action list at a published price. Peec AI is the measurement pick, from $95 a month. Scrunch starts at $250 a month billed annually and lists an Agent Experience Platform that generates an AI-friendly version of your site, a claim we have not tested. Otterly's Lite plan is cheaper but covers 15 prompts. Judge any agent here on whether recommendations become indexed, cited pages. Nobody publishes that evidence in a form we could check.

### Social media agents

Sprout Social is the low-risk pick, because Trellis drafts and summarizes inside a tool that already has approval workflows. The FTC's endorsement guidance matters most here: your company is responsible for what others do on its behalf, and material connections should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. If an agent drafts posts for creators or employees, you own the disclosure.

### Email marketing agents

ActiveCampaign is the example on this list, and it shows the main trap. Email is the one channel with a per-message penalty. The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide says each violating email can draw penalties of up to $53,088, requires honoring opt-outs within 10 business days, and says you cannot contract away responsibility. An agent that builds and sends a campaign needs your review of the unsubscribe path, the postal address and the sender identity. Read our notes on [email deliverability](/blog/email-deliverability/) before you let anything send on its own.

### Ads and paid media agents

Metadata is the most autonomous. Smartly is the most established. Lindy's media buyer role suits monitoring and reporting more than spending. Set daily and monthly caps outside the agent, in the ad platform, so a bad run cannot exceed them. Ask each vendor for an action log that shows what changed, when, and who approved it.

## How to choose

Solo marketers and tiny teams: Lindy for general work, Sprout's Trellis if social is the job, and keep spend under $100 a month while you measure hours saved. Teams of five to twenty: Gumloop if you have someone who can build, Goodie or AirOps for AI-search work, ActiveCampaign if email is the center. Larger organizations: Relevance AI for governed agents, Metadata or Smartly for paid media, and expect a security review. Agencies: Peec AI or Scrunch for client reporting.

Start where mistakes are cheap and reversible. Reporting and research first, content refresh second. Anything that sends, posts or spends goes last. Run a four-week pilot against a baseline, such as time to produce a weekly report and the approval rate of drafts.

## Governance and risk

**Our own position, again.** AISEOShift sells AI-search services, so we gain if brands care about AI visibility. That is a conflict, and the way we handle it is to show scores, link primary sources, say which pages we could not open, and rank trackers lower than action-taking agents where evidence is thin. No vendor paid us or saw this post before publication.

**You own the output.** The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide says both the company whose product is promoted and the company that sends the message may be held responsible. Its endorsement guidance says your company is ultimately responsible for what others do on your behalf. Its consumer review rule bans AI-generated fake reviews and testimonials that misrepresent they come from a person who does not exist, with civil penalties against knowing violators. Do not point an agent at review generation. This is not legal advice.

**Ask for the approval design.** For every agent that can send, post or spend, get answers to four questions: what needs approval, where is the log, how do you set a spending or send cap, and how do you turn it off. Lindy states an approval rule and Relevance AI lists approvals and audit logs. Several state nothing.

**Watch the data you feed in.** Agents connected to your CRM, ad accounts and analytics create a new access surface. Lindy and Relevance AI state SOC 2 Type II, and Goodie lists SOC 2 for Enterprise. Peec AI, AirOps and Gumloop stated none on the pages we opened.

**Budget for review time.** Gartner's cancellation forecast names cost and unclear value. If drafting gets faster and review gets longer, you saved nothing.

**Agent washing is real.** Ask the vendor to show the product acting on a trigger with nobody in the chat.

## 2027 outlook

Pricing will settle on credits. Lindy, Gumloop, Peec AI and Sprout already meter by credit or task, so bills will track how much the agent does.

Trackers will keep moving into action. The proof that those actions change citations is still missing, so keep your own before-and-after record.

Rules are tightening around synthetic content. The FTC's review rule already covers AI-generated testimonials, and Google's spam policy is explicit about scaled generation. An agent that cannot show approvals and logs will be hard to defend.

### What is an AI marketing agent?

It is software that takes a marketing goal, plans the steps, uses connected tools such as your ad account or email platform, and acts, with or without approval. A chatbot answers, a workflow tool follows fixed rules, and a copilot suggests. Gartner says many vendors relabel assistants and automation as agents, so check what the product does without you.

### Are AI marketing agents worth it for a small business?

Sometimes. Lindy publishes a plan from $29.99 per month and Gumloop's Pro tier was reported at $37 per month in September 2026, so the entry cost is low. The cost that matters is review time. Start with one job, such as reporting or content refresh, and measure hours saved before you add more.

### Can AI agents send marketing emails and post on my behalf legally?

Yes, but you stay responsible. The FTC's CAN-SPAM guide says you cannot contract away your legal responsibility, and penalties reach $53,088 per violating email. Its endorsement guidance says your company is responsible for what others do on its behalf. This is not legal advice.

### Do AI marketing agents change what gets cited in AI answers?

Indirectly. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special markup, only indexed, snippet-eligible pages. Agents change the supply: more pages published faster. Google's spam policy warns that generating many pages without adding value may be scaled content abuse, so volume alone does not earn citations.

### How much do AI agents for marketing cost?

As published in November 2026: Lindy Team from $29.99 per month, Peec AI from $95, Goodie from $295, Scrunch from $250 billed annually, and Sprout Social's Trellis Plus add-on at $28 per seat annually. Relevance AI, Metadata and Smartly publish no prices on the pages we opened.

### Should I use a visibility tracker or a full marketing agent?

Use a tracker if your question is where you appear in AI answers. Use an agent platform if your question is who does the work. Peec AI, Scrunch and Otterly measure. Goodie, AirOps and the general platforms also draft, fix and publish, which is where review rules matter most.
