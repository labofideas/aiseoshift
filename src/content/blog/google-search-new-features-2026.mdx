Google Search used to do one thing. You typed a query. It returned a list of links. You clicked one.

That version of Google is gone.

The version that exists in May 2026 builds custom dashboards from a sentence. It runs AI agents that monitor apartment listings while you sleep. It books restaurant reservations without you visiting a single website. It generates interactive budget trackers, flight comparison tools, and wedding planning interfaces on the fly, from a plain English description.

Google Search is no longer a search engine. It is an AI-powered operating system for information. And if you run a website, that changes everything about how your content gets discovered, consumed, and valued.

## What Google Search can actually do now

Let me walk through the specific capabilities that shipped at I/O 2026, because the gap between what most people think Google Search does and what it actually does right now is enormous.

### AI Mode: the conversational search layer

AI Mode launched a year ago and has crossed one billion monthly users. Queries more than double every quarter. Powered by Gemini 3.5 Flash, it turns Google from a query-response tool into a conversational research assistant.

You do not type keywords anymore. You describe what you need. "I am planning a two-week trip to Japan in October with a family of four, budget around $8,000, and my kids are 6 and 9." Google does not return a list of travel websites. It generates a structured itinerary, estimates costs, suggests family-friendly activities by region, and offers to refine the plan based on follow-up questions.

The implications for travel websites, hotel booking platforms, and travel content creators are obvious and uncomfortable. Google just became the travel agent. Your website is the brochure it reads to do the job.

### Information agents: Google that works while you do not

This is the feature that feels most like science fiction. You can now create AI agents inside Google Search that monitor the web continuously on your behalf.

Apartment hunting? Tell Google your requirements: two bedrooms, under $2,500, dog-friendly, in Brooklyn, near a subway. The agent scans listings around the clock and notifies you when something matches. You never search again. The agent searches for you.

Job hunting? Same thing. Price tracking? Same thing. Monitoring competitors? Same thing.

Information agents launch first for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers this summer, but the pattern they establish will reshape how discovery works. If a user's agent is scanning for "best CRM for small businesses" and your content matches, you get surfaced. If your content does not match the agent's criteria, you are invisible, and the user never even performed a search you could have ranked for.

### Generative UI: apps built from sentences

This one is hard to overstate. Google Search now generates interactive user interfaces from natural language descriptions. You say "build me a budget tracker for a home renovation project with categories for kitchen, bathroom, and flooring." Google builds it. Right there, in the search interface. No app download. No website visit. No subscription.

You say "compare flights from Austin to Tokyo in October for a family of four." Google does not show you Kayak or Google Flights as a link. It generates a custom comparison interface with real-time pricing, filters, and sorting, all within the search page.

The technology behind this is called generative UI. Google's AI reads your request, determines what kind of interface would best serve it, and assembles it from components. The result is a mini-app tailored to your exact need, generated in seconds.

For businesses that built products around aggregation, comparison, or simple utility, this is a direct competitive threat from Google itself.

### Multimodal search input

Google Search now accepts text, images, files, videos, and even Chrome tabs as search inputs. You can photograph a plant and ask "what is this and how do I care for it?" You can share a video of a plumbing problem and ask "how do I fix this?" You can drag a spreadsheet into the search box and ask "analyze this data."

The search box is no longer a text field. It is an input layer that accepts any format of information and returns contextual, useful responses.

### Agentic booking

Google is expanding direct booking capabilities. You can share criteria for a restaurant, a local service, or an experience, and Google returns real-time pricing and availability with direct booking links. The booking happens within Google. The business's website may never be visited.

For local businesses, this means your Google Business Profile data, reviews, and pricing accuracy are more important than your website. The transaction is moving upstream, into Google's interface, before the user ever lands on your site.

## What this means for websites

Here is where I stop describing features and start describing consequences. Because the consequences are significant.

### Your content is the training material, not the destination

Every capability I just described requires Google to read, understand, and synthesize content from the web. The apartment agent reads listing sites. The travel planner reads travel blogs and hotel databases. The generative UI pulls from comparison data and reviews.

Your content powers these features. But the user never visits your site to consume it.

This is the [zero-click reality](/blog/zero-click-search-strategy/) taken to its logical conclusion. Google does not just answer questions without clicks anymore. It builds entire experiences without clicks. Your website is the raw material. Google's AI is the manufacturer. The user gets the finished product inside Google.

### Discovery shifts from search to agents

When users delegate discovery to AI agents, the traditional search funnel collapses. There is no query to rank for because the user never typed one. There is no SERP to appear on because the agent evaluated your content programmatically.

The websites that get surfaced by agents are the ones with structured data that agents can parse, accurate and current information that agents can trust, and clear entity signals that agents can match to criteria.

[Schema markup](/seo/schema-markup/) is no longer a nice-to-have for search enrichment. It is the language agents use to decide whether your content matches a user's requirements. If your product page has no structured pricing data, the booking agent cannot include you. If your listing has no geo-coordinates, the local agent cannot find you.

### The value of a website visit increases

Here is the counterintuitive part. While total visits decline, the value of each visit increases dramatically.

When someone does click through to your site after interacting with Google's AI, they have already been qualified. They have already seen the summary, compared options, and decided they need more than what Google provided. These are high-intent visitors. They convert at significantly higher rates than casual browsers.

This means the websites that survive the AI search transition will be smaller in traffic but stronger in conversion. The ones that adapt their measurement to reflect this, tracking conversion value per visit rather than raw visit volume, will understand their real performance. The ones still measuring success by traffic alone will panic unnecessarily.

## How to adapt

I will not pretend there is a simple playbook for this. But there are clear strategic responses.

### Build what agents can read

[Structured data](/seo/schema-markup/) is now the minimum requirement for discovery. Product pages need Product schema with pricing, availability, and reviews. Service pages need Service schema with area served and pricing. Local businesses need LocalBusiness schema with every field populated.

If an AI agent cannot parse your data programmatically, you do not exist in the agent-mediated discovery layer. Period.

### Create experiences Google cannot replicate

Google can generate a budget tracker. Google cannot generate your proprietary methodology, your community, your hands-on workshops, or your personal consulting relationship.

The content and products that survive are the ones that require depth, nuance, expertise, or human connection that an AI-generated interface cannot replicate. Original research. Interactive tools with proprietary data. Communities where real people share real experiences. Expert consultation that requires understanding context no AI has.

### Own your audience directly

Every user who discovers you through Google's AI is a user Google can redirect at any time. Every user on your email list, your Discord, your YouTube subscriber base, or your podcast audience is yours.

The [omnichannel presence strategy](/blog/omnichannel-ai-brand-presence/) is no longer about SEO diversification. It is about survival. Build direct relationships with your audience through channels you control. Email lists. Paid communities. Subscriptions. Direct bookmarks.

### Optimize for the click that matters

When 83 percent of AI Overview interactions end without a click, the 17 percent that do click are extraordinarily valuable. Optimize your content to earn that click by providing something the AI summary cannot: depth, nuance, interactive tools, downloadable resources, or a perspective that only exists on your site.

This means your landing pages need to deliver immediate, obvious value that justifies the visit. If someone clicks through from an AI Overview and finds the same information they already read in the summary, they leave. If they find something deeper, they stay and convert.

## The bigger picture

I have been writing about the [AI SEO shift](/ai-seo-shift/) since this site launched. Every month, the shift accelerates. Every Google update makes the traditional search model a smaller share of total discovery.

Google Search can now do things that used to require Kayak, Zillow, a personal assistant, and a spreadsheet. That is not hyperbole. Those are the actual capabilities shipping to over a billion users.

The websites that thrive in this environment will not be the ones fighting for position in a results page that fewer people see. They will be the ones building [authority](/blog/seo-authority-not-rankings/) so deep that AI agents, AI Overviews, and generative interfaces all reference them as trusted sources.

Because in a world where Google builds the app, the value is in being the data source the app trusts.

## Frequently asked questions

### What new features did Google Search add in 2026?

Google Search at I/O 2026 introduced AI agents that monitor the web for you, generative UI that builds custom mini-apps from natural language, agentic booking for services and experiences, multimodal search input (text, images, video, files, Chrome tabs), and AI Mode powered by Gemini 3.5 Flash for all users globally. AI Mode has crossed one billion monthly users.

### What are Google Search information agents?

Information agents are AI agents you create inside Google Search that continuously monitor the web on your behalf. You define criteria (apartment requirements, job preferences, price thresholds) and the agent scans 24/7, notifying you when matches appear. They launch for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in summer 2026.

### How does Google generative UI work?

Generative UI lets Google Search build interactive tools and interfaces from plain English descriptions. You describe what you need ("compare flights from Austin to Tokyo in October") and Google assembles a custom interactive tool with real-time data, filters, and sorting, all within the search page. No app download or website visit required.

### Does Google Search replace websites now?

For simple queries, comparisons, and utility tasks, increasingly yes. Google's AI builds experiences that previously required visiting multiple websites. However, for deep expertise, original research, community interaction, and complex transactions, websites remain essential. The key shift is that website visits become higher-intent but lower-volume.

### How should websites adapt to these changes?

Focus on structured data so AI agents can read your content, create experiences Google cannot replicate (proprietary tools, communities, expert consultation), build direct audience relationships through email and subscriptions, and optimize for high-intent visitors who click through from AI surfaces. Measure conversion value per visit, not raw traffic volume.
