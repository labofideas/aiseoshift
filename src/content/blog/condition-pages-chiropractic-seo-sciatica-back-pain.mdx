Patients with back pain do not search "chiropractor in [city]." They search "sciatica relief," "herniated disc treatment," or "neck pain after car accident." The chiropractors getting cited in AI Overviews and ranking in the local pack for these queries are the ones with dedicated, deeply-built condition pages.

Most chiropractic clinic sites have one "Services" page listing their treatments. That is not enough. Each major condition needs its own page, authored by a licensed chiropractor, structured for AI extraction.

## Why condition pages matter

Three reasons:

1. **Patients search by condition.** Search behavior data shows 5 to 10x higher volume on condition queries than on generic "chiropractor" queries.
2. **AI engines need depth.** When ChatGPT answers "can a chiropractor help with sciatica," it pulls from clinic pages that go deep on sciatica specifically. Generic services pages do not get extracted.
3. **Condition pages convert better than generic pages.** A patient who lands on a sciatica page is 3 to 5x more likely to book than a patient who lands on a homepage.

## The conditions that need their own pages

A complete chiropractic content cluster covers:

### Back and spine

- Lower back pain
- Upper back and shoulder pain
- Sciatica
- Herniated and bulging discs
- Scoliosis
- Spinal stenosis
- Degenerative disc disease

### Neck

- Neck pain
- Whiplash and auto accident injuries
- Cervical herniation
- Tension headaches and cervicogenic headaches

### Extremities

- Sports injuries (general and sport-specific)
- Plantar fasciitis
- Tennis and golfer's elbow
- Knee pain
- Shoulder impingement

### Special populations

- Pregnancy chiropractic
- Pediatric chiropractic
- Senior chiropractic
- Auto accident injury care
- Personal injury cases

Not every clinic should cover all of these. Build pages for conditions you actually treat regularly. Depth and clinical experience matter more than breadth.

## The anatomy of a condition page that wins

A great condition page is 1,500 to 2,500 words, structured for both AI extraction and patient skim-reading. The template:

### Section 1: Hero with patient-language summary

Lead with a 60 to 120 word summary in plain patient language. AI engines extract these summaries directly.

Example: "Sciatica is pain that radiates from the lower back through the buttocks and down one leg, caused by irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve. Chiropractic care addresses sciatica by reducing pressure on the nerve through targeted spinal adjustments, soft tissue work, and rehabilitation exercises. Most patients see significant relief within 4 to 8 weeks of consistent care."

### Section 2: What is [condition]?

A 200 to 400 word explanation of the condition itself. Symptoms, anatomy, causes. Written in patient language but factually grounded.

### Section 3: How does chiropractic help with [condition]?

The mechanism. Specific to your clinic's approach. AI engines extract treatment descriptions that distinguish your practice.

### Section 4: What to expect at the first visit

Patients searching for condition information are evaluating whether to book. Take them through the first visit:

- Initial consultation length and what is covered
- Examination process
- Imaging if applicable
- Treatment plan discussion
- Whether they receive treatment that visit or just consultation

### Section 5: Treatment timeline and expected results

Honest expectations. AI engines reward content that acknowledges trade-offs and timelines.

### Section 6: When chiropractic may not be the right choice

The most underrated section. Acknowledge red flags that need imaging or referral (loss of bowel/bladder function, progressive neurological symptoms, etc.) and other conditions where chiropractic is not the primary intervention.

### Section 7: Doctor's perspective

A 200 to 400 word quoted section from the lead chiropractor with their clinical perspective on the condition. This is the strongest possible E-E-A-T signal — credentialed expert weighing in personally.

### Section 8: FAQs

8 to 12 frequently asked questions in `FAQPage` schema. Examples for a sciatica page:

- How long does sciatica take to heal with chiropractic care?
- Is chiropractic safe for sciatica?
- Can I exercise with sciatica?
- Will I need imaging?
- Does insurance cover chiropractic for sciatica?

### Section 9: Local context

This is what separates real condition pages from generic templates. Include:

- Local data (auto accident statistics for whiplash pages, sports injury frequency for sports injury pages)
- Patient stories (with consent and HIPAA compliance)
- Local context that demonstrates clinic embedded in community

### Section 10: Booking CTA

Make the next step obvious. Same-day appointment availability, phone number, online booking link.

## Schema markup for condition pages

Three schema types for every condition page:

### MedicalCondition schema

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "MedicalCondition",
  "name": "Sciatica",
  "alternateName": ["Sciatic nerve pain", "Lumbar radiculopathy"],
  "description": "Pain radiating along the path of the sciatic nerve...",
  "possibleTreatment": [
    {
      "@type": "MedicalTherapy",
      "name": "Chiropractic adjustment"
    },
    {
      "@type": "MedicalTherapy",
      "name": "Spinal decompression therapy"
    }
  ]
}
```

### Article schema with chiropractor author

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "MedicalScholarlyArticle",
  "headline": "Sciatica: Chiropractic Treatment Approach",
  "datePublished": "2026-03-15",
  "dateModified": "2026-05-20",
  "author": {
    "@type": "Person",
    "name": "Dr. Sarah Johnson",
    "honorificPrefix": "Dr.",
    "honorificSuffix": "DC",
    "url": "https://example.com/team/sarah-johnson",
    "hasCredential": {
      "@type": "EducationalOccupationalCredential",
      "credentialCategory": "license",
      "name": "Texas Chiropractor License",
      "identifier": "DC-12345"
    }
  }
}
```

## How to ship 15 condition pages without burning out

The two things that make it sustainable:

### 1. Build a template once

A document with placeholders for condition name, symptoms, treatment approach, FAQs, and schema. Filling a template is much faster than starting from blank.

### 2. Batch the clinical content

The lead chiropractor blocks 2 hours per week for content. In each session, they dictate or write the "Doctor's perspective" sections and review drafts. This pulls the clinical depth from the doctor without consuming days of their time.

A realistic cadence is one condition page per week. At that rate, 15 to 20 condition pages are live within four to five months.

## Updating cadence

Condition pages need maintenance. Schedule:

- **Quarterly:** Refresh patient stories, update any clinical research references
- **Annually:** Full review by the licensed chiropractor, with "Last reviewed by Dr. [Name] on [Date]" notation updated

AI engines weigh recency heavily for YMYL content. A condition page reviewed in the last 12 months by a credentialed doctor carries dramatically more weight than a page that has not been touched in three years.

## Common mistakes that kill condition pages

- **No doctor byline.** YMYL extraction fails without named credentialed authorship.
- **Identical structure across conditions.** Each condition needs unique content. Templated near-duplicates get demoted.
- **Overpromising results.** Phrases like "guaranteed pain relief" trip FTC and state board issues and signal untrustworthy content to AI engines.
- **No mention of when chiropractic is not the right choice.** Acknowledging limits is a major trust signal.
- **No FAQ section.** This is where AI Overviews extract most often.

## FAQs

### How many condition pages does a chiropractor need?

Cover every major condition you regularly treat. For most clinics, that is 10 to 20 condition pages. Solo practitioners specializing in a niche (sports medicine, pregnancy, pediatric) might have 5 to 10 deep pages instead.

### Can I use AI to draft condition pages?

For initial structure and patient-language explanations, yes. For clinical accuracy, treatment approach, and the "Doctor's perspective" section, the licensed chiropractor must contribute substantively. AI-drafted health content with no licensed reviewer fails YMYL extraction.

### Should patient testimonials appear on condition pages?

Yes, with consent and HIPAA compliance. Patient stories that are first-person, specific, and verified add powerful E-E-A-T signals. Generic "great clinic!" testimonials add little.

### How does state board regulation affect condition page content?

Each state's chiropractic board has rules about claims and language. Common restrictions: no diagnosis claims by non-DCs, careful use of "treatment" vs. "care" language, no comparative claims about other providers. Consult your board's guidelines before publishing.

### What about insurance and pricing information?

Include in FAQs at minimum. Pages that address insurance coverage and pricing convert significantly better than pages that hide that information.
