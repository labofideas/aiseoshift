Stop thinking of your page as one thing that ranks. Google stopped a while ago.

In its [July 2026 search talks](https://www.seroundtable.com/july-2026-google-webmaster-report-41591.html), Google spoke openly about chunking, breaking a page into passages and evaluating them semi-independently. Retrieval systems, AI Overviews, and AI Mode all lean on this. The unit that gets matched to a query is often a chunk, not the whole document.

That sounds technical. The consequence is not.

Your page is no longer a single competitor in a single race. It is a bundle of passages, each entered in its own race. One section can win "how does query fan-out work" while another section on the same page loses "what is a vector database." Same URL, different outcomes, decided chunk by chunk.

Once you see a page that way, a lot of confusing behavior makes sense.

## Why a strong page still loses queries it should win

Picture a long, genuinely good guide. It covers a topic thoroughly. By old logic, it should rank for everything in it.

Under chunking, it competes passage by passage. If the answer to a specific question is buried in a paragraph that also wanders into three other subtopics, that chunk is muddy. It does not cleanly answer any one query, so it loses to a competitor whose passage answers that exact question and nothing else.

The page is strong. The chunk is weak. And the chunk is what was in the race.

This is why thoroughness alone stopped being enough. A wall of comprehensive prose can lose to a thinner page that happens to have one clean, self-contained passage per question.

## Writing for chunks

The fix is not shorter content. It is content built so each chunk stands on its own.

Give each distinct question its own section, under a heading that names the question. When Google chunks the page, your section boundaries become natural chunk boundaries, and each chunk arrives at the query pre-labeled with what it answers.

Keep a section on one idea. The moment a passage covers a question and then drifts into two adjacent ones, it stops being a clean answer to any of them. One idea per chunk is the discipline.

Make each section make sense lifted out. A chunk gets evaluated without the three sections above it, so a passage that depends on earlier context reads as incomplete on its own. Write it so a reader landing only there still gets a whole answer.

Front-load the answer inside the chunk too, not just the page. The direct response goes in the first sentence or two of the section, then the detail. This is the same reason [most citations come from the top of a page](/blog/where-ai-engines-pull-citations/), applied one level down.

None of this fights readability. A page organized into clean, self-contained sections is easier for a human to scan and easier for a machine to chunk. The deeper mechanics are in [how AI search retrieves content](/blog/how-ai-search-retrieves-content/) and [structuring pages for AI extraction](/blog/how-to-structure-pages-ai-extraction/).

The old instinct was to make the whole page comprehensive and trust it to rank as a unit. The new one is to make every passage a clean, standalone answer, because Google is grading them one at a time.

Write pages a machine can take apart, because it already does.

## FAQs

### What is chunking in Google search?

Chunking is when a search or AI system splits a page into smaller passages and evaluates them semi-independently, matching individual chunks to queries rather than scoring the whole document as one unit. Google discussed this openly in its July 2026 search talks, and retrieval, AI Overviews, and AI Mode all rely on it.

### Why does chunking mean my strong page can still lose queries?

Because the chunk competes, not the page. If the passage that answers a specific query is muddy or wanders into other subtopics, it loses to a competitor whose passage answers that exact question cleanly, even if your overall page is more thorough. The page can be strong while the individual chunk is weak.

### How do I optimize content for chunking?

Give each question its own section under a descriptive heading, keep each section to one idea, make every section understandable on its own without the sections above it, and put the direct answer in the first sentence or two of the section. Clean section boundaries become clean chunk boundaries.
