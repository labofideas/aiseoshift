A restaurant's Google Business Profile is the single most important marketing surface in 2026. It shows up when diners search the restaurant's name. It populates the local pack for category and "near me" queries. It feeds AI engines the structured data they need to recommend the restaurant. It hosts the photos, menu, reservations, and reviews that determine whether a diner books a table.

Most operators treat GBP as a one-time setup task. The ones winning treat it as a daily content channel. This is the tactical playbook.

## Why GBP matters more for restaurants than for most verticals

Three reasons:

1. **Local pack rankings drive walk-ins.** "Restaurants near me" is one of the highest-volume local queries on the internet. The three businesses in the local pack get the foot traffic. Everyone else is invisible at the moment of decision.
2. **GBP is the canonical source for AI engines.** When ChatGPT recommends a restaurant, it pulls categories, hours, price range, and review profile from GBP. Mismatches between GBP and your website confuse AI engines and demote you in citations.
3. **The booking surface lives in GBP.** Reservations through Google now happen inside the GBP panel via OpenTable, Resy, and Reserve with Google integrations. A complete GBP means more bookings without a website visit.

## Section 1: Categories — get this right first

Categories determine which queries your restaurant is eligible to rank for.

### Primary category

Use the most specific cuisine type available:

- Italian Restaurant
- Thai Restaurant
- Sushi Restaurant
- Mexican Restaurant
- Pizza Restaurant

Generic categories like "Restaurant" are dramatically less effective than specific cuisine categories.

### Secondary categories

Layer on everything else that fits:

- Bar, Cocktail Bar, Wine Bar (if you have a meaningful bar program)
- Brunch Restaurant (if you serve brunch)
- Family Restaurant (if you genuinely cater to families)
- Vegan Restaurant or Vegetarian Restaurant (if you have specific menu strength)
- Coffee Shop (if you serve quality coffee, not just because you have coffee)

Do not add categories you do not genuinely serve. Google reconciles against reviews and photos. Mismatch gets the profile demoted.

## Section 2: Business description

You get 750 characters. Use them all. Most restaurants write 200 and stop.

A strong restaurant description includes:

- Cuisine specificity ("Northern Italian," not just "Italian")
- Atmosphere and occasion ("intimate dining room with date-night booth seating")
- Notable menu items or specialties
- Chef or owner story (if interesting)
- Dietary accommodations
- Reservation policy and walk-in availability
- Notable accolades or press

Avoid generic phrases like "we provide a great dining experience." AI engines extract distinctive content. Boilerplate gets ignored.

## Section 3: Hours, including special hours

Hours are higher-impact than most operators realize. Inaccurate hours produce poor reviews ("they were closed even though Google said they were open"), which directly damage local pack rankings.

Maintain:

- Standard weekly hours
- Special hours for holidays
- Updated hours during temporary closures or special events
- Brunch hours separately if applicable

Use the "additional hours" feature for kitchen close vs. bar close, takeout-only hours, or lunch service distinct from dinner.

## Section 4: Photos — quantity and recency both matter

Restaurant GBP photos drive both local pack visibility and booking conversion.

### What to upload

- 10+ food close-ups (signature dishes especially)
- 5+ interior shots showing the dining room atmosphere
- 3+ exterior shots
- 2+ team or owner photos
- Menu photos (high resolution, readable)
- Patio, bar, or special-occasion areas

### Cadence

Upload 5 to 15 new photos per month. Stale photo profiles lose local pack share. Encourage diners to upload as well — user-generated photos signal active business.

### Quality bar

Restaurant photos with good lighting and composition convert dramatically better than poor-quality phone shots. If budget is tight, one professional shoot every six months beats constant amateur photos.

## Section 5: Posts — weekly minimum

GBP posts appear inside the panel and signal active business. For restaurants, three post types work consistently:

1. **New menu items or seasonal additions** (with photo)
2. **Events** (live music, wine dinners, special menus, holidays)
3. **Specials and promotions** (real, time-bound, not perpetual)

Cadence: two to four posts per week. Restaurants posting less than weekly lose visibility to active competitors.

## Section 6: Reservation links

Restaurants that book via OpenTable, Resy, Yelp Reservations, or Reserve with Google should connect those integrations to their GBP. Bookings made through the panel skip the website entirely and convert at significantly higher rates.

If you take phone reservations only, ensure the phone number is correct and clickable from the mobile GBP panel.

## Section 7: Menu link or full menu upload

Three approaches:

1. **Direct menu URL** linked from GBP (acceptable, requires website maintenance)
2. **PDF menu uploaded to GBP** (works but less ideal — not machine-readable)
3. **Native menu in GBP** entered item-by-item (highest visibility, most maintenance)

The native menu in GBP allows individual menu items to appear in search results. For restaurants serious about discovery, this is worth the maintenance overhead.

Pair native GBP menus with structured menu schema on your website. See the companion post on [menu schema markup for restaurants](/blog/menu-schema-markup-for-restaurants/).

## Section 8: Q&A — seed the questions diners ask

The Q&A section is publicly editable. Most restaurants ignore it. The smart ones seed their own.

Common questions to pre-answer:

- Do you take reservations?
- Do you have outdoor seating?
- Are you family-friendly?
- Do you have vegan options?
- Is parking available?
- Are you pet-friendly on the patio?
- Do you have private dining?

Seeded Q&A populates the GBP panel and feeds AI Overviews directly.

## Section 9: Attributes — every attribute matters

The "attributes" section is the most-overlooked GBP feature. It includes 30+ specific signals:

- Outdoor seating
- Wheelchair accessible
- LGBTQ+ friendly
- Live music
- Live sports on TV
- Cozy
- Romantic
- Casual
- Upscale
- Late night
- Good for groups
- Good for kids
- Free Wi-Fi

These attributes feed AI engines extraction signals. A restaurant marked "cozy, romantic, intimate" appears in date-night queries. A restaurant marked "good for groups, sports, late night" appears in different queries. Be honest, but be complete.

## Section 10: Reviews and responses

Restaurant review strategy gets full coverage in the companion post on [restaurant review velocity and AI discovery](/blog/restaurant-review-velocity-ai-discovery/). The GBP-specific points:

- Respond to every review within 24 hours, both positive and negative
- Use the diner's first name and reference specific menu items or aspects of their experience
- Sign off with a real human name (chef, owner, manager)
- Never argue publicly — take disputes offline

## What to do this week

A 7-day GBP rebuild:

- **Day 1:** Audit categories, hours, and business description. Update.
- **Day 2:** Upload 20 new photos. Tag descriptively.
- **Day 3:** Build out attributes section completely.
- **Day 4:** Seed 8 to 12 Q&As.
- **Day 5:** Schedule first month of posts.
- **Day 6:** Verify reservation integration is live.
- **Day 7:** Audit and respond to last 30 reviews.

## FAQs

### How many GBPs do I need for a multi-location restaurant group?

One per location. Each location is a distinct entity with its own categories, hours, photos, and reviews. Trying to run a single GBP for multiple locations gets the profile suspended.

### Should I have a separate GBP for my private dining or events?

If they are a distinct service with a different name or entrance, yes. If they are part of the main restaurant, no — they belong as services within the main GBP.

### What about ghost kitchens or delivery-only operations?

Yes, with the "Online food service" attribute and no physical address listed publicly. Verification is more rigorous for delivery-only operations.

### How do I deal with bad attribution? (Someone uploaded a photo of the wrong restaurant?)

Report the photo through GBP. Photos that are clearly not of your restaurant get removed in 2 to 7 days. Persistent bad attribution may require contacting Google support.

### Can I appeal a Google review I think is fake?

Yes, through the standard flagging process. Be specific about why the review violates policies. Most clearly fake reviews (reviewer with one-review history, content unrelated to dining experience) get removed in 2 to 4 weeks.
