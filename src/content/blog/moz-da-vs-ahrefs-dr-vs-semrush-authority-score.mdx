Moz's Domain Authority (DA), Ahrefs' Domain Rating (DR), and Semrush's Authority Score are three separate metrics built from three different backlink indexes and three different formulas, and none of them is more "correct" than the others. DA is the original and most widely recognized name, built purely from Moz's link index. DR is Ahrefs' pure link-strength metric, generally regarded as backed by one of the largest, freshest backlink databases in the industry. Authority Score is Semrush's broader metric, deliberately blending backlink data with organic traffic and spam signals rather than measuring links alone. Pick one, track it consistently over time and against your real competitors, and never compare a raw number from one tool against a raw number from another.

Three companies, three formulas, three numbers for the same website. Here is what actually differs.

## Why the same site gets three different scores

Each company built its metric from its own crawled backlink index, and no two of those indexes cover the exact same set of links across the web. Beyond the raw data difference, the formulas weigh things differently: Moz and Ahrefs lean toward pure link-strength calculations, while Semrush deliberately folds in organic traffic estimates and spam signals to try to represent overall site health rather than link profile alone. A site can genuinely be DA 45, DR 58, and Authority Score 39 simultaneously, and none of those three numbers is wrong. They are different instruments pointed at a related but not identical target.

This is also worth restating plainly: none of the three is a metric Google uses or publishes. All three are third-party estimates that correlate with ranking ability because backlinks genuinely matter, not a readout of Google's actual internal scoring.

## The comparison

| Metric | Company | What it measures | Free access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain Authority (DA) | Moz | Pure link-based prediction, 0-100 | Limited free monthly checks with account |
| Domain Rating (DR) | Ahrefs | Pure backlink strength, 0-100 | Free basic checker, no account |
| Authority Score | Semrush | Links + traffic + spam signals, 0-100 | Limited free queries |

### Moz Domain Authority: the original, still the most recognized name

Moz created the Domain Authority metric, which is why "DA" functions almost as generic shorthand for site authority even among people who use other tools. It is calculated purely from Moz's own backlink index, and its free checker returns DA alongside Page Authority (the same concept for a single page) and linking-root-domain counts. DA remains the number most people mean by default when they informally ask for a site's "authority," which makes it the useful common currency when comparing notes with someone outside your own tool stack.

### Ahrefs Domain Rating: built on one of the biggest link indexes

Ahrefs' Domain Rating is a pure link-strength metric, and Ahrefs is widely regarded as running one of the largest and most frequently refreshed backlink indexes in the industry, which gives DR a reputation for data freshness and depth that practitioners trust for serious link-building work. Its free checker returns DR, referring domain counts, and total backlinks with no account required for a basic lookup. If your primary interest is the underlying link data itself, not just the summary score, Ahrefs' index quality is the reason to lean toward DR.

### Semrush Authority Score: a broader, blended signal

Semrush's Authority Score is deliberately not a pure link metric. It combines backlink data with organic traffic estimates and spam-signal detection, aiming to represent whether a site is a real, trafficked, trustworthy property rather than one propped up purely by link volume, which a pure link score can miss. That makes it a useful sanity check against a link-only number: a site with a high DA or DR but suspiciously low estimated traffic is a pattern worth investigating, and Authority Score is built to surface exactly that mismatch.

## A concrete example of why this confuses people

Picture a mid-size blog with a genuinely healthy backlink profile. Moz might return a DA of 42. Ahrefs might return a DR of 51 for the same domain, because its index picked up a larger set of the site's actual referring domains. Semrush might return an Authority Score of 38, slightly lower, because its formula discounts some of that link value against a traffic estimate that looks modest relative to the link count.

None of these three numbers is the "real" authority of the site. Each is a defensible estimate from a defensible methodology. The mistake that trips people up is taking one of these numbers to a client or a boss and treating it as an objective fact rather than one tool's opinion, then being confused when a different tool later contradicts it. State which tool a number came from every time you report it, and the confusion mostly disappears.

## Which one should you actually pick

If you want the most widely recognized number to reference when talking to other people in the industry, Moz DA is the common currency. If your priority is the depth and freshness of the underlying link data for active link-building work, Ahrefs DR is the stronger choice. If you want one number that already accounts for traffic and spam risk rather than links alone, Semrush's Authority Score does that work for you. None of these is a wrong answer; the mistake is switching between them and comparing raw numbers as if they were on the same scale.

The full picture of how to use any of these three honestly, including the traps of chasing the number itself, is in [best domain authority checker tools](/blog/domain-authority-checker/).

## The bottom line

Moz DA for the most recognized common reference. Ahrefs DR for the deepest, freshest link data. Semrush Authority Score for a blended signal that catches spam-inflated link profiles a pure link score would miss. Whichever you choose, track your own trend on it over months and compare only against competitors measured in the same tool.

## FAQs

### Which is more accurate, Moz DA, Ahrefs DR, or Semrush Authority Score?

None is more accurate than the others; they measure related but distinct things using different data and formulas. DA and DR are both pure link-strength metrics from different backlink indexes, while Authority Score deliberately blends in traffic and spam signals. Pick the one whose data source and approach fit your need, and track it consistently rather than searching for the "correct" one.

### Can I convert a DA score to a DR score?

Not reliably. There is no official conversion formula because the two metrics are built from different backlink indexes with different weighting, so a DA of 40 does not correspond to a fixed DR value. Some third-party tools offer rough estimated conversions, but treat them as approximations only, not a precise mapping.

### Why does Semrush's Authority Score include traffic and spam data?

Because a pure link count can be manipulated or misleading: a site with many low-quality links can show a deceptively high link-only score while having little real traffic or trust. Semrush's blended approach aims to catch that mismatch, giving a more holistic view of whether a site is genuinely authoritative rather than just heavily linked.

### Why did my authority score change without anything I did?

All three companies periodically update their crawling methodology, index size, and scoring formula, which can shift every site's score even when nothing on that site changed. A sudden move in either direction, especially if it lines up with an announced update from the tool's provider, is usually a recalibration of the whole index rather than a reflection of your site specifically. Check whether your competitors' scores moved by a similar amount before assuming the change is about you.
