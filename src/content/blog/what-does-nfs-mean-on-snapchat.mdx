NFS shows up constantly on Snapchat, and it means a different thing almost every time depending on what it's attached to. There's no single correct answer, which is exactly why people keep searching for it.

Here are all four meanings, in order of how often you'll actually run into them.

## 1. Not For Sale

The most common meaning by far. Someone posts a photo of a shoe, a collectible, a piece of art, anything that looks like merchandise, and tags it NFS to pre-empt the inevitable "how much?" comments. If the snap is a picture of an object, this is almost certainly what it means.

## 2. No Funny Stuff

Used in private chats and streaks, usually as a way of signaling the conversation should stay honest or serious. Someone might send "be real with me, NFS" before asking a direct question, or add it to a message to say they're not joking around. The tell here is that it's attached to a sentence, not a photo of a product.

## 3. No Filter Selfie

Given how central selfies and filters are to Snapchat specifically, this meaning has grown alongside the platform's own camera features. A selfie captioned NFS is the poster explicitly flagging that no filter, beauty mode, or lens was applied, the opposite of the heavily filtered norm.

## 4. No Further Screenshots

Sometimes shortened from "not for screenshots," this is a request, not an enforceable rule, Snapchat's screenshot notifications are the actual technical safeguard, NFS here is just the sender asking politely not to be screenshotted on top of that.

## How to tell which one someone means

Context does almost all the work here.

A photo of an object, almost always Not For Sale. A sincere or vulnerable message, No Funny Stuff. An unfiltered selfie, No Filter Selfie. A sensitive or private snap, No Further Screenshots.

When it's genuinely ambiguous, which happens less often than it sounds, just ask. Nobody will find that confusing, and getting it wrong in a reply is more awkward than a quick clarifying question.

## One more meaning, off Snapchat

Outside social media, NFS has an older, unrelated meaning as the abbreviation for Need for Speed, the racing game franchise, and it also appears in classified listings generally (not just Snapchat) to mean Not For Sale in the same way. If you see it in a gaming context or a marketplace post rather than a Snapchat story, those are the meanings in play, not any of the four above.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does NFS mean in a Snapchat caption under a product photo?

Not For Sale. If someone posts a snap of a collectible, an outfit, or an item and captions it NFS, they're heading off DMs asking to buy it. This is the most common meaning when the snap clearly shows an object rather than a person or a conversation.

### What does NFS mean in a private Snapchat chat?

Usually No Funny Stuff, meaning the person wants the conversation kept honest, serious, or drama-free, often used right before or after sharing something personal. Context is the giveaway: if it's attached to a sincere message rather than a photo of an item, it's almost never "Not For Sale."

### Does NFS always mean the same thing on Snapchat?

No, and that's exactly why it causes confusion. The same three letters cover Not For Sale, No Funny Stuff, No Filter Selfie, and No Further Screenshots, and which one applies depends entirely on what the snap or message is actually about. Read the content it's attached to, not the acronym alone.

### Is NFS specific to Snapchat?

No. It shows up across Instagram captions, Twitter/X, and text messages with the same range of meanings, plus an older, unrelated use as shorthand for the Need for Speed video game franchise and in general online marketplace listings meaning Not For Sale.
