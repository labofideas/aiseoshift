HVAC is the most seasonally concentrated business in home services. When the first 95-degree day of summer hits, search volume for "AC repair near me" spikes 400 to 700 percent compared to baseline. When the first hard freeze of fall arrives, "furnace not working" queries follow the same curve. The contractors who have spent the prior weeks building AI visibility own those spikes. The ones who start optimizing after the season begins are chasing a train that already left.

This is not a theoretical problem. In markets where AI Overviews appear for HVAC queries, the cited contractor's phone starts ringing 30 to 45 minutes ahead of the first 100-degree day, before their competitors even know the season has arrived. AI gives the advantage to companies that have already established citation authority. The playbook here builds that authority in the off-season so the in-season revenue follows automatically.

## The seasonal spike strategy: build before, harvest during

The core strategic insight for HVAC AI SEO is that AI citation authority is built over weeks and months, not days. A Google Business Profile gains ranking authority incrementally. Service pages accumulate indexing signals over time. Review velocity builds a historical record that AI engines weight heavily.

The HVAC contractors who dominate summer are the ones who updated their GBP, refreshed their AC repair page, added photos of spring tune-up jobs, and built review velocity in April and May. The contractors who start thinking about "AC repair SEO" in late June are already six weeks behind the citation curve.

The seasonal calendar for HVAC SEO:

**February and March:** Refresh your GBP with spring readiness content. Update the AC-related services. Add photos of maintenance plan setups. Start pushing review requests harder.

**April and May:** Publish or update the AC repair page, AC installation page, and maintenance plan page. Add spring tune-up FAQ content to the GBP Q&A section. Run posts about AC tune-up specials.

**June through August:** You are harvesting. The citation authority you built is working. Focus on review velocity and emergency HVAC service page visibility.

**September and October:** Same preparation cycle for heating season. Refresh the furnace repair page, heat pump content, and emergency heating content. Add photos of fall tune-up jobs.

**November through January:** Harvest heating season citations.

## Pillar 1: Google Business Profile for HVAC contractors

HVAC GBP optimization is more specific than most contractors realize.

**Primary category:** HVAC contractor. In some regions, "Heating contractor" and "Air conditioning contractor" appear as separate options. Use whichever is most searched in your market, then add the others as secondary categories.

**Secondary categories:** Air conditioning repair service, Furnace repair service, Heat pump installation, Air duct cleaning service, Indoor air quality company (if applicable), Boiler supplier (if you work with boiler systems).

**Services to list explicitly:**

| Service | Season |
|---|---|
| AC repair and diagnostics | Summer |
| AC installation and replacement | Summer |
| Furnace repair | Winter |
| Furnace installation and replacement | Fall/Winter |
| Heat pump installation | Year-round growth |
| Emergency HVAC service | Year-round |
| HVAC maintenance plans | Year-round |
| Air duct cleaning | Year-round |
| Indoor air quality assessment | Year-round |
| Mini-split installation | Growing query volume |

**Seasonal GBP posts:** Post weekly, with a seasonal lean. In May: "Summer's coming. Is your AC ready? We're booking tune-ups now." In September: "First cold snap is weeks away. Schedule your furnace inspection before our calendar fills." These posts are indexed by Google and contribute to freshness signals.

**Photo strategy:** Post photos of actual completed jobs. An AC installation photo with the unit model visible and the technician in branded gear is far more effective than a stock photo of a thermostat. Update photos after every notable job type.

## Pillar 2: Service pages built for depth, not breadth

The HVAC website should have individual pages for every major service. Generic "HVAC services" pages that list everything in bullet points rank for nothing specifically. Individual service pages rank for the specific queries that drive bookings.

**AC repair page:** The page that needs to perform in summer. It should open with a clear statement that you diagnose and repair all major brands, your response time for same-day or emergency service, and your service area. Cover the most common AC problems homeowners search about: unit not cooling, unit freezing up, unusual noises, refrigerant questions, and unit running constantly without cooling the home. Include cost ranges for common repairs. Add FAQs with schema markup.

**Furnace repair page:** Same structure for heating season. Cover all major furnace brands. Address the most common furnace problems homeowners type into search: furnace won't turn on, furnace blowing cold air, short cycling, pilot light issues, and error codes. Cost ranges by repair type. FAQ section with schema.

**Heat pump installation page:** This is the highest-growth query in HVAC right now. The combination of federal and state efficiency incentives, utility rebates, and rising gas prices is driving massive consumer interest in heat pumps as a replacement for separate heating and cooling systems. A dedicated heat pump installation page needs to cover: what a heat pump does and how it differs from traditional systems, when a heat pump makes financial sense versus a traditional system, cost ranges for installation by home size and system type, the brands you install and your certifications for those brands, and how to access local utility rebates. Heat pump pages that address the incentive question directly outperform those that ignore it because the incentive question is in many homeowners' minds when they start researching.

**Emergency HVAC page:** The same urgency architecture that works for plumbing and electrical applies here. 24/7 availability stated in the first 100 words. Response time commitment. A list of the emergencies you handle: no heat in winter, no cooling during a heat wave, gas smell near furnace, refrigerant leak, complete system failure. Credibility signals: years in business, NATE certification, response guarantee.

**Maintenance plan page:** Recurring revenue from maintenance plans is what separates growing HVAC companies from feast-or-famine ones. The maintenance plan page should explain exactly what is included in a plan, the cost per year versus per-visit pricing, and the priority scheduling benefit that plan members receive. This last point is important: "Plan members get priority scheduling during peak season" is both a conversion argument and an urgency trigger that drives plan purchases before summer.

## Pillar 3: E-E-A-T for HVAC technicians

HVAC credentials are the most formalized of any home services trade. AI engines look for these signals specifically when deciding which contractors to cite.

**NATE certification:** North American Technician Excellence certification is the most recognized HVAC credential in the US. If your technicians are NATE certified, state this explicitly on every relevant page and in the GBP description. "NATE-certified technicians" is a phrase AI engines extract as an authority signal for HVAC contractors.

**EPA 608 certification:** Required for handling refrigerants. State that your technicians are EPA 608 certified. This is a legal compliance signal that AI engines interpret as professional legitimacy.

**Manufacturer certifications:** Factory-authorized dealer or service provider status for Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Rheem, or other brands is a significant trust signal. "Carrier Factory Authorized Dealer" or "Trane Comfort Specialist" are recognized terms. List all manufacturer certifications prominently.

**Years in business and service area history:** "Serving [city] and [surrounding areas] since [year]" signals community presence and longevity. HVAC companies with 10 or more years in a market get citation preference when other signals are close.

**Team page with technician credentials:** A team page that lists each technician's NATE certification status, years of experience, and the brands they specialize in builds authority depth that a single about page cannot achieve. It also answers the implicit question homeowners have: "Who is actually going to show up at my house?"

## Pillar 4: HVACBusiness and FAQ schema

Schema markup makes your credentials and service coverage machine-readable. AI engines use schema to match your business against specific queries without needing to interpret page text.

The `LocalBusiness` schema type for HVAC contractors is `HVACBusiness`. This tells AI engines you are an HVAC contractor specifically, not just a generic home services company. Include:

- `areaServed` with your service cities and zip codes listed
- `hasCredential` referencing your state contractor license number
- `openingHoursSpecification` including emergency/after-hours availability if applicable
- `brand` or `additionalType` for your manufacturer certifications
- `aggregateRating` populated from your review data

`FAQPage` schema on your service pages creates extractable content. The most valuable FAQ entries for HVAC:

For AC repair: "How much does AC repair cost?", "Why is my AC running but not cooling?", "How long does AC repair take?", "Should I repair or replace my AC unit?"

For heat pump: "How much does a heat pump installation cost?", "Are heat pumps effective in very cold climates?", "What incentives are available for heat pump installation?", "How long does a heat pump last?"

For emergency HVAC: "Does emergency HVAC service cost more?", "What is considered an HVAC emergency?", "How quickly can an HVAC technician arrive?"

Write each FAQ answer as a complete, standalone paragraph that fully addresses the question. AI engines cite these answers verbatim in Overviews and ChatGPT responses.

## The heat pump opportunity in detail

Heat pump queries have grown more than 200 percent in two years, driven by a specific convergence of factors. The Inflation Reduction Act federal tax credit for heat pump installations (up to $2,000 for qualifying systems) created a search wave from homeowners who heard about it and are now researching what it means for their home. State-level efficiency programs in California, New York, Massachusetts, and others have added additional incentive layers. Utility company rebate programs in many markets make the economics more favorable than gas furnace replacements.

The contractors who built heat pump installation pages in 2024 and 2025 are now harvesting the 2026 query volume. The contractors building those pages now are six to twelve months behind but still well ahead of the average competitor.

The heat pump content that performs best answers the incentive question explicitly. A page that says "how to access the federal tax credit for heat pump installation in [state]" and then walks through the process will earn citations from homeowners doing incentive research, not just system-shopping research. Incentive-aware content captures a different, earlier stage of the buying journey.

## FAQs

### How far in advance should I prepare for seasonal HVAC SEO?

Six to eight weeks before the season start. For summer, that means late April to mid-May. For heating season, that means mid-September to early October. GBP posts, service page refreshes, and review velocity push should all be underway before the first weather event that drives search spikes.

### Should HVAC service pages include pricing?

Yes, as ranges. "AC repair costs typically range from $150 to $600 depending on the issue and parts required" answers the cost question that is in every searcher's mind. HVAC companies that publish cost ranges convert better on calls because the customer arrives pre-qualified. AI engines also favor pages that answer cost questions because users asked them.

### Do HVAC companies need pages for every brand they service?

For the top three or four brands in your market, brand-specific service pages can be effective. "Trane AC repair [city]" and "Carrier furnace repair [city]" are specific searches with transactional intent from homeowners who own those brands. These pages do not need to be long: 600 to 800 words focused on your brand expertise and experience.

### How does NATE certification affect AI citations?

NATE certification is a recognized credential that AI engines use as a quality signal when making HVAC contractor citations. It is especially influential in "best HVAC company" AI Overviews, where AI engines are implicitly assessing quality, not just proximity. Stating NATE certification in the GBP description, website homepage, and About page is high-leverage.

### What is the value of HVAC maintenance plans for SEO?

Maintenance plan pages serve two SEO purposes. First, they capture queries from homeowners actively searching for maintenance plans. Second, maintenance plan customers become review-writing customers because they have ongoing relationships with the company. Those reviews drive both GBP authority and AI citation frequency.
