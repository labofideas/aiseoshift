The discipline of AI SEO has accumulated its own vocabulary faster than most practitioners can track it. Terms get coined, redefined, conflated with adjacent concepts, and sometimes abandoned, all within a single product cycle. The result is inconsistent usage across agencies, client reports, conference talks, and vendor documentation.

This glossary defines 50 terms in the order you are most likely to encounter them when doing the work. Every definition is written for practitioners, not academics. Where a term has competing definitions in the field, the most operationally useful definition is given with a note on the variation. Where a term connects to a deeper topic on this site, a link points you there.

Use this as a reference document. Bookmark it. Send it to clients who ask what "GEO" means. Return to it when a vendor deck introduces a term that feels vaguely familiar but imprecisely defined.

---

## A

**AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)**, The practice of optimizing content so that AI-powered answer engines select it as a cited source when generating responses to user queries. AEO extends traditional SEO by targeting not just ranking positions but citation slots inside generated answers. For a full treatment of the practice, see [what is Answer Engine Optimization](/blog/what-is-answer-engine-optimization/).

**AI Crawlers**, Automated bots operated by AI companies that crawl the web to collect training data or build retrieval indexes. Major AI crawlers include GPTBot (OpenAI), ClaudeBot (Anthropic), and PerplexityBot. Unlike search engine crawlers, AI crawlers may use content for model training rather than real-time retrieval. Publishers can block specific AI crawlers via `robots.txt` without affecting search engine indexing.

**AI Overviews**, Google's AI-generated summary panels that appear at the top of search results for many informational queries. AI Overviews synthesize content from multiple sources and display attributed citations. Pages cited inside AI Overviews earn a distinct visibility signal that does not always correlate with traditional ranking position. See [AI Overviews SEO strategy](/blog/ai-overviews-seo-strategy/) for citation optimization tactics.

**AI SEO**, The set of practices, strategies, and technical optimizations that improve a website's visibility across both traditional search engines and AI-powered answer systems. AI SEO encompasses traditional on-page and technical SEO plus AEO, GEO, schema markup implementation, entity optimization, and AI citation monitoring. The discipline is distinct from AI-assisted SEO, which refers to using AI tools to perform SEO tasks.

**AI Visibility**, A composite measure of how prominently a brand, domain, or piece of content appears across AI-generated answers, summaries, and recommendations. Unlike a single ranking position, AI visibility aggregates presence across multiple platforms (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot) and query types. For a precise definition and measurement framework, see [what is AI visibility](/blog/what-is-ai-visibility/).

**Answer Engine**, Any system that generates direct answers to user queries rather than returning a list of links. Answer engines include AI chatbots with web access (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot), Google AI Overviews, and voice assistants. The defining characteristic is synthesis: the engine constructs a response rather than routing the user to a source.

**Answer-First Content**, A content structure in which the direct answer to the implied query appears in the opening paragraph, before supporting context, caveats, or methodology. Answer-first structure is associated with higher AI citation rates because language models can extract and attribute the answer without parsing the full document. It is the content equivalent of the inverted pyramid format in journalism.

**Authority**, The degree to which a domain or entity is recognized as a credible, expert source within a topic area. Authority in AI search is conferred through external citations, mentions on high-credibility domains, structured data that confirms expertise, and consistency of signal across the web. Authority is not a single score but a cluster of signals that AI systems weigh differently than traditional PageRank-based domain authority metrics.

## B

**Brand Search**, Queries that include a brand name, product name, or branded modifier. Brand search behavior is a strong signal of entity recognition in AI systems. When users ask ChatGPT or Perplexity about a specific brand by name, the quality of responses depends on how well that brand is represented in training data, knowledge graph entries, and structured data markup.

---

## C

**ChatGPT Search**, The web search functionality integrated into ChatGPT that enables real-time retrieval of web content when generating responses. ChatGPT Search uses Bing's index as its primary retrieval layer and can cite sources with links. Optimizing for ChatGPT Search requires the same foundational work as optimizing for traditional Bing indexing, plus answer-first content structure.

**Citation**, An attribution reference within an AI-generated answer that links a specific claim or passage back to a source URL. A citation is the primary unit of AI visibility, it indicates that an AI system identified a page as a credible source for a specific piece of information. Citation frequency, citation position (first vs. third in a list), and citation breadth (across platforms) are all distinct signals.

**Citation Rate**, The percentage of tracked queries for which an AI system cites your domain or content when generating a response. Citation rate is the central KPI of AEO performance. It is calculated by dividing the number of queries that produce a citation by the total number of tracked queries and multiplying by one hundred. For a full measurement framework, see [AEO KPIs and AI visibility metrics](/blog/aeo-kpis-metrics-ai-visibility/).

**ClaudeBot**, Anthropic's web crawler, used to collect training data and power Claude's web retrieval features. ClaudeBot respects `robots.txt` directives. Publishers who want to appear in Claude's responses but not contribute to training data may need to specify disallow rules for the training-specific user agent string while permitting the retrieval agent. Check Anthropic's documentation for current user agent strings before configuring exclusions.

**Content Depth**, The degree to which a piece of content covers a topic comprehensively, including subtopics, edge cases, definitions, and practical applications. Content depth is distinct from content length. A 500-word answer that fully addresses a specific question has high depth for that query. A 5,000-word article that superficially covers ten tangential topics has low depth. AI systems favor depth over breadth at the individual query level.

**Conversational Query**, A search query phrased in natural language, typically as a question or multi-clause statement, rather than a short keyword string. "What is the best schema markup for a service business that also sells products?" is a conversational query. "Schema markup service business" is a keyword query. AI answer engines are optimized for conversational queries, and content that addresses the implied intent behind conversational phrasing earns higher citation rates.

## D

**Dark Traffic**, Web traffic that arrives at a site without a referrer signal, making its source unattributable in standard analytics. Dark traffic has expanded significantly with AI search because ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity do not always pass referrer data when users click through from citations. A meaningful portion of what appears as direct traffic in Google Analytics 4 or similar tools may be AI-referred. [AI SEO attribution models](/blog/ai-seo-attribution-models/) covers methods for identifying this traffic segment.

## E

**E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)**, Google's framework for evaluating content quality, extended in 2022 to add "Experience" to the original E-A-T framework. In AI search, E-E-A-T functions as a filtering mechanism: content that cannot demonstrate real-world experience, credentialed expertise, third-party authority signals, and technical trust indicators is less likely to be selected for citation. For a comprehensive implementation guide, see [E-E-A-T in the AI era](/blog/eeat-in-the-ai-era/).

**Entity**, A named, uniquely identifiable thing, a person, organization, product, place, concept, or event, that can be described, referenced, and distinguished from other things. Entities are the building blocks of knowledge graphs. In AI SEO, entity optimization means establishing clear, consistent, machine-readable signals about what your organization is, what it does, and how it relates to other entities in your domain.

**Entity Authority**, The degree to which an entity (typically a brand or person) is recognized and positively characterized across independent web sources, structured data, and knowledge bases. Entity authority is built through consistent NAP data, Organization and Person schema, Wikipedia and Wikidata entries, verified profiles on high-authority platforms, and third-party mentions that reference the entity by name.

## F

**FAQPage Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/FAQPage) structured data type that marks up a list of questions and answers on a page. FAQPage schema enables rich results in Google's traditional SERP and signals answer-ready content to AI systems during retrieval. Each Question entity within the schema should contain the complete answer text, not a truncated version. See [schema markup for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/) for implementation guidance.

**Featured Snippet**, A selected search result that Google displays above the organic listings in a box format, showing a direct answer extracted from the page. Featured snippets are a precursor to AI Overviews: Google uses similar extraction logic to select both. Pages that hold featured snippets are disproportionately cited in AI Overviews, which means featured snippet optimization and AEO are substantially overlapping practices.

---

## G

**GBP (Google Business Profile)**, Google's free tool for managing how a business appears in Google Search and Google Maps. For local businesses, GBP is a primary structured data source that feeds directly into Google's knowledge panels and local AI Overviews. Complete, verified, and regularly updated GBP listings contribute to entity authority and local citation rates in AI-generated answers.

**GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)**, The practice of optimizing content specifically for retrieval and citation by generative AI systems, including large language models with web access. GEO overlaps significantly with AEO but emphasizes the generative nature of the answer, how the AI synthesizes and presents information, rather than the query-answering function. For a detailed comparison of GEO, AEO, and traditional SEO, see [GEO vs. AEO vs. SEO](/blog/geo-vs-aeo-vs-seo/).

**GPTBot**, OpenAI's web crawler, used to collect training data for GPT models and to power real-time web retrieval in ChatGPT. GPTBot respects `robots.txt` and `noindex` directives. Publishers can block GPTBot entirely, block it from specific directories, or allow full access. Blocking GPTBot from training data does not necessarily block ChatGPT Search retrieval, which uses a separate agent string.

---

## H

**HowTo Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/HowTo) structured data type that marks up step-by-step instructional content. HowTo schema helps AI systems extract procedural information accurately, including the number of steps, required materials, and time estimates. It is particularly effective for home improvement, cooking, and technical documentation content. For implementation details see [HowTo schema for AI search](/blog/howto-schema-for-ai-search/).

---

## J

**JSON-LD (JavaScript Object Notation for Linked Data)**, The preferred method for implementing structured data markup on web pages, recommended by Google over Microdata and RDFa. JSON-LD is placed in a `<script type="application/ld+json">{` tag in the page `<head>` or `<body>`. It does not alter the visible HTML of the page and is easier to maintain than inline Microdata. All Schema.org types can be implemented using JSON-LD syntax.

---

## K

**Knowledge Graph**, A structured database of entities and their relationships, used by search engines to understand and connect real-world concepts. Google's Knowledge Graph powers knowledge panels and feeds directly into AI Overview source selection. Appearing in the Knowledge Graph, through Wikipedia entries, Wikidata records, structured data, and consistent third-party references, is a prerequisite for robust entity authority in AI search.

---

## L

**LocalBusiness Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/LocalBusiness) structured data type that describes a physical business location, including name, address, phone number, hours, geo-coordinates, and service area. LocalBusiness schema is the foundation of local AI SEO. It feeds data directly to Google's local knowledge panels, AI Overviews for local queries, and third-party AI assistants that answer location-based questions.

**Long-Tail Query**, A search query that is longer, more specific, and lower in monthly search volume than head-term keywords, typically three or more words. Long-tail queries are disproportionately represented in AI search because users tend to phrase conversational questions in full sentences. Pages that answer specific long-tail queries with direct, complete answers earn high citation rates despite low traditional search volume.

---

## M

**MedicalProcedure Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/MedicalProcedure) structured data type for describing medical procedures, including preparation steps, how the procedure is performed, and follow-up care. YMYL medical content with MedicalProcedure schema is more precisely retrievable by AI systems and signals that the page meets the structured evidence standards expected of health information sources.

---

## N

**NAP (Name, Address, Phone Number)**, The three core data fields that define a business's local identity across the web. NAP consistency, identical formatting across your website, GBP, directories, citations, and structured data, is a foundational local SEO and entity authority signal. AI systems aggregate NAP data from multiple sources when generating local business answers, and inconsistent NAP creates ambiguity that suppresses citation rates.

---

## O

**Organization Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/Organization) structured data type that describes an organization's identity, including its legal name, URL, logo, contact information, social profiles, and founding date. Organization schema is among the highest-priority schema types for any website because it directly feeds AI systems' understanding of what a brand is and how to describe it accurately. It should be present on every site's homepage.

---

## P

**Perplexity**, An AI-powered answer engine that provides cited, real-time responses to queries by searching the live web and synthesizing results. Perplexity is distinct from ChatGPT in that citation and source attribution are central to its product design rather than an add-on feature. Perplexity uses its own crawler (PerplexityBot) and refreshes its index frequently, which makes it a responsive platform for testing the effect of content updates on citation behavior.

**Person Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/Person) structured data type that describes an individual, including their name, job title, employer, credentials, and social profiles. Person schema is essential for E-E-A-T implementation because it provides machine-readable evidence of the author's expertise and identity. It is most commonly implemented on author bio pages and About pages.

**Product Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/Product) structured data type that describes a product, including its name, description, image, brand, SKU, price, and review aggregate. Product schema is the entry point for rich results in Google Shopping and AI-generated product comparisons. For e-commerce AI SEO optimization see [product schema for AI search](/blog/product-schema-for-ai-search/).

---

## R

**Rich Results**, Enhanced search result formats in Google's SERP that display additional information extracted from structured data, such as star ratings, FAQ dropdowns, event dates, or recipe details. Rich results are triggered by valid Schema.org markup and signal to AI systems that a page is machine-readable and source-worthy. Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results) validates whether a page qualifies for these formats.

**Robots.txt**, A plain text file at the root of a domain (`/robots.txt`) that instructs web crawlers which pages or directories they are allowed or disallowed from accessing. In AI SEO, robots.txt is used to control which AI crawlers can access which content. Blocking a training-data crawler does not guarantee that your content is excluded from model training if it was crawled before the directive was added.

---

## S

**Schema Markup**, Structured data vocabulary drawn from the Schema.org specification that is added to web pages to help machines understand their content. Schema markup is the primary technical lever in AI SEO because it converts implicit page content into explicit, machine-readable assertions. The most impactful schema types for AI visibility are Organization, Person, FAQPage, HowTo, Product, and LocalBusiness.

**Search Console**, Google Search Console is Google's free tool for monitoring how a site appears in Google Search, including indexing status, search performance data, and rich results reports. In AI SEO, Search Console's "Search type: AI Overviews" filter provides partial visibility into AI Overview impressions and clicks, a valuable but incomplete signal for measuring AI citation performance.

**SoftwareApplication Schema**, A [Schema.org](https://schema.org/SoftwareApplication) structured data type that describes software, including its name, operating system, category, pricing, and review aggregate. SoftwareApplication schema is the foundation of AI SEO for SaaS products. It enables rich results in Google and helps AI systems accurately describe software features when users ask comparison or recommendation questions. For a detailed guide see [SoftwareApplication schema for SaaS](/blog/softwareapplication-schema-for-saas/).

**Structured Data**, Machine-readable information embedded in a web page that explicitly describes the page's content, entities, and relationships using a standardized vocabulary. Structured data is implemented most commonly using Schema.org vocabulary in JSON-LD format. It functions as a translation layer between human-readable content and the data models that AI systems and search engines use to index and retrieve information.

---

## T

**Topic Authority**, The degree to which a domain is recognized as a comprehensive, credible source within a specific subject area. Topic authority is built by publishing interconnected content that covers a topic at multiple levels of depth and specificity, earning external links from other authoritative sources in the same space, and structuring content so that AI systems can identify the domain as a go-to reference. For a practical building guide see [how to build topic authority](/blog/how-to-build-topic-authority/).

**Topic Cluster**, A content architecture pattern in which a central pillar page covering a broad topic links to multiple supporting pages that address specific subtopics in depth. Topic clusters are a primary tool for building topic authority. The cluster structure signals to both search engines and AI systems that a domain treats a subject comprehensively rather than superficially. For implementation guidance see [how to build a topic cluster](/blog/build-a-topic-cluster/).

**Training Data**, The corpus of text, images, and other content used to train large language models. Much of the public web constitutes training data for current AI models. When your content appears in training data, AI systems may reproduce or paraphrase your information in generated answers even without a live retrieval step. This means historically published authoritative content can influence AI responses independently of current indexing status.

---

## U

**URL Canonicalization**, The practice of designating a single, preferred URL for a piece of content when multiple URLs might return the same or similar content. In AI SEO, proper canonicalization ensures that citation authority consolidates on the intended page rather than fragmenting across duplicates. Canonical tags (`<link rel="canonical">`) and consistent internal linking both contribute to clean canonicalization.

---

## V

**Voice Search**, Queries submitted by voice to AI assistants (Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant) or AI-powered devices. Voice search queries are overwhelmingly conversational in phrasing and favor single, direct answers over lists of options. Content optimized for voice search, direct opening answers, FAQ structure, natural language phrasing, overlaps substantially with AEO optimization.

---

## W

**Web Vitals**, Google's set of user experience metrics (Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint, Cumulative Layout Shift) that function as ranking signals in traditional search and credibility signals in AI search quality evaluation. Pages with poor Core Web Vitals scores are less likely to be prioritized as citation sources because technical quality is factored into Google's E-E-A-T assessment.

---

## Y

**YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)**, Google's classification for content that could significantly impact a user's health, financial stability, safety, or life decisions. YMYL pages are subject to higher E-E-A-T standards because the cost of providing inaccurate information is high. In AI search, YMYL content must meet stricter credibility requirements, author credentials, organizational authority signals, and accurate structured data, to earn and retain citation status.

---

## Z

**Zero-Click Search**, A search session in which the user's query is answered directly on the results page, via an AI Overview, featured snippet, knowledge panel, or other rich result, without the user clicking through to any external website. Zero-click rates are increasing with the expansion of AI Overviews. For strategy under zero-click conditions, see [zero-click search strategy](/blog/zero-click-search-strategy/).

**Zero-Click Rate**, The percentage of search sessions for a given query or query set that end without a click to any external website. Zero-click rate is a macro-level measurement of how thoroughly the SERP answers queries without requiring off-site navigation. It is distinct from Citation Rate: a page can be cited in an AI Overview that produces a zero-click session. High zero-click rate for your target queries is not a signal to abandon AI SEO, it is a signal to optimize for citation visibility within the zero-click answer itself.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between AEO and GEO?**

AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) focuses on optimizing content to be selected as a cited source when an AI system answers a specific query. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) focuses more broadly on how content is represented and synthesized within generative AI outputs, including the framing and phrasing of information in generated text. In practice, the two disciplines use nearly identical tactics, direct answers, structured data, entity optimization, and many practitioners use the terms interchangeably. The functional distinction matters most in enterprise contexts where teams need to define scope and ownership precisely.

**Which schema types have the most impact on AI citation rates?**

FAQPage and HowTo schema have the most direct impact on AI citation rates because they explicitly structure the answer content that AI systems extract. Organization schema is the highest-priority foundational type because it establishes entity identity. After those three, the most impactful types depend on your content type: Product schema for e-commerce, LocalBusiness schema for local businesses, Person schema for content with named authors, and SoftwareApplication schema for SaaS products.

**How does robots.txt affect AI crawlers differently than search engine crawlers?**

Robots.txt blocks AI crawlers the same way it blocks search engine crawlers, by directing them not to access specified URLs. The important distinction is purpose: blocking a search engine crawler reduces indexing and ranking. Blocking an AI training crawler (like GPTBot's training-data user agent) theoretically reduces use of your content in model training, but it does not remove content that was crawled before the directive was added. Blocking the retrieval-specific user agent for a platform like ChatGPT Search reduces real-time citation visibility from that platform. These are separate user agents and require separate disallow rules.

**Is E-E-A-T a direct ranking factor or an evaluative framework?**

E-E-A-T is an evaluative framework used in Google's Quality Rater Guidelines to train human evaluators and, by extension, the AI systems that learn from their assessments. It is not a single algorithmic signal with a numeric score. However, the signals that demonstrate E-E-A-T, author credentials, third-party citations, accurate contact information, structured data, factual consistency, do correspond to measurable ranking and citation factors. Treating E-E-A-T as a checklist of signals to implement is the most operationally useful approach.

**What is the fastest way to start improving AI citation rates?**

The three highest-leverage changes, in order of implementation speed, are: (1) add answer-first structure to your existing content, move the direct answer to the opening paragraph of each article; (2) implement FAQPage schema on pages that already contain question-and-answer sections; and (3) add or complete your Organization schema with `sameAs` links to your verified social profiles and third-party listings. These three changes can be completed within a week on most sites and begin to affect citation rates within two to four weeks as AI platforms re-crawl updated pages.

**How do zero-click rates and citation rates interact strategically?**

High zero-click rates for your target queries mean that getting cited is more valuable than ever, because ranking without citation yields fewer clicks than it used to. If your pages are cited in the AI Overview or AI answer that produces the zero-click session, you gain brand visibility and entity reinforcement even without a click. If your pages are not cited, you receive neither clicks nor visibility. The strategic implication is clear: the scarce resource in high zero-click query sets is the citation slot, not the ranking position. Citation rate optimization becomes more important as zero-click rates rise.
