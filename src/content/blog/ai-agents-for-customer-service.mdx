The best AI agents for customer service in 2027 are Fin (formerly Intercom), Zendesk AI agents and NiCE Cognigy, with Salesforce Agentforce, Amazon Connect, Sierra, Parloa, Decagon, Freshworks Freddy and Ada completing our ten. We ranked them on published price, governance, reach, evidence and handoff controls, not on demo polish.

## Key takeaways

- The category consolidated in 2026. Salesforce bought Fin (formerly Intercom) and Zendesk bought Forethought, so two of the most-compared support agents now sit inside the two largest CRM and helpdesk vendors.
- Price per resolution is now public for some and secret for others. Fin lists $0.99 per outcome, Zendesk $1.50 to $2.00, and Salesforce $2 per conversation. Sierra, Decagon, Ada, Parloa and Cognigy give no figure.
- Vendor resolution rates are not comparable. Each vendor defines "resolved" differently, and the definition is also the billing unit.
- Analysts are split. Gartner forecasts agentic AI will resolve 80 percent of common service issues by 2029, and also that half of companies that cut staff for AI will rehire.
- Your help content is the real product. Every agent on this list answers from your articles, so stale content shows up as confident wrong answers.

## What changed in 2026

In 2025 the question was whether an AI agent could answer a support ticket. In 2026 the question became who owns it.

On June 15, 2026 Salesforce [announced a definitive agreement to acquire Fin](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-to-Acquire-Fin/), the company formerly known as Intercom, at about $3.6 billion. On September 10 it [announced the deal had closed](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Completes-Acquisition-of-Fin/default.aspx). Salesforce's closing release does not say what happens to the Intercom helpdesk or to Fin's support for other helpdesks. If you run Fin on Zendesk, that is an open question worth asking in writing.

Zendesk moved first in March. [TechCrunch reported on March 11](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/11/zendesk-acquires-agentic-customer-service-startup-forethought) that Zendesk was buying Forethought, and Forethought's homepage now reads "now part of Zendesk." Zendesk is also retiring its older bot builder and Essential AI agents after December 10, 2026, per its [support documentation](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6970583409690-About-generative-AI-agents-in-Zendesk). Customers on the legacy tools have a deadline.

The independents keep going. Sierra, Decagon, Ada and Parloa all sell to enterprises on demo-and-quote terms, and NiCE absorbed Cognigy, which now runs under the NiCE Cognigy brand. The pattern is plain. Support agents are being pulled inside platforms, and the independents are competing on outcome-based pricing and enterprise assurances.

One more signal comes from the buyers. Gartner's survey of 321 service leaders, [published in February 2026](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-18-gartner-survey-finds-ninety-one-percent-of-customer-service-leaders-under-pressure-to-implement-ai-in-2026), found 91 percent reported pressure from executives to implement AI. Pressure is not results. It does explain why vendors can sell on promise.

## What counts as an AI agent for customer service

Vendors use four labels interchangeably. They are different products.

**Chatbot.** Matches a question to a scripted flow or a knowledge article. The older Zendesk bot builder is an example of the type. It does not decide anything.

**Agent assist.** Drafts replies, summarizes cases and surfaces articles for a human, who still sends the message. Salesforce's rep-assistance product sits here.

**AI agent.** Talks to the customer directly, follows your policies, calls tools in your systems, such as checking an order or updating a booking, and hands off when it should. This is what the ten products below do, at varying depth.

**Voice agent.** The same idea over a phone line. Latency, interruption handling and telephony cost make it a separate engineering problem, which is why voice-first vendors such as Parloa and Cognigy are on the list.

Our test is simple. Can it change something in a system of record without a human approving each action? If it only answers questions, it is a better FAQ, which is still useful and cheaper to govern.

## The market in numbers

Every figure was opened in November 2026. Vendor-reported figures are labeled.

**Adoption pressure is high, staffing cuts are not.** Gartner's [December 2025 release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-12-02-gartner-survey-finds-only-20-percent-of-customer-service-leaders-report-ai-driven-headcount-reduction) found only 20 percent of service leaders had reduced agent staffing because of AI. Another 55 percent reported stable staffing while handling higher volumes. Gartner's newsroom also lists a November 5, 2026 release with a 21 percent figure, but we could not open the body, so we use 20 percent.

**Forecasts disagree with each other.** Gartner predicted in March 2025 that [agentic AI will autonomously resolve 80 percent of common issues by 2029](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-03-05-gartner-predicts-agentic-ai-will-autonomously-resolve-80-percent-of-common-customer-service-issues-without-human-intervention-by-20290), cutting operational costs by 30 percent. In February 2026 it predicted that [half of companies that cut customer service staff because of AI will rehire by 2027](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-03-gartner-predicts-half-of-companies-that-cut-customer-service-staff-due-to-ai-will-rehire-by-2027), often under different job titles. Both come from the same analyst house. Read the first as a ceiling on "common" issues and the second as a warning about how the savings get booked.

**Cost per resolution may rise, not fall.** Gartner's [January 2026 prediction](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-26-gartner-predicts-genai-cost-per-resolution-for-customer-service-will-exceed-offshore-human-agent-costs-by-2030) is that generative AI cost per resolution will exceed $3 by 2030, above many offshore human agents, because vendors move from subsidized growth to profitability. Today's list prices of $0.99 to $2 sit below that line. A multi-year contract should say what happens to the rate.

**Customers are not uniformly happy.** A Qualtrics XM Institute survey of more than 20,000 consumers in 14 countries found [nearly one in five who used AI for customer service saw no benefit](https://www.qualtrics.com/news/ai-powered-customer-service-fails-at-four-times-the-rate-of-other-tasks/), a failure rate almost four times higher than for AI use in general. It is a self-reported measure, fielded in the third quarter of 2025. Gartner's older survey of 5,728 customers, fielded in December 2023, found 64 percent preferred that companies not use AI in service. It predates the agent wave, so we flag the date.

**Vendors report their own wins.** Fin says it averages a [76 percent resolution rate across more than 12,000 customers](https://fin.ai/). Salesforce says Agentforce resolves 85 percent of its own service requests. These are vendor claims on vendor definitions.

**Service teams expect fast growth.** Salesforce's [State of Service report](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-service-report-announcement-2025/), a survey of 6,500 service professionals, found respondents estimate AI handles 30 percent of cases today and expect 50 percent by 2027. It is a vendor-run survey of its own audience and an estimate, not telemetry.

**Machine customers are arriving.** Forrester [predicted](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/2026-the-year-ai-gets-real-for-customer-service-but-its-not-glamorous-work/) that at least three major brands will see single-day call volume spikes 100 times above normal because of consumer AI agents. It is a prediction, but it points at a real change in who is on the other end of your queue.

**Regulators have been watching chatbots for years.** The CFPB's 2023 report, [Chatbots in consumer finance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/), found more than 98 million US users engaged with a bank chatbot in 2022 and named risks including inaccurate information and "doom loops" with no route to a human. It is finance-specific and dated, but the failure modes carry over.

A caution on all of it. Samples differ, years differ, and "resolved" means something different in every source. Do not chart these as one series.

## How we ranked them

This ranking is based on vendor documentation, vendor pricing pages and published analyst commentary, opened in November 2026. We did not run hands-on tests, and we did not receive anything from any vendor. That limits what we can say about answer quality, and we flag it where it matters.

Each product scores 1 to 5 on five criteria, for a maximum of 25. The scores are editorial judgment from the sources. Rescore them with your own weights.

| Criterion | What earns a 5 | What earns a 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Price clarity | A published unit price and a clear rule for what is billed | Demo or quote only |
| Governance | Named certifications and documented controls | Little beyond a trust page link |
| Channels and integrations | Voice, chat, email and messaging, with open integration to other helpdesks | One or two channels, closed to outside systems |
| Evidence | Checkable results, ideally measured by the customer | Marketing claims only |
| Handoff and QA | Documented human escalation, testing and monitoring tools | Handoff mentioned, no tooling shown |

Ties are broken by evidence, then governance, then handoff and QA. A published price is a point in a product's favor, not a verdict on quality: a secret price just means you must ask.

## The 10 agents, ranked

### 1. Fin (formerly Intercom)

**Score: 21 of 25** (price 4, governance 5, channels 5, evidence 3, handoff 4).

Fin is a customer agent across voice, chat, email, Slack and social channels. It is natively integrated with the Intercom helpdesk but, per its [site](https://fin.ai/), works with other helpdesks too. It learns from help articles, internal content, PDFs and web pages through a content library you can target by plan, location or brand.

Who it suits: support teams of any size that want a published unit price and do not want to switch helpdesk.

Pricing as published on [Intercom's pricing page](https://www.intercom.com/pricing) in November 2026: $0.99 per outcome, charged once per conversation, no seats needed with an existing helpdesk, with an unstated minimum monthly commitment and channel usage billed separately. [Fin's help documentation](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8205718-best-practices-for-using-fin-ai-agent) lists a resolution at $0.99, a procedure handoff at $0.99 and a qualification at $9.99, and says a resolution is deducted if the customer returns.

Governance is the strongest on the list. Fin's site lists SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 42001, AIUC-1, ISO 27701 and HIPAA, plus SSO, SCIM and regional hosting in the US, EU and Australia. The documentation says it hands off automatically on topics such as self-harm, minors and high-risk medical, legal or financial advice.

Real limits. The minimum commitment is not published. The Salesforce acquisition makes the long-term roadmap a question. And an "outcome" includes conversations where the customer simply stops replying, which can flatter the 76 percent figure.

Use case: a support team on another helpdesk puts Fin in front of its queue and pays only for conversations it closes.

Verdict: the best mix of published price and published controls. Ask what the Salesforce deal changes for you before you sign.

### 2. Zendesk AI agents

**Score: 19 of 25** (price 5, governance 3, channels 4, evidence 3, handoff 4).

Zendesk sells [autonomous AI agents](https://www.zendesk.com/service/ai/ai-agents/) for messaging, email and voice that handle multi-step requests and take actions in connected systems. The page lists 80 languages and says the agents can work on non-Zendesk platforms. A built-in QA layer audits outcomes and enforces policies.

Who it suits: teams already on Zendesk, and mid-market teams that want a per-resolution price on the page.

Pricing on [Zendesk's pricing page](https://www.zendesk.com/pricing/) in November 2026: an Automated Resolution costs $1.50 on a commitment or $2.00 pay-as-you-go, on top of plans from $19 per agent per month on Support Team (billed yearly) to $115 on Suite Professional. Each plan includes some resolutions, five per agent per month on Support Team and Suite Team and ten on Suite Professional. You pay only for requests resolved without escalation.

Real limits. The product page names no certifications, only a trust center link. Legacy bots are retired after December 10, 2026, so migrating customers have a deadline. And the headline customer figures are quotes: Hello Sugar, a salon chain, reports a 66 percent automation rate and $14,000 a month in savings.

Use case: a salon group grew from 81 to 160 locations without growing its reception staff, according to the Zendesk customer story.

Verdict: the clearest pricing among the established helpdesks. Governance detail is thinner than it should be for the score.

### 3. NiCE Cognigy

**Score: 18 of 25** (price 1, governance 4, channels 5, evidence 4, handoff 4).

[Cognigy.AI](https://www.cognigy.com/) is a platform for voice, chat and messaging agents plus an agent copilot, now branded NiCE Cognigy. Its "Composite Behavior" mixes language-model reasoning with deterministic rules. It lists runtime guardrails for policy, PII and topic, routing across several model providers, two-way MCP support and more than 100 languages. It is built to sit beside Amazon Connect, Genesys, NiCE CXone, Five9 and other contact center platforms.

Who it suits: enterprise contact centers, especially voice-heavy ones, that already run another telephony stack.

Pricing: none published.

Evidence is the best among the quote-only vendors, and it is customer-measured per the site. Lufthansa Group is listed with more than 16 AI agents and more than 16 million automated conversations a year, Henkel with 5 million interactions across 25 agents, and Salzburg AG with 30,000 calls saved a year.

Real limits. No price, and an engineering-heavy build.

Use case: an airline group runs many specialized agents on one platform, each with guardrails per use case.

Verdict: the strongest independent for complex voice and multi-channel estates.

### 4. Salesforce Agentforce for Service

**Score: 18 of 25** (price 4, governance 3, channels 4, evidence 3, handoff 4).

Agentforce combines a [service rep assistant](https://www.salesforce.com/service/ai/) with a customer-facing service agent that escalates with full context. Enterprise Knowledge grounds answers in connected third-party sources.

Who it suits: companies already on Salesforce Service Cloud.

Pricing is published but complicated. Per [Salesforce's Agentforce pricing](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/), list prices in November 2026 include Flex Credits at $500 per 100,000 credits, Conversations at $2 each, and a user license at $5 per user per month that requires Flex Credits. Flex Credits and Conversations cannot be used in the same org, and unused credits do not roll over. The service page lists the rep-assistance product at $125 per user per month billed annually.

Real limits. The credit and conversation model takes a spreadsheet to compare. The service page does not describe the trust layer, so we do not credit it here.

Use case: Salesforce reports that Agentforce resolves 85 percent of requests on its own help site, with more than a million requests solved. That is the vendor reporting on itself.

Verdict: sensible if you already live in Salesforce. Everyone else should compare cost per resolution before committing.

### 5. Amazon Connect Customer

**Score: 18 of 25** (price 4, governance 5, channels 4, evidence 2, handoff 3).

[Amazon Connect](https://aws.amazon.com/connect/) now reads "Amazon Connect Customer" and offers agentic self-service and voice, a no-code agent designer, agent assistance and full-context escalation.

Who it suits: teams with AWS engineering capacity that want usage-based pricing and strong compliance coverage.

Pricing on the [pricing page](https://aws.amazon.com/connect/pricing/) as read in November 2026: $0.010 per chat message, $0.038 per voice minute plus telephony, $0.080 per email and $0.014 per SMS or third-party message. The page says there are no tokens and no seat licenses. Re-check the figures before you budget, because they are per message and minute, not per resolution.

Governance is broad: HIPAA eligibility, PCI DSS, SOC, FedRAMP, ISO 27001 and GDPR support.

Real limits. Per-message pricing is hard to turn into a cost per resolved case. The evidence on the page is thin, a single headline about Cochlear scaling quality evaluations 22 times.

Use case: an in-house engineering team builds a voice agent and pays only for minutes used.

Verdict: strong on compliance and price transparency by unit, weak on proof. Budget engineering time.

### 6. Sierra

**Score: 17 of 25** (price 1, governance 5, channels 5, evidence 2, handoff 4).

[Sierra](https://sierra.ai/) sells a conversational platform. Ghostwriter builds agents from SOPs or transcripts, Agent Studio handles building, and Insights adds monitors and experiments. One agent can serve chat, SMS, WhatsApp, email, voice and ChatGPT.

Who it suits: large consumer brands that want a managed, outcome-priced agent.

Pricing: the site says you pay for outcomes. It publishes no figures, and the pricing page we tried returned a 404 in November 2026.

Governance logos are extensive: SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 42001, HIPAA, GDPR, EU AI Act, PCI DSS and FedRAMP, with no scope detail.

Real limits. Named customers such as CLEAR, Rocket Mortgage and SiriusXM have testimonials but no quantified results on the page. Without numbers, a score above 2 on evidence would be generous.

Use case: one agent serves a brand across messaging and voice, and also inside ChatGPT.

Verdict: strong assurances, no public proof or price. Fine for a shortlist, not for a signature.

### 7. Parloa

**Score: 17 of 25** (price 3, governance 5, channels 4, evidence 2, handoff 3).

[Parloa](https://parloa.com/) builds enterprise customer-facing agents with lifecycle management, authenticated actions such as updating a reservation, and human handoff. It positions itself as an IVR replacement.

Who it suits: enterprises replacing phone menus and routing.

Pricing is described but not quantified. The [pricing page](https://parloa.com/pricing/) says usage-based: voice per minute, chat per interaction, with volume packages, custom plans and an outcome-based option. That earns a 3 because the units are public even if the numbers are not.

Governance badges cover ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, PCI DSS, DORA and EU AI Act.

Real limits. The page's headline claim of 70 percent lower cost per contact is unattributed, so we do not treat it as a customer result.

Use case: customer logos include Booking.com, Zalando and SAP, though the page ties no figures to them.

Verdict: a credible voice-first option with the clearest pricing logic of the quote-only group.

### 8. Decagon

**Score: 15 of 25** (price 1, governance 2, channels 4, evidence 3, handoff 5).

[Decagon](https://decagon.ai/) describes itself as an AI concierge across voice, chat and email on a shared layer. Its standout is the tooling: Agent Operating Procedures in natural language, live A/B Experiments, simulations at scale, an always-on QA layer called Watchtower, and detection of customers' personal agents with a dedicated channel and permissions.

Who it suits: high-volume digital brands that want to test and tune their agent continuously.

Pricing: none published. The site asks for a demo.

Evidence is customer-reported on the vendor page: Chime at 70 percent chat and voice resolution, Duolingo at 80 percent deflection, Oura at 3 times CSAT, Rippling at a 32 percent increase in deflection. The measure differs per customer, so they do not add up to a benchmark.

Real limits. The page names no certifications, only a trust center. No price.

Verdict: the best handoff and QA tooling on the list, with the weakest published governance.

### 9. Freshworks Freddy AI Agent

**Score: 15 of 25** (price 5, governance 1, channels 3, evidence 3, handoff 3).

Freddy AI Agent runs inside Freshdesk for multilingual, multi-turn conversations, with agent-assist features such as summaries and tone adjustment.

Who it suits: small and mid-sized teams already on Freshdesk that want the cheapest published entry point.

Pricing on [Freshworks' pricing page](https://www.freshworks.com/freshdesk/pricing/) in November 2026: the first 500 sessions are free one time per account, then $49 per 100 sessions, across the Growth ($19 per agent per month yearly), Pro ($55) and Enterprise ($89) plans. That works out to about $0.49 per session, our arithmetic. An email session is a 72-hour window from the customer's first email.

Real limits. A session is not a resolution. One session can contain many turns, so cost per resolved case needs your own measurement. The product page makes no compliance claims.

Use case: Hobbycraft, a retailer with more than 120 stores, reports AI handling 30 percent of queries and 82 percent first-contact resolution, per a vendor article.

Verdict: the cheapest per-unit price and the thinnest governance page.

### 10. Ada

**Score: 14 of 25** (price 1, governance 4, channels 3, evidence 3, handoff 3).

[Ada](https://www.ada.cx/pricing/) sells an enterprise AI customer service agent. Its page claims SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR and PCI DSS compliance and AIUC-1 certification, shown as badges without scope, and says it serves more than 350 global enterprises.

Who it suits: enterprise support teams that want a vendor-built ROI model from their ticket volume.

Pricing: none published. A demo produces an ROI model.

Evidence is customer-reported: Dott raised automated resolution from 32 to 77 percent, Cebu Pacific reports more than 50 percent higher CSAT, monday.com 42 percent lower average handle time, and IPSY a 943 percent ROI in four months. There is no methodology behind any of them.

Real limits. Everything is vendor-showcased, and there is no price.

Verdict: a long-standing name in the category, and the least published detail of the ten.

## How support agents change who gets found and cited

This is the section most buyer guides skip, and it is why a search publication reviews support software.

**Your help center is now the source text.** Fin's documentation says it can [learn from public and private sources](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/7120684-fin-ai-agent-explained), including help articles, internal support content, PDFs and web pages. Zendesk agents answer from [trusted knowledge sources](https://www.zendesk.com/service/ai/ai-agents/), and Salesforce says Enterprise Knowledge gives answers grounded in trusted information. If your refund article is out of date, your agent will state the old policy with total confidence.

Gartner's February survey puts a number on the response: 58 percent of service leaders aim to upskill agents into knowledge-management specialists. The scarce skill is not prompt engineering. It is keeping a few hundred articles accurate.

**Customers will send their own agents.** Gartner's June 2025 research, based on 4,879 customers surveyed in early 2025, found [51 percent would use a GenAI assistant to handle service interactions on their behalf](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-identifies-three-trends-that-will-shape-the-future-of-customer-service). Gartner describes such assistants as both a channel and a customer proxy, and notes they may reduce the quality and volume of voice-of-customer data. Vendors are already building for this: Decagon lists detection of personal agents with permissions the customer controls, Cognigy lists two-way MCP support, and Lorikeet's homepage includes machine-directed instructions for querying its own support agent, which we ignored.

**Outside assistants quote what they can read.** A shopper's assistant that checks your return policy before buying reads the same public pages a search engine does. A policy that lives only inside a chat widget, or a PDF behind a login, gives it nothing to quote. That is a visibility problem, not a support problem, and it is cheap to fix.

**Disclosure rules touch what customers see.** Article 50(1) of the EU AI Act, per an [annotated copy of the text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/), requires that people be told they are interacting with an AI system unless that is obvious, and the page states Article 50 applies from August 2, 2026. We opened an unofficial copy, not EUR-Lex, and it flags later amendments, so confirm the date on the official text. California's [bot disclosure law](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-17941/) is narrower: it targets bots used to mislead people about their non-human nature to influence a sale or an election, and offers a safe harbor for a clear and conspicuous disclosure. This is not legal advice.

What we would do, in order of cost:

1. Put refund, shipping, cancellation and warranty policies on public HTML pages, with dates, not only inside the widget.
2. Give every article an owner and a review date, and retire contradictions before the agent finds them.
3. Add accurate structured data to the pages customers and assistants read. Our guide to [schema markup for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/) covers the method.
4. Check whether assistants quote your policies correctly. [How to measure AI visibility](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/) covers what to track.
5. Read the wider shift in [Google's search agents](/blog/google-search-agents/) and in [agentic commerce optimization](/blog/what-is-agentic-commerce-optimization/).

## How to choose

By setup.

**You already run a helpdesk.** Price your own vendor's agent first. Zendesk and Salesforce shops get native integration, and Fin works across helpdesks if you want to stay put.

**You run a contact center with phone lines.** Cognigy, Parloa and Amazon Connect are built for voice and telephony. Ask for latency and handoff data on your own call recordings.

**You are small.** Freshworks has the lowest published entry price, and Fin and Zendesk are close. Our [AI chatbots for websites](/blog/best-ai-chatbots-for-websites/) roundup covers lighter tools.

**You are large and regulated.** Governance lists decide. Sierra, Parloa, Cognigy and Fin publish the longest ones, and all of them ask you to verify scope.

By goal.

- **Cheapest per resolved case:** compare list prices first, then your own resolution definition.
- **Fastest to launch:** the agent attached to your helpdesk.
- **Most control:** Decagon's testing tools or Cognigy's guardrails.

Run a bake-off on the same 500 closed tickets from your own history. A vendor that will not let you do that has told you what you need to know.

## Implementation, governance and risk

**Define "resolved" before you sign.** Fin bills on outcomes, including a customer who stops replying. Zendesk bills on resolutions without escalation. Salesforce bills per conversation. Write your own definition: the customer's problem is fixed and they do not return within a set window.

**Plan for the doom loop.** The CFPB named customers stuck with a chatbot and no route to a human as a core harm. Every deployment needs a visible, fast handoff, and a rule for when the agent must hand off without being asked.

**Do not bank savings early.** Gartner's rehire prediction is a warning. Only 20 percent of leaders in its survey had cut staffing, and 42 percent were hiring for specialized AI-related roles. Treat the first year as a capacity project, not a headcount project.

**Test for hallucinated policy.** Run adversarial cases: refunds you do not offer, discounts the agent may not grant, medical or legal questions. Fin's documented auto-handoff on high-risk advice and Decagon's simulation tooling are examples of the controls to ask for.

**Watch the cost curve.** List prices of $0.99 to $2 look safe against Gartner's forecast of more than $3 by 2030. A multi-year deal needs a price cap or a re-opener.

**Give the agent the smallest permissions it needs.** An agent that can issue refunds needs a refund limit, a log and an alert. Review the logs weekly at first.

**Disclose.** Tell customers it is an AI. It is required in some markets and it protects trust in all of them.

## 2027 outlook

Expect three things.

Consolidation continues. With Intercom inside Salesforce and Forethought inside Zendesk, watch whether independent vendors keep their price lead or get folded in too.

Pricing converges on outcomes, and the arguments move to definitions. When every vendor charges per resolution, the contract language about what counts is where the money is.

Agent-to-agent traffic grows. Forrester's call-spike prediction and vendor features such as personal-agent detection point the same way. The brands that publish clear policies and offer machine-readable ways to act on them will handle that traffic better than those that rely on a chat widget.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which AI agent is best for customer service?

On our rubric, Fin (formerly Intercom) scores highest at 21 of 25, followed by Zendesk AI agents and NiCE Cognigy. The best fit still depends on your helpdesk: Zendesk and Salesforce shops should price their own vendor first, and voice-heavy contact centers should look at Cognigy, Parloa or Amazon Connect.

### How much do AI customer service agents cost per resolution?

Published list prices in November 2026 range from $0.99 per outcome for Fin and $1.50 to $2.00 per automated resolution for Zendesk to $2 per conversation for Salesforce Agentforce. Sierra, Decagon, Ada, Parloa and Cognigy publish no figures. Gartner forecasts that generative AI cost per resolution will exceed $3 by 2030.

### What is a good AI resolution rate?

Treat vendor numbers as a ceiling. Fin reports a 76 percent average across its customers, Zendesk customer stories quote 66 percent, and Decagon and Ada case studies quote higher or lower figures with no shared definition. Measure your own rate on a sample of closed conversations, counting a case as resolved only if the customer did not come back.

### Will AI agents replace human customer service teams?

Not in the near term on the evidence. Gartner's survey found 20 percent of service leaders had cut agent staffing because of AI, and Gartner predicts half of companies that attributed headcount cuts to AI will rehire for similar work by 2027. Gartner also expects no Fortune 500 company to eliminate human service by 2028.

### Do I have to tell customers they are talking to an AI?

In the EU, Article 50 of the AI Act requires that people be informed when they interact with an AI system, unless it is obvious. California's bot law protects a business that discloses a bot clearly when the bot is used to influence a sale. This is not legal advice, so check your own markets with counsel.

### Can an AI agent use my help center content?

Yes, and that is how most of them work. Fin, Zendesk and Salesforce agents answer from help articles, internal documents and connected knowledge sources. Out-of-date or contradictory articles become wrong answers at scale, so content upkeep becomes a core support job.

### Does this affect how my company shows up in AI search?

Indirectly. Gartner found 51 percent of customers would let a GenAI assistant handle service on their behalf, which means an AI agent may read your policies and pricing before a human does. Public, plain-text help content and policy pages are what both your own agent and outside assistants can quote.
