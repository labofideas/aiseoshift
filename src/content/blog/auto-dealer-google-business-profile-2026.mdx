The reviews, the photos, the response patterns, the active posting. Most shoppers do not visit dealer websites until after they have picked the dealer.

Each rooftop in your group needs a fully built, actively maintained GBP. This is the tactical playbook.

## Why GBP matters for dealers in 2026

Three reasons:

1. **Local pack drives showroom visits.** The three-pack at the top of search results for "Toyota dealer near me" or "[city] used cars" captures the highest-intent traffic.
2. **AI engines use GBP as a primary signal source.** When ChatGPT recommends a dealer, it pulls categories, hours, services, and reviews from GBP.
3. **Multi-rooftop differentiation happens here.** Within a brand network, the dealer with the better-maintained GBP wins the comparison.

## Section 1: Categories

Primary category: the dominant brand or vehicle type.

- Toyota Dealer (or whatever brand)
- Used Car Dealer (for independent used dealers)
- Car Dealer (when multi-brand)

Secondary categories — add every applicable one:

- Auto Body Shop (if you have collision)
- Auto Repair Shop (for service department)
- Car Detailing Service (if offered)
- Car Leasing Service
- Auto Parts Store (if you sell parts retail)
- Tire Shop (if applicable)

Each secondary category opens additional query eligibility.

## Section 2: Business description

You get 750 characters. Most dealers use 250. Use them all.

Strong descriptions include:

- Brands sold
- Year founded and ownership story (if compelling)
- New and used inventory counts (general ranges)
- Service department capabilities
- Financing and lease specialties
- Specific awards or rankings
- Community involvement

Avoid generic phrases like "providing exceptional customer service." AI engines extract distinctive content.

## Section 3: Service area

For franchise dealers, the service area is typically the metro you sell into. For internet-heavy dealers shipping nationwide, list the broader service area but be specific. Aspirational nationwide claims from a small dealer get filtered.

## Section 4: Services

List every distinct service:

- New vehicle sales
- Used vehicle sales
- Certified pre-owned
- Auto financing
- Auto leasing
- Service and maintenance
- Tire sales and rotation
- Oil changes
- Brake service
- Transmission service
- Auto body and collision

Each service gets 50 to 80 words of description. Service department services are particularly underused. Most dealers do not surface them.

## Section 5: Hours including service department

Dealerships often have sales hours and service department hours that differ. Use the "more hours" feature in GBP to clarify both.

Be accurate. Inaccurate hours produce poor reviews ("they were closed when Google said they were open") that hurt local pack visibility.

## Section 6: Photos

Auto dealer GBP photos drive both local pack visibility and showroom visit conversion. Upload:

- Showroom interior (multiple angles)
- Service drive and bays
- Inventory samples (representative new and used)
- Team photos including sales and service managers
- Exterior and signage
- Customer waiting areas
- Specialty equipment

Aim for 50 to 100 photos. Refresh with 5 to 15 new photos per month. User-generated photos from customers add powerful signals.

## Section 7: Posts

Three post types that work for auto dealers:

1. **New inventory arrivals** (specific models with photos)
2. **Lease and financing specials** (current month's offers)
3. **Service department promotions** (oil changes, tire deals, maintenance specials)

Cadence: 2 to 4 posts per week. Active posting profiles consistently outrank dormant ones in the local pack.

## Section 8: Q&A

Pre-empt common questions:

- Do you offer financing for buyers with bad credit?
- Do you accept trade-ins?
- Do you have [specific model] in stock?
- What is your service department's appointment availability?
- Do you offer test drives without an appointment?
- Do you sell pre-owned vehicles from other brands?

Seeded Q&A populates the GBP panel and feeds AI Overviews.

## Section 9: Attributes

Many auto dealer-relevant attributes get overlooked:

- Has financing options
- Has lease options
- Buys used cars
- Has service department
- Offers test drives
- Has wheelchair-accessible entrance
- Offers free Wi-Fi (waiting area)

Check every attribute that genuinely applies.

## Section 10: Multi-rooftop considerations

If you run a dealer group:

- One GBP per rooftop with that rooftop's address and phone
- Each rooftop has its own reviews aggregating to its profile
- Cross-link rooftops from a corporate domain rather than competing
- Each rooftop's photos and posts are unique to that rooftop

Combining multiple rooftops into a single GBP gets the profile suspended.

## What to do this week

A 7-day GBP rebuild per rooftop:

- **Day 1:** Audit categories, hours, and business description. Update.
- **Day 2:** Upload 30 new photos including service drive and team.
- **Day 3:** Build out services list with descriptions including service department.
- **Day 4:** Seed 10 to 15 Q&As.
- **Day 5:** Schedule first month of posts including new inventory and lease specials.
- **Day 6:** Verify hours and attributes.
- **Day 7:** Respond to last 30 reviews across Google.

## FAQs

### Can my dealership have a separate GBP for the service department?

Yes, if it operates as a distinct business (separate building, separate name, separate phone). For most dealerships, service is integrated with sales and shares one GBP using the "more hours" feature.

### What about used-only operations?

Same playbook but with "Used Car Dealer" as the primary category and brand-agnostic content.

### Should I respond to reviews about a salesperson who no longer works there?

Yes. Respond professionally without making the situation about the employee. "We appreciate the feedback and have noted the experience. Reach me directly at [phone] if you would like to discuss." The response signals active management even when the underlying situation has changed.

### How do I handle review filtering on Google?

Maintain steady review velocity, respond to all reviews, and report clearly fake reviews through the standard process. The aggregate impact of consistent management beats trying to remove individual reviews.
