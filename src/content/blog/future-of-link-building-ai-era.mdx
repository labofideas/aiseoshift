The short answer: links still matter. But the bar for what counts as a quality link has risen so sharply that most of what used to pass for a link building strategy is now either table stakes or actively harmful.

In 2020, a disciplined guest posting campaign, a solid directory submission list, and a few resource page outreach wins could move the needle meaningfully. In 2026, that same playbook produces noise at best and algorithmic penalties at worst. The reason is not just that Google's spam filters improved. It is that a new set of evaluators entered the room, AI systems that use link presence as one signal among dozens, and that weight editorial mentions in high-authority sources far above anything a scalable outreach operation can produce.

This post breaks down what changed, what still works, what stopped working, and how to build a link acquisition approach that compounds in the AI era rather than flattening out.

## The quick answer: links still matter, the bar just rose sharply

Backlinks remain the second-most important ranking factor in traditional search after content relevance. That fact has not changed. What changed is the quality threshold required for a link to produce a measurable ranking signal. According to [Ahrefs' link research](https://ahrefs.com/blog/link-building/), the correlation between high-authority editorial links and page-level ranking improvements has grown stronger as Google's spam detection improved, meaning the top of the link quality spectrum produces more lift than ever, while the bottom produces less.

For AI visibility, the picture is different again. AI systems do not pass authority through hyperlinks the way PageRank does. They evaluate sources based on training data exposure, entity recognition, and the quality of the publications where a brand or author appears. A backlink from a site that AI systems were never trained on, or that carries no editorial credibility, contributes almost nothing to AI citation likelihood. A mention, even without a hyperlink, in a publication that AI systems learned from extensively can influence whether those systems surface your brand in generated answers.

This distinction is the most important thing to understand about link building in 2026: backlinks and AI citations are related but separate goals, and the tactics that advance both are not the same as the tactics that advance only one.

## What changed: AI training data reshaped the authority map

When AI systems were trained on large corpora of web content, they developed implicit authority maps. Publications that appeared frequently, that were cited by other authoritative sources, and that demonstrated consistent editorial standards got weighted heavily in that training. The result is that the sites AI systems trust as sources are largely the same sites that have been considered high-authority in traditional SEO for years, but with the addition of a new class of specialized publications that traditional link building tools underweighted.

A link from Forbes, Search Engine Journal, or Harvard Business Review does something a directory link never could: it places your brand into the training ecosystem that AI systems draw on. When a journalist at one of those publications quotes your research or names your company as an example, that reference becomes part of the web that trained the next generation of AI models. Future AI systems will have encountered your brand in a credible context. That is a qualitatively different kind of authority than a dofollow link from a domain rating 40 niche site.

[Understanding what AI visibility actually means](/blog/what-is-ai-visibility/) is the prerequisite for understanding why training-data presence matters so much. The sites that appear in AI-generated answers are not always the ones with the most backlinks, they are the ones that appear in sources the AI was built to trust.

This also explains why the content that earns AI citations often comes from brands that have invested in earned media rather than outreach-driven link building. A single feature in a major publication does more for AI visibility than fifty guest posts on mid-tier blogs, even if those guest posts produce more raw backlink volume.

## Digital PR as the new link building: earned media over outreach spam

The shift from outreach-based link building to earned media acquisition is the single most consequential strategic change in modern SEO. Digital PR, the practice of creating content, research, or narratives that journalists and editors choose to cover, has replaced scalable outreach as the primary engine for acquiring high-authority links.

The economics are different from traditional link building. A well-executed digital PR campaign is expensive to produce but asymmetrically rewarding. A single original study published in the right context can generate editorial coverage across dozens of publications, produce backlinks from multiple high-authority domains simultaneously, and create a citation trail that AI systems absorb during training. The ROI on that kind of campaign, measured in both backlinks and brand authority, far exceeds what an equivalent budget spent on guest post outreach or link insertion campaigns would produce.

What makes a digital PR campaign work in 2026:

**Original data that journalists cannot find elsewhere.** Studies, surveys, and proprietary analyses that produce genuinely novel findings give journalists a reason to cover your brand rather than simply look up the same information from a primary source. The research needs to be rigorous enough to cite, sample sizes, methodology, and data transparency all matter because editors at high-authority publications evaluate those things before publishing.

**Expert perspectives on emerging topics.** When a new AI development, policy change, or industry shift creates demand for expert commentary, being available with a specific and well-reasoned point of view earns media placements that scalable outreach never produces. Speed matters here: the window for expert commentary is typically 48 to 72 hours after a news event before the story cycle moves on.

**Compelling visual assets and data stories.** High-authority publications embed visual content when it adds genuine value to their readers. An infographic that presents complex data clearly, or a visualization that makes a trend immediately legible, gets embedded with attribution links in editorial contexts that would never accept a pitched guest post.

The [comprehensive guide to link building strategies in 2026](/blog/link-building-strategies-2026/) covers the execution mechanics of digital PR campaigns in detail, including the specific outreach timing, pitch structure, and data requirements that produce editorial coverage.

## Entity co-citation: being mentioned alongside trusted entities

One of the least-discussed but most powerful link-building-adjacent tactics in 2026 is deliberately engineering entity co-citation. Entity co-citation is what happens when your brand, product, or author is mentioned in the same editorial context as other entities that AI systems recognize as authoritative and trustworthy.

When a publication describes your tool alongside Ahrefs and Semrush in a comparison article, the AI systems that indexed that article associate your entity with those established entities. When an author bio lists you alongside recognized industry figures, the same association forms. This co-citation mechanism is not the same as a hyperlink, it does not pass PageRank. But it does influence how AI systems classify and trust your brand when generating answers.

Practical ways to engineer beneficial entity co-citation:

**Contribute to roundup pieces alongside recognized experts.** When publications compile expert perspectives on a topic, being included positions your brand in editorial proximity to the other contributors. Choose roundups in high-authority publications over high-volume but low-authority ones.

**Pursue speaking slots and panel appearances that get written up.** Conference coverage in trade publications almost always mentions all speakers in the same article. That coverage creates entity co-citation at scale across multiple publications simultaneously.

**Collaborate with recognized industry voices on original content.** A co-authored study or jointly produced resource places your brand in association with an entity the AI systems already trust. The co-authorship does not have to be complex, a quoted contribution with clear attribution in the methodology section accomplishes the same associative effect.

Entity co-citation works alongside [E-E-A-T signals in the AI era](/blog/eeat-in-the-ai-era/) to build the kind of distributed authority that makes AI systems confident about citing your brand. Neither works as well without the other.

## The link types that still move the needle in 2026

Not all links declined in value equally. The ones that still produce measurable SEO and AI visibility improvements share several characteristics: editorial control by an independent third party, topical relevance to your core subject matter, and presence in publications that AI systems have substantive training exposure to.

**Editorial links from major publications.** A named link in Forbes, TechCrunch, Search Engine Journal, Wired, or comparable publications at the top of a vertical still produces significant ranking movement and meaningfully increases AI citation likelihood. These links are not producible at scale through outreach, they require media relationships, genuine news value, or original research worth covering.

**Links from topic cluster leaders.** Within any vertical, a handful of sites define the canonical resource landscape. In SEO, that includes publications like [Moz's blog](https://moz.com/blog) and Search Engine Land. In AI research, it includes academic publications and institutional sites. A link from one of these canonical sources carries disproportionate authority because the sites linking to them are also the sites AI systems learned from most heavily.

**Contextual links from long-form content on authoritative domains.** Links embedded in comprehensive, well-researched articles on established domains perform better than links in sidebars, author bylines, or resource lists. The surrounding content context signals relevance and editorial intentionality, the link exists because the author chose to reference your content, not because a plugin inserted it.

**Links from sources cited in AI training datasets.** This is an emerging area of research, but the pattern is consistent: sites that appear in well-known open training datasets (Common Crawl subsets indexed by reputable crawlers, Wikipedia citations, academic reference lists) carry higher AI visibility value than comparable sites that do not appear in those datasets. Getting into Wikipedia's reference list for a relevant topic is more valuable for AI visibility than a dozen outreach-built links.

For the technical side of how these signals interact with structured data, the post on [schema markup for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/) explains how structured data can amplify the authority signals that high-quality links establish.

## The link tactics that stopped working

Being direct about what stopped working is as important as understanding what still does. The following tactics produced results in 2020 and produced noise or penalties by 2026.

**Private blog networks (PBNs).** PBNs were the dominant grey-hat link building tactic for years. Controlled networks of sites with curated link profiles, they produced reliable ranking movement until Google's link spam updates began identifying them systematically. By 2026, PBN link profiles are a liability rather than an asset, the footprint detection has become sophisticated enough that sites with significant PBN link exposure see ranking suppression rather than improvement. AI systems, which do not pass authority through links at all, receive zero benefit from PBN links.

**Comment spam and forum link drops.** Automated comment links on blogs and forums produce nofollow links in most cases, and the domains hosting comment sections have largely been devalued to the point where even dofollow comment links contribute nothing measurable. The time cost of manual comment link building at quality exceeds any ranking benefit the approach could produce.

**Directory blasts and citation farms.** Mass submissions to web directories and citation aggregators were defensible when local SEO relied heavily on NAP consistency signals. For non-local content, directory links have been near-zero value for years. Even for local SEO, the value is now concentrated in a small number of authoritative directories, Google Business Profile, Yelp, and vertical-specific directories, rather than distributed across hundreds of generic submission sites.

**Scaled guest posting for links.** The guest posting grey area collapsed as Google's algorithms improved at identifying content published primarily for link value rather than editorial merit. Sites that accepted large volumes of guest posts saw their outbound link value reduced algorithmically. Sites that published at scale on other platforms saw diminishing returns on the links those posts produced. The tactic is not completely dead, a well-placed, high-quality guest post in a genuinely relevant publication still earns a useful link, but the economics of doing it at scale stopped working.

**Tiered link building.** The practice of building links to links, creating content specifically to pass authority to existing links, has become both detectable and counterproductive. The signals it creates are inconsistent with natural link acquisition patterns in ways that spam classifiers now identify reliably.

The [complete AI SEO audit checklist](/blog/ai-seo-audit-checklist/) includes a section on identifying and disavowing toxic link profiles, which is increasingly relevant for sites that have legacy link acquisition from these tactics.

## Building topical authority as the link-building multiplier

The single most underrated lever in modern link building is not a link tactic at all. It is topical authority, the degree to which a site is recognized as a comprehensive and reliable source on a specific subject matter. Topical authority acts as a multiplier on every link building activity because it determines how much authority each acquired link actually passes.

A site with strong topical authority in SEO that earns a link from Search Engine Journal receives more authority transfer than a general-purpose marketing blog that earns the same link. The reason is that Google and AI systems both evaluate link value in context, a link from a relevant authority source to a relevant authority destination passes more signal than the same link passed to a topically unrelated destination.

Building topical authority requires a deliberate content architecture: a core set of pillar pages covering the primary topics in your space at depth, surrounded by supporting content that covers adjacent subtopics and queries. The structure creates the depth of coverage that establishes topical recognition. Without that depth, individual link wins remain isolated gains rather than compounding assets.

For AI search specifically, topical authority and link authority converge in the same quality threshold. AI systems evaluate whether a source has consistent, comprehensive coverage of a topic before citing it for topic-related queries. The sites that answer the most questions about a topic, not just rank for them, but answer them thoroughly, are the ones that accumulate AI citations over time.

The relationship between topical authority and AI citation is detailed in the post on [how to get cited by AI search systems](/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-ai-search-systems/), which covers the content depth and structural requirements that make AI systems confident in citing a source repeatedly.

## What a modern link building strategy looks like

Pulling everything together, a link building strategy built for 2026 has four components operating simultaneously.

**Earned media acquisition through digital PR.** A consistent cadence of original research, expert commentary, and data-rich assets designed to produce editorial coverage in high-authority publications. One to two major campaigns per quarter, each designed to generate multiple editorial placements and associated brand mentions.

**Topical authority content development.** A systematic effort to achieve comprehensive coverage of your core topic area, creating the depth of content that makes individual link acquisitions compound rather than stay isolated. New content informed by gap analysis against competitors in AI search results, not just traditional SERP analysis.

**Entity co-citation engineering.** Active participation in the editorial contexts where your brand can appear alongside recognized entities, speaking opportunities, expert roundups, co-authored research, and industry publications that AI systems were trained on. The goal is not just links but the associative proximity to trusted entities that AI systems use to classify your brand.

**Legacy link hygiene.** An ongoing review of existing link profiles to identify and disavow toxic links from old-style tactics that now create liability rather than value. Sites with significant PBN or directory link exposure need to address that profile before new link acquisition can compound effectively.

The [full AI SEO shift framework](/ai-seo-shift/) provides the broader strategic context in which link building sits, including how authority signals integrate with content structure, schema markup, and E-E-A-T to determine AI citation likelihood.

Link building did not die in the AI era. It evolved. The quantity game was always a proxy for something else, authority, trust, editorial recognition. In 2026, you can pursue those things directly. The tactics that do that are harder than blasting directories, but they are also more durable, more compounding, and more aligned with the direction that both traditional search and AI search are moving.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do backlinks still matter for SEO in 2026?

Yes. Backlinks remain the second most important ranking factor in traditional search after content relevance. What changed is the quality threshold required for a backlink to produce measurable ranking movement. High-authority editorial links still move the needle significantly. Low-quality links from directories, PBNs, and comment spam contribute nothing and can create algorithmic liability.

### Do backlinks help with AI search visibility?

Not directly, but they correlate with it. AI systems do not pass authority through hyperlinks the way PageRank does. However, the same editorial contexts that produce high-quality backlinks, major publication coverage, expert roundups, original research citations, also produce the brand mentions and training-data presence that influence AI citation likelihood. Pursuing high-quality links and pursuing AI visibility are the same activity, even if the mechanism is different.

### What is entity co-citation and why does it matter?

Entity co-citation is when your brand is mentioned in the same editorial context as other entities that AI systems recognize as authoritative. When Search Engine Journal mentions your tool alongside Ahrefs and Semrush, AI systems trained on that content associate your entity with those established brands. This associative proximity influences how AI systems classify and trust your brand in generated answers, independent of whether any hyperlink exists.

### Is digital PR worth the investment for small teams?

It depends on execution capacity. A single well-executed digital PR campaign can produce editorial coverage that outperforms months of outreach-driven link building. The challenge is that original research and media relationship development require upfront investment in both time and budget. For small teams, a quarterly cadence of one data-led campaign, rather than continuous outreach at lower quality, is often more effective than trying to sustain high-volume guest posting.

### What link building tactics should I stop doing immediately?

Stop building PBN links, stop comment spam, and stop mass directory submissions. If you have historical exposure to these tactics, conduct a link audit and use Google's disavow tool to address the most toxic portions of your link profile. Scaled guest posting at mid-tier sites is not worth the effort but is not urgent to disavow, just stop investing in it and reallocate that budget toward digital PR.

### How long does it take for digital PR campaigns to affect AI citation?

Editorial coverage typically takes six to twelve weeks to influence AI citation behavior measurably. The timeline has two parts: the publication needs to be crawled and indexed by AI systems (usually two to four weeks for major publications), and then those systems need to incorporate the new training signal into their retrieval behavior (varies by platform, from weeks to months). Google AI Overviews tends to reflect recent high-authority coverage faster than other AI search systems.
