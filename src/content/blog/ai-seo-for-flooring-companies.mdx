Flooring is one of the most research-intensive home improvement purchases a homeowner makes. The average flooring project involves two to four weeks of research before any contractor gets a call. During that research period, the homeowner is asking ChatGPT to compare LVP and hardwood, asking Google for flooring cost estimates by material, asking Perplexity about the best flooring for pets, and reading reviews across multiple sources. The flooring company that appears as a cited source during that research phase enters the consideration set before it has spoken a single word to the customer. The companies that do not appear there are scrambling for attention later, when the homeowner already has three callbacks lined up. The [Google AI Overviews optimization hub](/ai-overviews-seo) explains how AI engines decide which businesses and sources to cite during exactly these research-phase queries.

This is the playbook for owning the research phase. Four pillars built for the flooring industry.

## Why the research phase is where flooring leads are won

A homeowner deciding between LVP and hardwood is not yet a lead. They are a prospect. The distinction matters because most flooring companies focus their marketing entirely on people ready to buy. They run Google Ads on "flooring installation [city]" and ignore the two weeks of research that precede that search.

AI engines have taken over the research phase. Google AI Overviews now answer "LVP vs hardwood flooring for dogs" with a synthesized comparison that cites specific sources. ChatGPT answers "how much does hardwood flooring installation cost" with a breakdown before the homeowner ever visits a website. Perplexity cites product guides and installation resources when homeowners research "best flooring for radiant heat."

The flooring company cited as a source in those research-phase answers is building familiarity before any competitor has made contact. When the homeowner is ready to call someone, the cited company is already in their mental model.

## Pillar 1: material comparison content

The highest-leverage content investment for flooring companies is material comparison content. These are the pages that AI engines cite when homeowners ask comparison questions, and the questions are specific enough to be ownable by a regional flooring company with real expertise.

The comparison pages to build:

| Comparison topic | Query type | Search intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LVP vs hardwood flooring | Direct comparison | Pre-purchase research |
| LVP vs tile for bathrooms | Room-specific | Pre-purchase research |
| Hardwood vs engineered hardwood | Material education | Pre-purchase research |
| Carpet vs LVP for bedrooms | Lifestyle-based | Pre-purchase research |
| Best flooring for pets | Lifestyle-based | Pre-purchase research |
| Best flooring for high-traffic areas | Use-case | Pre-purchase research |
| Best flooring for kitchens | Room-specific | Pre-purchase research |
| Flooring over radiant heat | Technical | High intent |

Each comparison page should be 1,200 to 2,000 words with a clear structure. Lead with the direct answer: which option wins for this specific use case and why. Then go deeper into the durability comparison, maintenance comparison, cost comparison, installation considerations, and which room types or lifestyles each is best suited for. Include specific numbers. Prices per square foot. Durability ratings. Warranty terms.

Pages with specific data are what AI engines cite. "LVP typically costs $2 to $7 per square foot for materials" is citable. "LVP is generally more affordable" is not.

## Pillar 2: cost guides as citation magnets

Flooring cost queries are among the highest-volume informational searches in the home improvement category. "How much does hardwood flooring installation cost" is asked tens of thousands of times per month nationally. At the local level, "hardwood floor installation cost [city]" and "LVP installation cost [city]" represent high-intent queries from homeowners who are close to calling.

The cost guide structure that earns citations:

Start with a direct cost range by material, then break down the components. Material cost per square foot. Labor cost per square foot. Subfloor preparation if needed. Old flooring removal and disposal. Room-by-room estimates for common room sizes.

A 12x15 living room hardwood installation cost estimate is more useful, and more citable, than a generic national cost range. AI engines are looking for specific answers to specific questions. A cost guide that says "a 180 square foot living room typically runs $1,800 to $2,700 for LVP including materials and labor in most markets" gives Perplexity something to cite. A cost guide that says "flooring costs vary by material" does not.

Include a cost comparison table across materials. Include the factors that change the cost: room size, existing subfloor condition, stair installation, furniture moving, old floor removal. This is the information homeowners need to understand their quote, and it is what positions you as the authoritative source when AI engines answer flooring cost questions.

## Pillar 3: service-specific pages for each flooring type

A homepage with a service list is not a content strategy. Each major flooring type you install needs its own dedicated service page with enough depth to rank and earn AI citations.

The service pages to build:

**Hardwood flooring installation:** cover wood species selection, solid vs engineered, nail-down vs glue-down vs floating, refinishing options, and what to expect during installation. Include your certifications with specific wood flooring products.

**LVP and luxury vinyl tile installation:** cover the quality spectrum (thickness, wear layer), waterproofing, underlayment, compatibility with radiant heat, and why LVP has become the dominant flooring choice for pet owners and families.

**Tile installation:** cover floor tile vs wall tile, grout selection, large format tile considerations, heated floor compatibility, and bathroom vs kitchen vs mudroom applications.

**Carpet installation and removal:** cover carpet types (cut pile, loop pile, berber), padding selection, allergen considerations, and carpet removal as a standalone service for homeowners replacing flooring with hard surfaces.

**Flooring over radiant heat:** this deserves its own page because it is a technical question homeowners with radiant heat systems ask specifically. Coverage here positions you as the expert for a subset of homeowners who will be loyal customers once they find a contractor who understands their situation.

Each page should be 1,000 to 1,500 words minimum. Author attribution matters: the content should be attributed to someone at the company with installation experience, not published anonymously.

## Pillar 4: Google Business Profile and local authority

GBP is the conversion surface where research-phase awareness becomes a phone call. The flooring company GBP needs to be built to handle the full range of flooring-related local searches.

The GBP requirements specific to flooring:

**Categories.** Primary category should be Flooring Store or Flooring Contractor depending on your model. Add secondary categories for each material specialty: Tile Store, Carpet Store, Hardwood Floor Refinishing Service.

**Services.** List every service with brief descriptions. AI engines and Google use GBP service data to match businesses to specific service queries. "Hardwood flooring installation" and "LVP installation" as distinct listed services, not bundled under "flooring installation," creates more specific citation eligibility.

**Photos.** This category matters more for flooring than most home services because visual selection is part of the purchase process. Build a photo library of completed projects organized by flooring type. Before-and-after transformations are particularly effective. Show a range of home styles and price points.

**Reviews mentioning specific flooring types.** A review that says "they installed our new LVP throughout the entire first floor" helps your citation eligibility for LVP-specific local queries. The [Google Business Profile posts guide](/blog/google-business-profile-posts-guide) covers how to use GBP posts alongside your review strategy to maintain consistent freshness signals between major project completions. A review that says "great work" does not. This is not about manufacturing reviews. It is about timing your review requests to capture the specific project details while they are fresh. Ask for a review within 48 hours of project completion and include the flooring type in your request message: "If you are happy with the LVP installation, would you share your experience in a Google review?"

## E-E-A-T for flooring: certifications that matter

AI engines apply trust signals to flooring content the same way they apply them to any home improvement service. The signals that differentiate a flooring company from a generic contractor listing:

**Manufacturer certifications.** Pergo certified installer status, Shaw Flooring certified installation, Mohawk FloorScore certification. These are named credentials that AI engines can verify and that homeowners recognize. List them explicitly on your website with the certification body name and what it means.

**National Wood Flooring Association membership.** NWFA membership and certifications are recognized authority signals for hardwood flooring content specifically.

**Years in business.** A flooring company with 15 years of installation history carries more authority signal than a company that launched last year. State this explicitly: "Family-owned flooring contractor with 18 years serving [metro area]."

**Warranty information.** Publish your workmanship warranty explicitly. A company that offers a 5-year installation warranty and says so on their website is signaling quality confidence that a company without that information cannot match.

**Schema markup.** Use LocalBusiness schema with your flooring contractor categories, HomeAndConstructionBusiness type, certifications listed in the credentialAwarded field, and aggregateRating from your review platforms. The [schema markup implementation guide](/blog/how-to-implement-schema-markup) covers the exact property structure for flooring and home improvement businesses. Adding [review schema](/blog/review-schema-for-ai-search) alongside your LocalBusiness type surfaces your star rating in AI-generated answers, which is particularly important for consideration-stage queries where trust signals drive the final call decision.

## FAQs for flooring queries

A FAQ section built around the questions homeowners actually ask gives AI engines directly extractable answers.

The questions worth structuring as explicit FAQs:

How long does hardwood floor installation take? (Answer: 1 to 3 days for most rooms, plus 24 hours of cure time before heavy foot traffic.)

Can LVP be installed over existing flooring? (Answer: yes, over clean, flat surfaces. Height increase may affect door clearance.)

What is the difference between LVP and laminate? (Answer: specific, technical, and a question asked constantly.)

Does flooring installation require moving furniture? (Answer: yes, standard expectation, and many contractors include it.)

How much does it cost to remove old flooring before installation? (Answer: give a specific per-square-foot range.)

Each FAQ answer should be 80 to 150 words and written in plain language. The goal is extractability. An AI engine should be able to pull the question and answer as a coherent unit and cite your page as the source.

Month 2: Three service-specific pages (hardwood installation, LVP installation, tile installation). Review velocity system live with flooring-type prompting. FAQ section live on main site.

Month 3: Remaining service pages (carpet, radiant heat flooring). Cost guide with room-by-room estimates. Bing Webmaster Tools verification and sitemap submission for ChatGPT and Perplexity coverage.

Twelve months in: the flooring company with this infrastructure is cited in AI answers for material comparison queries, cost queries, and local installation queries.
