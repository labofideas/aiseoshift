Google confirmed the May 2026 core update on May 21 at 8:43 a.m. Pacific Time. The rollout is expected to take up to two weeks. As I write this, we are five days in and the SERPs are in active flux.

This is Google's second broad core algorithm update of 2026, following the March update that was the most volatile in recent memory. If the early signals are any indication, May is going to be just as disruptive.

Here is what we know so far, what the data is showing, and what you should and should not do while the update is still rolling out.

## What this update is

A broad core algorithm update is Google's way of making significant changes to its overall ranking systems. Unlike targeted updates that address specific issues (like spam or product reviews), core updates reevaluate how Google assesses content quality, relevance, and authority across all types of queries.

Google does not publish the specific changes in core updates. What they have said: the May 2026 update continues the trajectory established since the Helpful Content Update was merged into core systems in late 2024. Every core update since then has tightened the same criteria. Content must demonstrate real expertise. It must serve actual reader intent. It must come from sources that Google's systems can evaluate as credible.

The May update builds directly on that foundation. No indication of a new evaluation framework. Just a sharper application of the existing one.

## The Google Dance is back

The term "Google Dance" refers to the wild ranking fluctuations that happen during and immediately after a core update, before results stabilize. It was a common phenomenon in early Google and had largely subsided in recent years as updates became more controlled.

The March 2026 update brought it back with force. The May update is doing the same.

The volatility numbers from March paint the picture. Only 20.5 percent of the top three results maintained their exact position during the March rollout. That is a massive drop from 33.1 percent in December 2025. Approximately 24.1 percent of pages that previously ranked in the top 10 fell completely out of the top 100, nearly double the 14.7 percent displacement rate from December.

We are seeing similar patterns in the May rollout. Health, finance, and legal verticals are showing the sharpest movement in the first 72 hours, consistent with every core update since March 2024. These are the [YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)](/seo/eeat/) categories where Google applies the strictest quality evaluation.

If your rankings are fluctuating wildly right now, that is expected behavior during a core update rollout. It does not necessarily mean you have been penalized. It means the algorithm is recalibrating.

## Who is winning

The early winners follow a consistent pattern across the March and May updates.

**Specialist and niche sites with deep topical coverage.** Sites that cover one subject comprehensively, with [topic clusters](/seo/content-strategy/), original insights, and clear expertise signals, are being rewarded. This is the topical authority signal in action. Google is not just evaluating individual pages. It is evaluating whether a site is a genuine authority on its core topics.

**Institutional and established brands.** Official sources, universities, government sites, and recognized industry publications are gaining visibility. This aligns with the E-E-A-T framework. Google's systems assign higher trust to sources with verifiable institutional credibility.

**Sites cited in AI Overviews.** This is the new dynamic that previous core updates did not involve. Brands that appear in Google's AI Overviews are reporting higher volumes of both organic and paid clicks compared to those that do not. Being included in the AI summary is now worth more than holding position three in the traditional listings. The AI Overview functions as a trust endorsement that lifts click-through rates on the organic results below it.

**Content with genuine first-hand experience.** The "Experience" component of [E-E-A-T](/seo/eeat/) is being weighted more heavily. Content that demonstrates the author actually did, used, tested, or experienced what they are writing about is outperforming content that summarizes information from other sources.

## Who is losing

The patterns here are equally consistent and increasingly severe.

**Aggregators and thin comparison sites.** Information aggregators that compile content from other sources without contributing original value are getting crushed. Job portals, travel aggregation services, generic dictionaries, and comparison portals that repackage information from stronger sources are seeing significant reach drops.

This has been the trend since 2024, but each update intensifies it. Google can now evaluate whether a page adds original value or merely reorganizes existing information. The bar for "original value" keeps rising.

**Generic affiliate content.** Affiliate sites that produce reviews without genuine product testing, comparison tables without original evaluation, and "best of" lists that read like they were assembled from other "best of" lists continue to lose visibility. The sites that survive in affiliate SEO are the ones with verifiable testing methodology, original photography, and author expertise you can trace to real qualifications.

**AI-generated content at scale.** Sites that published large volumes of AI-generated content without human expertise, fact-checking, or original insights are losing ground. This is not because Google detects AI content specifically. It is because [AI-generated content at scale](/blog/will-google-penalize-ai-content/) tends to be generic, thin, and lacking the E-E-A-T signals that core updates reward. The content fails the quality test, regardless of how it was produced.

**Over-optimized SEO content.** Pages that read like they were written for search engines rather than humans, with keyword-stuffed headings, unnaturally structured prose, and obvious optimization patterns, are being devalued. Google's language models can now identify content that prioritizes algorithmic signals over genuine usefulness.

## The AI Overviews connection

This is the most significant development in core update dynamics. The May 2026 update is not happening in isolation from AI Overviews. The two systems are increasingly intertwined.

Google's AI Overviews now appear on roughly half of all US searches. The sources that AI Overviews cite are drawn from the same quality evaluation that the core algorithm performs. When a core update reevaluates content quality, the effects cascade through AI Overviews simultaneously.

What this means in practice: a page that loses traditional ranking in the core update may also disappear from AI Overview citations. Conversely, a page that gains authority in the core update may begin appearing in AI Overviews for the first time.

The implication for SEO strategy is significant. You can no longer treat core update recovery and [AI visibility optimization](/seo/ai-search-optimization/) as separate efforts. They are driven by the same signals. Content quality, E-E-A-T, topical authority, structured data, and user experience all feed into both the traditional algorithm and the AI citation system.

## What to do right now

I want to be careful here because the update is still rolling out. Premature action based on mid-rollout data often causes more harm than good. Here is the measured approach.

### During the rollout (now through early June)

**Do not make major changes to your site.** Seriously. Wait until Google confirms the rollout is complete and then allow at least one additional week of stabilization. Mid-rollout ranking fluctuations are not reliable indicators of the update's final impact. I have seen sites drop 30 percent during a rollout and recover to their original positions once it stabilized.

**Monitor but do not react.** Track your rankings, traffic, and AI Overview appearances. Document what is changing. But resist the urge to start rewriting pages, disavowing links, or restructuring your site based on data that is still in flux.

**Check Google Search Console for manual actions.** Core updates are algorithmic, not manual. But if you notice a sudden, dramatic drop that coincides with a manual action notification in Search Console, that is a separate issue that should be addressed immediately.

### After the rollout stabilizes (mid-June)

**Identify which pages lost and which gained.** Look at page-level data, not just domain-level traffic. Some pages may have improved while others declined. The pattern of gains and losses tells you what the update valued and what it devalued.

**Compare against the quality criteria.** For pages that lost visibility, ask: Does this content demonstrate genuine expertise? Does it add original value? Is the author credible and verifiable? Is the content structured for both humans and machines? Does it serve real user intent or was it created primarily to capture keyword traffic?

**Prioritize E-E-A-T improvements.** Add named authors with verifiable credentials. Include original data, examples, and first-hand experience. Cite primary sources. Update outdated statistics. Remove thin sections that do not add value.

**Strengthen topical authority.** If competing pages with deeper topic coverage outrank yours, the signal is clear. Build out your [topic clusters](/seo/content-strategy/). Add supporting content that demonstrates comprehensive understanding. Connect everything with internal links.

**Improve content structure for AI extraction.** Add [schema markup](/seo/schema-markup/). Use clear heading hierarchy. Start sections with direct answers. Make your content easy for both traditional algorithms and AI systems to parse and cite.

## What not to do

**Do not panic-publish new content.** Flooding your site with new pages during or immediately after a core update does not help and can hurt if the content quality does not meet the elevated bar.

**Do not disavow your entire backlink profile.** Core updates evaluate content quality, not link spam specifically. Mass disavowing backlinks in response to a core update is almost always unnecessary and counterproductive.

**Do not remove pages that lost rankings.** A page that lost visibility may still serve users and may recover as the update stabilizes or in future updates. Removing content eliminates the possibility of recovery.

**Do not pay for "core update recovery" services.** No one can guarantee recovery from a core update because no one knows the specific algorithm changes. The recovery path is always the same: improve content quality, strengthen E-E-A-T signals, and build genuine topical authority.

## The bigger picture

Google is releasing core updates more frequently and they are more volatile than ever. The March and May 2026 updates, barely two months apart, suggest a cadence of roughly quarterly major updates with increasing impact.

Each update tightens the same criteria. The [AI SEO shift](/ai-seo-shift/) is not separate from core updates. It is the context in which they operate. Google is rebuilding its ranking systems around the same quality signals that AI Overviews use to select citation sources. The websites that build genuine authority, demonstrate real expertise, and create content that serves users rather than algorithms are the ones that will improve with each update rather than fearing the next one.

The Google Dance is back. Let it dance. The music it is dancing to rewards the same thing it has always rewarded, just more precisely than before: genuinely useful content from genuinely credible sources.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Google May 2026 core update?

The May 2026 core update is Google's second broad core algorithm update of 2026, launched on May 21. It reevaluates how Google assesses content quality, relevance, and authority across all query types. The rollout takes approximately two weeks. It continues the trajectory of rewarding genuine expertise, user-focused content, and strong E-E-A-T signals.

### Should I change my website during the core update rollout?

No. Wait until Google confirms the rollout is complete and allow at least one additional week of stabilization. Mid-rollout ranking fluctuations are temporary and do not represent the update's final impact. Acting on incomplete data often leads to changes that address temporary fluctuations rather than lasting shifts.

### Why did my rankings drop in the May 2026 update?

Common reasons include thin content that lacks original value, missing E-E-A-T signals (no named authors, no credentials, no cited sources), content created primarily for search engines rather than users, and aggregated content that does not add original analysis. Review your affected pages against these quality criteria after the rollout stabilizes.

### How long does the May 2026 core update take?

Google stated the rollout will take up to two weeks from the May 21 launch date, placing the expected completion around early June 2026. Allow an additional week after completion for rankings to stabilize before analyzing the full impact.

### Does the core update affect AI Overviews?

Yes. The core update and AI Overviews share underlying quality signals. Pages that gain authority in the core update may begin appearing in AI Overview citations. Pages that lose authority may disappear from AI Overviews simultaneously. Optimizing for one system now improves performance in both.

### How do I recover from a Google core update?

Focus on content quality improvements: add genuine expertise and first-hand experience, strengthen author credentials and E-E-A-T signals, build topical authority through comprehensive topic clusters, add schema markup for better AI extraction, and update outdated content with current data. Recovery typically takes one to three update cycles (3 to 9 months).
