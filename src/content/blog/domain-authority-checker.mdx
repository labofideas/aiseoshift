The best domain authority checkers in 2026 are Moz (the original Domain Authority metric), Ahrefs (Domain Rating), Semrush (Authority Score), and Majestic (Trust Flow and Citation Flow), with free lookups available from all of them plus SE Ranking and SEO Review Tools. A domain authority checker estimates how likely a website is to rank, on a 0 to 100 scale, based mainly on the size and quality of its backlink profile. The one thing to understand before you use any of them: these are third-party scores invented by SEO companies, not a metric Google publishes or uses. Treat them as a useful comparative shortcut, never as your actual goal.

Domain authority is a compass, not a destination. It points roughly the right way. It is not the place you are trying to reach.

## What a domain authority checker actually measures

Get this straight first, because it prevents the most common mistake.

Domain authority is a predictive score, on a 0 to 100 scale, that estimates how well a website could rank based mostly on its backlink profile: how many other sites link to it, and how trustworthy those linking sites are. A higher score means the tool thinks the site has more ranking power. It is a relative, comparative measure, not an absolute one.

The critical caveat: Google does not have or use a "domain authority" metric. Google's representatives have said repeatedly that there is no single site-wide authority score in their ranking systems. Domain Authority, Domain Rating, and Authority Score are all inventions of SEO companies, each built from their own backlink index and their own formula. They are useful because backlinks genuinely matter and these scores correlate with ranking ability, but they are estimates of a thing Google keeps private, not a readout of Google's actual opinion.

That is why the scores never agree. Moz's DA, Ahrefs' DR, and Semrush's Authority Score are computed from different link indexes with different math, so the same site scores differently in each. None is "right." They are different instruments measuring roughly the same thing.

### Moz: the original Domain Authority

Moz created the Domain Authority metric, which is why "DA" is often used generically to mean site authority at all. Moz's free Domain Authority Checker returns DA, Page Authority (the same idea for a single page), and the number of linking root domains for any URL. A free Moz Community account unlocks a set number of free lookups a month, which is enough for periodic checks.

DA is the number most people mean when they ask for your "domain authority," so if you only track one, this is the common currency. It is best for benchmarking against competitors on the metric everyone recognizes, and for the link-prospecting habit of checking a potential linking site's DA before pursuing it.

### Ahrefs: Domain Rating and the best link data

Ahrefs uses its own metric, Domain Rating, based on the strength of a site's backlink profile, and its free checker returns DR, referring domains, and total backlinks with no account required for basic lookups. Ahrefs is widely regarded as having one of the largest and freshest backlink indexes, so its DR and its underlying link data are trusted by many practitioners.

DR is best when you care about the raw link-strength picture and want reliable backlink counts alongside the score. If you are doing serious link building, Ahrefs' data quality is the draw, and its [link-building tooling](/blog/best-ai-link-building-tools/) extends from there.

### Semrush: Authority Score with more than links

Semrush's Authority Score is deliberately broader than a pure link metric. It combines backlink data with organic-traffic data and spam signals, so it tries to reflect not just how many links a site has but whether it is a real, trusted, trafficked site rather than one propped up by manipulative links. That extra input makes it a useful sanity check against link-only scores.

It is best for teams that want one number that already accounts for traffic and spam, and who may already run Semrush for the rest of their SEO. Free lookups are limited, with full access inside the suite.

### Majestic: Trust Flow versus Citation Flow

Majestic takes a different, and genuinely useful, angle by splitting authority into two numbers. Citation Flow measures the quantity of links pointing at a site, while Trust Flow measures their quality, based on proximity to a seed set of trusted sites. Seen together, they expose something a single score hides: a site with high Citation Flow but low Trust Flow has lots of low-quality links, a classic spam signature.

Majestic is best when you want to judge link quality, not just count links, which matters most when you are evaluating a site's backlink profile for risk or vetting a link source.

### SE Ranking and SEO Review Tools: quick free checks

For fast, free lookups without a heavy tool, SE Ranking offers a free website authority checker with clean data and reports, and SEO Review Tools provides a free DA and page-authority checker that handles quick, no-frills checks. Neither replaces a full suite, but both are handy when you just need a number for a site or two. The wider tool landscape is in [best AI SEO tools](/blog/best-ai-seo-tools/) and the [AI visibility tool stack](/blog/ai-seo-tools-visibility-optimization/).

## How to read a domain authority score without fooling yourself

The score is useful in exactly four ways, and dangerous in a few others.

Use it to compare. Domain authority shines as a relative measure. Your site at DA 40 against a competitor at DA 55 tells you they have more link strength, which is a real, actionable gap. Checking a prospective linking site's authority before you pursue it saves wasted outreach.

Use it to track your own trend. Watched over months on a single tool, a rising score is a reasonable sign your authority-building is working, and a falling one is a prompt to investigate. The trend matters more than the absolute number.

Use it to judge link quality, especially with Majestic's split. High quantity and low quality is a warning sign, whether you are auditing your own profile or vetting someone else's.

Now the traps. Do not compare scores across tools; DA 50 and DR 50 are not the same thing. Do not treat the score as something Google sees; it does not. Do not chase the number for its own sake, because you can inflate a link-based score with low-quality links while your actual rankings go nowhere or backward. And do not expect it to predict a specific ranking; it estimates general potential, not the outcome of any one query.

The honest summary: domain authority is a useful proxy that becomes a vanity metric the moment you make it the goal. The thing you are actually building is covered in [SEO is not about rankings, it is about authority](/blog/seo-authority-not-rankings/), where real authority means being trusted and cited, not scoring high on a third-party tool.

## Does domain authority still matter in the AI era?

It is a fair question, because AI answer engines are changing what visibility means. The honest answer is that the score matters less as a headline metric and the thing underneath it matters more.

Backlinks and site authority still feed AI citations. When ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI surfaces choose which sources to quote, they weigh trust signals, and a site's backlink profile is part of what makes it look trustworthy. One large 2026 study found that domain traffic was the strongest single predictor of AI citations, with high-traffic sites earning roughly three times more citations than low-traffic ones. Authority, real authority, is not becoming irrelevant. It is becoming the price of entry for AI visibility too.

What is fading is the usefulness of the number as a goal. In a world where being cited by an AI engine can matter as much as ranking a blue link, chasing a Moz DA of 60 is even more clearly a proxy for the real target. The real target is being the kind of trusted, well-linked, genuinely authoritative source that both Google and AI engines reach for. A domain authority checker is still a fine way to benchmark that, as long as you remember it is measuring the shadow, not the object. The shift itself is covered in [how AI is changing SEO](/blog/how-is-ai-changing-seo-2026/).

## How to use a DA checker for link prospecting

The single most practical use of a domain authority checker is vetting potential link sources before you spend time chasing them. Here is the workflow.

Check the authority before you pursue. When you find a site you might earn a link from, look up its authority score first. A relevant site with reasonable authority is worth outreach; a site with a very low score, or one whose authority looks inflated by spam, usually is not. This saves hours of outreach that would have earned links worth little.

Check quality, not just the headline number. This is where Majestic's Trust Flow versus Citation Flow earns its place. A prospective link source with high Citation Flow but low Trust Flow has lots of low-quality links, which is a sign its own authority is shaky and its link may carry risk rather than value. A quick two-number check filters those out.

Prioritize relevance over raw score. A link from a lower-authority site that is tightly relevant to your topic often beats a link from a higher-authority site with no topical connection. Use the score to filter out the genuinely weak sources, then let relevance drive which of the remaining prospects you pursue. The full method is in [how to get more backlinks](/blog/how-to-get-more-backlinks/).

Build a prospect list, not a one-off check. If you are doing this at any scale, the bulk-check features in tools like SEO Review Tools or a full suite let you score a whole list of prospects at once, so you can sort by authority and relevance and work the best first.

## How to actually raise your domain authority

Since the score is driven mostly by backlinks, raising it honestly means earning better links and building genuine authority, not gaming a number.

Earn editorial links from relevant, trusted sites. A handful of links from authoritative sites in your field move a score, and your rankings, far more than hundreds of low-quality links. The methods that still work are in [how to get more backlinks](/blog/how-to-get-more-backlinks/), and the strategic view is in [link building in 2026](/blog/link-building-strategies-2026/).

Publish things worth linking to. Original data, useful tools, and genuinely strong reference content attract links on their own, which is the only link-building that scales. This is the content side of authority.

Prune and consolidate weak links and pages. A profile full of spammy links or a site full of thin pages drags on both your score and your rankings. Cleaning up can help.

Be patient. Authority compounds slowly. A score does not jump in a week, and any tool promising to spike your DA fast is selling manipulated links that carry real risk. The durable version of this work, building the authority AI engines and search both reward, is in [AI citation building](/seo/ai-citation-building/).

## The bottom line

Use Moz for the industry-standard DA number, Ahrefs for the best link data behind DR, Semrush for a score that blends traffic and spam signals, and Majestic when you need to separate link quality from quantity. All offer free lookups. Pick one tool, track your own trend on it, and use it to compare against competitors and vet link sources.

Then remember what it is: a third-party estimate of a thing Google keeps private. A helpful compass, and a terrible goal.

## FAQs

### What is the best free domain authority checker?

Moz's free Domain Authority Checker is the standard, returning DA, Page Authority, and linking domains with a set number of free lookups on a free account. Ahrefs offers a free DR checker with no account for basic lookups, and Semrush, SE Ranking, and SEO Review Tools all provide free checks. Pick one and track it consistently rather than comparing across tools.

### Is domain authority a Google ranking factor?

No. Google does not have or use a domain authority metric, and its representatives have said so repeatedly. Domain Authority, Domain Rating, and Authority Score are third-party scores invented by SEO companies from their own backlink data. They are useful because backlinks matter and the scores correlate with ranking ability, but they are estimates, not something Google reads.

### Why do different tools show different authority scores?

Because each tool computes its score from its own backlink index with its own formula. Moz's Domain Authority, Ahrefs' Domain Rating, and Semrush's Authority Score use different data and math, so the same site scores differently in each. None is more correct; they are different instruments. Compare a site only against others measured with the same tool.

### What is a good domain authority score?

There is no universal threshold, because the score only means something relative to your competitors. A DA that beats the other sites ranking for your target queries is "good" for you, whether that is 30 in a low-competition niche or 70 in a competitive one. Focus on out-scoring your actual competition and on your own trend over time, not on hitting an arbitrary number.

### How can I increase my domain authority?

Earn editorial backlinks from relevant, trusted sites, publish content and tools worth linking to, and clean up spammy links and thin pages. Authority is driven mostly by your backlink profile, so better links raise it, while low-quality links can inflate the number without helping rankings or even hurting them. It compounds slowly, so be patient and avoid anything promising a fast spike.
