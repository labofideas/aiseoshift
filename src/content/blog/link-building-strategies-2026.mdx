I want to be specific about what this post is and what it is not.

This is not a list of link building tactics I read about in someone else's guide. This is a report on what has actually produced backlinks and measurable ranking improvements between March and May 2026. Three months. Real campaigns. Real results.

Some of what worked will surprise you. Some of what stopped working will make you uncomfortable if it was part of your strategy. And the biggest shift, the one most people have not fully processed yet, is that link building in 2026 is no longer just about backlinks. It is about the entire authority ecosystem, including signals that do not involve a clickable link at all.

Let me walk through what happened.

## The biggest shift: brand mentions now outperform backlinks for AI visibility

This is the data point that rewired how I think about link building.

Brand mentions correlate 3x more strongly with AI search visibility than traditional backlinks. The numbers from recent studies: 0.664 correlation for brand mentions versus 0.218 for backlinks. And 66 percent of SEO practitioners now track AI-generated citations as a primary KPI, a metric that did not exist a year ago.

Sit with that for a minute. The entire discipline of link building was built on the premise that clickable links from other sites are the primary authority signal. That premise is still true for traditional Google rankings. But for AI visibility, which is where a growing share of discovery happens, unlinked brand mentions carry more weight.

This does not mean backlinks stopped mattering. Google's traditional algorithm still treats backlinks as the second most important ranking factor after content relevance. A dofollow editorial link from Forbes still moves the needle. But if your link building strategy only produces backlinks and ignores brand mentions, you are optimizing for a shrinking share of total visibility.

The campaigns that worked best over the last three months produced both. A single well-placed digital PR piece generated a backlink AND a brand mention AND, in several cases, an AI citation. Three authority signals from one action.

## Digital PR with original data: the strategy that dominated

48.6 percent of SEO professionals now rank digital PR as the single most effective method for acquiring authoritative backlinks. That number is up from roughly 30 percent two years ago. The reason is straightforward: everything else got harder or stopped working, while digital PR kept compounding.

Here is what worked in the last 90 days, specifically.

**Publishing original research that journalists need.** When your brand produces the only publicly available data on a topic, every article written about that topic has to reference you. This is not a new idea, but the execution matters more than ever. The research needs to be genuinely novel, not a repackaging of publicly available statistics with a new chart.

One campaign I tracked published a study analyzing how AI Overviews affected organic CTR across 50,000 keywords. The study was cited by Search Engine Journal, Search Engine Land, and several industry newsletters within two weeks. That is three editorial backlinks from high-authority domains, plus dozens of brand mentions in social media discussions, plus AI systems picking up the data for future responses.

The ROI on that single study exceeded everything else the team did that quarter combined. Digital PR campaigns now generate an average ROI of 312 percent, significantly outpacing traditional link building.

**Expert commentary on breaking news.** When a major AI or search industry development hits, journalists need expert quotes fast. Being available with a specific, quotable perspective within hours of a news event produces editorial links from publications that would never respond to a cold outreach email.

The WordPress 7.0 release, Google I/O announcements, and Anthropic's Code with Claude event each created windows where expert commentary earned placements in major publications. The key is speed and specificity. A generic "this is interesting" quote gets ignored. A specific "this means X for businesses because Y" quote gets published.

**Data-driven infographics and visual assets.** Journalists and bloggers need visuals for their articles. If you produce a clean, data-accurate infographic on a trending topic, publications will embed it with a link. The production quality matters. A mediocre Canva graphic gets ignored. A polished, data-rich visual that tells a story gets shared.

## Content-led link acquisition: what actually earned links

Not every team has the resources for digital PR campaigns. The good news is that certain types of content consistently earn links without outreach, if the content is genuinely the best resource available on its topic.

**Comprehensive guides that become the default reference.** This worked repeatedly over the last three months. A 12-chapter [SEO guide](/seo/) that covers every dimension of modern SEO. A definitive comparison of [AEO and GEO tools](/blog/best-aeo-geo-tools-2026/). A pillar page that answers a core question more thoroughly than anything else available.

These resources earn links because other writers reference them when they need to link to "the complete guide on X." The link happens naturally because the content deserves to be cited. No outreach required.

The pattern: identify a topic where no definitive reference exists. Create one that is so comprehensive and well-structured that it becomes the default. Then maintain it. Content that gets updated earns more links over time than content that goes stale.

**Original tools and calculators.** Free tools earn links at a rate that content alone cannot match. An [SEO checker](/ai-seo-tools/) that anyone can use without signup generates ongoing link acquisition because people reference the tool when recommending resources in forums, blog posts, and social threads.

The investment in building a tool is higher than writing an article. But the link acquisition curve is fundamentally different. Articles earn most of their links in the first few weeks. Tools earn links continuously for years.

**Curated industry data and benchmarks.** Round-up posts that aggregate data from multiple sources with proper attribution earn links from the original sources and from anyone referencing the aggregated data. The key is adding analysis, not just compiling numbers. Anyone can list statistics. The value is in what the statistics mean.

## Broken link building and mention reclamation: still working

These are the tactical approaches that consistently produce results with reasonable effort.

**Unlinked mention reclamation.** Your brand is being mentioned online without links more often than you think. Tools like [Semrush](https://semrush.sjv.io/PyEbGz) and Ahrefs identify these mentions. A polite email saying "thanks for mentioning us, would you mind adding a link?" converts at 15 to 25 percent in my experience. That is a high conversion rate for any outreach campaign.

This worked consistently over the last three months. The mentions are real. The writers already know your brand. The ask is small. The link quality is high because the mention was editorial.

**Broken link building.** Finding broken links on relevant pages and offering your content as a replacement still works but requires more effort than it did five years ago. The sites with the most broken links tend to be older, less maintained sites. The links you replace need to be genuinely relevant, not tangentially related.

The campaigns that worked were highly targeted. Finding specific broken links on high-authority resource pages, creating content that genuinely matches or improves on the dead resource, and sending a concise email that makes the fix easy for the webmaster.

**Competitor backlink analysis.** If a domain links to three or four competitors in your space, they are clearly an active linker in your niche. That makes them a warm prospect. The approach: identify these domains using backlink analysis tools, create content that is genuinely better than what they currently link to, and reach out with a specific value proposition.

## Strategic guest posting: the version that still works

I need to be precise about this because "guest posting" means different things to different people.

Guest posting on real publications with engaged readerships, editorial standards, and genuine authority still builds links and brand visibility. Writing for Search Engine Journal, industry-specific publications, niche blogs with real traffic, and professional platforms like LinkedIn articles. These require effort. Pitching real angles. Writing quality content. Passing editorial review.

Guest posting on link farms, private blog networks, low-quality "write for us" sites, and publications that accept anything submitted. This does not work. Google's AI-powered spam detection has gotten sophisticated enough to identify and devalue these links systematically.

The dividing line is simple: would a real human in your target audience read this publication? If yes, the link has value. If the publication only exists because people need somewhere to place guest posts, the link is worthless or harmful.

## What stopped working

I want to be direct about this because some of these tactics are still being sold by agencies.

**Bulk directory submissions.** Devalued to near zero. Some industry-specific directories still have marginal value, but the mass submission approach is dead.

**PBN (Private Blog Network) links.** Google's detection of PBN patterns has improved to the point where the risk-reward ratio is unacceptable. I have seen sites lose significant rankings after PBN link patterns were detected.

**Mass guest posting for volume.** Publishing 20 guest posts per month on low-quality sites does not build authority. It builds a toxic backlink profile. Quality of the linking domain matters more than ever.

**Paid links without editorial value.** Google's spam team has stated explicitly that they can identify most paid link schemes. Sponsored content that provides genuine value to readers is fine. Paying for a link in someone else's article purely for the link is not.

**Comment spam, forum profile links, and social bookmark links.** These never worked well and they do not work at all now.

## The AI citation strategy: link building's new frontier

Here is where link building connects to the [AI SEO shift](/ai-seo-shift/) that is reshaping everything else.

[AI systems decide what to cite](/seo/ai-citation-building/) based on different signals than Google's traditional link-based algorithm. When ChatGPT or Perplexity cites your content, it is not because you have backlinks. It is because your content is structured clearly, factually precise, topically authoritative, and accessible to AI crawlers.

The link building strategies that produce AI citations in addition to traditional backlinks are the ones delivering the highest total return right now. Digital PR coverage gets indexed by AI systems. Original research gets incorporated into AI knowledge bases. Brand mentions across Reddit, LinkedIn, and YouTube create the entity signals that AI systems use to evaluate authority.

This is why the [omnichannel presence strategy](/blog/omnichannel-ai-brand-presence/) matters for link building. Your LinkedIn posts, Reddit comments, YouTube videos, and podcast appearances are not just brand building. They are creating the citation and mention ecosystem that both Google and AI systems evaluate when deciding whether to trust your content.

## The 90-day playbook

If I were starting a link building campaign today based on what worked in the last three months, here is what I would prioritize.

First 30 days: publish one piece of original research or a comprehensive resource that is genuinely the best available on its topic. Identify and reclaim 20 to 30 unlinked brand mentions. Set up monitoring for new mentions.

Days 30 to 60: pitch the original research to 10 to 15 relevant journalists and publications. Write 2 to 3 strategic guest posts on real industry publications. Begin building presence on LinkedIn and relevant Reddit communities with genuine, helpful content.

Days 60 to 90: analyze which placements produced the most referral traffic and AI citations. Double down on the channels that worked. Pitch follow-up angles to journalists who covered the first piece. Update the comprehensive resource with new data to keep it current and link-worthy.

Throughout: track both traditional backlinks and AI citations. The strategies that produce both are the ones worth scaling.

## The uncomfortable truth

Link building in 2026 is harder than it was in 2020. The easy tactics do not work anymore. The tactics that work require genuine expertise, original work, and patience.

But the flip side is that the links you earn now are worth more than ever. Because fewer people can produce them at scale, each genuine editorial link carries more authority. And because AI systems are adding a new layer of citation-based authority on top of Google's link-based algorithm, the brands that build real authority through legitimate link building and brand presence are pulling further ahead of those still trying to manufacture shortcuts.

The shortcuts are gone. What remains is the work that was always supposed to be the point.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does link building still work in 2026?

Yes. Backlinks remain the second most important ranking factor after content relevance for traditional Google rankings. What changed is that AI search visibility now depends more on brand mentions and citations than clickable links. The most effective 2026 strategies produce both backlinks and brand mentions simultaneously, particularly digital PR with original research.

### What is the best link building strategy right now?

Digital PR with original data is the highest-impact strategy, rated most effective by 48.6 percent of SEO professionals. Publishing original research that journalists need to cite, providing expert commentary on breaking news, and creating data-driven visual assets consistently produce editorial backlinks from high-authority domains with average ROI of 312 percent.

### How many backlinks do I need to rank?

There is no universal number. The backlinks needed depend on keyword competition, your existing domain authority, and content quality. A page with 10 high-quality editorial backlinks from relevant, authoritative sites will outrank a page with 1,000 low-quality links. Focus on earning links from domains that your target audience actually reads.

### Are guest posts still good for link building?

Strategic guest posting on real publications with editorial standards and genuine readership still builds links and brand visibility effectively. Guest posting on low-quality "write for us" sites, link farms, or private blog networks does not work and can trigger spam penalties. The test: would a real person in your target audience read this publication?

### Do brand mentions help SEO without a link?

Yes, increasingly so. Brand mentions correlate 3x more strongly with AI search visibility than traditional backlinks (0.664 vs 0.218 correlation). Google also uses brand mentions as entity signals to understand your brand's authority and topical relevance, even without a clickable link. The most effective strategies earn both backlinks and unlinked brand mentions.
