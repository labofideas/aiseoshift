Google buried the click.

For most of 2026, an AI Overview gave you a short answer and a button. Show more. You clicked it if you wanted the fuller version. That click was a signal, arguably the cleanest signal Google had that a user wanted depth on this specific query rather than a quick fact.

As of August 27-28, [Google confirmed it doesn't need that signal anymore](https://searchengineland.com/google-is-dynamically-expanding-ai-overviews-for-some-queries-486200). AI Overviews can now expand on their own, into something closer to a full AI Mode response, for queries Google's systems decide deserve it. No click. No button. Just more AI, occupying more of the page, by default.

Here is the part that should bother you more than the headline. If you've already scrolled past the Overview by the time it decides to expand, Google cancels the expansion. It's polite about not yanking the page out from under a reader who moved on. It is not polite about doing it to everyone else.

## What actually triggers it

Google's own language is deliberately thin: the expansion happens "for topics where our systems determine it's most useful for people." That's it. No published list of query categories. No stated frequency. No ranking signal you can point to and say, this is what earns the long version instead of the short one.

What we do know: the expanded view can carry an "Ask anything" follow-up prompt built into the result, and it takes up enough vertical space to push standard organic results meaningfully lower on the page. Not just below the Overview box. Below a bigger Overview box.

If you run SEO for a site that lives on informational queries, this is worth sitting with for a second. The distance between your listing and the top of the page just grew, on an unknown and growing subset of your traffic, and nobody at Google is going to tell you which subset.

## The Chrome address bar piece is older than the headlines suggest

A lot of September coverage frames AI Mode reaching Chrome's address bar as new. It isn't. [Google started building the full AI Mode prompt box into the Chrome omnibox back in February 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/02/21/chrome-address-bar-ai-mode/). What's happening now is the next stage of that rollout: US English users get direct address-bar access to AI Mode by the end of September, and more languages follow.

The real story isn't the address bar. It's the compounding. Google has spent 2026 opening every entry point it controls, the search results page, AI Mode as a standalone surface, the Chrome address bar, into the same conversational answer layer. Each one alone is a feature update. Together they are a default.

AI Mode passed a billion monthly users this year. That number doesn't happen from one interface change. It happens from removing friction at every point a user might have typed a query the old way instead.

## France, corrected

One thing worth getting right, since a lot of coverage has it wrong: France was supposed to get AI Overviews and AI Mode by a September 23 deadline Google gave French publishers. It didn't wait. [Google switched both on for French searchers on July 22, 2026](https://aioseo.fr/en/ai-overviews-and-ai-mode-landed-in-france-on-july-22-2026-two-months-before-thepromised-deadline/), two months early, across desktop, mobile web, and the Google app.

That's not a footnote. It's a pattern. Every deadline Google has given publishers this year has turned out to be a ceiling, not a floor.

## What this means if you write for search

Stop optimizing for the short AI Overview snippet as if it's the only version of the box you're competing for. There are two boxes now, functionally: the quick-fact one, and the one Google decided your query deserved more room for. You don't get to choose which one shows up. Your content does the choosing, by whether it gives Google enough structured, specific, extractable material to fill the longer version convincingly.

That means:

**Answer the follow-up before it's asked.** The expanded box is built to support a conversational "Ask anything" continuation. Content that already anticipates the two or three obvious follow-up questions to your main topic is more useful to that continuation than content that stops at the first answer.

**Specific beats exhaustive.** A longer AI Overview still needs discrete, citable facts to assemble, not more paragraphs of the same claim restated. This is the same principle behind [information gain](/blog/information-gain-optimization/): the expanded answer is stitched together from sources that each contributed something the others didn't, not from whoever wrote the most words about the topic.

**Structure for the part that survives being cut.** Google's systems decide, per query, whether to show the short version or the long one. Write so the short version is still true and useful on its own, and the long version is a superset of it, not a different argument. If your content only makes sense in full, it fails the majority of queries where the short box is what shows.
