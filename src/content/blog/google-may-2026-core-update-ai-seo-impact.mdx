The May 2026 Google core update started rolling out on May 21 and finished on June 2. It was one of the more volatile updates in recent memory, with significant ranking shifts seen the weekend after launch and again in the final days of the rollout.

If your traffic dropped during this period, this article explains what changed and what to do about it. If your traffic held or improved, it explains why and how to protect those gains in the next update.

## What this update actually targeted

Google core updates rarely have a single stated target, but the signals from this one are consistent across the sites that moved up and moved down.

**The update rewarded:**

- Content created by verifiable subject-matter experts with traceable credentials
- Sites with tight topical focus where every page reinforces the same area of expertise
- Pages that answer questions directly rather than building to the answer over several paragraphs
- Original data, research, and first-hand experience that cannot be replicated by AI generation
- Updated content where the modification date reflects genuine changes, not cosmetic re-touches

**The update penalized:**

- Sites publishing across many unrelated topics to capture traffic
- Content that aggregates what other sources say without adding original perspective
- Author attribution that is either absent or unverifiable
- Outdated pages that rank through historical link equity but have not been meaningfully updated
- Sites where the same content appears across multiple pages with minor variations

## Three site profiles: what happened and why

**Profile 1: The finance comparison site that dropped 60%**

A mid-size personal finance site published content covering credit cards, mortgages, student loans, insurance, crypto, budgeting apps, and retirement accounts. Each category was handled by freelance writers with generic "financial writer" bios. No CPA credentials, no verifiable experience, no original research.

This site took a significant hit. The issue is not that AI wrote the content. The issue is that none of it could be attributed to genuine expertise. A comparison page about balance transfer credit cards that was written by someone with no traceable experience in personal finance cannot survive an update that specifically rewards original expertise.

Recovery path: Audit which topics the site can genuinely claim expertise in, rebuild those with credentialed authors and original data, and deprecate or substantially improve the others before attempting recovery.

**Profile 2: The B2B SaaS blog that gained 40%**

A project management software company had spent 18 months building a tight cluster of content around one specific topic: remote team workflows. Every post was written by their own product and operations team, attributing real people with LinkedIn profiles and job histories. They published original survey data twice per year. They updated their core pieces every quarter.

This site gained significantly. Tight topical focus, verifiable authors, original research, regular updates -- these are exactly the signals the May update weighted more heavily.

**Profile 3: The local service business that barely moved**

A plumbing company had 12 pages on its site: service pages for each service type, a contact page, and two blog posts from 2023. It neither gained nor lost significantly.

This is actually a useful outcome to understand. Core updates primarily affect sites with content depth. A local service site with minimal content is not the target of the update in either direction. These sites move based on local ranking signals that a core update does not directly change.

## The E-E-A-T signals that moved rankings most

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness are not new concepts, but the May 2026 update appears to have tightened how granularly Google evaluates them.

**Experience signals that worked:**

An outdoor gear review site that required all reviewers to submit purchase receipts and photos of gear in actual field conditions saw gains. A personal finance blog written by a CFP who documented their own investment decisions with screenshots saw gains.

The pattern: provable first-hand experience, not just claimed experience.

**Expertise signals that worked:**

A medical information site that attributed every article to a licensed physician with their state license number visible, linked to their hospital profile, saw gains. A legal information site where every article linked to the attorney's bar association profile saw gains.

The pattern: externally verifiable credentials, not just "Dr. Smith has 15 years of experience."

**Authoritativeness signals that worked:**

Sites with consistent mentions in industry publications, Wikipedia citations, and academic or government resource links saw gains. Third-party validation is the authority signal. Self-declared authority is not.

**Trustworthiness signals that worked:**

Clear editorial policies, transparent ownership disclosure, updated privacy pages, and accurate contact information all contributed. Sites that listed real people as responsible for the content performed better than anonymous or corporate-voice-only sites.

## The connection to AI SEO: core update and citation eligibility overlap

This is not a coincidence. The signals Google's core update rewards are nearly identical to the signals AI engines use to select citation sources.

A page that ranks well post-update -- expert author, original research, topically authoritative, well-structured, factually reliable -- is also a strong candidate for citation in Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude.

A page that was penalized by the update -- generic, unattributed, derivative, thin -- is also unlikely to be cited by any AI engine.

The practical implication: recovery work from the May 2026 core update and AI SEO optimization work are largely the same work. Fixing the signals that lost you rankings will also improve your AI citation eligibility.

## Recovery checklist

If your site took a hit from the May 2026 update, work through this in order:

**1. Identify affected pages.** In Google Search Console, compare traffic from May 14 to 20 (pre-update) versus May 28 to June 2 (mid-update). Export the pages with the largest drops.

**2. Check authorship.** Do the affected pages have a named author with a linked profile? Is that author's expertise in the topic area verifiable? If not, add attribution or replace the author bio with someone whose credentials are relevant.

**3. Check topical coherence.** Are the affected pages on topics your site genuinely covers in depth, or are they outliers chasing traffic in unrelated areas? Pages that fall outside your site's topical focus are harder to recover. Consider whether to improve or remove them.

**4. Add original contribution.** What does each affected page say that is not available from other sources? Add a section with original data, your own experience, a unique case study, or a perspective only your expertise enables. This is the hardest part of recovery and the most important.

**5. Update factually.** Check every statistic, every recommendation, every product mention on the affected page. Update what is outdated. Add a visible "Last updated" date if the layout supports it.

**6. Add or improve structured data.** Author schema, Article schema with `dateModified`, and FAQPage schema on pages with FAQ sections all reinforce the E-E-A-T signals Google is now weighting more heavily.

**7. Wait before analyzing.** Google recommends waiting at least one full week after the update finishes before drawing conclusions from Search Console data. The rollout finished June 2 -- do not start your analysis before June 9.

## Timeline for recovery

Core update recovery is not instant. A page that improves its E-E-A-T signals after an update typically sees ranking movement four to eight weeks after the improvements are indexed, not four to eight days.

Prioritize pages that drive the most business value first. A complete recovery of your top 10 traffic pages with genuine E-E-A-T improvements is worth more than a surface-level refresh of 50 pages.
