## The Performance report, now with AI Overviews data

This is the most important report in Search Console for 2026. Google has added AI Overview appearance data to the Performance report, giving you the first direct measurement of how often your pages appear inside AI-generated answers.

### How to access AI Overview data

Open the Performance report and select "Search type: Web" from the top filter bar. Once you are inside the Web search view, look for the "Search appearance" filter. You will find an AI Overviews option that lets you filter impressions and clicks specifically for queries where Google showed an AI Overview.

What you are looking at when you apply this filter:

- **Impressions with AI Overviews shown:** How many times your page appeared in results on a page where an AI Overview was also displayed
- **AI Overview citations:** How many times your page was actually cited as a source inside an AI Overview (this requires the AI Overview appearance type to appear in the Search appearance dimension)

These are different numbers. Appearing on a page that has an AI Overview is not the same as being cited inside one. The citation metric is the one that matters most for strategic decisions.

### CTR interpretation when AI Overviews appear

When an AI Overview is present, CTR for organic results below it will be lower than on pages without AI Overviews. This is expected behavior, not a site problem. Users are reading the AI answer directly and clicking through less often. This is covered in depth in the [zero-click search strategy](/blog/zero-click-search-strategy/) post.

The actionable question is not "why is CTR low on AI Overview queries?" but rather "am I being cited inside the AI Overview, or am I just appearing below it?" Being cited and absorbing that lower CTR is significantly better than not being cited at all.

### Identifying citation gaps

Sort your queries by impressions in the AI Overviews view. Identify queries where you have strong impressions but low or zero citations. These are your gaps. Your content is ranking, Google considers you a relevant result, but something in your content structure or authority profile is preventing citation selection.

Common reasons for the gap:

- Content answers the query superficially rather than comprehensively
- No structured data to help Google understand the content type
- Author credentials are missing or not machine-readable
- Page fails Core Web Vitals assessment

Each of these is fixable with a targeted workflow.

### Tracking core update impact

The March 2026 and [May 2026 core updates](/blog/google-may-2026-core-update/) both caused significant movement in AI Overview citation patterns. Use the date comparison feature in the Performance report to set a before/after range around each update. Sort by "Clicks change" descending to see your biggest losers, and look for patterns: Are the losing pages all on one topic? Do they share a structural characteristic like missing author information or thin content? Patterns point to the fix.

## Branded search tracking, the Preferred Sources signal

Branded search is one of the strongest signals in the Preferred Sources framework. When users search for your brand by name and click through regularly, Google interprets this as a trust and preference signal that increases the likelihood of your site being cited in AI Overviews.

Search Console gives you direct visibility into this. Open the Performance report and add a query filter that contains your brand name. What you see:

- Total branded impressions over time
- Total branded clicks
- Average position for brand queries
- Which specific brand queries are driving traffic

### Why this matters beyond vanity metrics

Growing branded search volume is a Preferred Sources signal because it reflects real user behavior: people who have encountered your content before want to come back. AI systems, including Google's, weigh this return-visit signal when selecting which sources to cite repeatedly.

The relationship between branded search and AI citation is not just theoretical. Sites with strong branded search growth tend to appear more consistently across topic-adjacent AI Overviews, not just for queries directly about their brand. For a full breakdown of this mechanism, see the [Google Preferred Sources strategy guide](/blog/google-preferred-sources-seo-strategy/).

### Setting up a weekly branded search benchmark

Run this check every Monday. It takes under five minutes and tells you whether your brand-building work is compounding:

1. Open Performance, filter by your brand name, set the range to the last 28 days
2. Note total impressions and total clicks
3. Compare to the prior 28-day period using the date comparison toggle
4. Record both numbers in a simple spreadsheet

Flat or declining branded impressions despite publishing new content is an early warning sign. Rising branded impressions with steady or improving CTR is a healthy Preferred Sources signal.

## URL Inspection for AI crawlability

The URL Inspection tool does more than tell you whether a page is indexed. In 2026, it is the primary tool for diagnosing why a page might be eligible for AI citation but is not being selected.

### Verifying page rendering

Open URL Inspection, enter the URL of any page you want to diagnose, and click "Test live URL." Wait for Google to fetch and render the page. The rendered HTML view shows you exactly what Google sees after JavaScript execution. If your content depends on client-side rendering and the rendered view is missing key sections, those sections are invisible to Google's AI Overview pipeline.

This matters especially for sites using React, Vue, or other client-side frameworks where content is not server-side rendered. If Google sees a loading state instead of content, your page cannot be cited.

### Structured data validation

In the URL Inspection results, scroll to the Enhancements section. This shows you which structured data types Google detected on the page and whether any errors were found. The schema types that matter most for AI citation eligibility:

- **FAQPage:** Makes individual Q&A pairs extractable as citation sources
- **Article:** Confirms publication date, author, and publication for editorial content
- **Person:** Links the author byline to verifiable credentials and social profiles

If your schema is present in your source code but not appearing in URL Inspection, Google cannot parse it. This is almost always a JSON-LD syntax error or a conflict between multiple schema blocks. The <a href="https://search.google.com/test/rich-results" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Rich Results Test</a> will isolate the exact error line.

### The robots.txt check for AI crawlers

This is the most commonly missed issue in 2026 AI SEO audits. Many sites updated their robots.txt in 2023 or 2024 to block AI crawlers, and the blocks are still in place even after strategy shifted.

URL Inspection does not directly show you the robots.txt status for third-party bots like GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or PerplexityBot. To check those, open your robots.txt file directly and scan for any `Disallow` rules targeting those user-agents.

For Google's own crawlers, URL Inspection will tell you if Googlebot is blocked. If Googlebot is blocked from any of your important pages, AI Overview citation is impossible for those pages regardless of content quality.

## Core Web Vitals report

Core Web Vitals moved from a ranking signal to something closer to a citation eligibility gate in 2026. Google's systems deprioritize pages that fail the assessment when selecting AI Overview sources, particularly for competitive queries where multiple high-quality sources are available.

### The three metrics and their 2026 thresholds

- **LCP (Largest Contentful Paint):** Good is under 2.5 seconds. Poor is over 4 seconds. This measures how fast the main content of your page loads.
- **CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift):** Good is under 0.1. Poor is over 0.25. This measures visual stability as the page loads.
- **INP (Interaction to Next Paint):** Good is under 200 milliseconds. Poor is over 500 milliseconds. This replaced FID and measures responsiveness to user interactions.

All three must be in the "Good" range for the page to pass the assessment. A single failing metric fails the whole page.

### Prioritizing which pages to fix first

Open the Core Web Vitals report and switch to the "Poor URLs" view. The report groups pages by the issue causing failure. Cross-reference the failing URLs against your Performance report to identify which failing pages have the highest impression volume.

Fix the highest-impression failing pages first. A page that fails Core Web Vitals but gets 200 monthly impressions is a lower priority than a page that fails and gets 20,000 monthly impressions. The goal is to maximize the number of high-traffic pages passing the assessment.

Use <a href="https://pagespeed.web.dev" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google PageSpeed Insights</a> to diagnose specific issues on each failing URL. It gives you field data from real users alongside lab data with specific recommendations.

## Crawl Stats, monitoring AI bot access

The Crawl Stats report, found in Settings within Search Console, shows you a 90-day rolling view of how Google's crawlers are accessing your site. In 2026, this report has taken on new importance as a proxy for overall bot crawlability.

### What the report shows

- Total crawl requests per day
- Breakdown by crawler type (Googlebot, Googlebot Mobile, Googlebot Image, etc.)
- Response codes for crawl requests
- Average response time

A healthy pattern shows steady, consistent crawling with the majority of responses returning 200 status codes. Spikes in 4xx or 5xx errors indicate access problems. A sudden drop in crawl volume can indicate that Google has reduced crawl budget due to server errors or crawl anomalies.

### Identifying accidentally blocked crawlers

While the Crawl Stats report only shows Google's crawlers, the diagnostic workflow matters for AI bots too. If your server configuration or robots.txt is causing problems for Googlebot, it is very likely causing the same problems for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot.

Check your robots.txt for any `Disallow: /` rules that might apply to groups of bots, and verify that your server's rate limiting is not throttling legitimate crawlers. A healthy crawl pattern for an active site in 2026 should show at least daily crawls of your highest-priority pages.

## Manual Actions, the hard stop

A manual action is a penalty applied by a Google reviewer when a site is found to violate Google's spam policies. Unlike algorithmic penalties, a manual action is a deliberate human decision, and it has a clear effect: pages under a manual action are ineligible for AI Overview citation regardless of content quality.

### How to check for manual actions

In Search Console, open the Security and Manual Actions section. Then click Manual Actions. If the page shows "No issues detected," you are clear. If it shows one or more actions, each action comes with a description of the violation and instructions for filing a reconsideration request after fixing the problem.

Check this report any time you see an unexplained sudden drop in impressions or clicks. A manual action produces a sharp vertical drop in the Performance report, typically affecting either the whole site or a specific section.

### Common manual action causes in 2026

Google's public guidance and documented cases point to three primary triggers in the current period:

- **Scaled content:** Using AI to generate large volumes of content that was not meaningfully reviewed or enhanced by a human before publishing. Content quality is not the only test. The intent to game rankings with volume is the core issue.
- **Unnatural links:** Both building paid links and having a sudden large influx of low-quality links pointing to your site.
- **Structured data spam:** Using schema markup to claim credentials, reviews, or ratings that are not present on the page itself.

Manual actions are recoverable. The reconsideration process takes four to six weeks and requires demonstrating that the violating content or links have been removed, not just modified.

## The 5 Search Console workflows for AI era SEO

### Workflow 1: Weekly AI Overview check (10 minutes)

Run this every Monday alongside your branded search benchmark.

1. Open the Performance report, select "Search type: Web"
2. Apply the Search appearance filter and select AI Overviews
3. Look at total AI Overview impressions for the past 7 days compared to the prior 7 days
4. Click into the Queries tab to see which specific queries are triggering AI Overviews where your site appears
5. Note the CTR on those queries and compare to your average CTR across non-AI Overview queries
6. Flag any high-impression queries where you appear in results but show no AI Overview impressions, these are your citation gap candidates

Record the total AI Overview impressions number weekly. Trending up means your citation presence is growing. Flat or declining means something in your content or technical health needs attention.

### Workflow 2: Monthly core update impact review (30 minutes)

Run this in the week after any confirmed Google core update.

1. Set the date range in the Performance report to start 30 days before the update announced date and end 30 days after
2. Enable date comparison to see the before and after side by side
3. Sort by "Clicks change" ascending to surface your biggest losers first
4. Open the top 10 losing URLs in separate tabs
5. Look for patterns across the losing pages: Are they all on one topic? Do they all lack author information? Are they all long-form or all short-form? Do they share a structural issue?
6. The pattern points to the fix. Missing authors = authority gap. Topic cluster = semantic coverage gap. Page type = format mismatch.

For the specific changes made in the May 2026 update, see the [Google May 2026 core update breakdown](/blog/google-may-2026-core-update/).

### Workflow 3: Schema validation check (20 minutes)

Run this monthly for your top 20 highest-traffic posts.

1. Open the Performance report, sort by impressions descending, note the top 20 URLs
2. Open URL Inspection, paste the first URL, select "Test live URL"
3. Wait for the test to complete, then scroll to the Enhancements section
4. Note any schema types detected and any errors flagged
5. If FAQPage, Article, or Person schema is missing or has errors, add it to your fix list
6. Repeat for all 20 URLs

Batch your fixes. If 12 of your 20 top posts are missing Article schema, add it to all 12 in one pass rather than fixing one at a time. Resubmit via URL Inspection after each fix is deployed.

### Workflow 4: Branded search benchmark (5 minutes weekly)

1. Open Performance, add a query filter: "Queries containing [your brand name]"
2. Set the date range to the past 28 days
3. Note total impressions and total clicks
4. Toggle date comparison to see the prior 28-day period
5. Record both numbers

A week-over-week or month-over-month upward trend in branded impressions is the most direct Search Console signal that your Preferred Sources eligibility is growing. This is the metric to show stakeholders when they ask whether content investment is working.

### Workflow 5: New content indexing verification (5 minutes)

Run this within 48 hours of any new post or page publication.

1. Copy the URL of the newly published page
2. Open URL Inspection, paste the URL
3. If the status shows "URL is not on Google," click "Request Indexing"
4. Return 24 to 48 hours later and run the inspection again
5. If the page still is not indexed after 72 hours, check the crawl coverage report for any indexing errors affecting that URL
6. Submit an updated sitemap via the Sitemaps report to prompt a fresh crawl

New content that is not indexed cannot earn AI Overview citations. The sooner a page is indexed, the sooner it enters Google's quality assessment pipeline.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Google Search Console show AI Overview performance?**

Yes. The Performance report includes an AI Overviews filter under the Search appearance dimension. This shows impressions and clicks for queries where your pages appeared alongside or inside an AI Overview. The data is available for the Web search type.

**How do I know if my site is appearing in Google AI Overviews?**

Open the Performance report, select "Search type: Web," and filter by Search appearance, then AI Overviews. If you have impressions in this view, your site is appearing on pages where Google shows AI Overviews. If you also have citations, your pages are being used as sources inside the AI Overview itself.

**What should I do if my Search Console traffic dropped after the May 2026 core update?**

Start with the date comparison workflow above to identify which pages lost the most traffic. Then look for patterns across those pages. The May 2026 update focused heavily on content authority and E-E-A-T signals. Missing author information, thin content, and pages without structured data were disproportionately affected. The [May 2026 core update guide](/blog/google-may-2026-core-update/) has specific recovery steps.

**How do I check if my schema markup is working in Search Console?**

Use URL Inspection on each important URL and look at the Enhancements section. This shows which schema types Google detected and parsed correctly, along with any errors. For a more detailed diagnosis, use the <a href="https://search.google.com/test/rich-results" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Rich Results Test</a>, which shows exactly which schema properties Google can and cannot read on each page.

**Can Search Console show me if AI crawlers are blocked on my site?**

Search Console only shows Googlebot crawl data in the Crawl Stats report. To check whether third-party AI crawlers like GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or PerplexityBot are blocked, open your robots.txt file directly at yourdomain.com/robots.txt and look for Disallow rules targeting those user-agents. Many sites added these blocks in 2023 and have not revisited them since.
