## Why service-area pages exist

When a homeowner in Scottsdale searches "plumber Scottsdale," Google has to decide which plumbers to show. Two signals dominate that decision:

1. Is this plumber actually serving Scottsdale? (categories, service area, GBP, citations)
2. Is there a page on this plumber's site that specifically addresses Scottsdale customers?

The second signal is where service-area pages do their work. Without a Scottsdale-specific page, you are competing with plumbers who have one. You will lose.

For the broader local SEO framework, see [The Local SEO Checklist for 2026](/blog/local-seo-checklist-2026/).

## How many service-area pages do you need?

Cover every city, suburb, and zip code you actually serve. Typical ranges:

- **Solo operator, single city focus:** 5 to 10 pages
- **Multi-suburb single-metro coverage:** 15 to 30 pages
- **Multi-metro operator:** 30 to 80 pages
- **Multi-state franchise:** 100+ pages with state-level and city-level structure

Quality beats quantity. Ten well-built pages beat 50 thin ones.

## The doorway page trap

Doorway pages are near-identical pages built to rank for variations of a query. Google explicitly penalizes them. The line between "valid service-area pages" and "doorway pages" comes down to one question: does each page contain meaningfully different content?

If you swapped the city name in a service-area page and the rest of the content still made sense, the page is a doorway page. Google's algorithms detect this pattern and demote the entire site.

The four things that make a service-area page non-templated:

1. Specific local content (neighborhoods, landmarks, codes)
2. Local case studies or completed jobs
3. City-specific data (response times, technician coverage, permits)
4. Unique photos or media

## The anatomy of a service-area page that ranks

A great service-area page is 1,200 to 2,000 words, structured for both AI extraction and skim-reading. Here is the template section by section.

### Section 1: Hero with local hook

Lead with a sentence or two that immediately signals local relevance. "Phoenix Plumbing Pros has been serving Scottsdale homeowners since 2014, with a dedicated dispatch team for the 85254, 85255, 85258, and 85260 zip codes."

Include the city name in the H1, the first paragraph, and the title tag. Do not over-stuff, three to four mentions in the first 300 words is enough.

### Section 2: Services offered in this city

List every service you provide in this specific city. Some businesses serve every city with their full service catalog. Others have geographic specialization (commercial in one city, residential in another). Be accurate.

### Section 3: Local context that competitors do not have

This is the section that separates real service-area pages from templates. Include:

- Common service requests specific to the area (older neighborhoods have different needs than new construction)
- Local water authority, building code, or permit requirements
- Notable local references (specific homes, neighborhoods, business districts you have served)
- Local weather considerations (Phoenix HVAC has different urgency than Minneapolis)

### Section 4: Recent jobs in this area

Two to four recent job descriptions with the neighborhood, the work performed, and ideally a photo. This is the strongest possible signal that you actually serve the area.

### Section 5: Why this city's homeowners choose us

Specific, not generic. "We respond to Scottsdale emergency calls in 45 minutes average" beats "We provide excellent service." Include any local accolades, partnerships, or community involvement.

### Section 6: Service-area-specific FAQs

Six to ten questions homeowners in that specific city actually ask. Examples:

- What zip codes does [your business] serve in Scottsdale?
- Do you offer emergency plumbing in Scottsdale at night?
- Are you licensed to work in Scottsdale?
- What are common plumbing issues in Scottsdale homes?

Mark up with `FAQPage` schema. AI Overviews extract heavily from this section.

### Section 7: NAP and contact CTA

Phone number, hours, service-area-specific landing pages or contact forms. Make the conversion path obvious.

## Schema markup for service-area pages

Two schema types belong on every service-area page:

### LocalBusiness or Service schema

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Plumber",
  "name": "Phoenix Plumbing Pros - Scottsdale",
  "telephone": "+1-602-555-0100",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "addressLocality": "Phoenix",
    "addressRegion": "AZ",
    "postalCode": "85016"
  },
  "areaServed": {
    "@type": "City",
    "name": "Scottsdale, AZ"
  }
}
```

### FAQPage schema for the FAQ section

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What zip codes does Phoenix Plumbing Pros serve in Scottsdale?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "Phoenix Plumbing Pros covers 85254, 85255, 85258, 85260, 85262, and 85266 in Scottsdale, with a dedicated team and average response times under 60 minutes during business hours."
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

## Internal linking structure

Service-area pages should not be islands. The internal linking pattern that works:

- **From homepage:** Link to a top-level "Service Areas" hub page
- **Service Areas hub:** Lists all service-area pages, grouped by metro or region
- **Each service-area page:** Links back to the hub, sideways to 3 to 5 nearby service-area pages, up to relevant service pages
- **Service pages:** Mention the service areas served, link to the most relevant city pages

This creates a tight crawl graph that distributes authority and helps AI engines understand your geographic coverage.

## Production at scale

Building 30 to 80 service-area pages is intimidating. The two things that make it sustainable:

### Build a strong template

A document with placeholders for city name, zip codes, neighborhoods, local references, photo slots, and FAQ swaps. Filling a template is dramatically faster than starting from blank.

### Batch the local research

Spend one full day pulling local data for all your service areas at once. Zip codes, neighborhoods, water authority info, common housing stock, building permit nuances. Use that research to vary each page.

A realistic production cadence is two to four service-area pages per week. At three per week, you have 36 pages live in three months and 100+ in a year.

## Updating cadence

Service-area pages need maintenance. Schedule:

- **Quarterly:** Refresh recent jobs section, update any local references
- **Annually:** Full review including services offered, zip codes, contact information

Pages that have not been updated in 18+ months lose rank. Pages updated quarterly compound their authority.

## Common mistakes that kill service-area pages

- **Identical templates with only the city name swapped.** Google detects this pattern and demotes the entire site.
- **No phone number or contact CTA.** Pages with no conversion path get treated as low-value.
- **Listing zip codes you do not actually serve.** Mismatch between your actual coverage and your page claims gets flagged.
- **No internal linking.** Orphaned pages do not rank.
- **Stock photos.** Especially photos that show different cities or climates than the one the page is about.

## FAQs

### Can I use AI to write service-area pages?

Yes for first drafts, with significant rewrite required. AI produces structurally correct but generically observed local content. Expect to rewrite 50 to 70 percent of each draft with actual local knowledge.

### How thin can a service-area page be before it hurts?

Under 600 words is risky. Under 400 words is treated as thin content. Aim for 1,200 to 2,000 words per page, weighted toward genuinely local content.

### Should small suburbs have their own pages or share a regional page?

If you do meaningful volume in the suburb, dedicated page. If you respond to a few calls a year, share a regional page that covers multiple small suburbs.

### What if my CRM is not city-aware?

You can still pull the data from job invoices, dispatch records, or even Google Business Profile insights. The first cleanup pass is manual, future updates can be automated.
