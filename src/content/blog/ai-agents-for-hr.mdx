The best AI agents for HR in 2027 are Eightfold and Microsoft's Employee Self-Service agent, followed by HireVue, Ashby, Leena AI, Workable, LinkedIn Hiring Assistant, SAP SuccessFactors with SmartRecruiters, Workday's talent acquisition agent and Juicebox. We ranked them on bias disclosure, price clarity, coverage, evidence and human oversight.

## Key takeaways

- HR is where agent risk is legal, not just operational. Hiring tools sit inside discrimination law, and one live federal case already names a major vendor's tools.
- The rules moved in 2026. Colorado replaced its AI Act in May, and the EU pushed high-risk employment obligations to December 2, 2027. Many vendor and blog pages still show the old dates.
- Disclosure is uneven. Only one vendor on this list publishes how it audits for bias in enough detail to check. Several publish nothing on the product page.
- Adoption outruns measurement. SHRM found 56 percent of HR functions do not formally measure the success of their AI investments.
- Candidates are using agents too. Applications per recruiter at Greenhouse customers rose 203.7 percent between 2022 and 2025, and recruiter headcount fell.

## What changed in 2026

Two things happened at once. The vendors consolidated, and the law moved.

On consolidation, Workday [completed its Paradox acquisition on October 1, 2025](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-10-01-Workday-Completes-Acquisition-of-Paradox), putting the conversational recruiting assistant next to HiredScore in one talent acquisition suite. SAP [completed SmartRecruiters on September 11, 2025](https://news.sap.com/2025/09/sap-completes-smartrecruiters-acquisition/). HireVue bought Hireguide technology in March, and Greenhouse announced it would acquire Ezra AI Labs, a voice interviewing company, in May. ServiceNow closed Moveworks in December 2025, per secondary sources, so the employee-service agent many HR teams knew as an independent product is now a ServiceNow one.

On the law, the Colorado story is the clearest example of why dates matter. The original Colorado AI Act, SB 24-205, was widely quoted as taking effect June 30, 2026. The legislature's own page for [SB26-189](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-189) shows the Governor signed a replacement on May 14, 2026. It repeals and reenacts the earlier protections as a narrower automated decision-making technology regime that covers consequential decisions, employment among them. The June date never took effect as written.

The EU did something similar. Annex III of the AI Act lists recruitment and selection systems as high-risk. The [implementation timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/) we opened, an independent tracker last updated August 31, 2026, shows the application date for those systems moved from August 2, 2026 to December 2, 2027. The law firm [Garrigues](https://www.garrigues.com/en_GB/garrigues-digital/ai-digital-omnibus-regulation-has-been-published-redefining-deadlines-and) reports the digital omnibus regulation was published in the Official Journal on July 24, 2026. We could not confirm the text on EUR-Lex, so verify before you build a compliance calendar on it.

The skeptical reading: an agent that touches hiring decisions is a regulated activity in a growing number of places, sold by vendors whose product pages say very little about it.

## What counts as an AI agent in HR

HR vendors use "agent" for four different things.

**Assistant.** Answers an employee's question about leave or benefits from policy documents. Useful, low risk, and not much of an agent.

**Workflow agent.** Does the next step in a process: books interviews, sends reminders, creates an HR ticket, updates an employee record through a connector. Microsoft's and Leena AI's employee-service agents work this way.

**Screening agent.** Ranks, filters or scores candidates, or runs an interview. This is the category that triggers bias audits, notices and in some places the high-risk label.

**Sourcing agent.** Searches for passive candidates and drafts outreach. Lower legal risk, higher volume, and the main source of the spam recruiters send.

Our test: does the agent influence who gets hired, promoted, or let go? If yes, treat it as a decision system, whatever the vendor calls it. If it only answers questions, treat it as a help desk.

For the wider business view, see [AI agents for business](/blog/ai-agents-for-business/). For tools aimed at individual recruiters rather than platforms, see our [AI tools for recruiters](/blog/ai-tools-for-recruiters/) roundup.

## The market in numbers

All figures were opened in November 2026. Where we read a trade write-up instead of the original report, we say so.

**Adoption is real and uneven.** SHRM's State of AI in HR 2026, a survey of 1,908 HR professionals dated March 31, 2026, found [39 percent of organizations have implemented AI in HR](https://www.shrm.org/in/executive-network/insights/state-of-ai-hr-2026-5-critical-insights-chros), with 27 percent using it in talent acquisition and 60 percent adoption at organizations with 5,000 or more employees. Thirty-one percent have no AI initiatives planned. The percentages come from SHRM's article pages, not the gated report.

**Measurement lags.** The same SHRM source found 56 percent of HR functions do not formally measure AI investment success, and only 16 percent use ROI. It also found 57 percent of HR professionals in states with workforce AI rules were unaware of them, and 12 percent of those who were aware had taken compliance steps.

**Hiring managers use it, with humans mostly in the loop.** A Resume Genius survey of 1,000 US hiring managers, [reported by CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/03/03/8-in-10-hiring-managers-say-they-use-ai-during-the-recruitment-process/179155/) in March 2026, found 79 percent say their company uses AI somewhere in hiring, but only 6 percent say it can advance or reject candidates with limited human review. Resume Genius sells resume tools, and we read the write-up, not the report.

**Candidates meet AI interviewers.** Greenhouse's [2026 Candidate AI Interview Report](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/63-of-job-seekers-have-faced-an-ai-interview-most-havent-had-a-good-one-yet), based on 2,950 job seekers across five countries, found 63 percent had been interviewed by an AI, 70 percent were not clearly told upfront and 51 percent of those who finished one never heard back. Greenhouse sells AI interviewing, so this is vendor-reported.

**Applicant volume is exploding.** Greenhouse's [recruiting benchmarks](https://www.greenhouse.com/recruiting-benchmarks), covering more than 6,000 companies and 640 million applications, show applications per recruiter rising from 1,610 in 2022 to 4,890 in 2025, up 203.7 percent, while recruiters per organization fell 56 percent. You will see 412 percent quoted in trade coverage. We could not source it to a primary, and the benchmarks page shows 203.7.

**Candidates use agents to apply.** A Greenhouse report dated [October 20, 2026](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/6-in-10-job-seekers-are-rethinking-how-they-write-to-avoid-sounding-like-ai-even-as-most-use-it-in-their-search) found 77 percent of US job seekers used AI in their search, 69 percent would be comfortable using an AI agent, and only 5 percent would let one apply on its own. Of those who tried to get past automated screening, 40 percent added skills or keywords they do not have.

**Workers are worried.** Mercer's [Global Talent Trends 2026](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/about/newsroom/mercer-s-global-talent-trends-2026-report/), drawn from nearly 12,000 respondents, found 40 percent of employees concerned about AI job loss, up from 28 in 2024, and only 19 percent of HR leaders factoring AI's emotional impact into plans.

**Vendor efficiency claims.** LinkedIn says recruiters using its Hiring Assistant review [81 percent fewer profiles](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/hiring-assistant) to find a qualified match. Phenom says customers report 50 percent faster hiring. Both are unaudited vendor figures.

One caution. Samples, years and definitions all differ here. Do not treat the list as one dataset.

## The legal ground

Not legal advice. These are the facts we opened, with the gaps marked.

**New York City, Local Law 144.** Per [NYC's page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page), employers and agencies may not use an automated employment decision tool unless it had a bias audit within a year before use, audit information is public and required notices were given. Enforcement began July 5, 2023. We found no amendments.

**Illinois, HB 3773.** In force since January 1, 2026, per [Epstein Becker Green's summary](https://www.ebglaw.com/workforce-bulletin/illinois-prohibits-discriminatory-artificial-intelligence-in-employment-decisions). It bars AI that has the effect of discriminating in recruitment, hiring, promotion, discipline or discharge, bars zip codes as a proxy and requires notice. It has no bias audit mandate. Notice rules from the state agency appear not to be final, but we only saw that in secondary reports.

**California.** Automated-decision system regulations under the state's civil rights law took effect October 1, 2025, per the [Civil Rights Council](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/2025/06/30/civil-rights-council-secures-approval-for-regulations-to-protect-against-employment-discrimination-related-to-artificial-intelligence/). They add definitions, say automated tools can violate the law when they harm protected groups, and require employers to keep related records for at least four years.

**Colorado.** SB26-189, described above. Developer documentation duties start January 1, 2027, and deployers give notice and, after an adverse decision, a plain-language explanation within 30 days. The attorney general must adopt rules by January 1, 2027. We read the legislature's summary, not the full text.

**EU.** Recruitment and selection are high-risk under Annex III. Obligations now apply from December 2, 2027, subject to the confirmation caveat above.

**US federal.** The EEOC removed its AI employment guidance from its website in January 2025, per [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/federal-government-quietly-removed-its-ai-hiring-guidance-four-states-are-writing), and as of March 2026 we found no replacement. Private lawsuits continue regardless.

**Mobley v. Workday.** The case, before Judge Rita Lin in the Northern District of California, was [allowed to proceed as an age discrimination collective in May 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged). A [June 2026 order](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/06/24/california-federal-court-grants-in-part-and-denies-in-part-workdays-motion-to-dismiss-in-mobley-v-workday/) let some state-law and disability claims continue and dismissed others. A class tracker reports a Rule 23 certification motion filed September 14, 2026, naming HiredScore tools, with a hearing set for March 9, 2027. These are allegations. No court has found the tools biased, and no class is certified. We read law firm and tracker summaries because the docket was blocked.

The practical lesson: your vendor's silence does not shift your liability. Ask for the bias audit before you sign.

## How we ranked them

We did not run hands-on tests. Everything below comes from vendor documentation, vendor pricing pages and published reporting opened in November 2026. We received nothing from any vendor.

Each agent scores 1 to 5 on five criteria, for a maximum of 25.

| Criterion | What earns a 5 | What earns a 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Bias and compliance disclosure | Published audit method, certifications, summaries for local laws | Nothing beyond a principles statement |
| Price clarity | Public prices and a clear usage rule | Demo or quote only |
| Workflow coverage | Spans several HR processes | One narrow task |
| Evidence | Checkable data, ideally not vendor-supplied | Marketing claims only |
| Human oversight | Documented escalation, approval or user control | Nothing shown |

Ties are broken by evidence, then bias disclosure. The scores are editorial judgment. Rescore them with your own weights, and weight the first column heavily if you hire in New York, Illinois, California or the EU.

## The 10 agents, ranked

### 1. Eightfold

**Score: 17 of 25** (disclosure 5, price 1, coverage 4, evidence 3, oversight 4).

[Eightfold](https://eightfold.ai/products/) sells a talent intelligence platform with a family of Talent Agents, including an AI Interviewer, an Interview Companion and a Candidate Concierge, built on skills-based matching.

Who it suits: enterprise recruiting teams that must show regulators how a tool was tested.

Pricing: none published. The site points to a demo and an ROI calculator.

Its [responsible AI page](https://eightfold.ai/responsible-ai/) is the most detailed on this list. It says it commissions third-party bias audits annually for both the AI Interviewer and its matching model, publishes summaries for NYC Local Law 144, runs impact-ratio analysis and perturbation testing, and re-checks bias on each model update. It lists SOC 2 Type II, ISO 42001, ISO 27001 and a FedRAMP Moderate attestation, and an external AI ethics council.

Real limits. We did not open the linked audit result pages, so we cannot vouch for the auditor, dates or figures. Process disclosure is not outcome proof.

Use case: a retailer hiring in New York asks for the published audit summary during procurement, before the notice and posting steps.

Verdict: the best disclosure here. Read the audit, not the summary of the audit.

### 2. Microsoft Employee Self-Service agent

**Score: 17 of 25** (disclosure 3, price 4, coverage 3, evidence 3, oversight 4).

Microsoft's [Employee Self-Service agent](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/employee-self-service/overview) in Copilot Studio handles policy and benefits questions, reads and creates HR tickets through ServiceNow, and reads and writes employee profile data through Workday and SAP SuccessFactors connectors. It escalates complex or sensitive requests, such as legal or personnel decisions, to HR specialists and logs all interactions for auditability.

Who it suits: IT and HR teams already in Microsoft 365 that want an employee help desk.

Pricing mechanics are published in [Microsoft's billing documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/requirements-messages-management): Copilot Credits, with a classic answer at 1 credit, a generative answer at 2, an agent action at 5 and tenant graph grounding at 10. There is no charge when a user with a Microsoft 365 Copilot license uses it under their own identity. Dollar-per-credit figures appeared only in search snippets, so we do not quote them.

Real limits. It is an employee service agent, not a hiring tool. Mobile support has limits, and the cost depends on the credits your usage burns.

Use case: an employee asks about parental leave, and the agent answers from policy and opens a ticket if the case needs HR.

Verdict: the clearest billing logic and a sensible escalation design. It avoids the riskiest HR category by not hiring anyone.

### 3. HireVue

**Score: 15 of 25** (disclosure 4, price 1, coverage 3, evidence 3, oversight 4).

HireVue's [announcement of the Hireguide acquisition](https://www.hirevue.com/press-release/hirevue-acquires-hireguide-technology-to-accelerate-agentic-ai-hiring) describes agents that automate candidate qualification, guide structured interviews and align evaluations with validated competencies, with a voice AI interviewer as the first step. It claims more than 1,150 customers and 60 percent of the Fortune 100.

Who it suits: high-volume and enterprise hiring teams that want structured interviews at scale.

Pricing: not published. Third-party estimates exist, and we do not quote them.

Governance: HireVue says AI assists and does not replace human decision-makers and cites more than 2,000 model maintenance studies for fairness, and links an AI explainability statement. The release does not describe a bias-testing method.

Real limits. Customer and assessment counts are vendor figures. Interview scoring is exactly the activity the NYC and EU rules focus on.

Use case: a hospital system runs the same structured questions for every nursing applicant and has recruiters review the results.

Verdict: strong on process language, weaker on checkable detail than Eightfold.

### 4. Ashby

**Score: 15 of 25** (disclosure 1, price 5, coverage 3, evidence 3, oversight 3).

[Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/pricing) is an all-in-one recruiting platform with AI-assisted application review, fraud detection, talent rediscovery, interview summaries, an MCP server and custom AI agents.

Who it suits: startups and mid-sized companies that want an ATS with agents built in.

Pricing as published in November 2026: Foundations at $400 per month for up to 100 employees, with 1,500 AI credits per month. Plus and Enterprise tiers are quote only, with 2,500 and 12,500 credits per seat per year. Candidate texting costs $90 per 1,000 messages. The price of extra credits is not listed.

Ashby also publishes data: its [talent trends report](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report) says applications per hire in EMEA doubled since 2021, based on 109 million applications.

Real limits. The pricing page says nothing about bias testing. Fraud detection is a useful feature and also a decision system.

Use case: a 60-person company screens a flood of applications with AI-assisted review and flags suspicious submissions for a recruiter.

Verdict: strong on price and data, silent on bias.

### 5. Leena AI

**Score: 15 of 25** (disclosure 4, price 3, coverage 4, evidence 2, oversight 2).

### 6. Workable

**Score: 14 of 25** (disclosure 1, price 5, coverage 3, evidence 2, oversight 3).

[Workable](https://www.workable.com/pricing) is a recruiting platform where a Job Brief Agent is included on all plans, and sourcing, screening and engagement agents are add-ons priced in AI credits.

Who it suits: small employers that want a published price.

Use case: a 15-person agency screens inbound applicants and pays by the candidate screened.

Verdict: the easiest cost to model on the list. Ask about bias testing separately.

## How HR agents change who gets found and cited

This is the section most HR buyer guides skip.

**Candidates research you with an assistant first.** PerceptionX's June 2026 report, a survey of 306 job seekers in seven countries, [found](https://hrdive.com/press-release/20260601-perceptionx-finds-ai-reshaping-hiring-decisions-82-of-candidates-changed/) that 96 percent of AI-using job seekers used AI to research employers, learn about a role or prepare for an interview. Fifty-eight percent had caught AI giving inaccurate employer information, and 82 percent said AI, or their fact-checking of it, changed their mind about a role. The sample is small and PerceptionX sells employer brand monitoring, so treat it as directional.

We could not find an independent 2026 study of where AI answers source employer information. Be wary of any vendor percentage on that topic.

**Your job pages are structured data.** Google's [JobPosting guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting) require datePosted, description, hiringOrganization, jobLocation and title, recommend a real baseSalary, and say markup must be visible on the page, used only on single-job pages, and removed when the job is filled. An HR agent that drafts or publishes job descriptions can break those rules by pushing text without matching markup, or leave filled roles live.

**Applicants now include agents.** Greenhouse's data above shows 69 percent of job seekers would use an agent and 5 percent would let one apply alone. Volume is already up. Expect more machine-written applications and more detection tools, such as the fraud detection features Gem and Ashby list.

**Your own HR agent is a public-facing surface.** If your careers chatbot or employee agent quotes outdated pay bands or policies, candidates and assistants will repeat them.

What to do, in order of cost:

1. Keep a public careers and pay-range page current, in plain HTML.
2. Add accurate JobPosting markup, and close filled jobs.
3. Check what AI assistants say about you as an employer. Our guides to [what AI visibility is](/blog/what-is-ai-visibility/) and [how to measure it](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/) cover the method.
4. Mark up the pages that matter with [schema](/blog/schema-markup-for-ai-visibility/), consistent with the visible text.
5. Read the broader shift in [Google's search agents](/blog/google-search-agents/).

## How to choose

By the job.

**Employee questions.** Microsoft's agent if you run Microsoft 365, Leena AI if you want a vendor-neutral connector layer.

**High-volume hiring.** Eightfold or HireVue for structured screening, Workday if you already run it, and demand the audit materials first.

**Small teams.** Workable or Ashby for a published price, with your own checks for bias.

**Sourcing.** Juicebox or LinkedIn, and keep a human reviewing outreach.

By location. If you hire in New York, Illinois, California, Colorado or the EU, put legal review ahead of the demo. The law changed this year and it is changing again in 2027.

Run a pilot on past requisitions. Compare the agent's shortlist to your actual hires by demographic group, where lawful and with counsel, and keep the record.

## Implementation, governance and risk

**Ask for the bias audit.** If a vendor cannot provide one, or says "we are compliant" without showing the method, treat that as an answer.

**Know who decides.** SHRM found 52 percent of organizations do not involve HR in AI strategy. Name an owner for each agent.

**Notify.** NYC, Illinois, Colorado and the EU all involve notices in some form. Candidate disclosure also matters for trust: Greenhouse found 70 percent were not clearly told upfront about an AI interview.

**Keep a human in the loop for adverse decisions.** Only 6 percent of hiring managers in the Resume Genius survey said AI can reject candidates with limited human review. Stay in the cautious majority.

**Log everything.** California requires records for at least four years, and Colorado's regime asks for three. Check what each vendor exports.

**Watch sourcing agents.** Auto-email settings can generate outreach at volume. Review samples weekly.

**Measure.** Fifty-six percent of HR functions do not. Pick two numbers, such as time to schedule and adverse impact ratio, and track them before and after.

## 2027 outlook

Three things to watch.

Mobley moves. The class certification hearing is set for March 9, 2027. Whatever the outcome, it will shape how vendors talk about disclosure.

Compliance dates bite. Colorado's developer duties start January 1, 2027, and the EU's high-risk obligations follow on December 2, 2027.

Applicant agents meet screening agents. When candidates send agents and employers use agents, both sides optimize against each other. Structured interviews and skills checks tend to survive this better than keyword screening.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the best AI agents for HR?

On our rubric, Eightfold and Microsoft's Employee Self-Service agent tie at 17 of 25, followed by HireVue, Ashby and Leena AI at 15. For recruiting teams that need documented bias audits, Eightfold is the most transparent. For employee questions on top of Workday, SAP or ServiceNow, Microsoft's agent has the clearest published billing units.

### Are AI hiring tools legal?

Generally yes, with conditions that vary by place. New York City requires a bias audit and notices for automated employment decision tools. Illinois has barred discriminatory AI in employment decisions since January 1, 2026. California's rules on automated decision systems took effect October 1, 2025. This is not legal advice, so get counsel for your jurisdictions.

### Does the EU AI Act cover recruitment software?

Yes. Annex III lists AI used to screen or filter applications and to evaluate candidates as high-risk. The application date for those obligations moved to December 2, 2027 under the digital omnibus, per the AI Act tracker and a law firm summary we opened. Confirm the date on the Official Journal text before you plan around it.

### What happened to the Colorado AI Act?

Colorado's legislature replaced it. SB26-189, signed May 14, 2026, repeals and reenacts the original with a narrower automated decision-making technology regime. Developer documentation duties start January 1, 2027. The June 30, 2026 date still quoted in many articles did not take effect.

### How much do AI recruiting agents cost?

Prices published in November 2026 range from free tiers and $99 per seat per month for Juicebox to $299 per month for Workable and $400 per month for Ashby, plus credits. Workday, SAP, Eightfold, HireVue and LinkedIn do not publish prices. Microsoft publishes credit units for its employee-service agent.

### Do AI agents reduce hiring bias?

The evidence does not say so yet. Vendors that publish bias audits, such as Eightfold, show process, not proof of outcome. Mobley v. Workday alleges the opposite for age and remains unresolved. Treat bias reduction as a claim you test in your own funnel, with your own adverse impact data.

### How do HR agents affect employer visibility in AI search?

Candidates ask AI assistants about employers before they apply. PerceptionX found 96 percent of AI-using job seekers used AI to research employers or roles, and 58 percent caught it giving inaccurate employer information. Accurate careers pages, current pay ranges and valid job posting markup give those assistants something correct to quote.
