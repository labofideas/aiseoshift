At Google I/O on May 20, 2026, Google announced that AI Mode has surpassed 1 billion monthly active users, exactly one year after the feature's debut at I/O 2025. At the same session, the company released behavioral data showing how those billion users are actually searching: queries are three times longer than traditional searches, follow-up query volume is climbing 40% month-over-month, and more than 16% of sessions now include a multimodal input like an image, voice clip, or video.

This is not a beta product with a ceiling. This is the primary search surface for hundreds of millions of people, and the data Google shared is the clearest public picture yet of how search behavior is fundamentally shifting. If you have been treating AI Mode as a secondary optimization concern, these numbers make a strong case for changing that view.

[Our March 2026 explainer on AI Mode](/blog/google-ai-mode-what-seos-need-to-know/) covers the mechanics and foundational strategy. This post focuses on what the I/O 2026 data reveals, what it means for how you create and structure content, and where the real traffic opportunity sits in a world where position 1 organic CTR has dropped to 11%.

## The milestone in context

Google AI Mode launched in May 2025 as a user-initiated conversational interface built on Gemini, sitting inside Google Search rather than as a separate product. Within twelve months, it reached 1 billion monthly active users.

For reference, ChatGPT took approximately two years to reach 1 billion monthly users. Traditional Google Search took years to reach global scale before mobile smartphones accelerated adoption. AI Mode's trajectory is not typical.

The quarterly doubling of query volume is the more telling signal. Exponential growth at this scale means that AI Mode is capturing an increasing share of searches that would previously have gone to traditional blue-link results, not just supplementing them. Every quarter that passes, the proportion of Google searches that flow through AI Mode grows substantially.

What that means in practice is simple: [Google AI Mode](/ai-mode-seo/) is now too large to treat as a secondary optimization target. Strategies that center entirely on ranking positions for traditional results are optimizing for a surface that is shrinking in relative share.

## Breaking down the behavioral data

The headline number (1 billion users) tells you about scale. The behavioral data tells you about intent. These four signals are each worth examining closely, because they point to specific things you need to change about how you write, structure, and optimize content.

### Queries are three times longer than traditional searches

The most consequential data point from the I/O announcement is average query length. AI Mode queries average three times the length of traditional Google searches.

Consider what that shift looks like in practice. A traditional search might be "best CRM for small business." An AI Mode equivalent for the same underlying need might be: "I run a 12-person B2B SaaS company, we currently use HubSpot but our sales team says the pipeline view is confusing for multi-stage deals, and we use Slack heavily for deal updates, so what alternatives handle complex pipeline stages better and have a native Slack integration?"

Those two queries share a category but almost nothing else. The second query has context (12-person SaaS), a specific problem (confusing pipeline view, multi-stage deals), an existing constraint (HubSpot), a tool dependency (Slack), and a clear evaluative criterion. No single keyword phrase captures that intent.

Content that ranks well for "best CRM" has no advantage over content that specifically addresses multi-stage pipeline management with Slack integration, because AI Mode is routing a query that is actually asking about the second thing. Keyword-stuffed pages built around head terms cannot satisfy this intent. They do not have enough specificity to be the best available source for the sub-queries that AI Mode decomposes longer searches into.

Your content strategy needs to shift from "what keywords does this page rank for" to "what specific questions and contexts does this page answer better than any other source on the web."

### Follow-up queries are climbing 40% month-over-month

Users do not stop at one AI Mode answer. According to Google's I/O data, follow-up query volume is growing at 40% month-over-month. Users are drilling down, challenging answers, comparing options, and asking for deeper context within a single session.

This matters for content strategy because AI Mode's follow-up chain pulls from sources across the entire conversation arc, not just the initial query. If your content answers the opening question but does not go deep enough to satisfy the second or third question in the same topic thread, you lose citation visibility partway through the user's research session.

The implication is that topic depth is more valuable than topic breadth. One comprehensive page that covers a subject, its nuances, its common objections, and its practical applications is more likely to be cited across multiple follow-up queries than a shallow page that covers the main point and nothing else. This is precisely why [topic clusters](/blog/build-a-topic-cluster/) have become more strategically important under AI search: a cluster that covers a subject comprehensively can earn citations across an entire follow-up chain, not just for the entry query.

### More than 16% of sessions include multimodal input

At I/O, Google reported that over 16% of AI Mode queries now include a non-text input: a photo, a voice recording, or a video clip. That figure is likely to grow as mobile AI Mode usage deepens and Google integrates more camera and microphone entry points into the search experience.

A user photographing a product and asking "is this compatible with my existing setup" is conducting a search that has no text keyword at all. A voice query captures natural language that looks nothing like what a person would type into a search box. Video queries can describe complex situations, show physical spaces, or demonstrate a problem that would take paragraphs to describe in text.

For SEO, multimodal search makes several things mandatory that were previously optional:

- Alt text on images must be descriptive and contextual, not generic or keyword-stuffed. Google's vision models read alt text as part of the content context, not just as an accessibility signal.
- Every video on a page needs a transcript. AI Mode cannot extract value from a video it cannot read.
- Image schema (including `ImageObject` structured data) tells Google what an image depicts and how it relates to the page's topic.
- Voice queries favor content written in natural language, with complete sentences and conversational phrasing, because that is the register of speech.

[Multimodal SEO strategy](/blog/multimodal-seo-images-video-audio/) covers the technical and content requirements in detail. The key strategic point here is that any content workflow that produces images without alt text or videos without transcripts is already underperforming on 16% of AI Mode queries, a percentage that will only grow.

### Planning queries are growing at 80% of overall AI Mode growth

Google's I/O data identified planning queries as a standout growth category, growing at 80% of overall AI Mode's growth rate. This includes trip planning, purchase planning, project planning, content calendar creation, event coordination, and similar task-oriented searches.

Planning queries are not informational in the traditional sense. They blend information-gathering with task execution: the user is trying to produce something actionable, not just learn something. "Plan a week-long hiking trip in Colorado for two people with a $2,000 budget including gear rental" is an AI Mode planning query. So is "help me create a 3-month content calendar for a B2B SaaS blog targeting DevOps professionals."

From an SEO perspective, planning queries have high commercial value. Someone planning a trip is about to book accommodation, tours, and flights. Someone planning a SaaS content calendar is likely evaluating content tools. The content types that match planning intent (step-by-step guides, curated checklists, timelines, decision frameworks, comparison templates) tend to be exactly the content that captures commercial intent at the research stage.

If your content library skews toward explainers and definitions and does not include planning-oriented assets, you are leaving a large and high-value category of AI Mode traffic unaddressed.

## What this means for content strategy

The behavioral data points toward five specific content strategy shifts. None of them require abandoning what you know about SEO, but all of them require updating how you apply it.

### Write for conversations, not keywords

The shift to longer, contextual queries means the mental model of "target a keyword, write a page for it" is no longer sufficient as a primary organizing principle. When someone asks AI Mode a complex, context-laden question, the AI is looking for content that addresses the full question, including the situation, the constraint, and the specific decision being made.

In practice, this means writing content that addresses real use cases rather than abstract keyword targets. Instead of "best project management tools," write about "project management tools for engineering teams that need GitHub integration and sprint tracking." Instead of "email marketing guide," write about "email marketing for e-commerce businesses sending more than 50,000 emails per month."

You are not abandoning SEO. You are targeting the richer, more specific queries that AI Mode actually routes to sources, rather than the blunt keyword queries that traditional ranking focused on.

### Depth beats breadth

One comprehensive page that covers a topic thoroughly, addresses common objections, anticipates follow-up questions, and provides specific context outperforms ten shallow pages that collectively cover the same ground. This has always been true in principle, but AI Mode's follow-up chain mechanics make it commercially important in a way it was not before.

When a user's session involves three or four follow-up queries, each pulling from the best available source for that specific sub-question, a single deep page that answers multiple sub-questions will earn more cumulative citations than multiple thin pages that each answer one piece. [Deeper content strategy](/seo/content-strategy/), building pages that are genuinely the best resource on their topic, is directly rewarded by how AI Mode navigates follow-up chains.

### Multimodal assets are now SEO assets

This is a practical checklist item, not a philosophical shift. Every image on every page needs descriptive alt text. Every video needs a full transcript on the page. `ImageObject` structured data should be implemented wherever images are an important part of the content. Product images, instructional diagrams, screenshots, and comparison graphics are all subject to multimodal query routing.

The reason this matters: if 16% of AI Mode sessions are already multimodal, and that percentage grows as Google deepens camera and voice entry points in mobile search, then content without proper multimodal signals is already underperforming on a substantial and growing share of queries. [Multimodal SEO](/blog/multimodal-seo-images-video-audio/) covers the full technical implementation.

### Planning content is commercial content

The 80%-of-growth figure for planning queries should prompt a content audit. Go through your existing content and ask: how many of these pages help someone plan something? If the answer is "almost none," you have a significant gap in your coverage of high-value commercial intent.

Planning content includes step-by-step guides, decision frameworks ("how to choose the right X for Y situation"), project templates, timelines, comparison matrices, and checklist content. These formats match planning intent because they give users something they can act on, not just something they can read.

For most sites, the planning-to-informational content ratio is heavily skewed toward informational. Rebalancing that ratio is a meaningful SEO opportunity under AI Mode's growth profile.

### Structure for extraction

AI Mode synthesizes answers from sources. That means it needs to extract specific pieces of information from your content. Pages that put their key answer in the first 200 words, use clear H2 and H3 headings that signal what each section contains, and include FAQ blocks with question-and-answer formatting are structurally easier for AI systems to extract from.

This is not about gaming the algorithm. It is about writing clearly enough that both AI systems and human readers can immediately find what they came for. A page that buries its main point in paragraph four, uses vague section headings, and does not include any structured summary is harder to cite even if the underlying information is excellent. [Structuring pages for AI extraction](/blog/how-to-structure-pages-ai-extraction/) covers the specific formatting practices in detail.

## The CTR reality check

There is a traffic paradox at the heart of AI Mode growth that requires a frank discussion. Position 1 organic click-through rate for queries where AI features appear has dropped from approximately 27% to 11% (SISTRIX, March 2026). More AI Mode sessions, fewer clicks to individual pages. The volumetric traffic picture for non-cited content is genuinely worse.

But the picture for cited sources is different. Pages cited within AI Mode or AI Overviews see 35% more clicks than a position 1 result without citation. The conversion rate from AI-referred traffic is 4.4 times higher than average organic traffic (BrightEdge Research). Users who click through from a cited source arrive with significantly more context, higher intent, and greater readiness to convert.

The strategic implication is a shift in optimization target. The goal is no longer to maximize the volume of impressions from ranking positions. The goal is to earn citation status, which drives fewer but substantially higher-quality visits. [SEO authority over rankings](/blog/seo-authority-not-rankings/) explores this shift in detail.

This is a real change in how SEO performance should be measured. If your team is still evaluating success primarily through position tracking and organic traffic volume, the metrics will tell a misleading story. Citation frequency, AI-referred session quality, and branded search growth are the signals that map to actual business outcomes under AI Mode.

## Connecting AI Mode to Preferred Sources

There is one additional element of the AI Mode ecosystem that compounds everything discussed above: Google's Preferred Sources feature, which allows users to mark trusted websites that Google's AI will prioritize in responses.

Preferred Sources creates a direct feedback loop between AI Mode visibility and future AI Mode visibility. When your site earns a citation in an AI Mode response, users who find the cited content valuable are more likely to mark your site as a Preferred Source. That designation increases the frequency with which your content appears in future AI Mode responses for that user, which drives more direct visits, which strengthens the behavioral signals that Google uses to evaluate the site's authority.

Users who return to your site directly from AI Mode sessions are contributing to exactly the kind of behavioral signal that earns Preferred Source status at scale. This is why brand consistency, content quality, and the experience of visiting a page matter more in an AI Mode world than they did in a traditional ranking world. [Google Preferred Sources strategy](/blog/google-preferred-sources-seo-strategy/) covers the full mechanics.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many users does Google AI Mode have in 2026?

Google announced at I/O 2026 on May 20, 2026, that AI Mode has surpassed 1 billion monthly active users. This milestone was reached approximately one year after AI Mode launched at Google I/O 2025.

### How is AI Mode different from Google AI Overviews?

AI Overviews appear automatically above traditional search results for many informational queries and coexist with the standard list of blue links. AI Mode is a user-initiated conversational interface that replaces the blue links entirely with a Gemini-generated response. AI Mode supports multi-turn follow-up conversations, has agent capabilities for planning and task execution, and has a much higher zero-click rate (approximately 93% versus approximately 43% for AI Overviews). The two features use related but distinct source selection processes.

### Why are AI Mode queries so much longer than traditional searches?

AI Mode is designed for conversational interaction rather than keyword-based lookup. Users quickly learn that providing context produces better answers: specifying their situation, constraints, existing tools, and specific needs results in more useful, tailored responses. This feedback loop encourages users to front-load queries with context that they would never include in a traditional search box. The AI interprets and benefits from that context in ways that keyword matching cannot.

### Does AI Mode affect my website's organic traffic?

Yes, but the effect depends on whether your content earns citations. For content that is not cited in AI Mode responses, click-through rates from queries where AI features appear have dropped significantly (position 1 organic CTR fell from approximately 27% to 11% for AI-featured queries, per SISTRIX March 2026 data). For content that does earn citations, traffic quality improves substantially: cited sources see 35% more clicks than non-cited position 1 results, with 4.4× higher conversion rates (BrightEdge Research).

### How do I optimize content for AI Mode?

Focus on depth over breadth, write for specific use cases rather than abstract keyword targets, structure your pages so that key answers appear early and section headings are descriptive, implement structured data and proper image alt text, include planning-oriented content formats like step-by-step guides and decision frameworks, and ensure every video has a transcript. The underlying principle is to be the clearest, most comprehensive, most extractable source on your topic, not to optimize for a specific ranking position.

### What is a multimodal search query?

A multimodal search query includes a non-text input alongside or instead of a typed query. In AI Mode, this includes submitting a photograph (for example, photographing a product and asking whether it is compatible with existing equipment), a voice recording (speaking a query rather than typing it), or a video clip. Google reported that more than 16% of AI Mode sessions now include a multimodal input. These queries require that your content have strong image alt text, video transcripts, and image structured data to be eligible as a cited source.
