VidIQ is the stronger choice for keyword research and deciding what to make next, with search volume, a competition score, and an opportunity metric that highlights winnable topics, starting around $7.50 a month with a genuinely useful free tier. TubeBuddy is the stronger choice for optimization and bulk work once you know what you are making, with tag suggestions, thumbnail A/B testing, and bulk metadata tools built into YouTube Studio, starting around $4.50 a month. The honest answer for a serious creator is that these solve different halves of the same job, and many people who run a channel at any scale end up using both rather than picking a side.

Ideation versus execution. That is the actual divide, not a simple better-or-worse.

## The comparison

| | VidIQ | TubeBuddy |
|---|---|---|
| Core strength | Keyword research, opportunity scoring | Bulk metadata optimization, A/B testing |
| Where it lives | Own dashboard + browser extension | Browser extension inside YouTube Studio |
| Competitor tracking | Yes, velocity tracking on rival channels | Limited |
| Thumbnail testing | No | Yes, built in |
| Starting price | about $7.50/mo, usable free tier | about $4.50/mo, usable free tier |
| Best for | Deciding what to make | Optimizing what you made |

## VidIQ: built for deciding what to make

VidIQ's keyword research shows search volume, a competition score, and an "opportunity" number that weighs demand against how strong the currently-ranking videos are, which is the single most useful number for a smaller channel deciding where it can realistically break through. It also tracks competitor velocity, showing which channels are gaining ground on which topics, which turns competitive research into an ongoing signal rather than a one-time snapshot.

If your actual bottleneck is knowing what video to make next, not how to polish the one you already have, VidIQ's research tools are built specifically for that decision.

## TubeBuddy: built for optimizing what you already have

TubeBuddy is an optimization engine that lives inside YouTube Studio itself via its browser extension, adding tag suggestions, bulk metadata editing across your whole library, and built-in thumbnail A/B testing, none of which VidIQ offers in the same depth. Once you have chosen your keywords and topic, TubeBuddy is the tool that helps you apply that decision well and test whether your thumbnail choice is actually working, with real data rather than a guess.

If your bottleneck is applying good metadata consistently across a growing back catalog, or you want to test thumbnails scientifically instead of by feel, TubeBuddy's execution tools are the stronger fit.

## Why many creators use both

The two tools rarely compete directly for the same job, which is why the "versus" framing is slightly misleading. A creator doing serious keyword research in VidIQ to choose a topic, then applying that topic's keywords through TubeBuddy's bulk tools and testing the resulting thumbnail, is using both tools for what each does best rather than picking a favorite. Their free tiers make this combination realistic even for a smaller channel not ready to pay for two subscriptions; only once you outgrow the free limits on both does the cost of running both become a real consideration.

## What each free tier actually covers

Both tools deliberately keep a usable free tier, and knowing what each one gates behind payment helps you decide whether you even need to pay yet.

VidIQ's free tier includes basic keyword research and video scoring, enough for a new or small channel to start choosing topics with real data rather than guessing. The paid tiers add deeper competitor tracking, more detailed opportunity scoring, and higher usage limits that matter once you are researching topics daily rather than occasionally.

TubeBuddy's free tier includes basic tag suggestions and some bulk processing tools, but its most useful features for a growing channel, unlimited bulk processing across a large back catalog and full thumbnail A/B testing, sit behind the paid tiers. A channel with a handful of videos rarely feels the free tier's limits; a channel with hundreds does.

The practical implication: start both free, and only pay for the one whose free-tier limit you actually hit first. For most new channels, that takes longer than expected, and the free tiers alone cover the first several months of serious effort.

## The bottom line

Pick VidIQ if your real bottleneck is knowing what to make, its keyword research and opportunity scoring are built for exactly that decision. Pick TubeBuddy if you already know what to make and need to optimize and test the execution across a growing library. If your channel is serious enough to justify it, running both, one for ideation and one for optimization, is not redundant. It is using each tool for the half of the job it is actually built for.

## FAQs

### Is VidIQ or TubeBuddy better for YouTube SEO?

Neither is universally better; they solve different halves of the job. VidIQ is stronger for keyword research and deciding what topic to make next, with its opportunity scoring highlighting winnable ideas. TubeBuddy is stronger for optimizing and testing what you have already made, with bulk metadata tools and built-in thumbnail A/B testing that VidIQ does not offer.

### Can I use VidIQ and TubeBuddy together?

Yes, and many serious creators do, since the tools rarely overlap directly: VidIQ for the research and topic decision, TubeBuddy for applying metadata at scale and testing thumbnails. Both offer usable free tiers, which makes running both realistic even for a smaller channel before committing to paid plans on either.

### Which is cheaper, VidIQ or TubeBuddy?

TubeBuddy's paid tier starts slightly lower, around $4.50 a month, versus VidIQ's roughly $7.50 a month starting price. Both offer a free tier with real functionality for small channels, so cost is rarely the deciding factor between them; the better question is which job, ideation or optimization, is your actual bottleneck right now.

### Are the free versions of VidIQ and TubeBuddy actually usable?

Yes, for a new or small channel. VidIQ's free tier covers basic keyword research and video scoring, enough to choose topics with real data. TubeBuddy's free tier covers basic tag suggestions and some bulk tools, though its deepest features, unlimited bulk processing and full thumbnail A/B testing, require a paid plan. Most channels do not hit either free tier's real limits until they are publishing regularly at meaningful volume.
