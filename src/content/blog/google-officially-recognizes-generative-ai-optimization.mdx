Google's official documentation for businesses evaluating whether to hire an SEO professional has been updated to include a new bullet point under legitimate SEO services: **"Optimizing for generative AI."**

The change, spotted by Search Engine Roundtable's Barry Schwartz, appears in Google's "Do you need an SEO?" help page on Google Search Central. The page has existed for years as Google's clearest public statement of what SEO is, what it is not, and how to find a trustworthy provider. Adding generative AI optimization to that list is significant because Google does not add items to that list casually.

The full updated list of services Google says a legitimate SEO can provide now includes:

- Review of your site content or structure
- Technical advice on website development
- Content development
- Management of online business development campaigns
- Keyword research
- SEO training
- Expertise in specific markets and geographies
- **Optimizing for generative AI**

## The Warning Google Added Alongside the Recognition

The update is not just an endorsement. Google added a pointed cautionary section specifically for businesses evaluating AI SEO advice. Under "Evaluate your SEO's recommendations and tools they use," the page now includes:

"If they have advice on optimizing for AI experiences (also known as 'AEO' 'GEO' services), is their advice aligned with Google Search's official guidance on optimizing for generative AI features?"

This is Google doing two things simultaneously: validating that generative AI optimization is a real, legitimate service category, and warning businesses that the AI SEO space has enough bad actors to warrant specific vigilance.

The evaluation questions Google suggests asking any SEO advisor about their AI optimization claims:

1. Do they cite official Google documentation as supporting evidence for their recommendations?
2. Is their advice aligned with Google Search's official guidance on optimizing for generative AI features?
3. Do they use tools that are aligned with Google's guidance?

## Why This Update Matters

Before this update, a business owner evaluating an AI SEO agency had no authoritative external reference point. Terms like AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) existed in the industry but had no Google-level acknowledgment. A skeptical client could reasonably ask whether the whole thing was a marketing invention.

That position is now harder to maintain. Google listing generative AI optimization alongside keyword research and technical SEO on its official guidance page is the clearest form of institutional recognition the practice can receive without Google directly publishing an AI SEO ranking factor guide.

For businesses: this means the category is real, the question is only whether a specific agency is doing it correctly.

For agencies: the bar is now explicit. Google is telling your potential clients to ask whether your advice aligns with official documentation. Agencies that built their AI SEO practice on speculation, proprietary "AI ranking factors," or claims that contradict Google's actual published guidance are now exposed in a concrete way.

## What Google's Official Guidance on Generative AI Optimization Actually Says

Google's guidance on optimizing for generative AI features is documented across several pages in Google Search Central. The main signals Google has confirmed as relevant to AI Overviews and AI Mode include:

**Content quality signals.** AI features draw from the same quality assessment systems as regular search. E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) applies to AI citations as much as it applies to traditional rankings. Content that demonstrates first-hand experience and verifiable expertise is prioritized.

**Structured data.** Schema markup helps AI systems understand content structure, entity relationships, and factual claims. Google has confirmed that structured data is a positive signal for AI feature inclusion.

**Clear, direct answers.** AI Overviews and AI Mode are designed to provide direct answers to user queries. Pages that state their answer clearly in the first paragraph, rather than burying it after background context, are better positioned for citation.

**Page experience.** Core Web Vitals remain relevant. A page that loads slowly or is difficult to navigate on mobile provides a poor experience that affects its standing as a citation candidate.

**Helpful content alignment.** Google's Helpful Content system evaluates whether pages are written primarily for people rather than for search engines. This applies in AI contexts: pages written to manipulate AI citation signals rather than genuinely inform readers will not perform well long-term.

## What This Means If You Are Evaluating an AI SEO Agency

Use Google's own checklist. When an agency presents an AI SEO strategy, ask them to point to the Google documentation that supports each claim. Legitimate AI SEO practice builds on confirmed signals. Agencies that cannot connect their recommendations to documented Google guidance are improvising.

Watch for claims that cannot be verified. Statements like "we have a proprietary algorithm for getting cited in AI Overviews" or "our tool directly influences AI answer selection" have no basis in anything Google has published. The signals Google has confirmed are content quality, structured data, and page experience. The implementation of those signals is where expertise lives. The signals themselves are not secret.

The irony of the moment is worth noting. Google adding AEO and GEO to its official guidance page is partly a response to the volume of spam agencies that have latched onto AI SEO as a buzzword. The same update that validates the category is also calling out bad actors. For businesses, that dual signal is useful: the practice is real, but the vetting requirement is higher than it was for traditional SEO.
