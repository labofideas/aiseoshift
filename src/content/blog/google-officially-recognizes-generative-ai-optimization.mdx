There is a page on Google Search Central that has existed for years. It is called "Do you need an SEO?" and its purpose is simple: tell business owners what SEO actually is, what a legitimate SEO can do for them, and how to avoid being scammed. Google updates it rarely and deliberately.

On June 8, 2026, Google added a new bullet point to the list of legitimate SEO services on that page. The new item reads: **"Optimizing for generative AI."**

That addition changes something that has been unresolved since AI Overviews launched in 2024. Until today, every agency, consultant, and tool vendor selling AI SEO services was operating without any official Google acknowledgment that the category existed. Businesses buying those services had no authoritative reference point. Terms like AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) were industry inventions that Google had never validated or contradicted.

Now Google has validated them. And in the same update, it told businesses exactly how to identify when AI SEO advice is legitimate versus when it is not.

## What the Update Actually Says

The new item on Google's services list is brief. But the update to the evaluation section is more detailed and deliberately pointed. Under the heading "Evaluate your SEO's recommendations and tools they use," Google added a new sub-bullet specifically about AI optimization claims:

> "If they have advice on optimizing for AI experiences (also known as 'AEO' 'GEO' services), is their advice aligned with Google Search's official guidance on optimizing for generative AI features?"

This is followed by three evaluation questions Google is telling businesses to apply:

1. Do they cite official Google documentation as supporting evidence for their recommendations?
2. Is their advice on AEO/GEO aligned with Google's official generative AI features guidance?
3. Do they use tools that are aligned with Google's guidance?

Read together, the update communicates a clear position: the service is real, it belongs on the legitimate services list, and here is how to tell whether the person selling it to you is doing it correctly.

## The Gray Zone Is Over

Since AI Overviews launched in May 2024, the AI SEO industry has operated in a gray zone. Hundreds of agencies added AI optimization to their service menus. Dozens of tools claimed to track, improve, or guarantee AI visibility. Consulting packages appeared overnight with no methodological basis. And because Google had never officially acknowledged that generative AI optimization was a thing, businesses had no way to evaluate any of it against an authoritative standard.

The result was predictable. Legitimate practitioners, who built their work on documented signals like E-E-A-T, structured data, and content quality, competed for the same clients as vendors selling proprietary black-box AI ranking systems. Both groups used the same buzzwords. There was no easy way for a business to tell them apart.

Google's update creates that separation. Not completely, and not automatically. But for the first time, a business evaluating an AI SEO proposal has an official Google document they can reference. They can ask: does your advice align with this? They can ask for documentation citations. They can ask what tools you use and whether those tools are grounded in Google's guidance.

Those questions will be uncomfortable for a large portion of the AI SEO market.

## What Google's Official Guidance on Generative AI Actually Covers

The guidance Google refers businesses to is spread across several Google Search Central pages. Taking it as a whole, here is what Google has confirmed matters for generative AI feature inclusion:

**E-E-A-T remains the foundation.** Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness are not traditional SEO concepts that separately apply to AI. They are the same quality signals, applied consistently. Content demonstrating genuine first-hand experience from a credible author on a credible site is the baseline requirement.

**Structured data helps.** Schema markup gives AI systems explicit, machine-readable information about what a page contains, who created it, and what entities it references. Google has confirmed structured data as a positive signal for AI feature inclusion. This is one area where AI SEO work that goes beyond traditional SEO best practice has a legitimate, documented basis.

**Direct, clear answers are favored.** AI Overviews and AI Mode are built to answer questions directly. Pages that open with a clear answer and build depth from there are structurally better suited for AI citation than pages that bury conclusions after extensive setup.

**Helpful content principles apply in full.** The Helpful Content system, which evaluates whether content is written primarily to serve readers rather than to game search systems, does not stop at traditional search. AI feature citation favors content that would pass a helpful content evaluation: original, specific, written with genuine expertise, and not padded for length or keyword saturation.

**Page experience signals still matter.** Core Web Vitals, mobile usability, and HTTPS are not AI-specific requirements. But a page that performs poorly on these signals is a lower-quality candidate for citation, and Google's AI systems draw from the same quality pool as its traditional ranking systems.

## Two Audiences, Two Different Implications

**For businesses hiring AI SEO services:** This update gives you a checklist. Before signing any AI SEO contract, ask the agency to walk you through Google's published guidance on generative AI optimization and explain how their service maps to it. If they cannot do that, they are selling you a service built on speculation.

Be especially skeptical of guarantees. Google has not published a formula for guaranteed AI citation. Any agency claiming proprietary access to AI ranking signals, or guaranteed placement in AI Overviews, is making claims that have no basis in anything Google has documented.

**For agencies and consultants doing this work legitimately:** This is the validation the market needed. The skeptical client who asked "is AI SEO even real?" now has a definitive answer from Google. Use it. Ground your recommendations explicitly in Google's published documentation. Make the citation trail visible in your proposals and reports. The agencies that build on documented signals will be clearly distinguishable from those that do not.

## The Spam Email Pattern

Google's update also includes a section that feels aimed directly at the wave of cold-email AI SEO pitches that have proliferated since 2024. Under "Be wary of SEO firms and web consultants or agencies that email you out of the blue," Google added explicit context about the AI SEO version of this problem.

The comparison Google makes is worth quoting in spirit: treat unsolicited AI SEO emails with the same skepticism you would apply to spam emails claiming you can lose weight overnight or recover money from a foreign account. The structure of the scam is the same. The urgency is manufactured, the claims are unverifiable, and the goal is to capture payment before scrutiny can be applied.

This is not a description of the whole AI SEO industry. But it is an accurate description of a significant portion of the outbound-email segment of it.

## The Bigger Picture

Google adding "Optimizing for generative AI" to its official services list is one of those small documentation changes that carries disproportionate weight. It is not a ranking factor announcement. It is not an algorithm update. But it is Google putting on record that this work belongs in the same category as keyword research and technical SEO. That matters more than any individual algorithm signal.

The businesses that have been waiting to see whether AI SEO was real before investing in it now have their answer. The agencies that have been doing this work correctly now have a validation document they can reference. And the vendors selling AI SEO theater now have a standard they will visibly fail to meet when buyers know to ask the right questions.

The practice was always real. The documentation just caught up.
