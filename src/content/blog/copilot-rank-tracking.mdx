## Start with Microsoft's own free tool

Before you buy anything, use the tool Microsoft gives you. On February 9, 2026, Microsoft added an AI Performance report to Bing Webmaster Tools, and it is free. Unlike the old search-performance metrics that counted clicks and positions, AI Performance tracks when and how often your content is referenced as a source in AI-generated answers across Microsoft Copilot, Bing's AI summaries, and partner integrations.

That makes Bing Webmaster Tools the natural first stop for Copilot tracking, and it costs nothing. Verify your site, open the AI Performance report, and you can see which of your pages Copilot and Bing AI are citing and for what. For many small and mid-size sites, this free report plus manual testing is enough to start, and you can add a paid tool later only if you need competitive data the free report does not provide.

What the free report does not give you is share of voice against competitors or cross-engine coverage. That is where third-party tools come in.

## Why Copilot tracking is really Bing tracking

This is the point that makes Copilot different from every other engine, and it is good news.

Copilot does not maintain a separate index you have to optimize for. It grounds its answers in Bing's search index. So the single most effective way to improve your Copilot citations is to improve your Bing ranking and Bing's trust in your pages. A page Bing ranks well and treats as authoritative is a page Copilot is likely to cite.

That connection is why SE Ranking Visible, which shows Bing rank and Copilot visibility side by side, is such a natural fit, and why your Copilot strategy should lean on Bing fundamentals: verify in Bing Webmaster Tools, submit your sitemap, fix crawlability, and earn the authority signals Bing weights. One relevant data point from a large 2026 study: domain traffic was the strongest predictor of AI citations, with high-traffic sites earning roughly three times more citations than low-traffic ones, which underlines that overall authority, not a Copilot-specific trick, is what wins.

The optimization playbook for Copilot itself is in [how to optimize for Microsoft Copilot](/blog/how-to-optimize-for-microsoft-copilot/), which goes deeper on winning Bing AI citations.

## A Bing checklist to win the Copilot citations you track

Because Copilot is grounded in Bing, your tracker's gap list is really a Bing to-do list. When Copilot cites a competitor instead of you, the fix almost always lives in Bing fundamentals. Work through these in order.

Verify and submit in Bing Webmaster Tools. This is step zero. Verify your site, submit your sitemap, and use the URL inspection and indexing tools to make sure Bing has your important pages. Bing's index is smaller and less aggressively crawled than Google's, so pages that Google indexed automatically sometimes need a nudge in Bing. A page Bing has not indexed cannot ground a Copilot answer.

Fix crawlability for Bing specifically. Confirm bingbot is allowed in your robots.txt and that no rendering or JavaScript issue hides your content from it. The AI-crawler side of this is in [robots.txt for AI crawlers](/blog/robots-txt-for-ai-crawlers/) and [LLM crawler optimization](/blog/llm-crawler-optimization/). Copilot cannot cite what Bing never crawled.

Answer the question cleanly and early. Copilot, like every answer engine, lifts self-contained answers. The winning competitor page on a tracked question almost always states the answer plainly near the top. Rewrite your opening so the direct answer comes first, then support it. The extraction mechanics are in [how to structure pages so AI can extract answers](/blog/how-to-structure-pages-ai-extraction/).

Build overall authority and traffic. A large 2026 study found domain traffic to be the strongest single predictor of AI citations, with high-traffic sites earning roughly three times more citations than low-traffic ones. That is not a Copilot trick; it is a reminder that the durable path to Copilot citations is the same authority-building work as everywhere, covered in [AI citation building](/seo/ai-citation-building/). Bing tends to weight established, trusted domains, so authority pays off there as much as in Google.

Add structured data. Schema helps Bing understand and trust your content, which feeds Copilot's grounding. FAQ and Article schema on your key pages give Bing clean, machine-readable claims to work with.

The point of the checklist is that Copilot optimization is not mysterious. It is Bing SEO with an AI citation as the payoff, and the full engine-specific playbook is in [how to optimize for Microsoft Copilot](/blog/how-to-optimize-for-microsoft-copilot/).

## The Microsoft 365 angle: why Copilot reaches buyers you cannot see

Copilot is easy to underrate because you rarely watch someone use it, but its distribution is enormous and deeply embedded in work. It lives in Windows, in Edge, and across Microsoft 365, which means it reaches people inside the tools they already use for their jobs: drafting in Word, analyzing in Excel, answering questions in Teams. For B2B especially, that is a high-value context. The person asking Copilot a question mid-task is often exactly the professional buyer you want to reach, at exactly the moment they are making a decision.

This changes how you value Copilot tracking. The raw query counts may look smaller than ChatGPT's consumer volume, but the enterprise, in-workflow nature of Copilot usage means each citation can carry more weight for a business audience. If your buyers work inside the Microsoft ecosystem, being the source Copilot cites is a genuine advantage that few competitors are contesting.

It also means the questions you track for Copilot should reflect professional, in-workflow phrasing rather than casual consumer queries. Someone using Copilot in Excel to research vendors asks differently than someone idly querying a chatbot, and your tracked prompt list should match that reality.

## Setting up Copilot tracking

Verify in Bing Webmaster Tools first. It is free, it is Microsoft's own data, and the AI Performance report is your source of truth for Copilot citations. Everything else layers on top.

Write your tracked questions the way buyers ask Copilot. Copilot is often used inside Microsoft 365 for work tasks, so the questions can be practical and professional. Build a prompt list that reflects that, not a list of two-word keywords.

Name your competitors. The free Bing report will not tell you who Copilot cites instead of you. A third-party tool will, and that competitor list is where your content roadmap comes from.

Baseline, change one thing, re-check. Establish your starting Copilot citation rate, ship a specific Bing-focused improvement, and watch whether Copilot's behavior on the relevant questions moves. The content loop is in [how to get cited by AI search systems](/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-ai-search-systems/).

## What to report about Copilot visibility

Copilot reporting has a nice advantage: part of your data comes from Microsoft itself, which makes it credible to stakeholders. Structure the report around four things.

Citation rate from the Bing report. The AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools gives you Microsoft's own count of how often your content is referenced in Copilot and Bing AI answers. Lead with that number and its trend, because it is official and hard to argue with.

Competitive share of voice from your third-party tool. The Bing report will not tell you who Copilot cites instead of you. Your paid tool supplies that, framed as "we appear in X percent of Copilot answers where our named rivals appear in Y." This is the number that turns a status update into a strategy conversation.

The Bing-to-Copilot connection. Because the two move together, report your Bing ranking trend alongside your Copilot citation trend. When a Bing ranking improvement precedes a Copilot citation gain, that is the cause-and-effect story that justifies the work, and a tool like SE Ranking Visible shows both in one place.

The gap list. Two or three high-value questions where a competitor consistently wins in Copilot, with the page cited and the Bing fix that would close it. That list is the next quarter's work. ## Copilot versus the other AI engines

Copilot occupies a specific spot in the AI landscape, and knowing it helps you prioritize.

Against ChatGPT and Perplexity, Copilot's distinguishing feature is its grounding in Bing and its embedding in Microsoft's work tools. ChatGPT is broader and more consumer; Perplexity is research-focused and citation-heavy in its own right; Copilot is the one woven into the professional's workday inside Windows and Microsoft 365. That makes it disproportionately valuable for B2B and undervalued by competitors who think only about the consumer engines.

Against Google's AI surfaces, the contrast is index and audience. Google AI Overviews draw on Google's index and reach the mass search audience; Copilot draws on Bing's index and reaches the Microsoft ecosystem. Optimizing for one does not automatically win the other, because the indexes and the source-selection differ, which is the whole reason to track and optimize Copilot as its own channel. The strategic case for covering every surface rather than one is in [search everywhere optimization](/seo/search-everywhere/), and the single-engine guides for the others are the [best Perplexity rank tracker](/blog/best-perplexity-rank-tracker/), the [free Claude rank tracker](/blog/best-free-claude-rank-tracker/), and [DeepSeek rank tracking](/blog/rank-tracking-tool-deepseek/).

The takeaway: Copilot is not a smaller ChatGPT. It is a different engine, on a different index, reaching a different audience, and the low competition for it is the opportunity.

## Common mistakes when tracking Copilot

**Skipping the free Microsoft tool.** Teams pay for third-party Copilot tracking while ignoring the free, official AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools. Start there.

**Treating Copilot as separate from Bing.** Optimizing for Copilot without touching Bing is backwards. Copilot is grounded in Bing, so Bing SEO is the lever. Ignore Bing and you are guessing.

**Only tracking Google.** Copilot reaches a real, often enterprise, audience through Windows, Edge, and Microsoft 365. Tracking only Google leaves that visibility unmeasured and unwon.

**Expecting a Copilot-specific hack.** There is no secret Copilot trick. Authority, traffic, and strong Bing fundamentals are what earn citations, the same durable work as everywhere else.

## The Bing-index gaps that quietly cost Copilot citations

When your tracker shows Copilot ignoring you on a question you should win, the cause is usually one of a short list of Bing-specific gaps, and each is fixable.

Your page is not in Bing's index at all. This is the most common and most overlooked. Bing crawls less aggressively than Google, so pages Google picked up automatically can be missing from Bing entirely. If a page is not indexed by Bing, Copilot cannot cite it, full stop. Check coverage in Bing Webmaster Tools and submit anything missing.

Bing does not trust your domain enough yet. Bing leans toward established, higher-traffic domains, so a newer or lower-authority site may be indexed but rarely chosen to ground an answer. The fix is the slow one: build authority and traffic, the strongest predictor of AI citations. There is no shortcut around it, but the effort compounds across every engine.

Your answer is not extractable. Even an indexed, trusted page loses if Copilot cannot lift a clean claim from it. If your answer is buried in narrative or spread across the page, rewrite so the direct response sits near the top under a clear heading.

You are optimizing Google and ignoring Bing. Teams pour everything into Google and assume Bing follows. It does not always, because the index and ranking differ. A few hours in Bing Webmaster Tools, submitting your sitemap and checking coverage, often surfaces the exact gap costing you Copilot citations. That small, neglected effort is precisely why Copilot is winnable when competitors are not looking.

Remember the one fact that changes the strategy: Copilot runs on Bing, so you track Copilot but you fix Bing.
