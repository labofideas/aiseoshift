## Why practice area pages matter

Three reasons:

1. **Clients search by practice area.** Search volume on practice-area queries is 8 to 15x higher than on generic "lawyer" queries.
2. **AI engines need depth.** When ChatGPT answers "do I need a lawyer for X," it pulls from firm pages with substantive depth on X.
3. **Conversion is dramatically higher.** Practice area pages convert at 3 to 5x homepage rates.

## How granular should practice area pages get?

The hierarchy:

### Level 1: Practice area

- Personal Injury
- Family Law
- Criminal Defense
- Estate Planning
- Business Law
- Real Estate

### Level 2: Sub-practice areas

Within Personal Injury:

- Car accidents
- Motorcycle accidents
- Truck accidents
- Premises liability / slip and fall
- Medical malpractice
- Wrongful death
- Product liability
- Dog bites

Within Family Law:

- Divorce
- Child custody
- Child support
- Spousal support
- Property division
- Prenuptial agreements
- Domestic violence

### Level 3: Specific situations or jurisdictions

- Motorcycle accidents in [specific city]
- Uncontested divorce in [state]
- DUI defense for commercial drivers

Build Level 1 pages first. Then Level 2 for your highest-volume areas. Level 3 for the highest-converting niches.

## The anatomy of a practice area page

A great practice area page is 1,800 to 3,000 words. The template:

### Section 1: Plain-language summary

A 60 to 120 word summary AI engines extract.

Example: "Personal injury law in Texas allows accident victims to recover compensation for medical bills, lost wages, pain and suffering, and other damages caused by another party's negligence. Texas has a two-year statute of limitations on most personal injury claims, and follows a modified comparative fault rule, meaning your recovery can be reduced (or eliminated) based on your percentage of fault. Most personal injury cases settle without trial."

### Section 2: What is [practice area]?

A 200 to 400 word explanation. Legal definition, scope, who needs this type of representation.

### Section 3: Common situations

The specific scenarios that bring clients in. AI engines extract these for situational queries.

### Section 4: The legal framework

Relevant statutes, regulations, and jurisdiction-specific rules. This is what differentiates jurisdiction-specific practice area pages from generic legal content.

### Section 5: What to expect during a case

Walk the client through the process. Consultation, investigation, demand letters, negotiations, litigation if necessary, settlement or trial.

Include realistic timelines (most personal injury cases take 6 to 24 months, etc.).

### Section 6: How fees work

Contingency for personal injury, hourly for most litigation, flat fee for some matters. Honest about cost structure. Clients searching for fee information are high-intent.

### Section 7: How our firm handles [practice area]

Your firm's specific approach. Your attorneys, your case selection criteria, your settlement/trial approach. This is what distinguishes your page from generic legal content.

### Section 8: Attorney perspective

A 200 to 400 word quoted section from the attorney who leads this practice area. The strongest possible E-E-A-T signal.

### Section 9: When you need a lawyer now

Urgency framework. Statute of limitations, evidence preservation, why early representation matters. AI Overviews often extract these urgency sections.

### Section 10: FAQs

10 to 15 questions in `FAQPage` schema.

### Section 11: Recent case results (where bar rules permit)

Anonymized case results add powerful trust signals. Check your state bar's specific rules on case result disclosure.

### Section 12: Consultation CTA

Make the next step obvious. Free consultation, phone, online intake form.

## Schema for practice area pages

`LegalService` schema:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "LegalService",
  "name": "Car Accident Lawyer - Smith & Associates",
  "description": "Texas car accident representation for victims of negligent drivers...",
  "provider": {
    "@type": "Attorney",
    "name": "John Smith",
    "url": "https://example.com/attorneys/john-smith"
  },
  "areaServed": [
    { "@type": "State", "name": "Texas" },
    { "@type": "City", "name": "Austin" }
  ],
  "serviceType": "Personal Injury Representation - Car Accidents",
  "priceRange": "Contingency fee"
}
```

Plus `FAQPage` schema for the FAQ section. See the [legal schema post](/blog/legal-schema-markup-attorney-legalservice/) for full implementation.

## Bar compliance considerations

State bar advertising rules constrain practice area page content. Common restrictions:

- No specific outcome guarantees ("we always win")
- No comparative claims ("we are the best")
- Specialty designation rules (cannot claim "specialist" unless certified)
- Required disclaimers in some states
- Past results disclaimers when discussing case results

Have practice area page templates reviewed by your firm's marketing compliance lead before publishing. Most violations are subtle wording issues that are easy to fix once flagged.

## Production cadence

A realistic cadence is one practice area page per week. At that rate, 15 to 25 pages live within 6 months.

### Build a template

Document with placeholders for practice area name, legal framework, FAQ structure.

### Batch the attorney content

Lead attorney for each practice area blocks 1 to 2 hours per week to write or dictate the "Attorney perspective" sections and review drafts.

### Update for legal changes

Statutes change. Court decisions create new precedent. Quarterly review for currency.

## Common mistakes that kill practice area pages

- **No attorney byline.** YMYL extraction fails.
- **Identical structure across practice areas.** Each needs unique content.
- **Outcome guarantees.** Bar violation in most states.
- **No jurisdiction specificity.** Generic legal content underperforms jurisdiction-specific.
- **No urgency or statute of limitations content.** Misses major AI Overview citation opportunity.

## FAQs

### How many practice area pages does a firm need?

Multi-practice firms: 10 to 25 pages covering practice areas and sub-practice areas. Specialty firms: 8 to 15 deep pages on their specialty plus sub-practice variations.

### Can I use AI to draft practice area pages?

For initial structure and legal framework summaries, yes. Attorney perspective and bar compliance review require licensed attorney contribution.

### Should I cover practice areas I do not actively litigate?

If you accept clients in the area but refer out for complex cases, build the page. If you do not handle the area at all, build it only if you have a strong referral relationship with another firm.

### How do I handle multi-jurisdictional firms?

State-specific pages within each practice area. "Personal Injury Texas" and "Personal Injury California" as separate pages, each with state-specific legal framework.
