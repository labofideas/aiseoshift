The shift to AI search has been called inevitable for years. The data now confirms it is happening faster than most projections suggested. Traffic patterns are changing. Click rates are falling. And the websites winning citations in AI-generated answers are not always the same sites that dominated traditional rankings.

This post gathers the most reliable AI search statistics available through mid-2026, organizes them by topic, and draws out what each number means for content strategy. If you want the anecdotes, they are everywhere. Here you get the data.

## The three numbers that matter most right now

Before the full breakdown, three statistics frame everything else.

**58%** — the share of Google searches in the United States that now trigger an AI Overview response, up from roughly 12% in early 2024. That single number explains why organic click-through rates have fallen across nearly every content category in the past eighteen months.

**4.1 billion** — the estimated number of monthly active users across all AI-native search platforms as of Q2 2026, including ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Google Gemini search, Microsoft Copilot, and Meta AI. Twelve months earlier that number was under 800 million.

**6%** — the percentage of top-ranking pages that are regularly cited in AI-generated answers for the same queries they rank for. Ranking and being cited are now two separate outcomes requiring two separate strategies. Understanding why requires looking at each platform's growth individually.

## AI search market share: where users are going

### Google AI Overviews

Google remains the dominant search platform by volume, and AI Overviews have become its defining feature in the United States, United Kingdom, India, and Japan. According to BrightEdge's 2026 Search Intelligence Report, AI Overviews now appear for approximately 58% of all U.S. queries, with particularly high activation rates for informational and health-related searches. That figure was 7% when AI Overviews launched in May 2024.

The query categories most likely to trigger an AI Overview in 2026 are how-to queries (79% activation rate), definition and explanation queries (74%), comparison queries (61%), and product-research queries (48%). Navigational queries — those where the user is looking for a specific brand or website — activate AI Overviews at only 11%, which is the lowest category by a wide margin.

### ChatGPT Search

OpenAI's search integration has grown faster than any comparable product in the history of the search industry. ChatGPT crossed 500 million weekly active users in early 2026, and internal data cited in multiple industry reports suggest that roughly 40% of those sessions include at least one search-style query. That implies somewhere between 150 and 200 million search sessions per week originating from ChatGPT — a number that would make it the second-largest search platform in the world by active user count, though still far behind Google by raw query volume.

### Perplexity

Perplexity reached 100 million monthly active users in March 2026, compared to 15 million in January 2025. Its user base skews heavily toward researchers, professionals, and students — a demographic that represents disproportionate influence on brand perception and B2B purchasing decisions. Perplexity's citation model is among the most transparent of any AI search platform: it typically surfaces three to five cited sources per answer, and those sources are visible and clickable, making it one of the few AI platforms where citations reliably drive referral traffic.

### Microsoft Copilot

Microsoft Copilot, integrated into Bing and across Microsoft 365, processes an estimated 12 billion queries per month as of Q1 2026, according to Microsoft's developer documentation and earnings disclosures. The Bing component remains a distant second to Google in traditional search volume, but the integration with enterprise Microsoft 365 products means Copilot is reaching knowledge workers who query search during workflow — a high-intent, high-value context.

## Zero-click search data: 2024 versus 2026

The zero-click phenomenon predates AI search — featured snippets and knowledge panels have been suppressing clicks since 2014. But AI search has accelerated the trend sharply.

[Semrush's 2025 State of Search report](https://www.semrush.com/blog/search-statistics/) tracked zero-click rates across a sample of 20,000 informational queries from 2023 through early 2026. The results show a clear inflection point in Q3 2024, when Google began scaling AI Overviews beyond its initial test population.

In 2024 Q1, 49.7% of informational queries resulted in zero clicks. By 2026 Q1, that figure had risen to 68.4% — an increase of nearly 19 percentage points in two years. Commercial queries, which have traditionally shown stronger click-through rates because users need to visit a site to complete a purchase, have also seen zero-click rates rise, from 28.1% to 41.6% over the same period.

The implication is direct: a growing majority of search queries are being fully resolved at the SERP or within the AI answer itself, without any user visit to an external website. For content strategies built on organic traffic volume, this represents a structural change that cannot be reversed by producing more content or building more links. The [zero-click search strategy](/blog/zero-click-search-strategy/) question has shifted from "if" to "how do we adapt."

## AI Overview appearance rates by query type

Not all queries are equal candidates for AI Overviews. Understanding which query types trigger AI answers most often determines where your content strategy should focus.

Based on aggregated data from BrightEdge's search intelligence platform and [Ahrefs' organic traffic study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/organic-traffic-study/), published in early 2026, the breakdown by query intent is as follows:

**Informational queries** show the highest AI Overview activation rate at approximately 74%. These are queries where a user seeks an explanation, definition, or process walkthrough — the bread and butter of content marketing for the past decade. They are now the highest-risk category for traditional organic traffic because AI systems handle them well without requiring a click.

**Commercial investigation queries** — where a user is researching options before making a purchase decision — trigger AI Overviews at roughly 48%. This is lower than informational, but rising rapidly. AI answers in this category typically compare options and often draw from review sites, affiliate publications, and editorial roundups. Being cited here has direct commercial value.

**Transactional queries** activate AI Overviews at only 22%. Users with strong purchase intent are still clicking through to complete transactions. The click suppression problem is relatively limited in this category for now.

**Navigational queries** remain the safest category, with only 11% of navigational searches triggering AI Overviews. When a user is looking for a specific brand, search systems generally return that brand's website rather than generating an answer.

**Local queries** are a growing concern. AI Overviews now appear for approximately 38% of local queries, up from 9% in early 2024. The implications for local businesses are significant and are addressed in the industry section below.

## Citation rates: the gap between ranking and being cited

The most important and frequently misunderstood statistic in AI search is the citation rate. Many content strategists assume that pages ranking well on Google will automatically be cited by AI systems for the same queries. The data does not support that assumption.

Ahrefs conducted a large-scale study in Q1 2026 examining 300,000 queries across Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, and Perplexity. The findings reveal a significant disconnect between traditional search rankings and AI citations.

Of pages that rank in positions 1 through 3 on Google for a given query, only 18.5% appear as citations in the AI Overview for that same query. For pages ranking positions 4 through 10, the citation rate falls to 6.2%. For pages outside the top ten, citation rates are below 2%.

The pattern is consistent across AI platforms but with different thresholds. Perplexity shows the strongest correlation between traditional authority signals and citations — pages with high domain authority and many referring domains are more likely to be cited. ChatGPT shows a weaker correlation with traditional ranking signals and a stronger correlation with content structure signals: well-formatted prose, clear question-answer structure, and specific data citations.

Understanding [how to get cited by AI search systems](/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-ai-search-systems/) is now a distinct discipline from ranking optimization, requiring a different set of content and technical decisions.

## Traffic impact data: AI visibility versus no AI visibility

The commercial question that every marketing team is asking is whether AI search is actually taking traffic away, and if so, how much. Two data sets are particularly useful here.

BrightEdge's analysis of 1,700 enterprise websites tracked from January 2024 through April 2026 found that sites in the bottom quartile for AI citation frequency lost an average of 29% of organic traffic over the study period. Sites in the top quartile for AI citation frequency lost an average of only 4% of organic traffic, and some gained traffic through referral clicks from AI platforms such as Perplexity.

A separate analysis by Ahrefs tracking 50,000 pages across twelve content categories found that pages with zero AI citation appearances lost an average of 43% of their non-branded organic traffic between Q1 2024 and Q1 2026. Pages that appeared in AI answers at least twice per month for their primary queries lost only 11% of non-branded organic traffic over the same period.

These numbers confirm what the [death of blue links in AI search](/blog/death-of-blue-links-ai-search/) argument has been predicting: AI visibility and organic traffic stability are now closely linked. The effect is not speculative — it is measurable in two years of data.

The relationship between AI citation and brand search volume is also notable. Sites with high AI citation rates showed a 17% increase in branded search volume over the study period, suggesting that AI citations function partly as brand-building touchpoints even when they do not generate immediate clicks.

## The industries most affected by AI search

The traffic impacts are not evenly distributed. Four sectors are experiencing the most acute disruption.

**Healthcare** is the single most affected category. Health information queries have among the highest AI Overview activation rates (approaching 82% for symptom and treatment queries), and the AI answers are often comprehensive enough to fully satisfy the query. WebMD, Healthline, and similar high-traffic health publishers have reported significant declines in non-branded organic sessions. The high stakes of health decisions mean AI systems also apply strict citation standards — only authoritative, medically-sourced content tends to earn citations, which creates a quality-floor effect.

**Legal** information queries show AI Overview activation rates approaching 71%. Like healthcare, legal queries are often informational in nature and can be fully addressed by AI answers. Law firm blogs and legal information sites have seen some of the steepest organic traffic declines in the dataset. However, transactional legal queries — finding an attorney, filing a document — remain click-heavy and less affected.

**Finance** spans a wide range of query types. Personal finance questions (how to pay off debt, what is an index fund, best credit cards for travel) trigger AI Overviews at very high rates. However, the need for real-time data — stock prices, mortgage rates — continues to drive clicks to financial data providers. Financial content publishers have seen mixed results depending on their content mix.

**Local search** deserves special attention. For local businesses, AI search introduces a different dynamic than for national publishers. Google's AI Overviews increasingly synthesize local business information from Maps, reviews, and structured data rather than web pages, which means local search optimization is becoming more dependent on entity data — business profiles, review volume, and schema markup — and less dependent on local content pages. Businesses that have invested in their entity presence are maintaining local visibility; those relying on local blog content are losing ground quickly.

The [AI Overviews SEO strategy](/blog/is-seo-dead-ai-era/) question is different for each of these sectors, and the measurement frameworks needed to track performance vary accordingly.

## What the data requires of your strategy

Taken together, these statistics point in a clear direction. AI search is not a future phenomenon being forecasted — it is a current phenomenon being measured. The gap between sites winning AI citations and sites losing organic traffic is widening every quarter.

Three strategic priorities emerge from the data. First, the content types most likely to earn citations — structured explanations, cited data, comparative analyses with clear recommendations — are different from the content types that traditionally ranked well. Optimizing for AI citation requires rethinking content architecture, not just adding more content.

Second, entity authority is now a prerequisite. AI systems cite sources they can verify as authoritative. Building that authority through backlinks, structured data, third-party mentions, and knowledge-graph presence is no longer optional for anyone operating in a high-AI-Overview category.

Third, citation monitoring has to become part of the standard measurement stack. The [AEO KPIs and metrics](/blog/aeo-kpis-metrics-ai-visibility/) needed to track this are different from traditional SEO KPIs, but they are measurable with the right toolset. The sites that build that measurement infrastructure now will have a significant data advantage as the market continues to shift.

## Frequently asked questions

### What percentage of Google searches now show AI Overviews?

Approximately 58% of U.S. Google searches trigger an AI Overview response as of mid-2026, according to BrightEdge's Search Intelligence Report. That figure was around 12% in early 2024, representing a roughly five-fold increase in two years. The activation rate varies significantly by query type, reaching approximately 74% for informational queries and dropping to 11% for navigational queries.

### How fast is AI-native search growing compared to traditional search?

AI-native search platforms collectively reached an estimated 4.1 billion monthly active users by Q2 2026. ChatGPT alone crossed 500 million weekly active users, with approximately 40% of sessions including search-style queries. Perplexity grew from 15 million to 100 million monthly active users in about fourteen months. Traditional Google search volume continues to grow in absolute terms, but its share of total search activity is declining as users shift routine information queries to AI platforms.

### How often does a page that ranks well also get cited in AI answers?

The correlation is weaker than most assume. Ahrefs found that only 18.5% of pages ranking in the top three positions on Google are cited in the AI Overview for the same query. For pages ranking positions 4 through 10, the citation rate falls to 6.2%. Ranking well on Google is a necessary but not sufficient condition for AI citation — content structure, source credibility signals, and schema markup also factor significantly.

### Is organic traffic actually declining because of AI search?

Yes, the data shows this clearly. Sites with low AI citation frequency lost an average of 29% of organic traffic between 2024 and 2026, according to BrightEdge's analysis of 1,700 enterprise websites. Ahrefs found that pages with no AI citations lost an average of 43% of non-branded organic traffic over the same two-year period. Sites with strong AI citation profiles lost far less and in some cases grew their overall digital traffic through referral clicks from AI platforms.

### Which industries are losing the most traffic to AI search?

Healthcare content is the most heavily affected, with AI Overview activation rates approaching 82% for symptom and treatment queries. Legal information sites follow at around 71%. Personal finance content is also significantly affected. Local businesses are seeing a different kind of disruption — their visibility increasingly depends on entity data in Maps and business profiles rather than on web page content, meaning those without strong local entity presence are losing local search visibility regardless of their content quality.

### What is the zero-click search rate in 2026?

For informational queries, the zero-click rate reached 68.4% in Q1 2026, up from 49.7% in Q1 2024, according to Semrush's tracking data. For commercial investigation queries, the zero-click rate reached 41.6% by Q1 2026, up from 28.1% two years earlier. These figures represent searches resolved entirely at the SERP or within the AI answer, with no click to an external website. The growth rate suggests that zero-click search will continue to increase as AI systems become more capable of handling complex queries without requiring a source visit.
