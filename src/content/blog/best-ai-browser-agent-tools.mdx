Perplexity Comet is the default pick for most people in 2026: it is completely free, runs on Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android, and delivers strong agentic browsing and citation-backed research without platform restrictions. ChatGPT Atlas has the deepest automation stack, Agent Mode, browser memories, tab-level conversations, but is Mac-only and costs $20 a month for its Pro tier. Dia is the closest alternative to both, with customizable "Skills" for repetitive workflows, though its ecosystem is still maturing. An AI browser agent goes beyond a chat sidebar: it can browse, click, fill forms, and complete multi-step tasks across tabs on your behalf, which is the entire category's reason to exist.

Three real contenders, and the honest verdict as of mid-2026 is that your operating system decides more of this than any feature comparison does.

## What makes a browser "agentic"

The category name is new, but the idea is specific: instead of a chat window bolted onto a browser, the AI can act inside the page itself, clicking links, filling forms, navigating across tabs, and completing a multi-step task you describe in plain language, while you supervise. That is the line separating an AI browser agent from a browser with a chatbot sidebar, which most mainstream browsers already have. If it cannot take real actions across a real session on your behalf, it is not in this category yet.

### Perplexity Comet: the default pick for most people

Comet's core browser is free with no platform restrictions, running on Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android, which alone sets it apart from Atlas's Mac-only limitation. Its standout strength follows directly from Perplexity's identity: citation-backed answers and cross-tab context that make it genuinely strong for research-heavy work, academic tasks, or any job where tracing a claim back to its source matters. It delivers real agentic features, not a stripped-down free tier designed to push you toward a paid plan.

For the majority of people trying an AI browser for the first time, Comet is the sensible default: it costs nothing, works on whatever device you already have, and its research-grade synthesis is close to unmatched among the three.

### ChatGPT Atlas: the deepest automation, if you are on a Mac

Atlas has the strongest automation stack of the three: Agent Mode for multi-step autonomous tasks, browser memories that persist context across sessions, tab-level conversations, and a page-aware sidebar that understands what you are looking at without you explaining it. For a Mac user who wants a browser that genuinely handles complex, multi-step web tasks with minimal hand-holding, Atlas's depth is the strongest available right now.

The two real limitations are platform and price: it is Mac-only, which rules it out entirely for Windows and Android users, and its Pro tier costs $20 a month for the full automation experience, a real cost when Comet offers comparable agentic features free.

### Dia: built for people with repetitive browser workflows

Dia's differentiator is "Skills," customizable automations you build once and reuse, which makes it the strongest option specifically for people with recurring browser tasks: a content creator who needs consistent formatting across posts, a researcher who checks the same set of sites every morning, or anyone who fills out similar forms repeatedly. Its free tier is limited, and unlocking unlimited Skills use requires its Pro plan.

Its ecosystem is younger and thinner than Comet's or Atlas's, so expect fewer built-in integrations and a smaller community solving edge cases, but for a genuinely repetitive workflow, a purpose-built Skill can save more time than either competitor's general-purpose agent mode.

## The real tradeoff nobody puts in the marketing

Letting a browser act on your behalf, click, fill forms, navigate, means it needs meaningful access to your browsing session, and that access is the actual price of the convenience, not the subscription fee.

Browser memories, the feature that makes Atlas and similar agents feel smart across sessions, work by retaining context about what you did and looked at. Decide deliberately whether you want that retained, and check each tool's current settings for clearing or limiting it, rather than assuming a sensible default.

Agentic actions on sites that require login, banking, shopping, work accounts, deserve a manual pause before you approve an autonomous multi-step task. All three vendors build in confirmation steps for sensitive actions, but the whole point of agent mode is reduced friction, which is exactly the setting where a rushed approval causes a real problem.

None of this means avoid these tools. It means treat "let the browser act for me" with the same care you would give any tool with your login credentials, not as a neutral productivity feature.

## Why this matters for search visibility

AI browsers are a new surface where your content can be read, acted on, or cited, and their agentic nature changes what "getting found" means. When Comet answers a question with citations, or Atlas's agent researches something across several tabs, they are retrieving and evaluating your pages the same way any AI answer engine does, which means the fundamentals covered in [how AI search retrieves content](/blog/how-ai-search-retrieves-content/) and [AI citation building](/seo/ai-citation-building/) apply here too. A page that is clear, well-structured, and easy to extract from is easier for an agentic browser to act on correctly, not just easier for a human to read.

This is also an early-stage category, similar to where Perplexity itself was a couple of years ago: competition for visibility inside these browsers' answers is lower now than it will be once adoption grows, which is the same early-mover argument covered in [DeepSeek rank tracking](/blog/rank-tracking-tool-deepseek/) for a different reason.

## The bottom line

Install Perplexity Comet first: it is free, works everywhere, and its research quality is the strongest of the three. Move to ChatGPT Atlas if you are on a Mac and want the deepest available automation, accepting the cost and platform limit. Consider Dia specifically if you have a genuinely repetitive browser task that a custom Skill would save real time on.

There is no single winner yet, and picking based on your operating system first will get you further than picking based on a feature list.

## FAQs

### Which AI browser agent is best in 2026?

There is no single winner across every use case. Perplexity Comet is the default for most people: it is free, cross-platform, and strong for cited research. ChatGPT Atlas has the deepest automation stack but is Mac-only and costs $20 a month for Pro. Dia suits people with specific repetitive workflows they want to automate with custom Skills.

### Is Perplexity Comet really free?

Yes, its core browser and agentic browsing features are free with no platform restrictions, running on Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android. This distinguishes it from ChatGPT Atlas, whose full automation experience requires a $20-a-month Pro subscription and is limited to macOS.

### Why is ChatGPT Atlas only available on Mac?

Atlas launched as a macOS-only product, which OpenAI has not yet expanded to other platforms as of mid-2026. This is its most-criticized limitation, especially since Perplexity Comet offers comparable agentic features for free across every major platform, making Atlas a harder recommendation for non-Mac users regardless of its automation depth.

### Is it safe to let an AI browser act on my behalf?

Treat it with the same caution as any tool with access to your login sessions. Browser memories retain context across visits, which is convenient but worth reviewing in each tool's privacy settings. Pause and manually review before approving any autonomous multi-step action on a site involving banking, payments, or work credentials, since the reduced friction that makes agent mode useful is also what makes a rushed approval risky.
