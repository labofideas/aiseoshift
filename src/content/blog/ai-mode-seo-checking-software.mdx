Google AI Mode reached one billion users faster than any previous Google interface. It returns synthesized AI-generated answers powered by Gemini 3.5 Flash, with no traditional blue link results visible by default.

If your SEO strategy does not include a way to check your presence in AI Mode, you are missing an interface that a significant portion of Google's user base is now seeing instead of the results you optimized for.

Here is what the software landscape for AI Mode SEO looks like.

## What checking AI Mode SEO actually requires

AI Mode checking is fundamentally different from rank checking. You are not asking "what position does my page appear at." You are asking:

- Does my content appear as a cited source in AI Mode answers?
- Does my brand get mentioned in AI Mode synthesized responses?
- What do competitors appear in that I do not?
- What structured data or content signals are causing citation decisions?

The software tools that answer these questions fall into three categories: monitoring platforms, audit tools, and structured data validators.

## Monitoring platforms

### SemRush

[SemRush](https://semrush.sjv.io/PyEbGz) is the most accessible entry point for checking AI Mode presence. Its Position Tracking tool flags keywords where AI Overviews are showing for your tracked terms, and the AI toolkit surfaces which domains are being cited in those AI answers.

For AI Mode specifically, [SemRush](https://semrush.sjv.io/PyEbGz) tracks:

- Keyword-level AI Overview trigger rates in your tracked project
- Whether your domain appears as a cited source or is absent
- Competitor domain presence in AI answers for your tracked query set
- Historical trend data showing whether your AI Mode presence is growing or shrinking

The Sensor tool within SemRush also tracks Google algorithm volatility, which is useful context when your AI Mode citation rate changes suddenly.

### BrightEdge Generative Parser

For enterprise teams managing large query sets, BrightEdge's Generative Parser provides the most granular AI Mode checking available. It can process thousands of queries simultaneously, track share-of-voice across an entire competitor set, and flag specific URL-level citation changes.

BrightEdge also provides the most reliable data on YouTube citations within AI Overviews -- a growing source of AI Mode citation that most monitoring platforms do not track well.

### Authoritas

Authoritas checks AI Mode presence alongside ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Copilot in a single interface. For teams that need to check AI Mode presence in context with other AI engine performance, Authoritas prevents you from optimizing for Google AI Mode in isolation while neglecting the other surfaces your content appears in.

## Audit tools

### Screaming Frog with AI crawl simulation

Screaming Frog does not check AI Mode directly, but it is essential for the prerequisite audit. AI Mode cites sources that Googlebot (and related AI crawlers) can reliably access and parse. Screaming Frog audits:

- JavaScript rendering issues that block AI crawlers from seeing content
- Structured data implementation errors that reduce citation eligibility
- Thin or duplicate content that dilutes domain authority and reduces citation probability
- Crawl depth issues that bury content Googlebot is not reaching frequently

Run a full Screaming Frog crawl before diagnosing an AI Mode citation problem -- frequently the issue is technical, not content-related.

### Google Search Console

Google Search Console is the only source of verified first-party data on your AI Overviews performance. Since AI Mode answers often draw on similar source signals as AI Overviews, GSC data serves as a usable proxy for AI Mode content performance.

The AI Overviews report in GSC shows:
- Which queries are generating AI Overview appearances for your site
- Which pages are being cited
- Impression and click-through rate data for those appearances

Check the filter for "AI Overviews" in the Search type selector to isolate this data.

### seoClarity AI Search Grader

seoClarity's AI Search Grader analyzes why your content is or is not being selected as an AI Mode source. Rather than just reporting citation presence, it scores your content against the attributes that correlate with AI citation selection:

- Topical comprehensiveness relative to competing sources
- Structured data coverage
- E-E-A-T signal strength on the page and author level
- Answer format quality (does the page answer the question clearly in its first few hundred words)

The Grader produces a ranked list of recommended optimizations specific to each page you audit.

## Structured data validators

AI Mode, like AI Overviews, gives citation preference to well-structured content. These tools verify your structured data implementation:

**Google Rich Results Test** -- validates Schema.org markup and confirms which rich result types your pages are eligible for. FAQ, HowTo, and Article schema are particularly relevant for AI Mode citation.

**Schema Markup Validator** -- the broader Schema.org validator that catches markup errors the Google-specific tester does not flag.

**Merkle Schema Markup Generator** -- useful for building correct FAQ and Article schema without writing JSON-LD by hand.

## The AI Mode SEO checking workflow

Run this audit sequence quarterly or after major content changes:

1. Pull your AI Overview presence report from [SemRush](https://semrush.sjv.io/PyEbGz) -- identify which target queries show AI Mode features and whether you appear
2. Run Screaming Frog on pages that should be appearing but are not -- find crawl or render blockers
3. Validate structured data on those pages using the Google Rich Results Test
4. Check GSC AI Overviews impressions for those URLs -- confirm whether Googlebot is seeing the content
5. Run the seoClarity AI Grader on pages with confirmed crawlability but poor citation rate -- address content-level gaps
6. Recheck with SemRush four to six weeks after making changes

## What the software will not tell you

No AI Mode checking software provides complete transparency. Gemini's source selection involves signals these tools approximate but do not fully replicate. The tools are most useful for:

- Identifying gaps (where you should appear but do not)
- Attributing changes (what happened after an update)
- Competitor analysis (what signals winning pages have that yours lack)

They are less useful for predicting exactly which sources will be cited for a new piece of content before it is published. That still requires judgment informed by the patterns the tools surface.
