Six developments from the past few weeks that change how you measure, control, or earn AI search visibility. Each one comes with what happened and what to do about it.

## 1. GSC's Generative AI Performance Reports Are Now Live for Everyone

**What happened:** Google [announced dedicated generative AI performance reports](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) for Search Console in June, and the rollout reached all properties worldwide at the end of August. The report isolates impressions from AI Overviews, AI Mode, and generative AI features in Discover, broken down by page, country, and device with hourly to monthly granularity. One caveat when reading historical data: Search Console had a reporting bug around August 12 that caused temporary impression and click drops, so don't read that dip as a real visibility loss.

**What to do:** Open the report this week and record your baseline weekly AI impression level per page. There is no click, CTR, or query data yet. You get visibility without its traffic value, so pair the report with your own query-level checks. Our guide to [measuring AI visibility](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/) covers how to combine the two.

## 2. You Can Now Block Your Content from Google's AI Features

**What happened:** Google [added a control](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/) that lets site owners keep their content out of AI Overviews, AI Mode, and AI features in Discover while staying in regular web results. Until now the only lever was blocking Google-Extended (which affects Gemini training, not Search AI features) or snippet controls that also hurt classic listings.

**What to do:** For most sites, don't use it. AI surfaces are where a growing share of discovery happens, and opting out removes you from answers your competitors will fill. The control matters if you run paywalled or licensing-sensitive content where an AI summary substitutes for a visit. Decide from data: if your [zero-click reality](/blog/zero-click-search-strategy/) shows AI surfaces cannibalizing rather than adding, you now have a scalpel instead of a sledgehammer.

## 3. The August Spam Update Landed Fast, and the Volatility Didn't Stop

**What happened:** Google's August 2026 spam update started August 18 and completed August 21, an unusually fast three-day rollout, per [Search Engine Roundtable's September webmaster report](https://www.seroundtable.com/sept-2026-google-webmaster-report-41979.html). Ranking volatility continued into September, with unconfirmed fluctuations both before and after the official window.

**What to do:** If your traffic stepped down between August 18 and 21, treat it as a spam-policy problem rather than a general content-quality problem. Scaled content patterns, expired-domain abuse, and site-reputation abuse are the usual targets. If your drop doesn't line up with those dates, resist the urge to blame the update; check GSC's date dimension before diagnosing. We wrote about reading update damage honestly in our [May 2026 core update coverage](/blog/google-may-2026-core-update/).

## 4. AI Mode Is Merging Into AI Overviews, With More Links

**What happened:** Google keeps pushing AI Mode deeper into the default search experience. AI Overviews now hand off into AI Mode follow-ups with context preserved, link carousel cards appeared for developing stories, and Google says it is surfacing more links to original content inside both surfaces. AI Mode also gained travel tools (flight price tracking, hotel booking), and a test of AI-generated images inside Overviews was paused shortly after launch.

**What to do:** The "more links" shift is the opportunity. Link cards and carousels are earned by pages that answer one sub-question cleanly, not by pillar pages that answer everything vaguely. Structure content so each H2 section is a self-contained, extractable answer. This is the same passage-level game we describe in the [SEO in 2026 guide](/blog/seo-in-2026-complete-guide/).

## 5. Gemini 3.8 Flash Now Powers AI Mode

**What happened:** Google shipped Gemini 3.8 Flash on September 2 and [rolled it into AI Mode](https://searchengineland.com/gemini-3-8-flash-rolling-out-in-google-search-486630), the third Flash release in six weeks, following the I/O-announced intelligent Search box built on the 3.5 Flash generation. Faster models mean deeper query fan-out per search: more sub-queries issued, more sources evaluated, more grounding per answer.

**What to do:** Model upgrades reshuffle citations even when nothing changed on your site. If you track AI citations weekly, annotate September 2 on your log. Movement near that date is likely model churn, not content performance. Chase the trend line, not the week.

## 6. Small Print That Matters

**"goto" redirect parameters:** Google introduced goto URL parameters on search result clicks, which broke some third-party rank trackers until they updated. If your tracker showed strange data in early September, check for a tool update before trusting it.

**Reddit gets no ranking preference:** Google confirmed Reddit receives no special ranking treatment. Visibility of Reddit results reflects ranking systems, not a deal. Plan community-content strategy on merit, not on assumed favoritism.

**Site reputation abuse in the EEA:** Google won't enforce its site reputation policy in the European Economic Area for now. That regulatory carve-out is worth knowing if you operate parasite-adjacent content in both markets, and it is a reminder that enforcement geography now varies.

**"No special optimization needed" for AI:** Google repeated that generative AI responses require no special optimization. Take it the way experienced SEOs took "just make great content": true at the philosophy level, useless at the tactics level. Structure, extractability, and entity clarity demonstrably affect whether the same great content gets cited.

## The Thread Running Through All of It

September's pattern is consolidation. The measurement layer (GSC reports), the control layer (AI-feature blocking), and the surface itself (AI Mode inside Overviews) are all becoming official, first-party infrastructure.

The experimental phase of AI search is ending. The operational phase, where visibility is tracked and managed like any other channel, is what 2027 planning should assume.
