Generic AI SEO advice -- "write E-E-A-T content," "use schema markup" -- is everywhere. This guide skips the overview and goes straight to the five technical and strategic moves that have the clearest, most direct impact on AI engine citation rates.

## Strategy 1: Build entity completeness, not just keyword density

AI engines do not rank keywords. They recognize entities -- people, organizations, places, products, concepts -- and assess how trustworthy and well-connected each entity is within their knowledge graph.

Your brand is an entity. So are your authors, your products, and the topics you cover. Entity completeness means AI engines have enough consistent, cross-referenced information to confidently treat you as a reliable source.

**The technical moves:**

- Create a complete Organization schema on your About page with `name`, `url`, `logo`, `foundingDate`, `sameAs` pointing to your social profiles and Wikipedia page if one exists
- Add Person schema to every author page with `name`, `jobTitle`, `url`, `sameAs` linking to LinkedIn, Twitter/X, and any professional profiles
- Ensure your brand name, address, and description are consistent across your site, Google Business Profile, Wikipedia, Wikidata, LinkedIn, and Crunchbase
- Build or claim a Wikidata entry for your organization -- this is direct input into Google's knowledge graph

Entity completeness is a one-time investment with compounding returns. AI engines update their knowledge of entities gradually. The longer your entity signals have been consistent and cross-referenced, the more weight they carry.

## Strategy 2: Restructure pages to answer questions in the first 50 words

AI engines synthesize answers from source material. They look for pages where the answer to the query appears near the top of the content, clearly stated, before the context and elaboration.

This is the opposite of how most content is written, which builds context first and delivers the answer later.

**The technical moves:**

- For any page targeting a question-format query (how, what, why, best, should), open with a direct answer in the first paragraph -- two to four sentences maximum
- Use the question itself as an H2 heading, then answer it immediately below before any elaboration
- Add an FAQ section at the end of every substantial content page with five to ten questions and direct answers -- these are highly citeable units
- Implement FAQPage schema on pages with FAQ sections and HowTo schema on process pages

The pattern that AI engines cite most: a clear question heading, a direct answer of 40-80 words immediately below it, then supporting detail. Reformat your highest-traffic pages to this structure before creating new content.

## Strategy 3: Implement comprehensive structured data, not just the basics

Most sites implement basic Article schema and stop. Full structured data implementation is a citation signal multiplier because it makes content interpretable by AI systems at a machine level.

**The technical moves:**

**FAQPage schema** -- add to any page with question-and-answer content. Each FAQ unit is independently citeable.

**HowTo schema** -- add to any process, tutorial, or step-by-step content. Step-level markup makes individual steps citeable.

**Article schema with author markup** -- include `author` with Person schema nested inside, `datePublished`, `dateModified`, `publisher` with Organization schema, and `about` pointing to the primary topic entity.

**BreadcrumbList schema** -- signals content hierarchy and helps AI engines understand how a page fits into your overall site structure.

**Speakable schema** -- marks the sections of a page most suitable for voice and AI summarization. Explicitly recommended by Google for content that should appear in AI-generated answers.

Validate everything with the Google Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator. Invalid markup is treated as absent markup.

## Strategy 4: Establish topical authority through systematic coverage, not breadth

AI engines cite sources that demonstrate deep expertise on a topic, not sources that touch many topics lightly. Topical authority is built through systematic, comprehensive coverage of a defined subject area.

**The technical moves:**

- Define the four to six core topics your site will own completely
- For each core topic, build a hub page (comprehensive guide) and a cluster of supporting posts covering every meaningful subtopic and related question
- Interlink the hub page to every cluster page and every cluster page back to the hub
- Audit your topic clusters quarterly using tools like Clearscope or Frase -- identify questions your cluster does not answer and fill them

The goal is to be the most complete source on your core topics, not the most expansive source across many topics. An AI engine that has cited you ten times on a topic will cite you more readily on the eleventh question in that topic than a site with one strong page on that topic and nothing else.

## Strategy 5: Optimize specifically for the AI engines you care about most

The biggest strategic mistake in AI SEO is treating all AI engines as a single target. Perplexity's citation criteria differ from Claude's. Bing powers ChatGPT and Copilot with different signals than Google uses for AI Overviews. Each engine has its own emphasis.

**Platform-specific technical moves:**

**For Google AI Overviews and AI Mode ([see our full guide](/ai-overviews-seo)):**
- Core Web Vitals above threshold (AI Overviews favor fast, reliable pages)
- Server-side rendering for JavaScript-heavy sites (Googlebot struggles with client-rendered content)
- YouTube companion videos for your top content (BrightEdge found YouTube as a top-three citation source)

**For ChatGPT and Bing-powered engines ([see Bing SEO guide](/bing-seo)):**
- Allow Bingbot explicitly in robots.txt
- Submit sitemap to Bing Webmaster Tools
- Implement IndexNow for instant indexing signals
- Exact-match title tags with primary questions

**For Perplexity ([see Perplexity SEO guide](/perplexity-seo)):**
- Recent publication and update dates visible on page
- Statistical data with source citations
- Academic and research tone in content covering factual topics

**For Claude ([see Claude SEO guide](/claude-seo)):**
- Original research and data, not aggregated content
- Conservative factual claims with verification links
- Detailed methodology explanations

**For Copilot and Microsoft surfaces ([see Copilot SEO guide](/copilot-seo)):**
- Strong LinkedIn presence (Microsoft owns LinkedIn and uses it as a signal)
- Microsoft product integrations mentioned where relevant
- B2B-friendly content structure

Implement the general strategies (1-4) first. Then layer in the platform-specific tactics for the two or three engines most relevant to where your audience is.
