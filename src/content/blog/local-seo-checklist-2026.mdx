Local SEO used to be a relatively contained discipline. Claim your Google Business Profile, get some reviews, make sure your name and address are consistent across the web, and you were ahead of most competitors.

In 2026, local search is an AI-mediated experience. When someone asks "best Italian restaurant near me" or "emergency plumber in Austin," Google does not just show the local pack anymore. It synthesizes information from your Google Business Profile, your reviews, your website content, and third-party mentions into an AI Overview that answers the query before anyone clicks anything.

The businesses that show up in these AI-powered local answers are the ones that have every signal aligned. The ones with gaps get skipped. This checklist covers every step, in order of priority, so you can work through it systematically.

## Step 1: Google Business Profile setup and optimization

Your Google Business Profile accounts for roughly a third of all local pack ranking influence. This is the foundation everything else builds on.

### Claim and verify your profile

If you have not claimed your Google Business Profile at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com/), do that first. Verification methods include postcard, phone, email, or instant verification for some businesses. Until your profile is verified, it will not appear in the local pack.

### Complete every field

Profiles filled out entirely receive 70 percent more visits and appear 18 times more often in search results than incomplete profiles. Do not leave anything blank.

**Business name.** Use your exact legal business name. Do not stuff keywords into your business name. Google penalizes this and it can get your profile suspended.

**Primary category.** Choose the single most accurate category for your business. This is the most important category signal for local pack ranking. Be specific. "Italian Restaurant" is better than "Restaurant."

**Secondary categories.** Add all relevant secondary categories. A dentist might add "Cosmetic Dentist," "Pediatric Dentist," and "Emergency Dental Service." Each category expands which searches your profile appears for.

**Business description.** Write a natural, keyword-rich description of your business. Include your primary services, your location, and what makes you different. Do not stuff keywords. Write for humans first, then check that your target terms appear naturally.

**Hours of operation.** Keep these accurate and updated. Include special hours for holidays. Inaccurate hours are one of the most common reasons users leave negative reviews.

**Phone number and website.** Use a local phone number, not a toll-free number. Link directly to your website homepage or a dedicated landing page for that location.

**Service area or address.** If you serve customers at your location, list your full address. If you travel to customers (plumber, electrician, mobile services), define your service area instead.

**Photos and videos.** Upload at least 10 high-quality photos: exterior, interior, team, products, and services. Businesses with photos get 42 percent more direction requests. Add new photos monthly to signal an active, maintained profile.

**Products and services.** List every product and service with descriptions and pricing where applicable. Google uses this information for matching your profile to specific queries.

### Google Business Profile posts

Publish posts weekly. Google Business Profile posts appear in your knowledge panel and signal that your business is active. Post about offers, events, updates, and news. Include a call-to-action button (Book, Call, Learn more) on every post.

## Step 2: On-page local SEO

Your website needs to reinforce every signal your Google Business Profile sends. Consistency between your profile and your site is a core ranking factor.

### NAP consistency

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone number. Your NAP must be identical everywhere it appears: your website, your Google Business Profile, directories, social profiles, and any other online mention. Even small inconsistencies (like "St." versus "Street" or different phone number formats) can confuse search engines.

Place your full NAP in the footer of every page on your site. Use [LocalBusiness schema markup](/seo/schema-markup/) to make it machine-readable.

### Location-specific pages

If you serve multiple areas, create a dedicated page for each location or service area. Each page should include:

- The location name in the title tag and H1
- A unique description of what you offer in that area (not copy-pasted across locations)
- Your NAP for that specific location
- LocalBusiness schema with geo-coordinates
- Embedded Google Map
- Location-specific testimonials or case studies

Do not create thin, template-based location pages that differ only by city name. Google's spam policies specifically target this pattern. Each page needs genuinely unique content that demonstrates you actually serve that area.

### Title tags and meta descriptions

Include your primary service and location in title tags: "Emergency Plumber in Austin, TX | [Business Name]." Keep titles under 60 characters. Write meta descriptions that include your location, a key differentiator, and a call to action: "24/7 emergency plumbing in Austin. Licensed, insured, average 30-minute response. Call now."

### Local content

Publish content that demonstrates local expertise: neighborhood guides, local event coverage, partnerships with local organizations, case studies featuring local clients. This content signals to both Google and AI systems that you are genuinely part of the local community, not just targeting a keyword.

## Step 3: Reviews and reputation management

Google's AI now performs sentiment analysis on review text, extracting specific service mentions, emotional tone, and resolution patterns. The quantity, quality, and recency of your reviews directly affect both local pack ranking and AI local answer inclusion.

### Build a review generation system

Ask every satisfied customer for a review. The most effective methods: send a follow-up email or text with a direct link to your Google review page within 24 hours of service. Make it one click. The harder you make it, the fewer reviews you get.

Target at least 5 new reviews per month for most local businesses. Businesses in competitive markets may need 10 or more.

### Respond to every review

Respond to every review, positive and negative. Thank positive reviewers specifically for what they mentioned. For negative reviews, respond professionally, acknowledge the issue, and offer to resolve it offline. Google evaluates response rate and quality as trust signals.

### Monitor review sentiment

Track what customers mention in reviews. If multiple reviews mention long wait times, address the operational issue. If reviews consistently praise a specific service, feature that service more prominently in your marketing. Review sentiment data is intelligence about your business that also happens to affect your rankings.

### Diversify review platforms

Google reviews are the most important, but do not ignore Yelp, Facebook, industry-specific platforms (Healthgrades for doctors, Avvo for lawyers, Houzz for contractors), and Trustpilot. AI systems pull review data from multiple sources. A strong presence across platforms reinforces your authority.

## Step 4: Local citations and directory listings

Local citations are mentions of your NAP on other websites. They reinforce your business's existence, location, and relevance to local search.

### Core directories

Ensure your business is listed accurately on these high-priority directories:

- Google Business Profile (already covered)
- Apple Maps / Apple Business Connect
- Bing Places
- Yelp
- Facebook Business
- Better Business Bureau
- Industry-specific directories for your vertical

### Data aggregators

Submit your NAP to the major data aggregators that feed hundreds of smaller directories: Data Axle (formerly Infogroup), Neustar Localeze, and Foursquare. Accuracy here cascades across the directory ecosystem.

### Audit for inconsistencies

Use tools like [Semrush](https://semrush.sjv.io/PyEbGz) Listing Management, BrightLocal, or Moz Local to audit your citations across the web. Fix any NAP inconsistencies you find. Even old, incorrect listings on directories you have never heard of can confuse search engines.

## Step 5: Local link building

Local backlinks signal to Google that real organizations in your area recognize your business. These links carry geographic relevance that generic backlinks do not.

### Effective local link sources

**Local news and media.** Sponsor local events, participate in community initiatives, or provide expert commentary on local issues. Local news coverage earns links with strong geographic signals.

**Chamber of Commerce and business associations.** Membership in your local Chamber of Commerce typically includes a directory listing with a backlink. Industry associations offer similar opportunities.

**Local partnerships.** Cross-promote with complementary local businesses. A wedding photographer links to the florist they recommend. A dentist links to the orthodontist they refer patients to. These reciprocal relationships are natural and valued by search engines.

**Community sponsorships.** Sponsoring local sports teams, school events, or charity organizations earns backlinks from .org and .edu domains that carry significant authority.

**Local guest content.** Write for local blogs, magazines, or business publications. This builds both links and local brand awareness.

## Step 6: Technical local SEO

### LocalBusiness schema markup

Implement LocalBusiness (or a more specific subtype like Restaurant, Dentist, or Plumber) schema on your homepage and location pages. Include:

- Business name, address, phone
- Geo-coordinates (latitude, longitude)
- Opening hours
- Price range
- Service area
- Aggregate rating (if you have reviews on your site)

This structured data helps both Google and AI systems understand your business as a local entity. For implementation details, see our [schema markup guide](/seo/schema-markup/).

### Mobile optimization

Over 60 percent of local searches happen on mobile devices. Your site must load fast, display properly, and have click-to-call functionality on mobile. Test with Google's PageSpeed Insights and fix any mobile usability issues.

### Page speed

Local searchers are often in a hurry. If your site takes more than 3 seconds to load, you are losing potential customers before they see your content. Optimize images, enable caching, minimize JavaScript, and consider a CDN.

### HTTPS

Your site must use HTTPS. Google flags non-HTTPS sites as "Not Secure" in the browser, which destroys trust for local businesses where credibility is everything.

## Step 7: AI local search optimization

This is the 2026-specific step that most local SEO guides miss entirely. Google's AI Overviews now answer local queries by synthesizing your business data, reviews, and website content into a direct response.

### How AI local search works

When someone asks "best dentist for kids in Denver," Google's AI reads your Google Business Profile, your website content about pediatric dentistry, your reviews that mention kids, and third-party mentions of your practice. It synthesizes all of this into an answer that may or may not include a link to your site.

### Optimize for AI local answers

**Answer local questions on your website.** Create FAQ content that addresses the specific questions people ask about your services in your area. "How much does emergency plumbing cost in Austin?" or "What should I look for in a Denver pediatric dentist?" Direct answers to these questions increase your chances of being included in AI local responses.

**Ensure your reviews mention specific services.** When asking for reviews, suggest that customers mention the specific service they received. "Would you mind mentioning the teeth whitening in your review?" Reviews that mention specific services give AI systems more data to work with.

**Maintain consistent entity signals.** Your business name, services, and location should be consistent across your website, Google Business Profile, social profiles, and directory listings. AI systems build an entity profile from all of these sources. Inconsistencies reduce confidence.

**Allow AI crawlers to access your site.** Check your robots.txt to ensure GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot are not blocked. Local AI search results are increasingly influenced by AI systems beyond Google. For the technical details, see our [LLM crawler optimization guide](/blog/llm-crawler-optimization/).

## Step 8: Ongoing monitoring and maintenance

Local SEO is not a one-time project. It requires ongoing maintenance.

### Monthly tasks

- Publish 4 or more Google Business Profile posts
- Respond to all new reviews within 48 hours
- Add new photos to your Google Business Profile
- Check NAP consistency across directories
- Monitor local keyword rankings
- Track AI local search appearances for your target queries

### Quarterly tasks

- Audit all citations for accuracy
- Update website content with current information
- Refresh location pages with new testimonials or case studies
- Review and update business hours for upcoming holidays
- Analyze competitor local SEO tactics

### Annual tasks

- Complete local SEO audit using [Semrush](https://semrush.sjv.io/PyEbGz) or BrightLocal
- Refresh all directory listings
- Update all schema markup
- Review and refine local content strategy
- Set local SEO goals for the next year

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does local SEO take to work?

Most businesses see initial improvements in local pack visibility within 4 to 8 weeks of completing the foundational steps (Google Business Profile optimization, NAP consistency, initial reviews). Significant ranking improvements in competitive markets typically take 3 to 6 months of consistent effort across all checklist areas.

### What is the most important local SEO factor?

Google Business Profile optimization is the single most important factor, accounting for roughly a third of all local pack ranking influence. Within your profile, the primary category selection and review volume are the two strongest individual signals.

### How many Google reviews do I need?

There is no universal number, but aim for at least 20 reviews to establish credibility and 50 or more to compete in most markets. More important than total count is review recency. 5 new reviews per month signals an active, trusted business.

### Does local SEO help with AI search?

Yes. Google's AI Overviews now answer local queries by synthesizing Google Business Profile data, reviews, and website content. Businesses with complete profiles, strong reviews, and locally relevant website content are more likely to be included in AI-generated local answers.

### Do I need a website for local SEO?

Technically, you can rank in the local pack with only a Google Business Profile. But a website dramatically improves your chances. It provides additional content for Google and AI systems to evaluate, hosts your schema markup, and gives you control over your messaging. For competitive markets, a website is essential.
