You get more backlinks by publishing something genuinely worth citing, then getting it in front of the people who write and publish in your space. The reliable methods are digital PR with original data, creating linkable reference assets, guest contributions on real publications, reclaiming unlinked mentions, and earning links from suppliers, partners, and local organizations. Buying links or blasting outreach templates does not work anymore, and it carries real risk. Links are earned, and the earning starts with the asset.

A link is a vote someone casts with their own reputation. You have to give them a reason.

## The methods that still work

Ranked roughly from quickest to hardest.

**Reclaim unlinked mentions.** Someone already named your brand, product, or research in an article and did not link it. Find those mentions and ask, politely, for the link. This is the highest-converting outreach there is, because the writer already decided you were worth referencing.

**Earn links you already have a claim to.** Suppliers, partners, industry associations, local business groups, and the tools you integrate with often have "customers" or "members" pages. Get listed. These are real, relevant links most businesses never bother to collect.

**Contribute to real publications.** Not the spammy "write for us and pay us" networks. Actual trade publications and respected blogs in your field that accept expert contributions. One article on a site people in your industry actually read beats fifty links from sites nobody visits.

**Build reference assets.** A page so useful that writers link to it when they need to point somewhere: a definitive glossary, a free calculator, a well-maintained statistics page. Reference assets keep earning links for years without more outreach.

**Run digital PR with original data.** Publish something the press wants to cite: a survey, an analysis of your own data, a benchmark study. Journalists link to primary sources. This is the most work and the most durable payoff.

Paid links, private blog networks, and mass-templated outreach are exactly what [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) target. They can earn a manual action, and even when they do not, they rarely move anything. The whole strategic picture of what does work now is in [link building in 2026](/blog/link-building-strategies-2026/), and whether links still carry weight in AI answers is covered in [do backlinks still matter for AI search](/blog/do-backlinks-matter-ai-search/).

For a specific high-authority tactic, see [how to get .edu backlinks](/blog/how-to-get-edu-backlinks/). The authority signals that make all of this feed AI citations are in [AI citation building](/seo/ai-citation-building/).

Make the asset first. The outreach is the easy part once the thing is worth linking to.

## FAQs

### What is the fastest way to get backlinks?

Reclaiming unlinked mentions is the fastest. Find places that already named your brand or research without linking, and ask the writer to add the link. They have already decided you are worth referencing, so the conversion rate is far higher than cold outreach, and it takes minutes per link.

### Can I buy backlinks to rank faster?

No. Buying links violates Google's spam policies and can trigger a manual action that removes your site from results. Even when purchased links avoid a penalty, they add little lasting value, because search and AI systems weight editorial links that real publishers chose to give. Earned links are the only durable option.

### How many backlinks do I need to rank?

There is no fixed number. A handful of relevant, editorial links from trusted sites in your field outperform hundreds of low-quality links. Relevance and the linking site's authority matter far more than raw count, so the goal is quality and topical fit, not a target quantity.
