The latest SEO trends and algorithm updates focus on user experience, high-quality content, and AI-powered search results. In 2026, Google has accelerated changes that were visible but manageable in 2024 and early 2025. Two major core updates have reshuffled rankings across nearly every vertical. AI Overviews have matured from an experiment into a dominant search surface, now triggering on roughly 48% of all queries. The ranking signals that worked reliably three years ago, keyword density, exact-match anchor text, and thin topical coverage, no longer produce results. What does work is demonstrable expertise, content that directly and comprehensively answers questions, and a technical foundation that makes your pages easy for both users and AI systems to parse. This post covers everything that has changed, why it changed, and the specific actions that will move the needle on your organic visibility in the second half of 2026.

What these numbers reveal together is a fundamental structural change, not a temporary fluctuation. The 80% zero-click rate for AI Overview queries is the headline, but the 4.4x conversion uplift for AI-referred clicks is the counterpoint that changes the strategic calculus entirely. Fewer clicks are arriving from search. The clicks that do arrive convert at dramatically higher rates, because users who click through from an AI Overview have already been pre-qualified by the answer they read.

The collapse in top-10 citation dominance, from 76% to 38% in one year, is equally significant. Google's AI systems are drawing from a much broader source pool than traditional ranking would suggest. Pages ranking in positions 11 through 100 now account for roughly a third of all AI Overview citations. This means your content strategy cannot afford to treat any well-optimized page as "too low-ranking to matter." The AI citation opportunity is distributed, and your entire content library is in play.

The practical implication is that SEO in 2026 requires a two-track mindset: optimizing for traditional ranking to maintain baseline visibility, while simultaneously optimizing for AI source selection to capture the high-conversion AI-referred traffic that is becoming the most valuable slice of organic search.

### The March 2026 core update

The March update launched on March 1 and completed its rollout over approximately two weeks. By the time the dust settled, volatility trackers had recorded it as one of the most turbulent core updates since the helpful content system overhaul of 2023. The defining statistic is stark: 24.1% of pages that held top-10 positions before the update had dropped entirely out of the top 100 by the time the rollout finished.

The pattern of winners and losers was clear. Sites that gained rankings shared a common profile: strong author credentials with verifiable real-world expertise, content that went beyond synthesizing other sources to include original research, proprietary data, or first-hand testing, and tight topical focus rather than broad coverage across disparate subjects. Specialist sites, academic and institutional publishers, and brands with genuine subject-matter depth all saw meaningful gains.

The losers were equally identifiable. Aggregator sites that pulled information from primary sources without adding substantive analysis lost heavily. Sites that had published large volumes of AI-generated content without editorial oversight dropped sharply. Over-optimized pages, articles clearly written around keyword phrases rather than around genuine user intent, saw consistent declines. Google's systems have become significantly better at distinguishing content created for people from content created for search engines.

### The May 2026 core update

The May update began rolling out on May 21 and is still in progress at the time of writing. Early data shows the sharpest initial impact in your money or your life (YMYL) verticals: health, finance, and legal. Within the first 72 hours, these categories showed more ranking movement than any other sector, which is consistent with Google's documented focus on trustworthiness and authority signals for topics where low-quality information can cause real-world harm.

For health and medical content specifically, the early signals suggest that Google is applying stricter scrutiny to whether authors have verifiable clinical credentials, whether claims are supported by cited sources, and whether the content represents a genuine point of view rather than a recombination of existing material. Finance and legal content appear to be under similar pressure. If your site operates in any of these verticals, the May update warrants close monitoring through at least mid-June.

For sites outside YMYL categories, the May update's early footprint is smaller, but the underlying signals are consistent with the March update: content depth, author authority, and topical specialization continue to be rewarded. The [Google May 2026 Core Update](/blog/google-may-2026-core-update/) page tracks the rollout in real time and will be updated as the picture becomes clearer.

## The top 7 SEO trends you need to act on

The algorithm updates above are the acute events. The trends below are the structural shifts that will define organic search through the rest of 2026 and beyond. Each one requires a different tactical response, but together they point toward the same strategic north star: be genuinely useful, genuinely authoritative, and genuinely easy for AI systems to extract and cite.

### 1. Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)

[Answer Engine Optimization](/aeo) is the practice of structuring your content to be selected and cited by AI-powered answer systems, including Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, and other generative search tools. AEO is not a replacement for traditional SEO but an extension of it, focused on the specific patterns that make content extractable and trustworthy to AI citation systems.

The core techniques of AEO include writing clear, direct answers at the top of each section, covering the full question space for a topic rather than just the head query, using structured formatting that AI systems can parse (numbered lists, definition-style prose, explicit question-answer patterns), and ensuring every factual claim is supported by citable evidence. The single biggest AEO lever in 2026 is semantic completeness. Ahrefs research across 863,000 keywords found that content scoring 8.5 out of 10 or higher on semantic completeness is 4.2 times more likely to be cited in AI Overviews.

### 2. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)

[Generative Engine Optimization](/geo) takes AEO a step further by focusing on how your content performs across the full spectrum of generative AI systems, not just Google. GEO addresses the specific behaviors of large language models when they decide which sources to trust and cite: how they evaluate entity associations, how they weight recency and citation patterns, and how they handle conflicting information across sources.

GEO in practice involves building consistent brand and entity signals across your web presence, earning citations from high-authority sources (which train AI models to associate your brand with specific topics), and creating content that covers topics with enough depth that AI systems can draw multiple pieces of extractable information from a single page rather than needing to synthesize across many sources. The goal is to become a primary reference in your category, not just a ranking page.

### 3. E-E-A-T is no longer a differentiator, it is the entry fee

[Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness](/seo/eeat/) used to be a factor that strong sites optimized for to get an edge. In 2026, following two E-E-A-T-focused core updates, it has become a binary gate. Sites that do not demonstrate genuine E-E-A-T signals are not competing for AI citations and are increasingly struggling to hold traditional rankings in competitive verticals.

What this means practically: every piece of content on your site needs a credentialed author with a verifiable, detailed biography. Author credentials need to be specific and real, not vague ("expert in the field"). Content needs to demonstrate first-hand experience wherever relevant, not just report what others have found. Your site needs to earn mentions and links from sources that Google already trusts. And your about page, contact information, and editorial standards documentation need to be complete and easy to find. These are not optional enhancements. They are table stakes.

### 4. Zero-click search: the traffic model has changed

The 80% zero-click rate for AI Overview queries means that [your content strategy needs to account for a fundamentally different traffic model](/blog/google-ai-search-taking-your-content/). For many informational queries, your primary goal is no longer to get the click. It is to get cited. The traffic comes later, when a cited user finds your brand compelling enough to seek you out directly, or when the pre-qualified user who does click converts at 4.4 times the rate of a standard organic visitor.

This shift has real implications for how you measure success. Sessions and pageviews are increasingly poor proxies for the value your content is generating. Branded search volume, direct traffic, and conversion rates from organic traffic are becoming better indicators of whether your content strategy is actually working. It also has implications for content investment: you should be creating content you would be proud to have cited as an authoritative source, because that is increasingly what drives downstream brand and revenue value.

### 5. Video and visual content for AI surfaces

YouTube is now cited in approximately 23% of AI Overviews, making it one of the most frequently referenced sources in Google's generative answers. This is a significant shift that most content strategies have not yet absorbed. If your content exists only as text, you are not competing for roughly a quarter of the AI citation surface.

The practical implication is not that you need to move your entire operation to video. It is that for your highest-value topics, a companion video that covers the core content in a clear, structured way creates an additional citation pathway. YouTube content also benefits from Google's trust in the platform as an entity, from the transcript text that Google indexes, and from the authority signals that come from channel subscribers and watch time. Treat YouTube as a content distribution channel for your expertise, not as a separate content silo.

### 6. Core Web Vitals and page experience remain table stakes

Interaction to Next Paint (INP), Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) remain ranking signals and, more importantly, user experience requirements. Pages that fail Core Web Vitals thresholds lose visitors before they finish reading, which in turn degrades the behavioral signals Google uses to assess content quality.

The 2026 benchmarks for "Good" scores are LCP under 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds, and CLS under 0.1. Run your key pages through <a href="https://pagespeed.web.dev" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google PageSpeed Insights</a> to identify specific issues. The most common problems in 2026 are render-blocking third-party scripts (especially analytics and ad tags), unoptimized image delivery, and layout shifts caused by ads or embeds loading after the initial render. These are fixable problems, and fixing them is a prerequisite for competing in any other area of SEO.

### 7. Structured data and schema markup for AI extraction

[Schema markup](/seo/schema-markup/) is no longer primarily about rich snippets in traditional search results. It has become a key signal that helps AI systems understand the structure, context, and authority of your content. FAQ schema, HowTo schema, Article schema with author markup, and BreadcrumbList schema all help AI Overview systems parse your content more accurately and attribute it to the correct entity.

The most impactful schema investments in 2026 are Author markup (linking to a named person with a verifiable identity), Organization schema (establishing your brand's entity clearly), and Speakable schema (marking the specific passages in your content that directly answer a question). AI systems preferentially extract content from clearly marked, well-structured passages. If your content is not marked up, you are asking AI systems to do more inferential work to use it, and they will often choose a better-marked alternative instead.

## What AI Overviews changed about ranking strategy

The most common misunderstanding about AI Overviews is that citation equals ranking. It does not, and the distinction matters enormously for how you allocate your SEO effort.

Google's AI Overview system draws from a candidate pool that extends well beyond the top-10 ranking pages for any given query. When Google upgraded AI Overviews to Gemini 3 in January 2026, it fundamentally changed the source selection model. The system now decomposes queries into multiple sub-queries, retrieves candidates for each sub-query independently, and then synthesizes across all candidate sets. A page ranking in position 47 for the head query might be the strongest source for one of the sub-queries, and could be cited prominently even though a user searching the head query would never scroll to it organically.

This creates a two-tier strategy that Google has not explicitly confirmed but that the data strongly supports. First, you maintain traditional ranking effort because pages with strong ranking signals are more likely to appear in the candidate pool across multiple sub-queries. Second, you optimize specifically for citation by ensuring your content provides clear, direct, extractable answers to questions in your domain, regardless of where those pages rank for the head query.

The shift from position 1 to source selection is also changing how brand authority compounds over time. Research from BrightEdge shows that brands already cited in AI Overviews receive 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks from those search result pages compared to non-cited pages. The amplification effect is real and meaningful. AI Overviews function as a brand recommendation, not just an information source. When a user reads an AI-generated summary and sees your brand cited as an authoritative source, the click they make later, whether organic or paid, carries stronger commercial intent and converts at higher rates.

The implication for budget allocation is that content you might previously have considered "too informational" to merit significant investment now has a dual return. An authoritative how-to article in your category does not just rank for informational queries. It positions your brand as the expert source that AI systems pull from when users ask questions in your domain. The brand amplification from repeated AI citation compounds over months and creates a brand trust signal that has both search and direct-response value.

Ahrefs data from a study of 863,000 keywords reinforces the depth requirement. Pages that provide comprehensive, multi-faceted coverage of a topic are significantly more likely to be cited across multiple sub-queries, which means they appear in the AI Overview more frequently and with more prominent attribution. Thin content, even if it ranks for a head query, rarely generates AI citation because it cannot satisfy the sub-query decomposition that the Gemini-powered system applies.

## Your 2026 SEO action checklist

The trends and updates above point to a clear set of high-impact actions. Work through these in order, because each layer depends on the one before it.

### Foundation

- **Audit Core Web Vitals for your top 20 pages.** Run each through Google PageSpeed Insights and fix any pages scoring below "Good" on LCP, INP, or CLS. Prioritize pages that generate the most organic traffic.
- **Verify full crawlability and indexation.** Use Google Search Console's Coverage report to identify crawl errors, excluded pages, and indexation gaps. Ensure your most important content is indexed and reachable within three clicks from the homepage.
- **Implement and validate schema markup.** At minimum: Article or WebPage schema on all content pages, Author schema linked to credentialed author profiles, and BreadcrumbList schema for hierarchical navigation. Use Google's Rich Results Test to validate each page.

### Content

- **Audit every page for E-E-A-T signals.** Ensure each piece of content has a named author with a detailed, credentialed bio, that claims are supported by cited sources, and that wherever relevant the content demonstrates genuine first-hand experience rather than secondary synthesis.
- **Run a semantic completeness review on your top content.** For your ten most important pages, map all the questions a user might have about the topic and ensure the page addresses each one. Aim for the 8.5+ semantic completeness threshold Ahrefs identifies as the AI citation tipping point.
- **Build or strengthen topic clusters.** Choose three to five core topics where you want category authority. Create or consolidate content so you have a comprehensive pillar page and three to eight supporting articles for each topic. Internal link systematically from supporting pages to the pillar.

### Authority

- **Update all author bios with verifiable credentials.** Generic bios do not satisfy E-E-A-T requirements after the 2026 core updates. Each bio should include specific credentials, relevant experience, institutional affiliations where applicable, and links to external profiles (LinkedIn, professional organizations).
- **Pursue authoritative external mentions.** Identify the top three to five high-authority publications in your vertical and create a plan to earn genuine editorial mentions. This means producing original research, data, or analysis that those publications would want to cite. Paid placements do not generate the same authority signal as earned editorial coverage.

### AI Visibility

- **Add Speakable schema to your direct-answer passages.** Mark the specific paragraphs on each page that provide clear, extractable answers to likely sub-queries. This reduces the inference burden on AI systems and increases the probability of citation.
- **Create companion videos for your highest-value content.** Start with your top pillar pages. A well-structured 8 to 12-minute YouTube video covering the same material adds an additional citation pathway and captures the 23% of AI Overviews that cite YouTube as a source.
- **Monitor AI Overview citations with a tracking system.** Use available tools (Semrush, BrightEdge, or manual search sampling) to track which of your pages are appearing in AI Overviews for your target queries. Pages that are cited but not ranking in the top 10 reveal topics where you have AI authority but not traditional ranking strength, and vice versa. Both gaps have different remedies.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the biggest SEO trends in 2026?

The biggest SEO trends in 2026 are the rise of AI Overviews as a primary search surface, the shift toward Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), the hardening of E-E-A-T from a differentiator into a baseline requirement, and the growing importance of structured data and schema markup for AI extraction. Zero-click search rates have reached record highs, changing how marketers measure content value. Video content, particularly YouTube, has become a significant AI citation source. Core Web Vitals remain a foundational technical requirement. Taken together, these trends point toward an SEO environment that rewards genuine expertise, comprehensive content, and technical execution more heavily than at any previous point.

### What did Google's 2026 core updates change?

Google's March 2026 core update was one of the most volatile on record, with 24.1% of top-10 ranking pages dropping entirely out of the top 100 during the rollout. The update rewarded specialist sites with verifiable author expertise, original research, and topical focus, while penalizing aggregator sites, mass-produced AI-generated content, and over-optimized pages built primarily for search engines rather than users. The May 2026 core update, still rolling out at publication, has shown particularly sharp early impact in health, finance, and legal verticals, with similar signals favoring E-E-A-T strength and genuine content depth. Both updates reflect Google's ongoing effort to surface content created for people over content created for algorithms.

### Is SEO still worth investing in with AI search growing?

Yes, SEO is worth investing in, but the investment thesis has changed. The volume of searches going to traditional organic clicks is declining for informational queries because AI Overviews answer many questions directly. However, the traffic that does convert from search is converting at higher rates, BrightEdge data shows a 4.4x conversion rate uplift for AI-referred clicks compared to standard organic traffic. SEO has also expanded to include AI citation visibility, which functions as brand-building at scale. Being cited repeatedly in AI Overviews increases branded search volume, direct traffic, and paid click-through rates. The ROI calculation is different from 2022, but the investment still produces strong returns for brands that execute well.

### What is Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)?

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of structuring and formatting content to be selected and cited by AI-powered answer systems, including Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, and similar tools. AEO focuses on semantic completeness (covering all aspects of a topic, not just the head query), extractability (writing clear direct answers that AI systems can pull without heavy inference), and E-E-A-T signals (demonstrating the expertise and trustworthiness that AI citation systems use as a quality gate). AEO is complementary to traditional SEO, not a replacement for it. Pages that rank well and are optimized for AI citation get the best of both surfaces.

### How do I get cited in Google AI Overviews?

Getting cited in Google AI Overviews requires a combination of strong traditional SEO signals and specific AEO optimizations. The most important factors are: semantic completeness (content that covers a topic comprehensively enough to satisfy multiple sub-queries); demonstrated E-E-A-T (verifiable author credentials, cited sources, first-hand experience signals); clear extractable structure (direct answers near the top of each section, structured formatting, explicit question-answer patterns); and schema markup (Article schema, Author schema, and Speakable schema to reduce the inference burden on AI systems). Pages do not need to rank in the top 10 to be cited. Ahrefs data shows only 38% of AI Overview citations come from top-10 pages, so even mid-ranking content can earn citation if it is sufficiently authoritative and well-structured.

### Does E-E-A-T still matter in 2026?

E-E-A-T matters more in 2026 than at any previous point. Following the March and May core updates, Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness has effectively become a binary gate for competitive rankings and AI citation eligibility rather than a differentiating factor among already-good pages. Content that does not demonstrate verifiable author expertise, cite its sources, and show genuine first-hand experience is increasingly filtered out at the candidate stage, both for traditional rankings in competitive verticals and for AI Overview citations. The "Experience" component of E-E-A-T, added in 2022, has become the most important tie-breaker in 2026. When two pages cover the same topic with similar factual accuracy, the one that demonstrates real-world testing, personal insight, or first-hand evidence is consistently preferred by both ranking algorithms and AI citation systems.

### How important are Core Web Vitals in 2026?

Core Web Vitals remain a direct ranking signal and a foundational user experience requirement in 2026. The current threshold for "Good" scores is LCP under 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds, and CLS under 0.1. Pages that fail these thresholds face two compounding penalties: a direct ranking disadvantage from Google's page experience signal, and an indirect disadvantage because poor performance degrades the engagement metrics (time on page, scroll depth, return visits) that Google uses as behavioral quality signals. For AI citation purposes, page speed matters less than content quality and structure, but a page that users abandon due to slow load times generates weak behavioral signals that undermine its overall authority score. Treat Core Web Vitals as a mandatory baseline, not an optional enhancement.

### What is the difference between AEO and GEO?

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) focuses specifically on getting your content cited and extracted by AI-powered answer systems in search results, primarily Google AI Overviews. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is a broader discipline covering how your content and brand perform across the full ecosystem of generative AI tools, including ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and other AI assistants. AEO tactics tend to be more specific to Google's citation selection system: structured content, semantic completeness, extractable answer formatting. GEO tactics focus more on entity-building (ensuring AI training data and real-time retrieval systems associate your brand with specific topics), earning citations in sources that AI models trust and frequently draw from, and creating content that satisfies the information needs of AI systems operating outside a single search engine's ecosystem. In practice, most AEO optimizations also improve GEO performance, but GEO requires additional attention to brand presence across platforms that fall outside Google's direct ecosystem.
