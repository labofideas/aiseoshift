# The Click Was Always a Proxy

For about fifteen years, my job had a number attached to it. Rank higher, get more clicks, win.

Everyone in this business measured their worth in clicks. I did too.

I want to tell you why that number is quietly turning into a lie, and why that might be the best thing to happen to your website in a decade.

Sit with this for a second. Last year, a little over forty percent of Google searches ended without anyone clicking anything at all. Someone typed a question, read the answer right there on the page, and moved on. No visit. No pageview. Nothing for your analytics to count.

When Google's newer AI Mode is doing the answering, that number climbs past ninety percent.

If you came up in SEO the way I did, your stomach just dropped a little. Ninety percent of people getting what they need without ever touching your site sounds like the end of the business.

It isn't. But to see why, you have to let go of an idea you have been holding so long you forgot you were holding it.

The click was never the thing you actually wanted.

## What you were really buying

Think about what a click was for.

You never wanted the click itself. You wanted what the click stood for. A person, paying attention, who might become a customer. The click was just the turnstile they walked through on the way to mattering to you.

For fifteen years that turnstile was the only way in, so we treated it like the destination. We optimized the turnstile. We A/B tested the turnstile. We built entire careers on the turnstile.

And now the building has more doors.

Here is the shift, in plain terms. Search used to work like a phone book. You asked it something, it handed you a list, and you walked yourself over to one of the options. The walking was the click.

Search increasingly works like asking a friend who happens to know everything. You ask, and the friend just tells you. The friend does not hand you ten pamphlets. The friend says, go to this one, the pharmacist there actually knows what she is talking about.

Notice what changed. You are no longer trying to be on the list. You are trying to be the name the friend says out loud.

That is the whole game now. Not ranking. Being repeated.

## Here is where it gets uncomfortable

The uncomfortable part is that being repeated is harder to fake than being ranked.

You could game a list. Stuff some keywords, build some links, climb a few spots. The machine was matching strings of text, and strings of text can be manipulated.

The friend is different. The friend is doing something closer to judgment. It reads everything, decides who it trusts, and only repeats the sources it would not be embarrassed to have recommended.

So the question stops being how do I climb, and becomes something much older and much more demanding. Why would anyone trust you enough to repeat you.

When I explain this to people who have done SEO for years, I watch them get quiet. Because they already know the answer, and the answer is more work than the old one.

You earn it the way humans have always earned it. You actually know something. You have done the thing you are writing about. You say something true that the other ten sites are too cautious or too lazy to say. You put a real name on it, a person who can be checked, instead of hiding behind a faceless brand.

None of that is new advice. It is the oldest advice there is. What is new is that the machine now rewards it directly instead of rewarding the people who were best at faking it.

I find that strangely hopeful.

## The traffic question everyone is afraid to ask

Let me say the scary thing out loud, because pretending otherwise helps no one.

Your raw traffic is probably going to go down. For a lot of sites it already has. If your entire business is selling ads against pageviews, that is a real problem and you need a different plan.

But for most of us, traffic was never the point either. It was another proxy, one more turnstile we mistook for the destination.

And here is the part that surprised even me. The visitors who do still come, the ones an AI engine actually sends your way, tend to arrive warmer. The engine already understood what they wanted. It already decided you were a fit. By the time they reach you, half the convincing is done.

Fewer people, further along. I will take that trade most days of the week.

So the real move is not to fight for the clicks that are leaving. It is to become the source the engine trusts enough to put your name in its mouth, and then to give the people who do arrive somewhere worth going.

That means owning the things no engine can take from you. Your email list. Your community. The reason someone types your name directly instead of asking a question and hoping you come up. When the machine sits between you and your customer, the direct relationship is the one asset nobody can disintermediate, which is a long word that just means get in between.

## What I would actually do

I am wary of tidy advice, so I will keep this to what I would do if it were my site, starting Monday.

I would stop asking where I rank and start asking whether I get mentioned. I would type my own important questions into Google, into ChatGPT, into Gemini, and see whether my name comes up and who it comes up next to. That is my real scoreboard now.

I would make my best pages easy to quote. Answer the question in the first two sentences, then earn the depth underneath. An engine cannot repeat a point you buried under six paragraphs of warming up.

I would put a real human name on everything, with credentials someone could verify, because trust is the currency now and anonymity spends like a counterfeit bill.

And I would publish at least one thing my competitors cannot, because it came from my own data or my own experience. That is the part a machine cannot generate on its own, which means it is the part it has to come to you for.

For fifteen years we optimized the turnstile. The turnstile is leaving. What it was guarding, attention from people who might trust you, is still right there, waiting for whoever is willing to earn it the slow way.

The click is dying. Being worth repeating never will.
